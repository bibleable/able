## Acts 17:1
They passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia and came to Thessalonica. A Jewish synagogue was there. 📖

## Acts 17:2
Paul entered the synagogue as usual. For three Sabbaths, he reasoned with the people from Scripture. 📖

## Acts 17:3
He explained and proved that the Christ had to suffer and rise from death. He declared, “The Jesus I proclaim to you is the Christ.” 📖

## Acts 17:4
Some Jews were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas. So did many Greeks who feared God and many leading women. 📖

## Acts 17:5
The Jews became jealous. They gathered troublemakers from the marketplace, formed a mob, and caused an uproar in the city. They raided Jason’s house, seeking Paul and Silas to bring them before the people. 📖

## Acts 17:6
They did not find them. They dragged Jason and some other brothers before the city officials. They shouted, “These men have turned the world upside down! Now they have come here. 📖

## Acts 17:7
Jason welcomed them into his home. They all defy Caesar’s decrees. They say another king exists, named Jesus!” 📖

## Acts 17:8
These words greatly disturbed the crowd and city officials. 📖

## Acts 17:9
The officials collected bond from Jason and the others, then released them. 📖

## Acts 17:10
As soon as night came, the brothers sent Paul and Silas to Berea. They arrived and entered the Jewish synagogue. 📖

## Acts 17:11
The Bereans were more noble in mind than the Thessalonians. They eagerly accepted the message. They examined Scripture daily to see whether these teachings were true. 📖

## Acts 17:12
Many believed as a result. Many prominent Greek women and men also believed. 📖

## Acts 17:13
The Thessalonian Jews heard that Paul also proclaimed God’s word in Berea. They went there to stir up and disturb the crowds. 📖

## Acts 17:14
The brothers immediately sent Paul to the coast. Silas and Timothy stayed in Berea. 📖

## Acts 17:15
Paul’s escorts brought him to Athens. Then they returned with instructions for Silas and Timothy to join him as soon as possible. 📖

## Acts 17:16
Paul waited in Athens. Seeing the city full of idols deeply disturbed his spirit. 📖

## Acts 17:17
He reasoned in the synagogue with Jews and Gentiles who feared God. Each day, he also reasoned with people he met in the marketplace. 📖

## Acts 17:18
Epicurean and Stoic philosophers debated with him. Some asked, “What is this babbler trying to say?” Others said, “He seems to promote foreign gods.” They said this because he proclaimed the good news of Jesus and the resurrection. 📖

## Acts 17:19
They took Paul to the Areopagus and asked, “May we know about this new teaching you present? 📖

## Acts 17:20
You bring strange ideas to our ears. We want to know what they mean.” 📖

## Acts 17:21
The Athenians and foreigners living there spent their time hearing and discussing new ideas. 📖

## Acts 17:22
Paul stood in the Areopagus meeting. He said, “Men of Athens, I see you are very religious in every way. 📖

## Acts 17:23
I walked around and examined your objects of worship. I even found an altar inscribed, ‘TO AN UNKNOWN GOD.’ What you worship as unknown, I now proclaim to you. 📖

## Acts 17:24
God made the world and everything in it. He is Lord of heaven and earth. He does not live in temples made by human hands. 📖

## Acts 17:25
Human hands do not serve Him as though He needed anything. He Himself gives everyone life, breath, and everything else. 📖

## Acts 17:26
From one man, He made every nation to live across the whole earth. He determined their appointed times and their lands’ boundaries. 📖

## Acts 17:27
He intended them to seek Him and perhaps reach out and find Him. Yet He is not far from each of us. 📖

## Acts 17:28
‘In Him we live, move, and have our being.’ Some of your own poets said, ‘We are His offspring.’ 📖

## Acts 17:29
We are God’s offspring. Therefore, we should not think the Divine Being resembles gold, silver, or stone shaped by human skill and imagination. 📖

## Acts 17:30
God overlooked the ignorance of earlier times. Now He commands everyone everywhere to repent. 📖

## Acts 17:31
He set a day to judge the world justly through the Man He appointed. He gave everyone proof by raising Him from death.” 📖

## Acts 17:32
Some mocked him when they heard about the resurrection of the dead. Others said, “We want to hear you speak about this again.” 📖

## Acts 17:33
Paul then left the Areopagus. 📖

## Acts 17:34
Some joined him and believed. Among them were Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others with them. 📖
