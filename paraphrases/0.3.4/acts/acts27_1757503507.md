## Acts 27:1
The decision was made for us to sail to Italy. Paul and other prisoners were handed to Julius, a centurion of the Imperial Regiment. 📖

## Acts 27:2
We boarded an Adramyttian ship sailing to ports along Asia’s coast and put out to sea. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, accompanied us. 📖

## Acts 27:3
The next day, we landed at Sidon. Julius treated Paul kindly. He let him visit his friends and receive their care. 📖

## Acts 27:4
We sailed from there on Cyprus’ sheltered side because the winds opposed us. 📖

## Acts 27:5
We crossed the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia. We reached Myra in Lycia. 📖

## Acts 27:6
The centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing to Italy. He put us aboard. 📖

## Acts 27:7
After many days of slow sailing, we arrived off Cnidus. The wind blocked our progress. We sailed on Crete’s sheltered side, opposite Salmone. 📖

## Acts 27:8
We moved along the coast with difficulty. We reached Fair Havens, near Lasea. 📖

## Acts 27:9
Much time had passed. The voyage was dangerous because the Fast had already passed. Paul advised them. 📖

## Acts 27:10
“Men, I see disaster and great loss ahead on this voyage. Ship, cargo, and our lives are at risk.” 📖

## Acts 27:11
But the centurion followed the pilot and shipowner’s advice instead of Paul’s. 📖

## Acts 27:12
The harbor was unsuitable for winter. Most decided to sail on, hoping to reach Phoenix and spend winter there. Phoenix was a Cretan harbor facing southwest and northwest. 📖

## Acts 27:13
A gentle south wind began blowing. They thought their opportunity had come. They raised anchor and sailed close along Crete’s coast. 📖

## Acts 27:14
Soon, a cyclone called the Northeaster swept down across the island. 📖

## Acts 27:15
The wind caught the ship. We could not sail into it. We gave way and let it drive us along. 📖

## Acts 27:16
We passed on the sheltered side of a small island, Cauda. We barely managed to secure the lifeboat. 📖

## Acts 27:17
The crew lifted it aboard and tied ropes under the ship to support it. They feared grounding on Syrtis’ sandbars. They lowered the sea anchor and let the wind drive them along. 📖

## Acts 27:18
The storm tossed us violently. The next day, the men began throwing cargo overboard. 📖

## Acts 27:19
On the third day, they threw the ship’s tackle overboard with their own hands. 📖

## Acts 27:20
For many days, we saw neither sun nor stars. The great storm kept battering us. We lost all hope of being saved. 📖

## Acts 27:21
The men had gone a long time without food. Paul stood among them. He said, “Men, you should have followed my advice and stayed in Crete. You would have avoided this disaster and loss. 📖

## Acts 27:22
Now I urge you to take courage. No lives will be lost. Only the ship will be lost. 📖

## Acts 27:23
Last night, an angel stood beside me. He came from the God to whom I belong and whom I serve. 📖

## Acts 27:24
He said, ‘Do not fear, Paul. You must stand before Caesar. God has granted you the lives of everyone sailing with you.’ 📖

## Acts 27:25
Take courage, men. I believe God. Everything will happen just as He told me. 📖

## Acts 27:26
But we must run aground on an island.” 📖

## Acts 27:27
On the fourteenth night, we were still being driven across the Adriatic Sea. About midnight, the sailors sensed land was near. 📖

## Acts 27:28
They measured the water’s depth at twenty fathoms. A little farther on, they measured again. It was fifteen fathoms deep. 📖

## Acts 27:29
They feared grounding on rocks. They dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for dawn. 📖

## Acts 27:30
The sailors tried to escape from the ship. They lowered the lifeboat into the sea, pretending to lower anchors from the bow. 📖

## Acts 27:31
Paul told the centurion and soldiers, “Unless these men stay with the ship, you cannot be saved.” 📖

## Acts 27:32
The soldiers cut the lifeboat’s ropes and let it drift away. 📖

## Acts 27:33
Until dawn, Paul kept urging everyone to eat. He said, “Today is your fourteenth day waiting anxiously without eating anything. 📖

## Acts 27:34
Eat something for your own preservation. Not one hair on your heads will be lost.” 📖

## Acts 27:35
After saying this, Paul took bread and thanked God before everyone. He broke it and began eating. 📖

## Acts 27:36
Everyone took courage and ate some food. 📖

## Acts 27:37
There were 276 of us aboard. 📖

## Acts 27:38
After eating enough, the men lightened the ship by throwing the grain into the sea. 📖

## Acts 27:39
At dawn, they did not recognize the land. They saw a bay with a sandy beach. They decided to run the ship onto it if possible. 📖

## Acts 27:40
They cut the anchors free and left them in the sea. They also loosened the rudder ropes. Then they raised the foresail to the wind and headed toward the beach. 📖

## Acts 27:41
The ship hit a sandbar and ran aground. The bow stuck firmly and would not move. The waves began breaking the stern apart. 📖

## Acts 27:42
The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners. They wanted to prevent any from swimming away and escaping. 📖

## Acts 27:43
The centurion wanted to save Paul’s life, so he stopped their plan. He ordered those who could swim to jump overboard first and reach land. 📖

## Acts 27:44
The others would follow on planks and other ship parts. Everyone reached land safely this way. 📖
