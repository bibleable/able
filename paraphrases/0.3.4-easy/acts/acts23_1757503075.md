# Acts 23 — A plot discovered

**About this chapter**

Paul faces the council, receives encouragement from the Lord, and escapes a murder plot under armed guard.

## Acts 23:1

Looking directly at the Sanhedrin, Paul said, “Brothers, I lived before God with a good conscience until today.” 📖

## Acts 23:2

The high priest Ananias ordered nearby men to strike his mouth. 📖

## Acts 23:3

Paul said, “God will strike you, whitewashed wall! 📖

You judge by law but break it by ordering me struck.”

**Help:** Whitewashed wall is a picture of an outwardly good appearance hiding wrongdoing.

## Acts 23:4

Those nearby said, “How dare you insult God's high priest!” 📖

## Acts 23:5

Paul replied, “Brothers, I did not know he was high priest; Scripture forbids speaking evil of your people's ruler.” 📖

## Acts 23:6

Knowing the council included Sadducees and Pharisees, Paul cried, “I am a Pharisee, a Pharisee's son. 📖

I stand trial for my hope in resurrection.”

## Acts 23:7

The Pharisees and Sadducees disputed, dividing the assembly. 📖

## Acts 23:8

Sadducees deny resurrection, angels, and spirits; Pharisees accept them all. 📖

## Acts 23:9

An uproar began, and Pharisee law experts argued, “We find nothing wrong; perhaps a spirit or angel spoke to him.” 📖

## Acts 23:10

The commander feared the violent dispute would tear Paul apart and ordered soldiers to take him forcibly into the barracks. 📖

## Acts 23:11

Next night, the Lord stood beside Paul, saying, “Take courage; you must witness in Rome as you did in Jerusalem.” 📖

## Acts 23:12

At dawn, Jews plotted, swearing not to eat or drink until they killed Paul. 📖

## Acts 23:13

More than forty joined the plot. 📖

## Acts 23:14

They told chief priests and elders, “We swore a solemn oath to eat nothing until we kill Paul. 📖

## Acts 23:15

Ask with the Sanhedrin for his return, pretending you will examine his case more carefully. 📖

We are ready to kill him on the way.”

## Acts 23:16

Paul's sister's son heard the plot, entered the barracks, and told Paul. 📖

## Acts 23:17

Paul asked a centurion to take the young man to the commander with his news. 📖

## Acts 23:18

He brought him, saying, “Paul the prisoner asked me to bring him; he has something to tell you.” 📖

## Acts 23:19

Taking his hand, the commander drew him aside and asked what he needed to say. 📖

## Acts 23:20

He answered, “The Jews will ask you to bring Paul to the Sanhedrin tomorrow, pretending to seek more information. 📖

## Acts 23:21

Do not agree; more than forty men wait to ambush him, sworn not to eat or drink until killing him. 📖

They are ready, awaiting your consent.”

## Acts 23:22

The commander dismissed him, saying, “Tell no one you reported this.” 📖

## Acts 23:23

He called two centurions and gave orders. 📖

“Prepare two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen for Caesarea at the night's third hour.

## Acts 23:24

Provide mounts to bring Paul safely to Governor Felix.” 📖

## Acts 23:25

He wrote this letter. 📖

## Acts 23:26

“From Claudius Lysias to His Excellency Governor Felix: greetings. 📖

## Acts 23:27

Jews seized this man and nearly killed him; I rescued him with troops, having learned he was Roman. 📖

**About this letter:** The commander describes his actions here; the earlier account gives the sequence in Acts 21–22.

## Acts 23:28

I brought him to their Sanhedrin to understand the accusations. 📖

## Acts 23:29

They concerned their law, with no charge deserving death or prison. 📖

## Acts 23:30

Hearing a plot, I sent him immediately and told his accusers to present their case to you.” 📖

## Acts 23:31

Obeying, soldiers took Paul by night to Antipatris. 📖

## Acts 23:32

Next day, they returned to barracks, leaving the horsemen to escort him. 📖

## Acts 23:33

At Caesarea, the horsemen delivered the letter and presented Paul to the governor. 📖

## Acts 23:34

Reading it, he asked Paul's province and learned it was Cilicia. 📖

## Acts 23:35

He said, “I will hear you when your accusers come,” and ordered him guarded in Herod's Praetorium. 📖

**Help:** A praetorium was the governor's residence or headquarters.
