# Acts 27 — Storm and shipwreck

**About this chapter**

Paul sails towards Italy as a prisoner.

A storm destroys the ship, but everyone reaches land alive.

## Acts 27:1

For our voyage to Italy, Paul and other prisoners were handed to Julius, a centurion of the Imperial Regiment. 📖

## Acts 27:2

We boarded an Adramyttian ship bound for Asia's coastal ports, with Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica. 📖

## Acts 27:3

Next day at Sidon, Julius kindly let Paul visit friends for care. 📖

## Acts 27:4

Because the wind opposed us, we sailed in Cyprus's shelter. 📖

## Acts 27:5

Crossing open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we reached Myra in Lycia. 📖

## Acts 27:6

The centurion put us aboard an Alexandrian ship bound for Italy. 📖

## Acts 27:7

Slow sailing brought us near Cnidus after many days; blocked by wind, we sheltered along Crete opposite Salmone. 📖

## Acts 27:8

Following the coast with difficulty, we reached Fair Havens near Lasea. 📖

## Acts 27:9

Much time passed, and sailing was dangerous because the Fast had ended, so Paul warned them. 📖

**Help:** The Fast refers to a Jewish fasting observance used here to mark the season.

## Acts 27:10

“Men, I foresee disaster and great loss for ship, cargo, and our lives.” 📖

## Acts 27:11

The centurion trusted the pilot and shipowner rather than Paul. 📖

## Acts 27:12

The harbour was unsuitable for winter, so most decided to sail on, hoping to reach Phoenix. 📖

That harbour in Crete faced southwest and northwest.

## Acts 27:13

A gentle south wind seemed their opportunity; they raised anchor and sailed close along Crete. 📖

## Acts 27:14

A violent storm called the Northeaster soon swept down from the island. 📖

## Acts 27:15

Caught and unable to face the wind, we let the ship drift. 📖

## Acts 27:16

Sheltering behind little Cauda, we barely secured the lifeboat. 📖

## Acts 27:17

The crew hoisted it and reinforced the ship underneath with ropes. 📖

Fearing Syrtis's sandbars, they lowered the sea anchor and drifted.

**Help:** A sea anchor slows a drifting ship.

## Acts 27:18

We were tossed violently; next day the men began throwing cargo overboard. 📖

## Acts 27:19

On the third day, they threw the ship's equipment overboard with their own hands. 📖

## Acts 27:20

With neither sun nor stars for many days and the storm raging, we lost all hope of rescue. 📖

## Acts 27:21

After a long time without food, Paul said, “You should have listened and stayed in Crete, avoiding this loss. 📖

## Acts 27:22

Take courage; no life will be lost, only the ship. 📖

## Acts 27:23

Last night, an angel of the God I belong to and serve stood beside me. 📖

## Acts 27:24

He said, ‘Do not fear, Paul; you must stand before Caesar, and God granted you everyone sailing with you.’ 📖

## Acts 27:25

Take courage; I believe God will do exactly what he told me. 📖

## Acts 27:26

But we must run aground on an island.” 📖

## Acts 27:27

On the fourteenth night, still driven across the Adriatic Sea, sailors sensed land about midnight. 📖

## Acts 27:28

They measured water twenty fathoms deep, then farther on fifteen fathoms. 📖

**Help:** A fathom measures water depth.

## Acts 27:29

Fearing rocks, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for dawn. 📖

**Help:** Stern means the ship's rear; bow means its front.

## Acts 27:30

Sailors lowered the lifeboat to escape, pretending they would lower anchors from the bow. 📖

## Acts 27:31

Paul told the centurion and soldiers, “Unless these men stay aboard, you cannot be saved.” 📖

## Acts 27:32

The soldiers cut the lifeboat's ropes and let it drift away. 📖

## Acts 27:33

Until dawn, Paul urged everyone to eat, saying, “This is your fourteenth day waiting anxiously without food. 📖

## Acts 27:34

Eat for your survival; not one hair of your head will be lost.” 📖

## Acts 27:35

Before everyone, Paul took bread, thanked God, broke it, and began eating. 📖

## Acts 27:36

Encouraged, everyone ate. 📖

## Acts 27:37

There were 276 of us aboard. 📖

## Acts 27:38

After eating their fill, they threw grain into the sea to lighten the ship. 📖

## Acts 27:39

At dawn, they saw unfamiliar land and a sandy bay, deciding to beach the ship if possible. 📖

## Acts 27:40

They cut the anchors away, left them in the sea, and loosened the rudders' ropes. 📖

Raising the foresail, they headed for the beach.

**Help:** Rudders steer a ship; a foresail is its front sail.

## Acts 27:41

The ship struck a sandbar; its bow stuck fast, and waves broke the stern apart. 📖

## Acts 27:42

The soldiers planned to kill prisoners so none could swim away. 📖

## Acts 27:43

Wanting Paul alive, the centurion stopped them and ordered swimmers to jump first and reach land. 📖

## Acts 27:44

The others followed on planks and ship parts, and everyone reached land safely. 📖
