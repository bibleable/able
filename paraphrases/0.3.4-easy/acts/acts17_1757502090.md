# Acts 17 — Thessalonica, Berea, and Athens

**About this chapter**

Paul and Silas teach amid belief and opposition.

In Athens, Paul speaks about the Creator, repentance, and resurrection.

## Acts 17:1

They passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia to Thessalonica, where there was a Jewish synagogue. 📖

## Acts 17:2

As usual, Paul went in and reasoned from Scripture on three Sabbaths. 📖

## Acts 17:3

He explained and proved the Christ must suffer and rise from death, saying, “This Jesus I preach is the Christ.” 📖

## Acts 17:4

Some Jews were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas, with many God-fearing Greeks and quite a few leading women. 📖

## Acts 17:5

Jealous Jews gathered marketplace troublemakers, formed a mob, and stirred the city. 📖

They raided Jason's house, seeking Paul and Silas to bring them before the people.

## Acts 17:6

Not finding Paul and Silas, they dragged Jason and other believers before the officials. 📖

They shouted, “The men turning the world upside down have come here!

## Acts 17:7

Jason welcomed them; they defy Caesar's decrees, claiming another king, Jesus!” 📖

**About these words:** These are the mob's accusations, rather than the narrator's judgment of the believers.

## Acts 17:8

The crowd and officials were deeply disturbed. 📖

## Acts 17:9

They took bond money from Jason and the others and released them. 📖

**Help:** Bond is money given as security to obtain release.

## Acts 17:10

That night, believers sent Paul and Silas to Berea, where they entered the Jewish synagogue. 📖

## Acts 17:11

The Bereans showed a better attitude than the Thessalonians. 📖

They eagerly received the message and checked Scripture daily to see whether the teachings were true.

## Acts 17:12

Many believed, including quite a few prominent Greek women and men. 📖

## Acts 17:13

Hearing Paul preached in Berea, Thessalonica's Jews came and stirred up its crowds. 📖

## Acts 17:14

The believers immediately sent Paul to the coast, while Silas and Timothy stayed. 📖

## Acts 17:15

His escorts brought him to Athens and returned with orders for Silas and Timothy to join him quickly. 📖

## Acts 17:16

Waiting in Athens, Paul was deeply troubled to see the city full of idols. 📖

## Acts 17:17

He reasoned with Jews and God-fearing Gentiles in the synagogue, and daily with people in the marketplace. 📖

## Acts 17:18

Epicurean and Stoic philosophers debated him, some asking, “What does this babbler mean?” 📖

Others said he promoted foreign gods, because he preached Jesus and resurrection.

**Help:** Epicureans and Stoics followed different schools of philosophy, or thought about life.

Resurrection means being raised from death.

## Acts 17:19

They brought him to the Areopagus, asking, “What is this new teaching? 📖

**Help:** The Areopagus was an Athenian council and the place associated with its meetings.

## Acts 17:20

Your ideas sound strange, and we want to know their meaning.” 📖

## Acts 17:21

Athenians and resident foreigners spent their time hearing and discussing new ideas. 📖

## Acts 17:22

Paul stood in the Areopagus meeting and said, “Athenians, I see you are very religious in every way. 📖

## Acts 17:23

I examined your worship objects and found an altar marked TO AN UNKNOWN GOD. 📖

I now proclaim what you worship without knowing.

## Acts 17:24

God made the world and everything in it. 📖

He is Lord of heaven and earth and does not live in temples made by human hands.

## Acts 17:25

Human hands do not serve him as though he needs anything; he gives everyone life, breath, and everything else. 📖

## Acts 17:26

From one man, he made every nation to inhabit all earth, fixing their times and land boundaries. 📖

## Acts 17:27

He intended people to seek him, perhaps reaching out and finding him, though he is not far from each person. 📖

## Acts 17:28

‘In him we live, move, and exist.’ 📖

Your poets also said, ‘We are his offspring.’

## Acts 17:29

As God's offspring, we must not picture him as gold, silver, or stone shaped by human skill and imagination. 📖

**Help:** Offspring means children or descendants.

## Acts 17:30

God overlooked earlier ignorance, but now commands everyone everywhere to repent. 📖

**Help:** Repent means turn from sin towards God.

## Acts 17:31

He fixed a day to judge the world justly through his appointed Man. 📖

He proved this to everyone by raising him from death.”

## Acts 17:32

Hearing resurrection, some mocked, but others said, “We want to hear more about this.” 📖

## Acts 17:33

Paul left the Areopagus. 📖

## Acts 17:34

Some joined him and believed, including Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others. 📖

**Help:** Dionysius belonged to the Areopagus.
