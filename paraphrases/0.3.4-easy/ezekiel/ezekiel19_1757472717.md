# Ezekiel 19 — A lament for Israel’s princes

**About this chapter**

Israel’s rulers are pictured first as lions, then as branches of a vine.

The poem describes violence, captivity, and the loss of a ruler’s scepter.

## Ezekiel 19:1

“Take up a lament for Israel’s princes, 📖

**Help with lament**

A lament is a song or statement of grief.

## Ezekiel 19:2

“and say, ‘What was your mother? 📖

A lioness among lions!

She lay among young lions and raised her cubs.

## Ezekiel 19:3

“‘She raised one cub into a young lion. 📖

He learned to tear prey and ate men.

## Ezekiel 19:4

“‘The nations heard of him and trapped him in their pit. 📖

They led him with hooks to Egypt.

## Ezekiel 19:5

“‘She saw her waiting was useless and her hope lost. 📖

She took another cub and made him a young lion.

## Ezekiel 19:6

“‘He prowled among lions and became a young lion. 📖

He learned to tear prey and ate men.

## Ezekiel 19:7

“‘He destroyed their fortresses and ruined their cities. 📖

The land and everything in it shook at his roar.

## Ezekiel 19:8

“‘Nations from provinces on every side came against him. 📖

They spread their net and trapped him in their pit.

## Ezekiel 19:9

“‘They caged him with hooks and brought him to Babylon’s king. 📖

They took him captive so his roar was no longer heard on Israel’s mountains.

## Ezekiel 19:10

“‘Your mother was like a vine in your vineyard, planted beside water. 📖

It bore fruit and many branches because of plentiful water.

## Ezekiel 19:11

“‘Its strong branches were fit for a ruler’s scepter. 📖

It rose above the thick branches, noticeable for its height and dense leaves.

**Help with scepter**

A scepter is a rod carried as a sign of royal authority.

## Ezekiel 19:12

“‘But fury uprooted it and threw it to the ground. 📖

The east wind dried its fruit, and its strong branches were stripped, withered, and burned.

## Ezekiel 19:13

“‘Now it is planted in wilderness, in dry and thirsty land. 📖

## Ezekiel 19:14

“‘Fire came from its main branch and consumed its fruit. 📖

No strong branch fit for a ruler’s scepter remains.’

This is a lament and must be used as one.”
