# Ecclesiastes 2 — Pleasure, work, wisdom, and death

**About this chapter**

The Teacher recounts his search for enjoyment and achievement.

He reflects on death, inheritance, and enjoyment as God’s gift.

## Ecclesiastes 2:1

I told myself, “Come, I will test you with pleasure. 📖

Enjoy what is good!”

But this proved futile.

## Ecclesiastes 2:2

I said about laughter, “It is foolishness.” 📖

I said about pleasure, “What does it achieve?”

## Ecclesiastes 2:3

I tried cheering my body with wine and embracing foolishness while wisdom still guided my mind. 📖

I wanted to see what was worthwhile for people during their few days under heaven.

## Ecclesiastes 2:4

I enlarged my projects. 📖

I built houses and planted vineyards for myself.

## Ecclesiastes 2:5

I made gardens and parks. 📖

I planted every kind of fruit tree there.

## Ecclesiastes 2:6

I built reservoirs to water my groves of growing trees. 📖

**Help with reservoirs and groves:** Places storing water, and groups of trees.

## Ecclesiastes 2:7

I acquired male and female servants. 📖

Servants were also born in my house.

My herds and flocks exceeded everyone’s in Jerusalem before me.

## Ecclesiastes 2:8

I gathered silver, gold, and treasures of kings and provinces. 📖

I acquired male and female singers and human delights: many concubines.

**Help with concubines:** Women in sexual household relationships without the full status of wives.

## Ecclesiastes 2:9

I became greater than everyone in Jerusalem before me. 📖

My wisdom stayed with me.

## Ecclesiastes 2:10

I denied myself nothing my eyes desired. 📖

I refused my heart no pleasure.

My heart delighted in all my work.

This was the reward of all my labor.

## Ecclesiastes 2:11

Yet I considered everything my hands achieved and all my hard work. 📖

Everything was futile, a pursuit of wind.

There was no gain under the sun.

**Help with futile and pursuit of wind:** Producing no lasting gain, pictured as chasing something that cannot be held.

## Ecclesiastes 2:12

I turned to consider wisdom, madness, and foolishness. 📖

What more can the next king do than what has already been done?

## Ecclesiastes 2:13

I saw that wisdom is better than foolishness, as light is better than darkness. 📖

## Ecclesiastes 2:14

A wise man has eyes in his head. 📖

A fool walks in darkness.

Yet I realized the same fate comes to both.

## Ecclesiastes 2:15

I said, “The fool’s fate will happen to me too. 📖

What have I gained by being wise?”

I told myself this too is futile.

## Ecclesiastes 2:16

Wise people are not remembered forever, just like fools. 📖

Both will be forgotten in coming days.

Sadly, a wise man dies just like a fool!

## Ecclesiastes 2:17

So I hated life. 📖

Work under the sun grieved me.

Everything is futile and chasing the wind.

## Ecclesiastes 2:18

I hated all my work under the sun. 📖

I must leave it to the man after me.

## Ecclesiastes 2:19

Who knows whether he will be wise or foolish? 📖

Yet he will control everything I worked skillfully for under the sun.

This too is futile.

## Ecclesiastes 2:20

My heart began to lose hope over all my work under the sun. 📖

## Ecclesiastes 2:21

A man works with wisdom, knowledge, and skill. 📖

Yet he must give his share to someone who did not work for it.

This too is futile and a great evil.

## Ecclesiastes 2:22

What does a man gain from all his hard work and striving under the sun? 📖

## Ecclesiastes 2:23

All his days are full of grief. 📖

His task brings sorrow.

Even at night his mind does not rest.

This too is futile.

## Ecclesiastes 2:24

Nothing is better for a man than eating, drinking, and enjoying his work. 📖

I saw that this too comes from God’s hand.

## Ecclesiastes 2:25

Who can eat or find enjoyment apart from Him? 📖

## Ecclesiastes 2:26

God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy to the person pleasing Him. 📖

But He gives sinners the task of gathering and storing for someone pleasing God.

This too is futile and chasing wind.
