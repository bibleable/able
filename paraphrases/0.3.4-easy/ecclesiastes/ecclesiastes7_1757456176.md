# Ecclesiastes 7 — Wisdom, sorrow, and human limits

**About this chapter**

The Teacher gives difficult observations about wisdom, wrongdoing, and death.

His statements about men and women remain attributed to him.

## Ecclesiastes 7:1

A good name is better than fine perfume. 📖

A person’s death day is better than his birth day.

## Ecclesiastes 7:2

Better to enter a mourning house than a feasting house. 📖

Death ends everyone’s life.

Living people should take this to heart.

## Ecclesiastes 7:3

Sorrow is better than laughter. 📖

A sad face is good for the heart.

## Ecclesiastes 7:4

A wise heart is in the mourning house. 📖

But fools’ hearts are in the pleasure house.

## Ecclesiastes 7:5

Better to hear a wise man’s rebuke than fools’ songs. 📖

**Help with rebuke:** Strong correction.

## Ecclesiastes 7:6

A fool’s laughter is like thorns crackling beneath a pot. 📖

This too is futile.

**Help with this image:** The noisy burning thorns picture laughter without lasting gain.

## Ecclesiastes 7:7

Extortion surely makes a wise man foolish. 📖

A bribe corrupts the heart.

**Help with extortion and bribe:** Taking through threats, and a gift used to make someone act dishonestly.

## Ecclesiastes 7:8

A matter’s end is better than its beginning. 📖

A patient spirit is better than a proud one.

## Ecclesiastes 7:9

Do not become angry quickly in your spirit. 📖

Anger settles in a fool’s lap.

## Ecclesiastes 7:10

Do not ask, “Why were old days better than these?” 📖

Asking this is unwise.

## Ecclesiastes 7:11

Wisdom, like an inheritance, is good. 📖

It benefits people who see the sun.

## Ecclesiastes 7:12

Wisdom is a shelter, like money. 📖

Knowledge has this advantage: wisdom preserves its owner’s life.

## Ecclesiastes 7:13

Consider God’s work. 📖

Who can straighten what He bent?

## Ecclesiastes 7:14

Be joyful in prosperous days. 📖

But in adversity, consider that God made both.

A man cannot discover anything coming after him.

## Ecclesiastes 7:15

I saw both in my futile life. 📖

A righteous man died in his righteousness.

A wicked man lived long in his wickedness.

**Help with righteous:** Right before God.

## Ecclesiastes 7:16

Do not be overly righteous or make yourself too wise. 📖

Why destroy yourself?

## Ecclesiastes 7:17

Do not be excessively wicked or be a fool. 📖

Why die before your time?

## Ecclesiastes 7:18

Grasp one warning without letting the other slip from your hand. 📖

Anyone fearing God will follow both.

**Help with fearing God:** Respecting Him and taking His authority seriously.

The preceding difficult warnings are retained without replacing them with a different rule.

## Ecclesiastes 7:19

Wisdom makes a wise man stronger than ten city rulers. 📖

## Ecclesiastes 7:20

No righteous man on earth does good and never sins. 📖

## Ecclesiastes 7:21

Do not attend to every spoken word. 📖

You may hear your servant cursing you.

## Ecclesiastes 7:22

Your heart knows you have often cursed others yourself. 📖

## Ecclesiastes 7:23

I tested all this by wisdom, saying, “I will be wise.” 📖

But it was beyond me.

## Ecclesiastes 7:24

What exists is far away and very deep. 📖

Who can understand it?

## Ecclesiastes 7:25

I set my mind to understand, explore, and seek wisdom and explanations. 📖

I sought to understand wickedness’s stupidity and madness’s foolishness.

## Ecclesiastes 7:26

I find the woman who is a snare more bitter than death. 📖

Her heart is a net, and her hands are chains.

A man pleasing God escapes her, but a sinner is caught.

**Help with snare, net, and chains:** Pictures of being trapped and held.

## Ecclesiastes 7:27

The Teacher says, “Look, I found this by adding one thing to another to seek an explanation. 📖

## Ecclesiastes 7:28

My soul still searched without finding. 📖

Among a thousand, I found one upright man.

But among all these I found no such woman.

**About this statement:** The Teacher reports his search and observation.

It is not an editorial claim that women have less worth than men.

## Ecclesiastes 7:29

This alone I found: God made humanity upright. 📖

But people sought many schemes.”
