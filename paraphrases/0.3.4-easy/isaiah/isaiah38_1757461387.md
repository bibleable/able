# Isaiah 38 — Hezekiah’s illness and prayer

**About this chapter**

Hezekiah becomes seriously ill and receives a promise of fifteen more years.

His writing describes his fear of death and thanks for recovery.

## Isaiah 38:1

In those days, Hezekiah became ill and was close to death. 📖

The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came to him with the LORD’s message.

“Put your house in order.

You are about to die.

You will not recover.”

**Help with putting a house in order**

This means preparing one’s affairs before death.

## Isaiah 38:2

Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD. 📖

## Isaiah 38:3

He said, “Please, LORD, remember how I have lived before You faithfully and with wholehearted devotion. 📖

I have done what is good in Your sight.”

Hezekiah wept bitterly.

## Isaiah 38:4

The LORD’s word came to Isaiah. 📖

## Isaiah 38:5

“Go and tell Hezekiah what the LORD, the God of his father David, says. 📖

‘I have heard your prayer and seen your tears.

Look!

I will add fifteen years to your life.

## Isaiah 38:6

“‘I will rescue you and this city from Assyria’s king. 📖

I will defend this city.

## Isaiah 38:7

“‘This will be the LORD’s sign to you that He will do what He promised. 📖

## Isaiah 38:8

“‘I will make the sun’s shadow on Ahaz’s stairway go back ten steps.’” 📖

So the sunlight went back the ten steps it had gone down.

## Isaiah 38:9

This is a writing by Hezekiah king of Judah after his illness and recovery. 📖

## Isaiah 38:10

I said, “In the prime of my life, I must go through Sheol’s gates. 📖

I must lose the rest of my years.”

**Help with Sheol**

Sheol names the place of the dead.

## Isaiah 38:11

I said, “I will not see the LORD again, even the LORD, in the land of the living. 📖

I will no longer see human beings among those living in this world.

## Isaiah 38:12

“My home is picked up and taken from me like a shepherd’s tent. 📖

I have rolled up my life like a weaver.

He cuts me off from the loom.

From day until night, You bring me to an end.

**Help with the loom**

A loom is a frame used to weave cloth.

Hezekiah pictures his life ending like cloth cut from a loom.

## Isaiah 38:13

“I calmed myself until morning. 📖

Like a lion, He breaks all my bones.

From day until night, You bring me to an end.

## Isaiah 38:14

“I chirp like a swallow or crane and moan like a dove. 📖

My eyes grow weak as I look upward.

Lord, I am oppressed.

Be my security.”

**Help with the birds**

The bird sounds picture Hezekiah’s weak cries and grief.

## Isaiah 38:15

What can I say? 📖

He has spoken to me, and He Himself has done this.

I will walk slowly all my years because of the deep suffering within me.

## Isaiah 38:16

Lord, by such things people live. 📖

In all of them, my spirit finds life.

You have restored my health and let me live.

## Isaiah 38:17

My great suffering was surely for my good. 📖

But Your love rescued me from the pit of being forgotten.

You have thrown all my sins behind Your back.

**Help with sins behind God’s back**

This image describes God no longer holding those sins against him.

## Isaiah 38:18

Sheol cannot thank You. 📖

Death cannot praise You.

Those who go down to the Pit cannot hope for Your faithfulness.

**About these words**

These are words in Hezekiah’s writing about his illness and recovery.

## Isaiah 38:19

The living, only the living, can thank You, as I do today. 📖

Fathers will tell their children about Your faithfulness.

## Isaiah 38:20

The LORD will save me. 📖

We will play songs on stringed instruments in the LORD’s house all the days of our lives.

## Isaiah 38:21

Isaiah had said, “Prepare a lump of pressed figs and put it on the boil. 📖

He will recover.”

**Help with a boil**

A boil is a painful swelling on the skin.

This reports treatment in the story; it is not medical advice for readers.

## Isaiah 38:22

Hezekiah had asked, “What will be the sign that I will go up to the LORD’s house?” 📖
