# Isaiah 14 — The fall of proud rulers

**About this chapter**

A song mocks Babylon’s fallen king.

Messages about Assyria and Philistia follow.

The text includes enslavement, death, and judgment on children.

## Isaiah 14:1

The LORD will have compassion on Jacob. 📖

He will choose Israel again and settle them in their own land.

Foreigners will join them and unite with Jacob’s house.

## Isaiah 14:2

The nations will escort Israel and bring them to their homeland. 📖

In the LORD’s land, Israel’s house will possess the nations as male and female servants.

They will capture those who captured them and rule over those who oppressed them.

**About this difficult passage**

The text describes a reversal of captivity, including possession of people as servants.

That detail has not been removed.

## Isaiah 14:3

The LORD will give you rest from your pain and suffering. 📖

He will free you from the hard work you were forced to do.

## Isaiah 14:4

On that day, you will sing this mocking song against Babylon’s king. 📖

“The oppressor has stopped!

His fury has ended!”

**About the song**

The words that follow address the fallen king and describe his defeat.

## Isaiah 14:5

The LORD has broken the wicked person’s staff and the rulers’ royal rod. 📖

**Help with the staff and rod**

They picture the rulers’ power.

## Isaiah 14:6

In anger, that rod struck the peoples with blows that never stopped. 📖

In fury, it brought nations under its power and pursued them without rest.

## Isaiah 14:7

The whole earth is at peace and rest. 📖

People burst into song.

## Isaiah 14:8

Even the cypresses and Lebanon’s cedars rejoice over you. 📖

They say, “Since you were brought down, no woodcutter comes against us.”

## Isaiah 14:9

Sheol below is eager to meet you when you arrive. 📖

It wakes the spirits of the dead to greet you, all the earth’s rulers.

It makes all the nations’ kings rise from their thrones.

**Help with Sheol**

Sheol names the place of the dead.

The song pictures the dead rulers greeting Babylon’s fallen king.

## Isaiah 14:10

They will all say to you, “You have become weak like us! 📖

You have become like us!”

## Isaiah 14:11

Your splendor has been brought down to Sheol, along with the music of your harps. 📖

Maggots are your bed, and worms are your blanket.

## Isaiah 14:12

Day star, son of the dawn, how you have fallen from heaven! 📖

Destroyer of nations, you have been cut down to the ground!

**About day star**

The song uses this image while mocking the king’s proud rise and fall.

## Isaiah 14:13

You said in your heart, “I will go up to the heavens. 📖

I will raise my throne above God’s stars.

I will sit on the mountain of assembly, in the far north.

## Isaiah 14:14

“I will rise above the tops of the clouds. 📖

I will make myself like the Most High.”

**Help with Most High**

This title refers to God.

## Isaiah 14:15

But you will be brought down to Sheol, to the lowest depths of the Pit. 📖

## Isaiah 14:16

Those who see you will stare and think about what happened to you. 📖

They will ask, “Is this the man who shook the earth and made kingdoms tremble?

## Isaiah 14:17

“Is this the man who turned the world into a desert and destroyed its cities? 📖

Did he refuse to let prisoners return home?”

## Isaiah 14:18

All the nations’ kings lie in honor after death, each in his own tomb. 📖

## Isaiah 14:19

But you are thrown out of your grave like an unwanted branch. 📖

Bodies of people killed by swords cover you.

You are dumped into a rocky pit like a dead body trampled underfoot.

## Isaiah 14:20

You will not join the other kings in burial. 📖

You destroyed your land and killed your own people.

The children of the wicked will never be mentioned again.

## Isaiah 14:21

Prepare a place to kill his sons because of their ancestors’ wrongdoing. 📖

They will never rise to possess a land or cover the earth with their cities.

**About this difficult command**

The judgment includes killing the king’s sons because of earlier generations’ wrongdoing.

The command’s severity is retained.

## Isaiah 14:22

The LORD of Hosts declares, “I will rise against them. 📖

I will wipe out Babylon’s name, its survivors, its children, and all its future generations,” declares the LORD.

## Isaiah 14:23

“I will make Babylon a place for owls and swamps. 📖

I will sweep it away with the broom of destruction,” declares the LORD of Hosts.

## Isaiah 14:24

The LORD of Hosts has sworn, “What I have planned will surely happen. 📖

What I have decided will stand.

## Isaiah 14:25

“I will break Assyria in My land and trample him on My mountain. 📖

His yoke will be taken off My people.

His burden will be removed from their shoulders.”

**Help with a yoke**

A yoke is a wooden bar laid on working animals.

Here it pictures Assyria’s control over the people.

## Isaiah 14:26

This is the plan made for the whole earth. 📖

This is the hand stretched out over all the nations.

## Isaiah 14:27

The LORD of Hosts has decided this. 📖

Who can stop Him?

His hand is stretched out.

Who can turn it back?

## Isaiah 14:28

This message was received in the year King Ahaz died. 📖

## Isaiah 14:29

All you Philistines, do not rejoice because the rod that struck you is broken. 📖

A viper will spring from the snake’s root.

A flying snake will come from its egg.

**Help with the snakes**

The images warn of a further danger after an earlier power falls.

## Isaiah 14:30

The firstborn of the poor will find pasture. 📖

The needy will lie down safely.

But God says, “I will kill your root through famine.

Your survivors will be killed.”

**Help with pasture and root**

Pasture pictures food and security for poor people.

The root pictures the source of Philistia’s continued life.

## Isaiah 14:31

Wail, gate! 📖

Cry out, city!

Melt away, all you Philistines!

A cloud of smoke comes from the north.

No one in its ranks lags behind.

## Isaiah 14:32

What answer will be given to that nation’s messengers? 📖

“The LORD has established Zion.

His suffering people will find safety there.”
