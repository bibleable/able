# Proverbs 26 — Foolishness, laziness, and deceit

**About this chapter**

These sayings concern wisdom, speech, justice, and daily conduct.

Righteous means right before God; upright means honest and right.

## Proverbs 26:1

Honor does not fit a fool, like snow in summer or rain at harvest. 📖

## Proverbs 26:2

An undeserved curse does not settle, like a fluttering sparrow or darting swallow. 📖

## Proverbs 26:3

A whip for a horse, a bridle for a donkey, and a rod for fools’ backs! 📖

**Help with bridle and rod:** Equipment for controlling an animal, and a stick used for punishment.

## Proverbs 26:4

Do not answer a fool according to his foolishness, or you will become like him. 📖

## Proverbs 26:5

Answer a fool according to his foolishness, or he may think himself wise. 📖

**About verses 4–5:** Both contrasting instructions are in the source.

They require judgment about the situation; neither has been removed.

## Proverbs 26:6

Sending a message through a fool is like cutting off your feet or drinking violence. 📖

## Proverbs 26:7

A proverb in a fool’s mouth is like legs hanging limp, unable to walk. 📖

**Help with this picture:** The comparison concerns a proverb not being used wisely.

It does not say that difficulty walking is a moral fault.

## Proverbs 26:8

Giving a fool honor is like tying a stone into a sling. 📖

**Help with this picture:** The sling image shows honor being put where it does not fit.

## Proverbs 26:9

A proverb in a fool’s mouth is like a thorn entering a drunk man’s hand. 📖

**Help with this picture:** The thorn image pictures danger when a proverb is handled foolishly.

## Proverbs 26:10

Hiring a fool or passerby is like an archer wounding people at random. 📖

## Proverbs 26:11

A fool repeats his foolishness as a dog returns to its vomit. 📖

## Proverbs 26:12

Do you see a man wise in his own eyes? 📖

A fool has more hope than he does.

## Proverbs 26:13

A person avoiding work says, “A lion is in the road! 📖

A fierce lion roams the public square!”

## Proverbs 26:14

A lazy person turns on his bed like a door on its hinges. 📖

## Proverbs 26:15

A lazy person buries his hand in the dish. 📖

Bringing it to his mouth wears him out.

## Proverbs 26:16

A lazy person thinks himself wiser than seven men giving sensible answers. 📖

## Proverbs 26:17

A passerby interfering in someone else’s quarrel is like someone grabbing a dog’s ears. 📖

## Proverbs 26:18

A man out of control shoots burning objects and deadly arrows. 📖

## Proverbs 26:19

A man deceiving his neighbor and saying, “I only joked!” is like him. 📖

**About this comparison:** The source’s reckless attack image condemns deceptive harm.

It is not a diagnosis of mental illness.

## Proverbs 26:20

A fire goes out without wood. 📖

Conflict stops without gossip.

## Proverbs 26:21

An argumentative man feeds conflict like charcoal feeding embers or wood feeding fire. 📖

## Proverbs 26:22

Gossip’s words are like delicious mouthfuls. 📖

They go deep inside a person.

## Proverbs 26:23

Burning lips and a wicked heart are like glaze covering an earthen pot. 📖

**Help with glaze:** A smooth outer covering.

The picture contrasts outward speech and the heart.

## Proverbs 26:24

A hateful man hides behind his speech. 📖

But he stores deceit in his heart.

## Proverbs 26:25

Do not believe his gracious speech. 📖

Seven detestable things fill his heart.

## Proverbs 26:26

He may hide hatred through deceit. 📖

But his wickedness will be exposed in the gathering.

## Proverbs 26:27

Anyone digging a pit falls into it. 📖

Anyone rolling a stone has it roll back on him.

## Proverbs 26:28

A lying tongue hates the people it crushes. 📖

A flattering mouth brings ruin.
