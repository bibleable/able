# Nahum 3 — Nineveh’s cruelty and downfall

**About this chapter**

The message describes Nineveh’s violence, humiliation, and fall.

The chapter includes sexual imagery and the killing of children.

## Nahum 3:1

Woe to the city of blood! 📖

It is full of lies and stolen goods.

It always has prey.

**Help with woe:** Woe announces disaster or judgment.

**Help with city of blood and prey:** These describe Nineveh’s killing and taking from others.

## Nahum 3:2

Hear the crack of the whip and the rumble of wheels! 📖

Horses gallop, and chariots bounce along!

**Help with chariots:** Chariots are vehicles pulled by horses, often used in war.

## Nahum 3:3

Riders charge, swords flash, and spears shine! 📖

There are heaps of killed people and piles of dead bodies without end.

People stumble over the dead.

## Nahum 3:4

This happens because of the prostitute’s many acts of prostitution. 📖

She is a woman skilled in magic who lures people to her.

She betrays nations through her prostitution and family groups through her witchcraft.

**Help with this picture:** The prostitute represents Nineveh, the city addressed in this message.

Prostitution means selling sexual acts.

The sexual and magic imagery describes the city’s harmful dealings with other peoples.

It is retained as the source’s accusation, for further editorial assessment.

## Nahum 3:5

The LORD of Hosts declares, “I am against you. 📖

I will lift your skirts over your face.

I will show your nakedness to the nations and your shame to the kingdoms.

**Help with LORD of Hosts:** This title describes the LORD as ruler of heavenly armies.

**About this picture:** Nineveh is pictured as a woman publicly exposed and shamed.

The degrading image is retained from the source’s judgment against the city.

## Nahum 3:6

I will throw filth at you and treat you with scorn. 📖

I will make you a sight for everyone to stare at.

## Nahum 3:7

Everyone who sees you will shrink back and say, ‘Nineveh is ruined. 📖

Who will grieve for her?’

Where can I find people to comfort you?”

## Nahum 3:8

Are you better than Thebes, which stood beside the Nile with water around her? 📖

The sea was her defence, and water was her wall.

**Help with Thebes:** Thebes was a city in Egypt.

The question compares Nineveh’s safety with that city’s safety.

## Nahum 3:9

Cush and Egypt gave Thebes strength without limit. 📖

Put and Libya were her allies.

**Help with allies:** Allies are peoples or nations who agree to help each other.

## Nahum 3:10

Yet Thebes was forced away into captivity. 📖

Her babies were smashed to pieces at the head of every street.

People cast lots for her important leaders.

All her nobles were tied in chains.

**Help with cast lots:** People used marked objects to decide how to divide what they had taken.

**Help with nobles:** Nobles are important leaders.

**About the babies:** This describes killing children in the attack.

The violence is retained from the source.

## Nahum 3:11

Nineveh, you too will become drunk. 📖

You will hide and seek safety from the enemy.

## Nahum 3:12

All your fortresses are like fig trees with the first ripe figs. 📖

When the trees are shaken, the figs fall into the eater’s mouth!

**Help with fortresses:** Fortresses are strong places protected from attack.

**Help with the figs:** The picture says these defences will fall easily.

## Nahum 3:13

Look at your troops! 📖

They are like your women!

The gates of your land stand wide open to your enemies.

Fire burns up their bars.

**About the comparison:** The source uses a comparison to women to mock the troops’ strength.

This reflects the accusation in the passage.

It is not a general claim that women are weak.

## Nahum 3:14

Draw water for the siege. 📖

Strengthen your fortresses.

Work the clay and tread the mortar.

Repair the brick kiln!

**Help with siege:** A siege is an attack that surrounds a city and cuts off supplies.

**Help with fortresses:** Fortresses are strong places protected from attack.

**Help with building words:** Mortar joins building materials.

A kiln is a furnace used to make bricks hard.

## Nahum 3:15

There, fire will eat you up. 📖

The sword will cut you down and consume you like a young locust.

Make yourselves many like young locusts!

Make yourselves many like swarming locusts!

**Help with locusts:** Locusts are insects that gather in large numbers and eat plants.

These pictures describe destruction and large numbers.

## Nahum 3:16

You have made your merchants more numerous than the stars in the sky. 📖

The young locust strips the land and flies away.

**Help with merchants:** Merchants buy and sell goods.

**Help with locust:** A locust is an insect that eats plants.

This picture links taking goods with stripping the land.

## Nahum 3:17

Your guards are like swarming locusts. 📖

Your scribes are like clouds of locusts on the walls on a cold day.

When the sun rises, they fly away, and no one knows where.

**Help with scribes:** Scribes are people who write and keep records.

**Help with locusts:** These insects picture large numbers that suddenly leave.

## Nahum 3:18

King of Assyria, your shepherds sleep, and your officers sleep. 📖

Your people are scattered on the mountains, with no one to gather them.

**Help with shepherds:** Here, shepherds pictures those responsible for leading and caring for the people.

## Nahum 3:19

There is no healing for your injury. 📖

Your wound is severe.

Everyone who hears the news claps at your downfall.

Who has not suffered from your constant cruelty?

**Help with injury and wound:** The city’s defeat is pictured as an injury that cannot heal.
