# 2 Samuel 1 — David hears of Saul’s death

**About this chapter**

A young man reports the deaths of Saul and Jonathan.

David orders an execution and sings a song of grief.

## 2 Samuel 1:1

After Saul died, David returned from killing the Amalekites. 📖

David stayed in Ziklag for 2 days.

## 2 Samuel 1:2

On the 3rd day, a man arrived from Saul’s camp. 📖

His clothes were torn, and dust was on his head.

He fell to the ground before David to show honour.

**Help with torn clothes and dust:** These were signs of grief.

## 2 Samuel 1:3

David asked, “Where have you come from?” 📖

The man answered, “I escaped from Israel’s camp.”

## 2 Samuel 1:4

David asked, “What happened? 📖

Please tell me.”

The man said, “The troops ran from the battle.

Many fell and died.

Saul and his son Jonathan are dead too.”

## 2 Samuel 1:5

David asked the young man who brought the news, “How do you know Saul and Jonathan are dead?” 📖

## 2 Samuel 1:6

The young man answered, “I happened to be on Mount Gilboa. 📖

Saul was leaning on his spear.

Chariots and horsemen were closing in on him.

**Help with chariots:** Chariots are vehicles pulled by horses, often used in war.

**About this report:** The young man is telling David his account.

The draft keeps his claims distinct from the narrator’s account.

## 2 Samuel 1:7

Saul turned, saw me, and called to me. 📖

I answered, ‘Here I am!’

## 2 Samuel 1:8

Saul asked, ‘Who are you?’ 📖

I told him, ‘I am an Amalekite.’

## 2 Samuel 1:9

Saul begged, ‘Stand over me and kill me. 📖

Terrible pain has seized me, but I am still alive.’

## 2 Samuel 1:10

I stood over Saul and killed him. 📖

I knew he could not survive after falling.

I took the crown from his head and the band from his arm.

I have brought them here to my lord.”

## 2 Samuel 1:11

David took hold of his clothes and tore them. 📖

All the men with him did the same.

**Help with torn clothes:** Tearing clothes was a sign of grief.

## 2 Samuel 1:12

They grieved, cried, and fasted until evening. 📖

They did this for Saul, his son Jonathan, the LORD’s people, and Israel’s family.

They had died by the sword.

**Help with fasted:** Fasted means did not eat for a time.

## 2 Samuel 1:13

David asked the young man who brought the report, “Where are you from?” 📖

He answered, “I am the son of a foreigner.

I am an Amalekite.”

## 2 Samuel 1:14

David asked, “Why were you not afraid to raise your hand to destroy the LORD’s anointed one?” 📖

**Help with anointed one:** This means someone chosen and set apart for a role.

Here, David means Saul as king.

## 2 Samuel 1:15

David called one of the young men and said, “Go and execute him!” 📖

The young man struck the Amalekite down, and he died.

**Help with execute:** Execute means put someone to death as a punishment.

## 2 Samuel 1:16

David had told the Amalekite, “Your blood is on your own head. 📖

Your own mouth has given evidence against you.

You said, ‘I killed the LORD’s anointed one.’”

**Help with blood on your head:** David says the man is responsible for his own death.

**Help with anointed one:** Here, this means Saul, chosen as king.

## 2 Samuel 1:17

David sang this song of grief for Saul and his son Jonathan. 📖

## 2 Samuel 1:18

David ordered that Judah’s sons be taught the Song of the Bow. 📖

It is written in the Book of Jashar.

## 2 Samuel 1:19

“Israel, your glory lies dead on your heights. 📖

How the mighty have fallen!

**Help with your glory:** This describes the honour associated with those killed.

**Help with the mighty:** This means the strong fighting men.

## 2 Samuel 1:20

Do not tell it in Gath or announce it in Ashkelon’s streets. 📖

Otherwise, the Philistine women might rejoice, and the daughters of the uncircumcised might shout with joy.

**Help with uncircumcised:** Circumcision removes the skin covering the tip of the penis.

Here, this word identifies the Philistines in David’s song.

## 2 Samuel 1:21

Mountains of Gilboa, may you have no dew or rain and no fields producing grain for offerings. 📖

The mighty warrior’s shield was defiled there.

Saul’s shield is no longer rubbed with oil.

**Help with dew:** Dew is moisture that forms on cool surfaces.

**Help with defiled:** Defiled means spoiled or dishonoured.

The shield is described as sharing the defeat.

## 2 Samuel 1:22

Jonathan’s bow did not turn back from the blood of the killed or the fat of the mighty. 📖

Saul’s sword did not return empty.

**Help with these pictures:** The song describes both men fighting and killing their enemies.

A bow is a weapon for shooting arrows.

## 2 Samuel 1:23

Saul and Jonathan were loved and delightful in life and not separated in death. 📖

They were faster than eagles and stronger than lions.

## 2 Samuel 1:24

Women of Israel, cry for Saul. 📖

He dressed you in rich red clothes and luxury.

He put gold ornaments on your clothes.

## 2 Samuel 1:25

How the mighty have fallen in the middle of battle! 📖

Jonathan lies dead on your heights.

## 2 Samuel 1:26

I grieve for you, Jonathan, my brother. 📖

You brought me delight.

Your love for me was extraordinary, greater than the love of women.

**Help with my brother:** David addresses Jonathan with a word of close affection.

**About this love:** The comparison is retained without adding an account of their relationship beyond the source.

## 2 Samuel 1:27

How the mighty have fallen! 📖

The weapons of war have perished!”
