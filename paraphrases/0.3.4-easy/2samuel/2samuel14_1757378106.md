# 2 Samuel 14 — Absalom returns to Jerusalem

**About this chapter**

Joab arranges a woman’s appeal to David.

David allows Absalom to return, but at first will not see him.

## 2 Samuel 14:1

Joab son of Zeruiah realised the king longed for Absalom. 📖

## 2 Samuel 14:2

Joab sent for a wise woman from Tekoa. 📖

He said, “Pretend to be grieving.

Wear clothes for grief and do not put oil on yourself.

Act like a woman who has grieved for the dead a long time.”

## 2 Samuel 14:3

Joab told her, “Go to the king and say these words.” 📖

Joab gave her the words to say.

## 2 Samuel 14:4

The woman from Tekoa came to the king and bowed with her face to the ground. 📖

She said, “Help me, king!”

## 2 Samuel 14:5

The king asked, “What troubles you?” 📖

She answered, “I am a widow.

My husband is dead.

**Help with widow:** A widow is a woman whose husband has died.

**About the story:** Joab has instructed the woman to make this appeal.

It is presented as her story, not as events confirmed by the narrator.

## 2 Samuel 14:6

Your servant had 2 sons. 📖

They fought in the field, with no one to separate them.

One struck the other and killed him.

## 2 Samuel 14:7

The whole family group has risen against your servant. 📖

They demand, ‘Hand over the man who killed his brother, so we can kill him for his brother’s life.

We will cut off the heir too!’

They would put out my last glowing coal.

They would leave my husband no name or descendants on earth.”

**Help with heir:** An heir is someone who receives family property after a death.

**Help with glowing coal:** This pictures the woman’s one surviving son.

**Help with descendants:** Descendants are children and later family.

## 2 Samuel 14:8

The king said, “Go home. 📖

I will give orders for you.”

## 2 Samuel 14:9

The woman of Tekoa said, “My lord the king, let any blame be on me and my father’s family. 📖

Let the king and his throne be innocent.”

**Help with throne:** Here, throne represents the king’s rule.

## 2 Samuel 14:10

The king said, “If anyone speaks to you, bring him to me. 📖

He will not trouble you again!”

## 2 Samuel 14:11

She said, “Please call on the LORD your God to stop the avenger of blood causing further destruction. 📖

Then my son will not be destroyed!”

The king swore, “As surely as the LORD lives, not one hair on your son’s head will fall.”

**Help with avenger of blood:** This means a relative claiming responsibility to punish a killing.

**Help with not one hair:** The king promises protection.

## 2 Samuel 14:12

The woman asked, “Please may your servant say something to my lord the king?” 📖

He answered, “Speak.”

## 2 Samuel 14:13

The woman asked, “Why have you planned something like this against God’s people? 📖

Does the king not condemn himself by saying this?

He has not brought back his own banished son.

**Help with banished:** Banished means sent away and kept from returning.

## 2 Samuel 14:14

We will certainly die, like water poured onto the ground that cannot be gathered up. 📖

Yet God does not take away a life.

He plans ways so the banished person is not kept away from him.

**About God:** These are the woman’s claims in her appeal.

They are retained without using them to erase other statements in the Bible.

## 2 Samuel 14:15

I came to tell my lord the king because the people frightened me. 📖

I thought, ‘I will speak to the king.

Perhaps he will grant his servant’s request.

## 2 Samuel 14:16

The king will hear and rescue his servant from the man threatening us. 📖

That man would cut both me and my son off from God’s inheritance.’

**Help with inheritance:** Here, inheritance refers to a share among God’s people.

## 2 Samuel 14:17

Your servant says, ‘May my lord the king’s word bring me rest. 📖

Like God’s angel, my lord the king can tell good from evil.

May the LORD your God be with you.’”

**Help with angel:** An angel is a messenger from God.

## 2 Samuel 14:18

The king told the woman, “I will ask you something. 📖

Do not hide it from me!”

She answered, “Let my lord the king speak.”

## 2 Samuel 14:19

The king asked, “Is Joab behind all this?” 📖

She answered, “As surely as you live, my lord the king, no one can turn from what you say.

Your servant Joab gave me orders.

He told your servant exactly what to say.

## 2 Samuel 14:20

Joab did this to change the situation. 📖

But my lord has wisdom like God’s angel, knowing everything that happens in the land.”

**Help with angel:** An angel is a messenger from God.

## 2 Samuel 14:21

The king told Joab, “I grant this request. 📖

Go and bring back the young man Absalom.”

## 2 Samuel 14:22

Joab bowed with his face to the ground and blessed the king. 📖

Joab said, “Today your servant knows you have shown him favour, my lord the king.

You granted his request.”

## 2 Samuel 14:23

Joab got up, went to Geshur, and brought Absalom to Jerusalem. 📖

## 2 Samuel 14:24

The king added, “He may go home, but he must not see my face.” 📖

Absalom went home and did not see the king.

## 2 Samuel 14:25

No man in Israel was as handsome or as highly praised as Absalom. 📖

From his feet to his head, he had no flaw.

## 2 Samuel 14:26

Absalom shaved his hair every year because it became so heavy. 📖

It weighed 200 shekels by the royal standard.

**Help with shekels:** A shekel was a measure of weight.

## 2 Samuel 14:27

Absalom had 3 sons and a daughter named Tamar. 📖

Tamar was a beautiful woman.

## 2 Samuel 14:28

Absalom lived in Jerusalem for 2 years without seeing the king. 📖

## 2 Samuel 14:29

Absalom sent for Joab to send him to the king, but Joab would not come. 📖

Absalom sent a 2nd time, but Joab still would not come.

## 2 Samuel 14:30

Absalom told his servants, “Joab’s field is beside mine and has barley growing there. 📖

Go and set it on fire!”

Absalom’s servants burned the field.

**Help with barley:** Barley is a grain grown for food.

## 2 Samuel 14:31

Joab came to Absalom’s house and demanded, “Why did your servants burn my field?” 📖

## 2 Samuel 14:32

Absalom said, “I sent for you because I wanted to send you to the king. 📖

I wanted to ask, ‘Why did I return from Geshur?

I would be better off still there.’

Now let me see the king.

If I am guilty of wrongdoing, let him kill me.”

## 2 Samuel 14:33

Joab went and told the king. 📖

David called for Absalom, who came and bowed with his face to the ground before him.

The king kissed Absalom.
