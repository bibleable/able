# 2 Samuel 11 — David, Bathsheba, and Uriah

**About this chapter**

David has sex with Bathsheba and arranges for her husband Uriah to be killed.

The chapter states that David’s actions were evil before the LORD.

## 2 Samuel 11:1

In spring, when kings go to war, David sent Joab, his servants, and Israel’s whole army. 📖

They destroyed the Ammonites and surrounded Rabbah.

David stayed in Jerusalem.

## 2 Samuel 11:2

One evening, David got out of bed and walked on the palace roof. 📖

From there, he saw a very beautiful woman bathing.

## 2 Samuel 11:3

David sent someone to ask about her. 📖

He was told, “She is Bathsheba daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite.”

## 2 Samuel 11:4

David sent messengers to bring Bathsheba. 📖

She came to David, and he had sex with her.

She had just purified herself from her uncleanness.

Then she went home.

**Help with purified and uncleanness:** These words concern religious rules about being ready for worship.

They do not describe Bathsheba as morally bad.

The account does not state that she invited David’s action.

## 2 Samuel 11:5

Bathsheba became pregnant and sent David a message. 📖

She said, “I am pregnant.”

## 2 Samuel 11:6

David sent Joab an order, “Send me Uriah the Hittite.” 📖

Joab sent Uriah to David.

## 2 Samuel 11:7

When Uriah arrived, David asked about Joab, the troops, and how the war was going. 📖

## 2 Samuel 11:8

David told Uriah, “Go to your house and wash your feet.” 📖

Uriah left the palace, and a gift from the king followed him.

## 2 Samuel 11:9

But Uriah slept at the palace door with all his master’s servants. 📖

He did not go home.

## 2 Samuel 11:10

David heard that Uriah had not gone home. 📖

David asked Uriah, “Have you not just come from a journey?

Why did you not go home?”

## 2 Samuel 11:11

Uriah answered, “The ark, Israel, and Judah stay in tents. 📖

My master Joab and his soldiers camp in the open field.

How could I go home to eat, drink, and have sex with my wife?

As surely as you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this!”

**Help with ark:** The ark was a sacred chest associated with God’s covenant with Israel.

## 2 Samuel 11:12

David told Uriah, “Stay here one more day. 📖

Tomorrow I will send you back.”

Uriah stayed in Jerusalem that day and the next.

## 2 Samuel 11:13

David invited Uriah to eat and drink and made him drunk. 📖

That evening, Uriah went to lie on his bed with his master’s servants.

He did not go home.

## 2 Samuel 11:14

The next morning, David wrote Joab a letter and sent it with Uriah. 📖

## 2 Samuel 11:15

The letter said, “Put Uriah at the front of the fiercest battle. 📖

Then pull back from him, so he will be struck down and killed.”

## 2 Samuel 11:16

Joab surrounded the city and placed Uriah where he knew the strongest enemy soldiers were. 📖

## 2 Samuel 11:17

The city’s men came out to fight Joab. 📖

Some of David’s servants died, and Uriah the Hittite died too.

## 2 Samuel 11:18

Joab sent David a full report of the battle. 📖

## 2 Samuel 11:19

Joab told the messenger, “Finish giving the king the full battle report. 📖

## 2 Samuel 11:20

If the king becomes angry, he may ask these questions. 📖

‘Why did you go so close to the city to fight?

Did you not know they would shoot from the wall?

## 2 Samuel 11:21

Who struck Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth? 📖

Was it not a woman who dropped an upper millstone from the wall and killed him at Thebez?

Why did you go so close to the wall?’

If he asks this, say, ‘Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too.’”

**Help with millstone:** A millstone is a heavy stone used to grind grain.

**About the questions:** Joab predicts what David might ask.

This is not an account of David actually asking these questions.

## 2 Samuel 11:22

The messenger left and told David everything Joab had sent him to say. 📖

## 2 Samuel 11:23

The messenger said, “The men overpowered us and came against us in the field. 📖

But we drove them back to the gate’s entrance.

## 2 Samuel 11:24

Archers shot at your servants from the wall. 📖

Some of the king’s servants were killed.

Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too.”

**Help with archers:** Archers are people who shoot arrows.

## 2 Samuel 11:25

David told the messenger, “Tell Joab not to be upset. 📖

The sword eats up one person as well as another.

Tell him to strengthen the attack and destroy the city.

Encourage him with these words.”

**Help with sword eats up:** David uses this picture to describe death in battle.

## 2 Samuel 11:26

Uriah’s wife heard that her husband had died and grieved for him. 📖

## 2 Samuel 11:27

After the time of grief, David brought her to his house. 📖

She became his wife and gave birth to his son.

But what David had done was evil in the LORD’s sight.
