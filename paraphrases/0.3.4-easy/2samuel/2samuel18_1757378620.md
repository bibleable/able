# 2 Samuel 18 — Absalom dies and David grieves

**About this chapter**

David’s army defeats Israel’s army.

Absalom is killed despite David’s order to treat him gently.

## 2 Samuel 18:1

David inspected his troops and appointed commanders over groups of 1,000 and 100. 📖

## 2 Samuel 18:2

David sent one third under Joab, one third under Joab’s brother Abishai son of Zeruiah, and one third under Ittai. 📖

Ittai was the Gittite.

The king told the troops, “I will march out with you too.”

## 2 Samuel 18:3

The people pleaded, “You must not go out! 📖

If we flee, they will not care about us.

Even if half of us die, they will not care.

You are worth 10,000 of us.

It is better for you to support us from the city.”

## 2 Samuel 18:4

The king answered, “I will do what seems best to you.” 📖

He stood beside the gate as the troops marched out in hundreds and thousands.

## 2 Samuel 18:5

The king had ordered Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, “Treat the young man Absalom gently for my sake.” 📖

Everyone heard the king’s orders about Absalom to each commander.

## 2 Samuel 18:6

David’s army went out to fight Israel. 📖

The battle took place in Ephraim’s forest.

## 2 Samuel 18:7

David’s servants defeated Israel’s people there. 📖

There was great killing that day: 20,000 men.

## 2 Samuel 18:8

The battle spread across the countryside. 📖

That day, the forest ate up more people than the sword.

**Help with forest ate up:** This pictures the forest causing more deaths than the fighting.

## 2 Samuel 18:9

Absalom met David’s servants while riding his mule. 📖

As the mule passed under a large oak’s thick branches, Absalom’s head became trapped in the tree.

The mule kept going, leaving Absalom hanging in the air.

**Help with mule:** A mule is an animal with a horse and a donkey as parents.

**About his head:** The source says his head was caught.

The draft does not replace this with a claim about his hair.

## 2 Samuel 18:10

A man saw this and told Joab, “I saw Absalom hanging in an oak!” 📖

## 2 Samuel 18:11

Joab exclaimed, “You saw him! 📖

Why did you not strike him down there?

I would have given you 10 silver shekels and a warrior’s belt!”

**Help with shekels:** A shekel was a measure of weight used for silver payment.

## 2 Samuel 18:12

The man answered, “Even for 1,000 silver shekels, I would not raise my hand against the king’s son. 📖

We heard the king order you, Abishai, and Ittai to protect Absalom for his sake.

**Help with shekels:** A shekel was a measure of weight used for silver payment.

## 2 Samuel 18:13

If I had put my life at risk, you would have abandoned me. 📖

Nothing is hidden from the king.”

## 2 Samuel 18:14

Joab said, “I will not wait like this with you!” 📖

Joab took 3 spears and drove them through Absalom’s heart while he was still alive in the oak.

## 2 Samuel 18:15

10 young men carrying Joab’s armour surrounded Absalom, struck him, and killed him. 📖

## 2 Samuel 18:16

Joab blew the ram’s horn and stopped the troops from chasing Israel. 📖

**Help with ram’s horn:** This is an instrument made from a male sheep’s horn.

## 2 Samuel 18:17

They threw Absalom into a large pit in the forest and piled a huge heap of stones over him. 📖

All the Israelites fled home.

## 2 Samuel 18:18

During his life, Absalom had set up a pillar in the King’s Valley. 📖

He had said, “I have no son to preserve the memory of my name.”

He named the pillar after himself.

It is still called Absalom’s Monument today.

**About no son:** Absalom’s statement is retained alongside the earlier report of his sons.

The draft does not invent events to explain the difference.

## 2 Samuel 18:19

Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, “Please let me run and tell the king good news. 📖

The LORD has given him revenge against his enemies.”

## 2 Samuel 18:20

Joab answered, “You must not take good news today. 📖

You may take it another day.

Today you must not, because the king’s son is dead.”

## 2 Samuel 18:21

Joab told a Cushite, “Go and tell the king what you saw.” 📖

The Cushite bowed to Joab and ran off.

## 2 Samuel 18:22

Ahimaaz son of Zadok insisted, “Whatever happens, please let me run after the Cushite too!” 📖

Joab answered, “My son, why run when you will get no reward?”

## 2 Samuel 18:23

Ahimaaz said, “Whatever happens, I want to run!” 📖

Joab told him, “Then run!”

Ahimaaz ran across the plain and passed the Cushite.

## 2 Samuel 18:24

David sat between the 2 gates. 📖

The watchman climbed to the roof of the gateway beside the wall and looked out.

He saw one man running alone.

## 2 Samuel 18:25

The watchman called and told the king. 📖

The king said, “If he is alone, he brings good news.”

The first runner came nearer.

## 2 Samuel 18:26

The watchman saw another man running and called to the gatekeeper. 📖

“Look!

Another man is running alone!”

The king said, “He brings good news too.”

## 2 Samuel 18:27

The watchman said, “The first man seems to run like Ahimaaz son of Zadok.” 📖

The king said, “He is a good man and comes with good news.”

## 2 Samuel 18:28

Ahimaaz called to the king, “All is well!” 📖

He bowed with his face to the ground.

He said, “Blessed be the LORD your God!

He handed over the men who rose against my lord the king.”

**Help with blessed:** Here, blessed expresses praise to God.

## 2 Samuel 18:29

The king asked, “Is the young man Absalom safe?” 📖

Ahimaaz answered, “When Joab sent the king’s servant and your servant, I saw a great disturbance.

I do not know what it was.”

## 2 Samuel 18:30

The king said, “Move aside and stand here.” 📖

Ahimaaz stepped aside.

## 2 Samuel 18:31

The Cushite arrived and said, “May my lord the king hear good news! 📖

Today the LORD has given you revenge against everyone who rose against you!”

## 2 Samuel 18:32

The king asked the Cushite, “Is the young man Absalom safe?” 📖

He answered, “May my lord the king’s enemies be like that young man.

May everyone who rises to harm you be like him.”

## 2 Samuel 18:33

The king was deeply shaken and went up to the room over the gate, crying. 📖

As he walked, he cried, “My son Absalom!

My son, my son Absalom!

If only I had died instead of you!

Absalom, my son, my son!”
