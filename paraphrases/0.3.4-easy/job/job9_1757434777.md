# Job 9 — Job asks how he can answer God

**About this chapter**

Job answers Bildad.

He speaks about God’s power and his own suffering.

His accusations express his distress.

## Job 9:1

Then Job answered: 📖

## Job 9:2

Yes, I know this is so. 📖

But how can a human being be right before God?

## Job 9:3

If someone wanted to argue with God, he could not answer Him once in a thousand times. 📖

## Job 9:4

God is wise in heart and powerful in strength. 📖

Who has resisted Him and succeeded?

## Job 9:5

He moves mountains without their knowing it. 📖

In His anger He turns them over.

## Job 9:6

He shakes the earth out of its place. 📖

Its foundations tremble.

**Help — foundations:** The supports pictured as holding up the earth.

## Job 9:7

He commands the sun not to shine. 📖

He seals off the stars.

## Job 9:8

He alone stretches out the heavens. 📖

He walks on the waves of the sea.

## Job 9:9

He made the Bear, Orion, the Pleiades, and the groups of stars in the south. 📖

**Help — Bear, Orion, and Pleiades:** Names for groups of stars.

## Job 9:10

He does great things that no one can fully understand. 📖

His wonders cannot be counted.

## Job 9:11

If He passed by me, I would not see Him. 📖

If He moved, I would not recognize Him.

## Job 9:12

If He takes something away, who can stop Him? 📖

Who dares ask Him, “What are You doing?”

## Job 9:13

God does not hold back His anger. 📖

Rahab’s helpers crouch in fear beneath Him.

**Help — Rahab:** A name used here in a picture of forces opposed to God.

## Job 9:14

How then can I answer Him? 📖

How can I choose words to argue against Him?

## Job 9:15

Even if I were right, I could not answer. 📖

I could only beg my Judge for mercy.

## Job 9:16

If I called Him and He answered, I do not believe He would listen to my voice. 📖

## Job 9:17

He would crush me with a violent storm. 📖

He would give me many wounds without cause.

## Job 9:18

He does not let me catch my breath. 📖

He fills me with bitterness.

## Job 9:19

If the question is strength, He is certainly mighty! 📖

If the question is justice, who can call Him to court?

## Job 9:20

Even if I were innocent, my own mouth would condemn me. 📖

Even if I were blameless, it would declare me guilty.

## Job 9:21

I am blameless, but I do not care about myself. 📖

I hate my own life.

## Job 9:22

It is all the same. 📖

So I say, “He destroys both blameless people and wicked people.”

## Job 9:23

When a disaster brings sudden death, He mocks innocent people’s despair. 📖

## Job 9:24

The earth is handed over to wicked people. 📖

He covers its judges’ eyes.

If He does not do this, who does?

## Job 9:25

My days are faster than a runner. 📖

They flee without seeing good.

## Job 9:26

They rush past like papyrus boats. 📖

They pass like an eagle diving on its prey.

**Help — papyrus:** A plant used to make these boats.

Prey is an animal hunted for food.

## Job 9:27

Suppose I said, “I will forget my complaint. 📖

I will change my expression and smile.”

## Job 9:28

I would still fear all my suffering. 📖

I know You will not declare me innocent.

## Job 9:29

Since I have already been found guilty, why should I work for nothing? 📖

## Job 9:30

Even if I washed myself with snow and cleaned my hands with lye, 📖

**Help — lye:** A strong substance used for cleaning.

## Job 9:31

You would plunge me into a pit. 📖

Even my own clothes would hate me.

**Help — washing and the pit:** Job pictures trying to prove his innocence and being made guilty again.

## Job 9:32

God is not a man like me, so I cannot answer Him. 📖

We cannot take each other to court.

## Job 9:33

There is no one between us to settle our case. 📖

There is no one to put a hand on us both.

## Job 9:34

Let God take His rod away from me. 📖

Then His terror will no longer frighten me.

**Help — rod:** A stick used for punishment.

Job pictures his suffering as punishment.

## Job 9:35

Then I would speak without fearing Him. 📖

But as things are, I am on my own.
