# Lamentations 4 — The precious city is brought low

**About this chapter**

The poem contrasts former wealth and health with starvation, violence, and exile.

It ends with different words for Zion and Edom.

## Lamentations 4:1

How the gold lost its shine, and pure gold became dull! 📖

The temple’s gems lie scattered on every street corner.

## Lamentations 4:2

Zion’s precious sons once were worth their weight in pure gold. 📖

Now people value them like clay jars made by a potter’s hands.

## Lamentations 4:3

Even jackals nurse their young at their breasts. 📖

But the daughter of my people became cruel, like an ostrich in the wilderness.

**About the comparison**

The poem compares the community’s failure to care for children with an ostrich.

The comparison is part of the lament.

## Lamentations 4:4

A nursing baby’s tongue sticks to the roof of his mouth from thirst. 📖

Little children beg for bread, but no one gives any.

## Lamentations 4:5

Those who ate fine food now have nothing in the streets. 📖

Those raised in crimson clothing huddle in ash heaps.

**Help with crimson**

Crimson is a deep red color.

The clothing here pictures former wealth.

## Lamentations 4:6

The daughter of my people suffered greater punishment than Sodom. 📖

Sodom was overthrown in an instant, without a hand turning to help her.

## Lamentations 4:7

Her high-ranking people were brighter than snow and whiter than milk. 📖

Their bodies were redder than rubies, and their appearance like sapphires.

**Help with the colors and stones**

Rubies and sapphires are precious stones.

The rich colors describe the people’s former health and splendor.

## Lamentations 4:8

Now they look blacker than soot and are not recognized in the streets. 📖

Their skin has shriveled on their bones and become dry as a stick.

**About the appearance**

The poem contrasts starvation with former well-being.

The colors do not rank ethnic groups or skin colors in moral worth.

## Lamentations 4:9

Those killed by swords are better off than those dying of hunger. 📖

They waste away, pierced with pain because the fields produce no food.

## Lamentations 4:10

Compassionate women cooked their own children with their hands. 📖

Their children became food during the destruction of the daughter of my people.

**About this difficult passage**

The lament describes cannibalism during the destruction.

Cannibalism means eating human flesh.

The horrifying detail is retained.

## Lamentations 4:11

The LORD poured out all His wrath and fierce anger. 📖

He lit a fire in Zion that consumed her foundations.

## Lamentations 4:12

Neither earth’s kings nor any people in the world believed an enemy could enter Jerusalem’s gates. 📖

## Lamentations 4:13

But this happened because of her prophets’ sins and her priests’ guilt. 📖

They shed righteous people’s blood within her.

## Lamentations 4:14

They wandered blind in the streets, polluted by blood. 📖

No one dared touch their clothes.

**Help with polluted**

The passage links their pollution to bloodshed.

It does not make ordinary blindness a moral fault.

## Lamentations 4:15

People shouted, “Go away! 📖

Unclean!

Away, away!

Do not touch us!”

They fled and wandered.

Among nations, people said, “They cannot stay here anymore.”

## Lamentations 4:16

The LORD’s presence scattered them, and He regards them no more. 📖

Priests receive no honor, and elders find no favor.

## Lamentations 4:17

Our eyes kept failing as we looked for help that never came. 📖

We watched from towers for a nation unable to save us.

## Lamentations 4:18

They stalked every step so that we could not walk in our streets. 📖

Our end came near, our time ran out, and our end arrived!

## Lamentations 4:19

Those chasing us were faster than sky eagles. 📖

They pursued us over mountains and waited to attack in the wilderness.

## Lamentations 4:20

The LORD’s anointed, the breath of our life, was caught in their pits. 📖

We had said, “Under his shadow, we will live among nations.”

**Help with anointed and shadow**

Anointed means set apart for a role, here the ruler.

His shadow pictures the protection the people expected from him.

## Lamentations 4:21

Daughter Edom, living in Uz’s land, rejoice and be glad. 📖

Yet the cup will pass to you too.

You will become drunk and expose yourself.

**Help with cup**

The cup pictures receiving judgment.

The word to Edom leads into a warning.

## Lamentations 4:22

Daughter Zion, your punishment is complete, and He will not prolong your exile. 📖

But Daughter Edom, He will punish your wrongdoing and expose your sins.

**Help with exile**

Exile means being forced away from one’s own land.
