# Song of Solomon 6 — The lovers belong to one another

**About this chapter**

**Speaker guide — editorial**

Others ask the woman a question in verse 1; she answers in verses 2–3.

The man praises her in verses 4–9.

Verse 10 asks another question; its speaker is not named.

The first-person speaker in verses 11–12 is not named.

Verse 13 includes a call to the Shulammite and a responding question.

The guide is provisional.

## Song of Solomon 6:1

Most beautiful woman, where has your beloved gone? 📖

Which way did he turn?

We will look for him with you.

## Song of Solomon 6:2

My beloved went down to his garden, to the spice beds. 📖

He feeds his flock in the gardens and gathers lilies.

## Song of Solomon 6:3

I belong to my beloved, and he belongs to me. 📖

He feeds his flock among lilies.

## Song of Solomon 6:4

My darling, you are beautiful as Tirzah, lovely as Jerusalem, majestic as troops with banners. 📖

**Help with Tirzah and banners:** A city name, and flags or signs carried by troops.

## Song of Solomon 6:5

Turn your eyes from me. 📖

They have overcome me.

Your hair is like goats streaming down Gilead.

## Song of Solomon 6:6

Your teeth are like sheep coming from washing. 📖

Each has its twin.

Not one is missing.

## Song of Solomon 6:7

Your brow behind your veil is like a pomegranate slice. 📖

## Song of Solomon 6:8

There are sixty queens, eighty concubines, and young women without number. 📖

**Help with concubines:** Women in sexual household relationships without the full status of wives.

## Song of Solomon 6:9

But my dove, my perfect one, is unique, her mother’s favorite. 📖

Young women see her and call her blessed.

Queens and concubines praise her.

## Song of Solomon 6:10

Who shines like dawn, fair as the moon, bright as the sun, majestic as stars in procession? 📖

## Song of Solomon 6:11

I went to the walnut grove to see the valley’s blossoms. 📖

I looked for budding vines and flowering pomegranates.

## Song of Solomon 6:12

Before I knew it, my desire placed me among my people’s royal chariots. 📖

**About this image:** The source wording is retained; its precise sense and speaker remain for editorial review.

## Song of Solomon 6:13

Come back, come back, Shulammite! 📖

Come back, come back, so we may look at you.

Why gaze at the Shulammite as at Mahanaim’s dance?

**Help with Shulammite and Mahanaim:** Names or designations used in the poem.

Their precise reference here remains for editorial review.
