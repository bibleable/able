# Song of Solomon 1 — The lovers praise one another

**About this chapter**

**Speaker guide — editorial**

The woman speaks in verses 2–7, 12–14, and 16–17.

A male voice praises her in verses 9–10 and 15; a group speaks in verse 11.

The reply in verse 8 and the plural voice within verse 4 are not explicitly identified.

These assignments are provisional.

## Song of Solomon 1:1

This is Solomon’s Song of Songs. 📖

## Song of Solomon 1:2

Let him kiss me with his mouth’s kisses! 📖

Your love delights me more than wine.

## Song of Solomon 1:3

Your perfume smells pleasing. 📖

Your name is like perfume poured out.

No wonder the young women adore you!

## Song of Solomon 1:4

Take me away with you. 📖

Let us hurry!

May the king bring me into his rooms.

We will rejoice and delight in you.

We will praise your love more than wine.

They are right to adore you.

## Song of Solomon 1:5

Daughters of Jerusalem, I am dark, yet lovely. 📖

I am like Kedar’s tents and Solomon’s curtains.

**Help with this comparison:** The woman describes her dark appearance and beauty together.

Dark skin is not described here as a moral fault.

## Song of Solomon 1:6

Do not stare because I am dark. 📖

The sun has looked on me.

My mother’s sons were angry with me.

They made me care for vineyards, but I neglected my own vineyard.

**Help with the sun:** The woman describes the sun darkening her skin.

Her own vineyard may also be a poetic picture; its precise sense remains for review.

## Song of Solomon 1:7

You whom I love, tell me where you feed your sheep. 📖

Where do you rest them at noon?

Why should I be like a veiled woman beside your companions’ flocks?

**Help with veiled:** Wearing a cloth covering over the face or head.

## Song of Solomon 1:8

If you do not know, most beautiful woman, follow the flock’s tracks. 📖

Feed your young goats near the shepherds’ tents.

## Song of Solomon 1:9

My darling, I compare you to a mare among Pharaoh’s chariots. 📖

**Help with mare and chariots:** A female horse, and wheeled vehicles.

This is an admiring comparison in the poem.

## Song of Solomon 1:10

Ornaments make your cheeks beautiful. 📖

Strings of jewels decorate your neck.

## Song of Solomon 1:11

We will make you gold ornaments set with silver beads. 📖

## Song of Solomon 1:12

My perfume spread its fragrance while the king was at his table. 📖

## Song of Solomon 1:13

My beloved is a sachet of myrrh resting between my breasts. 📖

**Help with sachet and myrrh:** A small bag, and a fragrant substance.

The woman compares her beloved to perfume kept close to her body.

## Song of Solomon 1:14

My beloved is a cluster of henna flowers in En-gedi’s vineyards. 📖

**Help with henna:** A flowering plant in this comparison of beauty and fragrance.

## Song of Solomon 1:15

You are beautiful, my darling! 📖

So very beautiful!

Your eyes are like doves.

## Song of Solomon 1:16

You are handsome, my beloved! 📖

So delightful!

Soft grass is our bed.

## Song of Solomon 1:17

Our house’s beams are cedars. 📖

Our rafters are fragrant fir trees.

**Help with beams and rafters:** Roof supports.

The lovers picture their surroundings as a house.
