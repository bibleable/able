# Psalms 137 — Grief and anger in Babylon

**About this chapter**

The exiles remember Jerusalem and describe their captors’ demands.

The ending expresses a disturbing wish for revenge, including the killing of infants.

## Psalms 137:1

By Babylon’s rivers we sat and wept when we remembered Zion. 📖

**Help with Zion:** A name associated with Jerusalem.

## Psalms 137:2

We hung our harps on the willow trees there. 📖

## Psalms 137:3

Our captors asked for a song. 📖

Our tormentors demanded joyful songs: “Sing us a song of Zion.”

## Psalms 137:4

How can we sing the LORD’s song in a foreign land? 📖

## Psalms 137:5

Jerusalem, if I forget you, may my right hand stop working. 📖

## Psalms 137:6

May my tongue stick to my mouth’s roof if I do not remember you. 📖

May this happen if I do not make Jerusalem my greatest joy!

## Psalms 137:7

LORD, remember Edom’s sons on the day Jerusalem fell. 📖

They said, “Destroy it!

Tear it down to its foundations!”

## Psalms 137:8

Daughter of Babylon, doomed to destruction, blessed is the one who repays what you did to us. 📖

**Help with Daughter of Babylon:** A poetic name for Babylon and its people.

## Psalms 137:9

Blessed is the one who seizes your infants and smashes them against the rocks. 📖

**About this verse:** This is the exiles’ terrible wish for revenge in their song of grief and anger.

It is not an editorial instruction to harm children.
