# Ezra 2 — The families who returned home

**About this chapter**

Families travel from Babylon to Judah. This chapter records who returned, what work some of them did, and what they gave for the temple.

## The people return from Babylon

## Ezra 2:1

King Nebuchadnezzar had taken people from Judah to Babylon.

Now people returned from exile to Jerusalem and Judah.

Each family went back to its own town.

## Ezra 2:2

Zerubbabel and Jeshua went with them.

So did Nehemiah, Seraiah, Reelaiah, Mordecai, Bilshan, Mispar, Bigvai, Rehum, and Baanah.

Here is the count of the men of Israel who returned.

**Reading help**

Some groups are named after an ancestor. Others are named after a town. Each number tells how many men returned in that group.

## Some families search for records

## Ezra 2:59

Some people came from Tel-melah, Tel-harsha, Cherub, Addan, and Immer.

They could not show that their families came from Israel.

## Ezra 2:60

These were the families of Delaiah, Tobiah, and Nekoda.

They numbered 652 in all.

## Ezra 2:61

Three families also wanted to serve as priests.

Their ancestors were Hobaiah, Hakkoz, and Barzillai.

Their ancestor Barzillai had married a daughter of another Barzillai, a man from Gilead.

He had taken her family’s name.

## Ezra 2:62

These men searched for their family records.

They could not find them.

So the men were considered unclean for priestly service. They could not serve as priests.

## Ezra 2:63

The governor told them to wait before eating the most holy food.

First a priest would have to ask God for a decision using the Urim and Thummim.

**Reading help**

The Urim and Thummim were sacred objects a priest used to ask God for guidance.

## People and animals on the journey

## Ezra 2:64

## Ezra 2:66

## The families give and settle at home

## Ezra 2:68

The families reached the temple site in Jerusalem.

Some family leaders freely gave gifts to rebuild God’s temple where it had stood before.

![The rooms of Solomon’s temple, the earlier temple in Jerusalem.](../pictures/solomons-temple-rooms.svg)

## Ezra 2:69

They gave what they could for the work.

Their gifts included 61,000 gold coins called darics.

They also gave 5,000 minas of silver and 100 sets of clothes for priests.

**Reading help**

A mina was a unit of weight. The people weighed their silver.

## Ezra 2:70

The priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, and temple servants settled in their towns.

Some other Israelites settled there too.

The rest of the Israelites went to live in their own towns.
