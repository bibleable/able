# Ezra 4 — Enemies oppose the rebuilding in Jerusalem

**About this chapter**

The families rebuilding the temple face opposition. Years later, officials write to another king about Jerusalem’s walls. The chapter includes their letter and the king’s reply.

## The family leaders answer an offer

## Ezra 4:1

The families who returned from exile began to build a temple.

It was for the LORD, the God of Israel.

The enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard about it.

## Ezra 4:2

They went to Zerubbabel and the family leaders.

“Let us help you build,” they said. “We seek your God too.

King Esar-haddon of Assyria brought us here.

We have offered sacrifices to your God ever since.”

## Ezra 4:3

Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the other family leaders answered them.

“Cyrus, king of Persia, gave this work to us alone.

We will build the temple for the LORD, the God of Israel.”

## Neighbors try to stop the temple work

## Ezra 4:4

Then people who lived nearby tried to discourage the builders from Judah.

They tried to make them afraid to build.

## Ezra 4:5

They paid advisers to keep the builders’ plans from succeeding.

This went on while Cyrus ruled Persia and continued until Darius ruled.

## Later accusations against Jerusalem

## Ezra 4:6

Later, when Xerxes began to rule, enemies accused the people of Judah and Jerusalem of wrongdoing.

**Reading help**

Here the story looks ahead to other kings. The letter to Artaxerxes concerns Jerusalem’s walls. Verse 24 returns to the earlier story of the temple.

## Ezra 4:7

When Artaxerxes ruled Persia, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and their companions wrote to him.

They wrote in Aramaic. Someone then translated the letter.

## Ezra 4:8

Rehum the commander and Shimshai the secretary wrote to King Artaxerxes against Jerusalem.

Here is what their letter said.

## Ezra 4:9

Rehum, Shimshai, and their companions sent the letter together.

They included judges and officials over Tripolis, Persia, Erech, and Babylon.

People from Elam who lived in Susa also joined them.

## Ezra 4:10

Others joined them too. Long before, the great and honored king Ashurbanipal had forced their people to move.

He settled them in the towns of Samaria and elsewhere west of the Euphrates River.

## The officials’ letter to the king

## Ezra 4:11

The letter began, “To King Artaxerxes, from your servants west of the Euphrates River.

![An open scroll with lines of writing.](../pictures/scroll.svg)

**Reading help**

The officials are speaking in this letter. They want the king to stop the building work.

## Ezra 4:12

We want you to know what the Jews who came here from your land are doing.

They have returned to Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that wicked city that rebelled against kings.

They are rebuilding its walls and repairing its foundations.

## Ezra 4:13

If they finish the city and its walls, they will stop paying you taxes and fees.

You will lose that money.

## Ezra 4:14

We serve in your palace. We cannot stand by while people dishonor you.

That is why we are writing.

## Ezra 4:15

Please search the records your ancestors kept.

You will find that Jerusalem stirred up trouble long ago.

Its people rebelled and caused harm to kings and their provinces.

That is why Jerusalem was destroyed.

## Ezra 4:16

If they rebuild the city and its walls, you will lose control of the land west of the Euphrates.”

**Pause and answer**

What did the officials ask the king to search?

## King Artaxerxes replies

## Ezra 4:17

King Artaxerxes sent a reply.

“Greetings to Rehum, Shimshai, and your companions in Samaria and elsewhere west of the Euphrates.

## Ezra 4:18

My servants translated your letter and read it to me.

## Ezra 4:19

I ordered a search of the records.

My servants found that Jerusalem had rebelled against kings long ago.

Its people had urged others to rebel.

## Ezra 4:20

Powerful kings once ruled in Jerusalem.

They ruled the whole region west of the Euphrates.

People paid them taxes and fees.

## Ezra 4:21

Give the men an order to stop work.

They must wait for my order before they rebuild the city.

## Ezra 4:22

Act on this order. Why let the danger grow and cause the king more harm?”

## Work comes to a stop

## Ezra 4:23

Rehum, Shimshai, and their companions heard the king’s reply.

They went straight to the Jews in Jerusalem.

They used force to stop the work.

## Ezra 4:24

Work on God’s temple in Jerusalem had also stopped.

It stood still until the second year of King Darius of Persia.
