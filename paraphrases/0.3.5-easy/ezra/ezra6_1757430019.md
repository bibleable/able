# Ezra 6 — The people finish the temple and celebrate

**About this chapter**

A scroll shows what Cyrus ordered years earlier. Darius tells his officials to help pay for the temple. When the work is done, the people gather to worship.

## The searchers find Cyrus’s order

## Ezra 6:1

King Darius ordered a search of the records kept with the treasures in Babylon.

## Ezra 6:2

The searchers found a scroll in the fortress at Ecbatana, in Media.

It held this record of an earlier order.

![A scroll unrolled to show its writing.](../pictures/scroll.svg)

## Ezra 6:3

In his first year as king, Cyrus gave this order.

“Rebuild God’s temple in Jerusalem so people can offer sacrifices there.

Give it a firm foundation.

Make it 60 cubits high and 60 cubits wide.

**Reading help**

A cubit was a measure of length. Sixty cubits is about 27 metres.

## Ezra 6:4

Use three layers of cut stone and one of wood.

Pay for the work with the king’s money.

## Ezra 6:5

Nebuchadnezzar took gold and silver objects from God’s temple to Babylon.

Return the objects to their places in God’s temple in Jerusalem.”

## Darius tells the officials to help

## Ezra 6:6

Now Darius gave his own order to Tattenai, governor west of the Euphrates River.

He also addressed Shethar-bozenai and the other officials there.

“Stay away from the building site.

## Ezra 6:7

Let the Jewish governor and elders carry on the work.

They may rebuild God’s temple where it stood before.

## Ezra 6:8

Here is how you must help those elders.

Use my share of the taxes from west of the Euphrates to pay them.

Pay the whole cost so the work can continue.

**Pause and answer**

Where did Darius say the money for the work should come from?

## Ezra 6:9

Give the priests in Jerusalem whatever they ask for each day.

They need young bulls, rams, and lambs for burnt offerings to the God of heaven.

Give them wheat, salt, wine, and oil too.

## Ezra 6:10

Then they can offer sacrifices whose smell pleases the God of heaven.

They can pray for my life and the lives of my sons.

## Darius warns against stopping the work

## Ezra 6:11

Anyone who interferes with my order will die on a beam from his own house.

His house will become a heap of broken stones.

## Ezra 6:12

God chose Jerusalem as the place for his name.

May God bring down any king or people who change this order or destroy his temple.

I, Darius, have given this order. Carry it out with care.”

## The builders finish the temple

## Ezra 6:13

Tattenai, Shethar-bozenai, and the other officials carefully obeyed King Darius.

## Ezra 6:14

The prophets Haggai and Zechariah, son of Iddo, spoke God’s messages.

Their words helped the Jewish elders keep building.

The people finished the temple as the God of Israel had commanded.

Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes, kings of Persia, had also given orders for the work.

## Ezra 6:15

The builders finished the temple on the third day of the month called Adar.

It was the sixth year of Darius’s rule.

## The people dedicate the temple

## Ezra 6:16

The priests, the Levites, and the others who had returned from exile celebrated together.

They were glad to set the temple apart for God.

## Ezra 6:17

They offered 100 bulls, 200 rams, and 400 lambs.

They also offered 12 male goats for the sins of all Israel.

There was one goat for each tribe.

**Reading help**

To dedicate the temple meant to set it apart as a place to worship God.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the people bring 12 goats?

## Ezra 6:18

They put the priests and the Levites into groups to serve God in Jerusalem.

They followed the instructions in the Book of Moses.

## The people share the Passover meal

## Ezra 6:19

On the fourteenth day of the first month, the people who had returned celebrated Passover.

## Ezra 6:20

The priests and Levites had all made themselves ready for worship as the law required.

The Levites killed the Passover lambs for the people who had returned, for the priests, and for themselves.

## Ezra 6:21

The Israelites who had come home ate the Passover meal.

Others joined them. They had turned away from the practices of the surrounding peoples that made them unclean for worship.

They wanted to worship the LORD, the God of Israel.

## Ezra 6:22

For seven days they celebrated the Feast of Unleavened Bread.

The LORD had given them joy. He had moved the king of Assyria to show them kindness.

The king had helped them rebuild the temple of Israel’s God.

**Reading help**

Unleavened bread is bread made without yeast. Passover reminded the people how the LORD had brought their ancestors out of Egypt.
