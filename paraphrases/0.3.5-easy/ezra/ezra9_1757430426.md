# Ezra 9 — Ezra prays about his people’s guilt

**About this chapter**

Leaders tell Ezra about marriages among the families who returned from exile. Ezra mourns. In his evening prayer, he remembers God’s kindness and admits what his people have done.

## The leaders bring Ezra a report

## Ezra 9:1

After this, the leaders came to me, Ezra.

They said, “Israel’s people have not kept apart from the peoples around us.

Even our priests and Levites have joined them.

Their practices are like those God hated among the Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, and Jebusites.

The Ammonites, Moabites, Egyptians, and Amorites also did these things.

## Ezra 9:2

Men of Israel have married daughters of these peoples.

They have also chosen wives from these peoples for their sons.

So the people God set apart have joined these families by marriage.

Our leaders and officials have led the way in breaking faith with God!”

**Reading help**

Ezra is telling the story. In these first two verses he reports what the leaders said to him.

## Ezra tears his clothes

## Ezra 9:3

When I heard this, I tore my tunic and cloak.

I pulled hair from my head and beard.

Then I sat down, horrified.

![A cloak, the outer garment Ezra tore when he heard the report.](../pictures/cloak.svg)

**Reading help**

Ezra wore his cloak over his tunic. Tearing both showed how deeply the news upset him.

## Ezra 9:4

People who trembled at the words of Israel’s God gathered around me.

They knew the people who had returned from exile had been unfaithful.

I sat there in shock until the evening offering.

## Ezra prays at the evening offering

## Ezra 9:5

At the evening offering, I got up, still wearing my torn clothes.

I knelt and stretched out my hands to the LORD my God.

## Ezra 9:6

I prayed, “My God, I am too ashamed to look up at you.

Our sins rise higher than our heads.

Our guilt reaches all the way to heaven.

**Reading help**

Ezra pictures the people’s guilt piled higher than their heads. He means they have done a great deal wrong. In his prayer he says “we” because he is speaking for his people.

## Ezra 9:7

Our people have been guilty since our ancestors’ time.

Because of our sins, foreign kings gained power over us, our kings, and our priests.

They killed some of us and took others prisoner.

They stole from us. We still live with shame today.

## Ezra remembers God’s kindness

## Ezra 9:8

Now, for a little while, you have shown us kindness, LORD our God.

You have kept some of us alive.

You have given us a secure place in your holy house.

Even as slaves, we have new life. Our eyes are bright again.

## Ezra 9:9

We are slaves, yet you have stayed with us.

You moved the kings of Persia to show us kindness.

You gave us new life so we could rebuild your ruined temple.

Your protection is like a wall around us in Judah and Jerusalem.

## Ezra recalls what God commanded

## Ezra 9:10

Our God, what can we say to you now?

We have turned away from your commands.

## Ezra 9:11

You gave us those commands through your servants the prophets.

You said, ‘You are entering a land to make it your own.

The people there have filled it from end to end with practices I hate.

What they have done has made the land unclean.

## Ezra 9:12

Do not give your daughters in marriage to their sons.

Do not take their daughters as wives for your sons.

Never seek peace or wealth for those peoples.

Then you can stay strong and eat the good food the land gives you.

You can pass the land on to your children forever.’

## Ezra admits the people’s guilt

## Ezra 9:13

Our evil deeds brought all this trouble on us.

Our God, you punished us less than we deserved.

You let this small group of us survive.

## Ezra 9:14

How can we break your commands again?

How can we marry into families that do these things you hate?

Would you not be angry enough to destroy us?

Would anyone be left alive?

## Ezra 9:15

LORD, God of Israel, you do what is right.

A small group of us is still alive today.

Here we are before you, guilty.

With such guilt, none of us can stand before you.”
