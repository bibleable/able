# 2 Kings 25 — Jerusalem falls, and its people go into exile

**About this chapter**

Babylon’s army captures Jerusalem and burns the temple. Some people stay in Judah to farm. Years later, Jehoiachin’s life changes in Babylon.

## Jerusalem runs out of food

## 2 Kings 25:1

Zedekiah was in his ninth year as king.

On the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar came with his whole army.

They camped outside Jerusalem and built a wall around it.

## 2 Kings 25:2

Babylon’s army kept Jerusalem surrounded until Zedekiah’s eleventh year as king.

## 2 Kings 25:3

By the ninth day of the fourth month, the people inside Jerusalem had no food left.

**Reading help**

An army that surrounds a city can stop food from reaching the people inside. This is called a siege.

## Babylon captures Zedekiah

## 2 Kings 25:4

The army broke through Jerusalem’s wall.

That night Judah’s soldiers fled through a gate near the king’s garden, between two walls.

They headed toward the Arabah, though Babylon’s soldiers surrounded the city.

## 2 Kings 25:5

Babylon’s soldiers caught up with Zedekiah on the plains of Jericho.

All his soldiers left him.

## 2 Kings 25:6

Babylon’s soldiers seized Zedekiah. They took him to their king at Riblah.

There he heard his sentence.

## 2 Kings 25:7

They killed Zedekiah’s sons in front of him.

Then they blinded him, bound him in bronze chains, and took him to Babylon.

## The temple burns

## 2 Kings 25:8

Nebuzaradan was captain of the guard for Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon.

In Nebuchadnezzar’s nineteenth year as king, Nebuzaradan entered Jerusalem.

It was the seventh day of the fifth month.

## 2 Kings 25:9

Nebuzaradan burned the LORD’s temple and the royal palace.

He burned the houses in Jerusalem and every important building.

## 2 Kings 25:10

Babylon’s army, under Nebuzaradan, tore down the walls around Jerusalem.

## 2 Kings 25:11

Nebuzaradan took the people still in the city into exile.

He also took those who had already gone over to Babylon’s king and the rest of the people.

## 2 Kings 25:12

Nebuzaradan left some of the poorest people in Judah.

They stayed to care for the vineyards and fields.

## The bronze from the temple goes to Babylon

## 2 Kings 25:13

Babylon’s soldiers broke up the bronze pillars and stands in the temple.

They also broke up the huge bronze basin called the Sea.

They carried the bronze to Babylon.

## 2 Kings 25:14

They took the pots, shovels, lamp trimmers, and dishes.

They took all the bronze tools used in temple worship.

## 2 Kings 25:15

Nebuzaradan took the pans for burning incense and the bowls for sprinkling.

He took everything made of pure gold or fine silver.

## 2 Kings 25:16

Solomon had made the two pillars, the Sea, and the movable stands for the temple.

Together they contained more bronze than anyone could weigh.

![The small temple basins and the large basin called the Sea, before the soldiers took the bronze away.](../pictures/solomons-bronze-basins.svg)

## 2 Kings 25:17

Each pillar stood about eight metres tall.

The bronze top on one pillar was about a metre and a half high.

All around it was bronze netting decorated with shapes like pomegranates.

The other pillar had the same decoration.

## Nebuzaradan takes prisoners to Riblah

## 2 Kings 25:18

Nebuzaradan seized Seraiah the chief priest and Zephaniah, the priest next in rank.

He also took the three men who guarded the temple doors.

## 2 Kings 25:19

In the city he found an official in charge of soldiers and five of the king’s advisers.

He took them and the army secretary who enrolled men for service.

He also took 60 other men.

## 2 Kings 25:20

Nebuzaradan brought all these prisoners to the king of Babylon at Riblah.

## 2 Kings 25:21

There in Riblah, in the land of Hamath, Babylon’s king had them killed.

So the people of Judah went into exile, far from their land.

## Gedaliah governs the people still in Judah

## 2 Kings 25:22

Nebuchadnezzar chose Gedaliah to govern the people left in Judah.

Gedaliah was Ahikam’s son and Shaphan’s grandson.

## 2 Kings 25:23

Judah’s army commanders heard about Gedaliah. They came with their men to Mizpah.

There was Ishmael son of Nethaniah, and Johanan son of Kareah.

Seraiah son of Tanhumeth from Netophah came too.

So did Jaazaniah, whose father came from Maacah.

## 2 Kings 25:24

Gedaliah gave them his word. “Do not be afraid of Babylon’s officials.

Stay here and serve Babylon’s king. Then things will go well for you.”

## The people flee to Egypt

## 2 Kings 25:25

In the seventh month, Ishmael came to Mizpah with ten men.

Ishmael belonged to the royal family. His grandfather was Elishama.

They killed Gedaliah and the people of Judah and Babylon who were with him.

## 2 Kings 25:26

The rest of the people, of every rank, fled to Egypt with the commanders.

They were afraid of what the Babylonians would do.

## Jehoiachin leaves prison

## 2 Kings 25:27

Jehoiachin, king of Judah, had spent 37 years in exile.

That year Evil-merodach became king of Babylon.

On the twenty-seventh day of the twelfth month, he set Jehoiachin free from prison.

**Reading help**

Jehoiachin had ruled Judah before his uncle Zedekiah. [Read how Jehoiachin came to Babylon.](2kings24_1757418465.md)

## 2 Kings 25:28

Babylon’s king spoke kindly to Jehoiachin.

He gave Jehoiachin a place of greater honor than the other kings with him in Babylon.

## 2 Kings 25:29

Jehoiachin took off his prison clothes.

He ate regularly at the king’s table for the rest of his life.

## 2 Kings 25:30

Every day of Jehoiachin’s life, Babylon’s king provided for him.
