# 2 Kings 8 — A woman gets her land back

**About this chapter**

A family returns home after seven years away. In Aram, Elisha brings news about who will rule next. In Judah, a father and then his son become king.

## A family leaves home during a famine

## 2 Kings 8:1

Elisha had brought a woman’s son back to life.

Later he told her, “Take everyone in your household.

Find a place to live in another country.

The LORD has decided there will be a famine here.

For seven years, people will have too little food.”

## 2 Kings 8:2

The woman did what Elisha said.

She and her household went to the land of the Philistines.

They lived there as foreigners for seven years.

## 2 Kings 8:3

After seven years, the woman came home.

She went to the king of Israel.

She asked for her house and land back.

## Gehazi recognizes the woman

## 2 Kings 8:4

The king was talking to Gehazi, Elisha’s servant.

“Tell me about the great things Elisha has done,” he said.

## 2 Kings 8:5

Gehazi was telling the king how Elisha brought someone back to life.

Just then, the woman came to ask for her property.

Gehazi said, “My king, here is the woman!

This is the son Elisha brought back to life.”

## 2 Kings 8:6

The king asked her about it. She said it was true.

The king told an officer, “Give back everything she owned.

Give her all her field has earned since she left.”

## A sick king sends Hazael to Elisha

## 2 Kings 8:7

Elisha came to Damascus. Ben-hadad, king of Aram, was sick.

Someone told the king, “The man of God is here.”

## 2 Kings 8:8

Ben-hadad told Hazael, “Take a gift to Elisha.

Ask him what the LORD says. Will I get well?”

## 2 Kings 8:9

Hazael took fine things from Damascus as a gift.

It took 40 camels to carry them.

Hazael stood before Elisha and said, “Your son Ben-hadad, king of Aram, sent me.

He asks, ‘Will I get well?’”

**Reading help**

Ben-hadad calls himself Elisha’s son to show respect.

## 2 Kings 8:10

Elisha said, “Tell him, ‘You will get well.’

Yet the LORD has shown me that he will die.”

## Elisha cries for Israel

## 2 Kings 8:11

Elisha looked steadily at Hazael until Hazael felt uneasy.

Then Elisha began to cry.

## 2 Kings 8:12

Hazael asked, “Sir, why are you crying?”

Elisha answered, “I know the harm you will do to Israel.

You will burn their forts and kill their young men.

You will kill their little children and tear open pregnant women.”

## 2 Kings 8:13

Hazael said, “How could someone like me do such a terrible thing?

I am only your servant, as low as a dog.”

Elisha answered, “The LORD has shown me you will rule Aram.”

**Reading help**

Hazael compares himself to a dog to say he has little power.

## 2 Kings 8:14

Hazael went back to Ben-hadad.

The king asked, “What did Elisha say?”

Hazael answered, “He told me you would get well.”

## 2 Kings 8:15

The next day Hazael killed Ben-hadad.

Then Hazael became king of Aram.

## Jehoram rules Judah

## 2 Kings 8:16

Joram, Ahab’s son, was in his fifth year as king of Israel.

In Judah, Jehoram became king after his father Jehoshaphat.

## 2 Kings 8:17

Jehoram was 32 when he became king of Judah.

He ruled in Jerusalem for eight years.

## 2 Kings 8:18

Jehoram married one of Ahab’s daughters.

He followed the example of Ahab’s family, the kings of Israel.

The LORD saw the evil Jehoram did.

## 2 Kings 8:19

Yet the LORD chose to spare Judah for David’s sake.

David had served him. The LORD had promised that a lamp would always burn for David and his family.

![An oil lamp with a burning flame.](../pictures/davids-lamp.svg)

**Reading help**

The burning lamp is a picture of God’s promise. David’s family line would continue.

## Edom chooses its own king

## 2 Kings 8:20

While Jehoram ruled, Edom broke away from Judah.

The people of Edom chose their own king.

## 2 Kings 8:21

Jehoram took all his chariots to Zair.

Edom’s army surrounded him and his chariot commanders.

Jehoram fought the Edomites that night.

But his troops ran home.

## 2 Kings 8:22

Edom still refused to obey Judah when this account was written.

Libnah also broke away from Judah at that time.

## 2 Kings 8:23

The Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah tells more about Jehoram.

It records the other things he did.

## 2 Kings 8:24

Jehoram died. His people buried him with his ancestors in the City of David.

His son Ahaziah became king of Judah.

## Ahaziah visits the wounded king of Israel

## 2 Kings 8:25

Ahaziah became king of Judah in the twelfth year that Joram, Ahab’s son, ruled Israel.

## 2 Kings 8:26

Ahaziah was 22 when he became king.

He ruled in Jerusalem for one year.

His mother was Athaliah. Her grandfather Omri had ruled Israel.

## 2 Kings 8:27

Ahaziah had married into Ahab’s family. He followed their example.

Like them, he did what the LORD saw was evil.

## 2 Kings 8:28

Ahaziah joined Joram, king of Israel, at Ramoth-gilead.

They fought against Hazael, king of Aram.

Soldiers from Aram wounded Joram.

## 2 Kings 8:29

Joram went back to Jezreel to get well.

He was recovering from his wounds from the battle at Ramah.

Ahaziah, king of Judah, went to Jezreel to visit him.
