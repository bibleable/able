# 2 Kings 12 — Workers repair the temple

**About this chapter**

Joash wants the temple in Jerusalem repaired. Jehoiada puts a chest by the altar. The money people bring pays for workers, wood, and stone.

## Jehoiada teaches King Joash

## 2 Kings 12:1

Joash became king of Judah in Jehu’s seventh year as king of Israel.

Joash ruled in Jerusalem for 40 years.

His mother was Zibiah from Beersheba.

## 2 Kings 12:2

While Jehoiada the priest taught him, Joash did what the LORD said was right.

## 2 Kings 12:3

The places of worship on the hills remained.

People still offered sacrifices and burned incense there.

**Reading help**

Incense gives off a sweet smell when people burn it.

## Joash asks the priests to repair the temple

## 2 Kings 12:4

Joash told the priests, “Collect the money people bring to the LORD’s temple.

Some pay when the people are counted.

Some bring money they promised to give.

Others bring money because they want to give.

## 2 Kings 12:5

Each priest should collect money from the people he serves.

Use it to fix whatever is broken in the temple.”

## The work has still not begun

## 2 Kings 12:6

By the twenty-third year of Joash’s rule, the temple still needed repairs.

The priests had not done the work.

## 2 Kings 12:7

Joash called Jehoiada and the other priests.

“Why haven’t you repaired the temple?” he asked.

“Stop collecting money from the people you serve.

Hand over the money for the repairs.”

## 2 Kings 12:8

The priests agreed to stop collecting money from the people.

They also agreed to leave the repairs to others.

## A chest holds the gifts

## 2 Kings 12:9

Jehoiada made a hole in the lid of a chest.

He placed the chest beside the altar.

It was on the right as people entered the temple.

The priests who guarded the entrance put the people’s money inside.

![A chest with a hole in its lid for the money people brought.](../pictures/temple-money-chest.svg)

## 2 Kings 12:10

When the chest held a lot of money, two men came.

They were the king’s secretary and the chief priest.

Together they counted the money and tied it in bags.

## The money reaches the workers

## 2 Kings 12:11

They gave the money to the men in charge of repairs.

Those men paid the carpenters and builders.

## 2 Kings 12:12

They paid the men who cut and laid stone.

They bought wood and stone cut to fit.

They also paid the other costs of fixing the temple.

**Pause and answer**

What materials did the men buy for the repairs?

## 2 Kings 12:13

The priests did not spend this money on silver bowls, lamp trimmers, bowls for sprinkling, or trumpets.

They bought no gold or silver objects for the temple with it.

## 2 Kings 12:14

The money went to the workers.

They used it to repair the LORD’s temple.

## 2 Kings 12:15

No one asked the men paying the workers to account for each payment.

People trusted them because they were honest.

## 2 Kings 12:16

Money from guilt offerings and sin offerings belonged to the priests.

It did not go into the temple collection.

**Reading help**

Guilt offerings and sin offerings were gifts people brought to God when they had done wrong.

## Joash sends gifts to Hazael

## 2 Kings 12:17

About that time, King Hazael of Aram captured Gath.

Then Hazael decided to attack Jerusalem.

## 2 Kings 12:18

Joash took the gifts that earlier kings of Judah had set apart for God.

Those kings were Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, and Ahaziah.

Joash added his own gifts and all the gold from the temple and palace stores.

He sent everything to Hazael. Then Hazael left Jerusalem alone.

## Amaziah becomes king

## 2 Kings 12:19

The Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah tells more about Joash.

It records the other things he did.

## 2 Kings 12:20

Some of Joash’s servants plotted against him.

They killed him at Beth-millo, on the road down to Silla.

## 2 Kings 12:21

The servants who killed Joash were Jozabad and Jehozabad.

Jozabad was the son of Shimeath. Jehozabad was the son of Shomer.

People buried Joash with his ancestors in the City of David.

His son Amaziah became king in his place.
