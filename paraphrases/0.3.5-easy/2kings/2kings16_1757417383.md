# 2 Kings 16 — Ahaz brings a new altar to Jerusalem

**About this chapter**

Ahaz asks Assyria’s king to help defend Judah. Later, Ahaz sees an altar in Damascus. He wants one like it at the temple in Jerusalem.

## Ahaz rules Judah

## 2 Kings 16:1

Ahaz, son of Jotham, became king of Judah.

Pekah, son of Remaliah, was then in his seventeenth year as king of Israel.

## 2 Kings 16:2

Ahaz was 20 when he became king.

He ruled in Jerusalem for 16 years.

His ancestor David did what pleased the LORD.

Ahaz did what the LORD his God saw was wrong.

## 2 Kings 16:3

Ahaz followed the example of Israel’s kings.

He even killed his own son as an offering in a fire.

The LORD hated this practice. The nations he drove out before Israel had done it too.

## 2 Kings 16:4

Ahaz offered sacrifices and burned incense at hilltop places of worship.

He did the same on the hills and under every leafy tree.

**Reading help**

Incense gives off a sweet smell when people burn it.

## Two kings attack Jerusalem

## 2 Kings 16:5

Two kings attacked Jerusalem. They were Rezin of Aram and Pekah of Israel.

Their armies surrounded Ahaz, but they could not defeat him.

## 2 Kings 16:6

Rezin took back the city of Elath for Aram.

He forced the men of Judah to leave.

He sent people from Edom to live there.

They still lived there when this account was written.

## Ahaz asks Assyria for help

## 2 Kings 16:7

Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria.

“I am your servant and your son,” Ahaz said.

“The kings of Aram and Israel are attacking me. Please come and save me.”

**Reading help**

Ahaz calls himself the king’s son to show that he accepts the king’s authority. Ahaz hopes the king will protect him.

## 2 Kings 16:8

Ahaz took silver and gold from the LORD’s temple.

He also took silver and gold from the palace storerooms.

He sent them as a gift to Assyria’s king.

## 2 Kings 16:9

The king of Assyria answered Ahaz’s call for help.

He captured Damascus and took its people away as prisoners to Kir.

He also killed Rezin.

## Uriah builds an altar

## 2 Kings 16:10

Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Assyria’s king.

There Ahaz saw an altar.

He sent Uriah the priest a model of it.

He also sent plans showing how to build it.

**Reading help**

An altar is a place where people put offerings during worship.

## 2 Kings 16:11

Uriah followed all the plans Ahaz sent from Damascus.

He finished building the altar before Ahaz came home.

## 2 Kings 16:12

Ahaz returned from Damascus and saw the new altar.

He went up to it and gave offerings there.

## 2 Kings 16:13

Ahaz gave a burnt offering and a grain offering.

He poured out a drink offering.

He sprinkled blood from his peace offerings on the altar.

## Ahaz moves the bronze altar

## 2 Kings 16:14

The old bronze altar stood between the new altar and the LORD’s temple.

Ahaz moved the bronze altar to the north side of the new one.

## 2 Kings 16:15

Ahaz told Uriah, “Use the large new altar for the morning burnt offering and the evening grain offering.

Use it for my burnt offerings and grain offerings too.

Bring the people’s burnt offerings, grain offerings, and drink offerings there.

Sprinkle the blood from all the burnt offerings and sacrifices on it.

I will use the bronze altar when I need guidance.”

## 2 Kings 16:16

Uriah did everything King Ahaz told him to do.

**Pause and answer**

## Ahaz changes more things at the temple

## 2 Kings 16:17

Ahaz cut the frames off the movable stands.

He took the bronze bowl off each stand.

Bronze oxen held up a much larger bowl called the Sea.

Ahaz took the Sea down and set it on a stone base.

## 2 Kings 16:18

Ahaz also removed the covering built at the temple for the Sabbath.

He closed the king’s entrance outside the temple.

He made these changes because of the king of Assyria.

## 2 Kings 16:19

The Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah records the other things Ahaz did.

## 2 Kings 16:20

Ahaz died. His people buried him with his ancestors in the City of David.

His son Hezekiah became king of Judah.
