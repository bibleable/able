# 2 Kings 18 — Assyria challenges Hezekiah’s trust in the LORD

**About this chapter**

Hezekiah trusts the LORD. When Assyria’s army comes to Jerusalem, an official speaks for Assyria’s king. He tries to make the people stop trusting Hezekiah and the LORD.

## Hezekiah rules Judah

## 2 Kings 18:1

Hezekiah, son of Ahaz, became king of Judah.

Hoshea, son of Elah, was in his third year as king of Israel.

## 2 Kings 18:2

Hezekiah was 25 when he became king.

He ruled in Jerusalem for 29 years.

His mother was Abi, daughter of Zechariah.

## 2 Kings 18:3

Hezekiah did what pleased the LORD, just as his ancestor David had done.

## 2 Kings 18:4

Hezekiah removed the hilltop places of worship.

He smashed the stone pillars and cut down the poles used to worship Asherah.

Moses had made a bronze snake called Nehushtan.

People still burned incense to it, so Hezekiah broke it apart.

## Hezekiah trusts the LORD

## 2 Kings 18:5

Hezekiah trusted the LORD, the God of Israel.

No king of Judah before or after him trusted the LORD as he did.

## 2 Kings 18:6

Hezekiah stayed faithful to the LORD.

He kept the commands the LORD had given Moses.

## 2 Kings 18:7

The LORD was with Hezekiah. Hezekiah succeeded wherever he went.

He rebelled against Assyria’s king and refused to serve him.

## 2 Kings 18:8

Hezekiah defeated the Philistines as far as Gaza and its border.

His victories reached from watchtowers to walled cities.

## Assyria takes Samaria

## 2 Kings 18:9

In Hezekiah’s fourth year, Shalmaneser, king of Assyria, surrounded Samaria.

It was Hoshea’s seventh year as king of Israel.

## 2 Kings 18:10

After three years, Assyria’s army captured Samaria.

Hezekiah was in his sixth year as king of Judah.

Hoshea was in his ninth year as king of Israel.

## 2 Kings 18:11

Assyria’s king forced the Israelites to leave for Assyria.

They had to live in Halah, in Gozan by the Habor River, and in the cities of the Medes.

## 2 Kings 18:12

This happened because the Israelites refused to listen to the LORD.

They broke their covenant with him.

They would not obey the commands his servant Moses had given them.

## Assyria takes Judah’s walled cities

## 2 Kings 18:13

In Hezekiah’s fourteenth year, Sennacherib, king of Assyria, attacked Judah.

He captured all its fortified cities.

## 2 Kings 18:14

Hezekiah sent a message to Sennacherib at Lachish.

“I have done wrong. Leave me, and I will pay whatever you ask.”

Sennacherib demanded 300 talents of silver and 30 talents of gold.

**Reading help**

A talent was a large unit of weight. People weighed silver and gold to make payments.

## 2 Kings 18:15

Hezekiah gave him all the silver in the LORD’s temple and in the palace storerooms.

## 2 Kings 18:16

Hezekiah had covered the temple doors and doorposts with gold.

Now he stripped off that gold and gave it to Assyria’s king.

## Officials meet outside Jerusalem

## 2 Kings 18:17

Still, Assyria’s king sent three officials and a large army from Lachish to Jerusalem.

The officials held the titles Tartan, Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh.

They stopped beside the channel that carried water from the upper pool.

It was on the road to the Launderer’s Field.

## 2 Kings 18:18

The officials called for Hezekiah. Three men came out to meet them.

Eliakim, son of Hilkiah, managed the palace.

Shebnah was the secretary. Joah, son of Asaph, kept the records.

## 2 Kings 18:19

The Rabshakeh spoke for Assyria.

“Tell Hezekiah what the great king of Assyria says.

‘Why are you so sure of yourself?

## 2 Kings 18:20

You talk about plans and strength for war. That is just talk.

Who are you trusting, now that you have rebelled against me?

## The spokesman mocks Judah’s trust

## 2 Kings 18:21

You lean on Egypt like a man leaning on a broken reed.

The reed pierces his hand.

That is what Pharaoh, king of Egypt, is like to everyone who trusts him.’

![A reed with a long stem bending to one side.](../pictures/bending-reed.svg)

**Reading help**

A reed is a plant with a long stem. The spokesman compares Egypt to a broken walking stick. He means that trusting Egypt will hurt Judah.

## 2 Kings 18:22

Perhaps you will say, ‘We trust the LORD our God.’

Didn’t Hezekiah tear down his hilltop places of worship and his altars?

Didn’t he tell Judah and Jerusalem to worship at this one altar in Jerusalem?

## 2 Kings 18:23

Make a bargain with my master, the king of Assyria.

I will give you 2,000 horses if you can find enough riders!

## 2 Kings 18:24

You depend on Egypt for chariots and riders.

How could you drive back even one of my master’s lowest officers?

## 2 Kings 18:25

Do you think I came to destroy this place without the LORD?

The LORD himself told me to come and destroy this land.”

**Reading help**

The spokesman claims the LORD sent him. These are the Assyrian spokesman’s words.

## Judah’s officials ask him to speak Aramaic

## 2 Kings 18:26

Eliakim, Shebnah, and Joah asked the Rabshakeh, “Please speak to us in Aramaic.

We understand it. If you speak Hebrew, the people on the wall can hear us.”

**Pause and answer**

Which language did Judah’s officials ask the spokesman to use?

## 2 Kings 18:27

The Rabshakeh answered, “Do you think my master sent me only to you and your king?

This message is for the people on the wall too.

They will have to eat their own dung and drink their own urine, just as you will.”

## The spokesman calls to the people

## 2 Kings 18:28

Then the Rabshakeh stood and shouted in Hebrew.

“Listen to the great king of Assyria!

## 2 Kings 18:29

The king says, ‘Do not let Hezekiah fool you.

He cannot save you from me.

## 2 Kings 18:30

Hezekiah tells you to trust the LORD.

He says the LORD will save you and keep this city from me.

Do not believe him.

## 2 Kings 18:31

Listen to me. Make peace with me and come out.

Each of you can eat your own grapes and figs.

You can drink water from your own storage tank.

## 2 Kings 18:32

Later I will take you to a land like yours.

It has grain and new wine, bread and vineyards, olive trees and honey.

You will live. Do not let Hezekiah fool you by saying the LORD will save you.

## The people give no answer

## 2 Kings 18:33

Has any nation’s god saved its land from the king of Assyria?

## 2 Kings 18:34

Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad?

Where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?

Did any of them save Samaria from me?

## 2 Kings 18:35

Which of their gods kept me from taking their land?

How then can the LORD save Jerusalem from me?’”

## 2 Kings 18:36

The people stayed silent. No one answered the Rabshakeh.

Hezekiah had told them, “Do not answer him.”

## 2 Kings 18:37

Eliakim, Shebnah, and Joah went back to Hezekiah with their clothes torn.

They told him what the Rabshakeh had said.

**Reading help**

Tearing their clothes showed how upset the officials were.
