# 2 Samuel 1 — David sings for Saul and Jonathan

**About this chapter**

A man brings David news from Mount Gilboa. David mourns for Saul and Jonathan. He asks the people of Judah to learn his song.

## A messenger comes to Ziklag

## 2 Samuel 1:1

Saul had died.

David returned from fighting and killing the Amalekites.

He stayed in Ziklag for two days.

## 2 Samuel 1:2

On the third day, a man came from Saul’s camp.

His clothes were torn. He had dust on his head.

He bowed low before David to show respect.

**Reading help**

People tore their clothes and put dust on their heads to show grief.

## 2 Samuel 1:3

David asked, “Where have you come from?”

The man answered, “I escaped from Israel’s camp.”

## 2 Samuel 1:4

David asked, “Please tell me what happened.”

The man said, “Our soldiers ran away.

Many died in the battle.

Saul and his son Jonathan died too.”

**Pause and answer**

What news did the messenger bring?

## David asks how the messenger knows

## 2 Samuel 1:5

David asked the young man, “How do you know Saul and Jonathan are dead?”

## 2 Samuel 1:6

The man answered, “I happened to be on Mount Gilboa.

I saw Saul leaning on his spear.

Chariots and men on horses were coming closer to him.

## 2 Samuel 1:7

Saul turned and saw me. He called to me.

I answered, ‘Here I am.’

## 2 Samuel 1:8

He asked me who I was.

I told him I was an Amalekite.

## 2 Samuel 1:9

Saul begged me to kill him.

He said, ‘I am in terrible pain, but I am still alive.’

## 2 Samuel 1:10

I stood over Saul and killed him.

I knew he could not live after he fell.

I took his crown and the band on his arm.

My lord, I have brought them to you.”

## David and his men mourn

## 2 Samuel 1:11

David tore his clothes.

All his men tore their clothes too.

## 2 Samuel 1:12

They cried and went without food until evening.

They mourned for Saul and Jonathan.

They mourned for the LORD’s people, the Israelites who died in battle.

## David judges the messenger by his words

## 2 Samuel 1:13

David asked the messenger, “Where are you from?”

He answered, “My father is a foreigner living among you.

I am an Amalekite.”

## 2 Samuel 1:14

David asked, “How could you kill the king the LORD anointed without being afraid?”

**Reading help**

Samuel poured oil on Saul’s head to show that the LORD had chosen him as king. This is what David means by “anointed.”

## 2 Samuel 1:15

David called one of his young men.

He ordered him to put the messenger to death.

The young man struck the messenger, and the messenger died.

## 2 Samuel 1:16

David had told him, “You are responsible for your own death.

Your own words speak against you.

You said, ‘I killed the king the LORD anointed.’”

**Pause and answer**

Whose words did David use when he judged the messenger?

## Judah learns David’s song

## 2 Samuel 1:17

David sang a song of grief for Saul and his son Jonathan.

## 2 Samuel 1:18

David ordered the people of Judah to learn the Song of the Bow.

The Book of Jashar records this song.

## 2 Samuel 1:19

David sang, “Israel, those who brought you honor lie dead on your hills.

How the mighty have fallen!

**Reading help**

David repeats “How the mighty have fallen!” He is grieving because strong fighters have died.

## 2 Samuel 1:20

Keep this news out of Gath and the streets of Ashkelon.

Do not let the Philistine women hear it and dance for joy.

## David remembers the two fighters

## 2 Samuel 1:21

Mountains of Gilboa, may no dew or rain fall on you!

May your fields yield no grain to offer to God.

There the shield of a mighty fighter lay in disgrace.

It was Saul’s shield. No one rubs it with oil anymore.

## 2 Samuel 1:22

Jonathan never drew back his bow from powerful enemies.

Saul’s sword never came back empty.

They struck down mighty fighters.

## 2 Samuel 1:23

People loved Saul and Jonathan in life.

Even in death they stayed together.

They were faster than eagles and stronger than lions.

**Reading help**

David compares Saul and Jonathan to fast birds and strong animals. These pictures honor the men he has lost.

## 2 Samuel 1:24

Women of Israel, weep for Saul!

He dressed you in fine red clothes.

He gave you gold to wear on them.

**Pause and answer**

Who does David ask to weep for Saul?

## David grieves for his friend

## 2 Samuel 1:25

How the mighty have fallen in battle!

Jonathan lies dead on your hills.

## 2 Samuel 1:26

I grieve for you, Jonathan, my brother.

You brought me such joy.

Your love for me was wonderful.

It meant more to me than the love of women.

**Reading help**

David calls Jonathan his brother here. Jonathan was his close friend and the brother of his wife, Michal.

## 2 Samuel 1:27

How the mighty have fallen!

Their weapons of war are gone!”
