# 2 Samuel 11 — David, Bathsheba, and Uriah

**About this chapter**

David sends for Bathsheba while her husband Uriah is away. Later, David orders Joab to let Uriah die in battle.

## David stays in Jerusalem

## 2 Samuel 11:1

Spring came, the season when kings went to war.

David sent Joab, his servants, and Israel’s whole army.

They defeated the Ammonites and surrounded the city of Rabbah.

David stayed in Jerusalem.

## 2 Samuel 11:2

One evening David got out of bed.

He walked on the roof of his palace.

From there he saw a very beautiful woman bathing.

## David sends for Bathsheba

## 2 Samuel 11:3

David sent someone to find out who she was.

The answer came back, “Her name is Bathsheba.

Her father is Eliam.

Her husband is Uriah the Hittite.”

## 2 Samuel 11:4

David sent messengers to bring Bathsheba to him.

Bathsheba had just washed to be clean for worship after her period.

When she came, David had sex with her.

Then Bathsheba went home.

## 2 Samuel 11:5

Bathsheba became pregnant.

She sent David a message saying, “I am pregnant.”

## Uriah returns from the war

## 2 Samuel 11:6

David sent Joab an order.

He said, “Send me Uriah the Hittite.”

So Joab sent Uriah to David.

## 2 Samuel 11:7

When Uriah arrived, David asked about Joab and the soldiers.

David also asked how the war was going.

## 2 Samuel 11:8

David told Uriah, “Go home and wash your feet.”

Uriah left the palace.

David sent a gift after him.

## 2 Samuel 11:9

Uriah slept at the palace door with the king’s servants.

He did not go home.

## Uriah explains why he stays

## 2 Samuel 11:10

David heard that Uriah had stayed at the palace.

He asked Uriah, “You just came back from a journey.

Why didn’t you go home?”

## 2 Samuel 11:11

Uriah answered, “The ark is in a tent.

The men of Israel and Judah are in tents too.

My commander Joab and his soldiers camp in the open.

How can I go home to eat and drink and have sex with my wife?

As surely as you live, I will never do that!”

**Reading help**

The ark was a sacred chest. Uriah remembers the people who still live in tents while the army is away.

## 2 Samuel 11:12

David said, “Stay here one more day.

Tomorrow I will send you back.”

Uriah stayed in Jerusalem that day and the next.

## 2 Samuel 11:13

David invited Uriah to eat and drink with him.

David got Uriah drunk.

That evening Uriah lay on his bed beside David’s servants.

Again, Uriah did not go home.

## Uriah carries David’s letter

## 2 Samuel 11:14

The next morning David wrote Joab a letter.

Uriah carried the letter to Joab.

## 2 Samuel 11:15

David’s letter ordered Joab to leave Uriah without help where the fighting was worst.

David wanted Uriah to die in battle.

## 2 Samuel 11:16

Joab’s army still surrounded the city.

Joab sent Uriah where the strongest enemy soldiers were.

## 2 Samuel 11:17

The men of the city came out to fight Joab’s army.

Some of David’s men died.

Uriah the Hittite died too.

## Joab sends word to David

## 2 Samuel 11:18

Joab sent a messenger to David with news of the battle.

## 2 Samuel 11:19

Before the messenger left, Joab told him what to do.

Joab said, “Tell the king everything that happened.

## 2 Samuel 11:20

The king may get angry.

He may ask why we went so close to the city.

He may ask if we knew the enemy could shoot from the wall.

## 2 Samuel 11:21

He may remind you how Abimelech died in Thebez.

Abimelech was Jerubbesheth’s son.

A woman dropped a heavy grinding stone from the wall.

It hit Abimelech and killed him.

If the king asks why we went so close, tell him this.

Say, ‘Your servant Uriah the Hittite also died.’”

**Reading help**

Joab is talking about an earlier battle. The woman in that story used a stone that people used to grind grain.

## David hears that Uriah has died

## 2 Samuel 11:22

The messenger went to David.

He told David everything Joab had sent him to say.

## 2 Samuel 11:23

The messenger said, “The enemy came out into the field.

At first they were too strong for us.

Then we drove them back to the city gate.

## 2 Samuel 11:24

Men on the wall shot arrows at us.

Some of your soldiers died.

Your servant Uriah the Hittite died too.”

## 2 Samuel 11:25

David told the messenger, “Go and encourage Joab.

Tell him, ‘Do not let this upset you.

Anyone can die in battle.

Fight harder against the city and destroy it.’”

## Bathsheba mourns for Uriah

## 2 Samuel 11:26

Bathsheba heard that her husband Uriah had died.

She mourned for him.

## 2 Samuel 11:27

When the time for mourning ended, David sent for Bathsheba.

She came to his house and became his wife.

She gave birth to David’s son.

The LORD saw what David had done.

In the LORD’s eyes, it was evil.
