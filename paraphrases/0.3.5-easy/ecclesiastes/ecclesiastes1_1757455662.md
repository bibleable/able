# Ecclesiastes 1 — Chasing the wind

**About this chapter**

The Teacher watches the sun, the wind, and the rivers. They keep moving. He wonders what lasts after a lifetime of work.

## The Teacher’s question

## Ecclesiastes 1:1

These are the words of the Teacher.

He was David’s son and a king in Jerusalem.

## Ecclesiastes 1:2

“Nothing lasts,” says the Teacher.

“What does it all come to?

All that effort comes to nothing!”

## Ecclesiastes 1:3

People work hard under the sun.

When the work is done, what have they gained?

“Under the sun” means here on earth.

## What keeps coming back

## Ecclesiastes 1:4

One generation dies and another comes along.

The earth stays where it is.

## Ecclesiastes 1:5

The sun rises.

The sun sets.

It hurries back to rise again.

![The sun shines above the hills.](../pictures/judges-song-sun.svg)

**Find the sun**

Picture the hills growing dark at sunset. Picture light reaching them again the next morning. The Teacher watches this happen day after day.

## Ecclesiastes 1:6

The wind blows south and then turns north.

Around it goes, coming back the same way again.

## Ecclesiastes 1:7

Every river flows into the sea.

Yet the sea never fills up.

The streams keep flowing again from where they began.

## What people see and forget

## Ecclesiastes 1:8

It all wears us out.

Words cannot say how tiring it is.

We keep looking and still want to see more.

We keep listening and still want to hear more.

## Ecclesiastes 1:9

What happened before will happen again.

People will do again what others have already done.

There is nothing new under the sun.

## Ecclesiastes 1:10

Someone may say, “Look, this is new!” Yet it was here long before we were born.

## Ecclesiastes 1:11

We forget the people who lived before us.

People born after us will also be forgotten.

Those who come later will forget them too.

## What the Teacher tried to learn

## Ecclesiastes 1:12

I am the Teacher.

I ruled over Israel from Jerusalem.

## Ecclesiastes 1:13

I set out to study everything people do on earth.

I used all my wisdom to understand it.

What a hard task God has given us!

## Ecclesiastes 1:14

I looked at everything people do under the sun.

It all seemed like chasing the wind.

You run after it, but your hands close on nothing.

## Ecclesiastes 1:15

What is crooked cannot be made straight.

How can you count something that is missing?

## The cost of knowing more

## Ecclesiastes 1:16

I said to myself, “I have grown wiser than all who ruled Jerusalem before me.

I have studied much and learned much.”

## Ecclesiastes 1:17

I tried to understand wisdom.

I also studied wild and foolish behavior.

I found that this too was like chasing the wind.

## Ecclesiastes 1:18

With much wisdom comes much sorrow.

The more I learn, the more I grieve.
