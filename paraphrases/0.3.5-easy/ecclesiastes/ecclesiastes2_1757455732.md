# Ecclesiastes 2 — What do I gain from my work?

**About this chapter**

The Teacher plants gardens and builds houses. He enjoys making them, then wonders who will own them when he dies.

## Trying pleasure

## Ecclesiastes 2:1

I said to myself, “Try pleasure.

Enjoy the good things in life.” I tried it, but it gave me nothing I could keep.

## Ecclesiastes 2:2

Laughter seemed foolish to me.

What did pleasure really achieve?

## Ecclesiastes 2:3

I tried wine to cheer myself up.

I tried doing foolish things, though I still let wisdom guide me.

I wanted to know what was worth doing in our short lives.

## Houses, vineyards, and gardens

## Ecclesiastes 2:4

I began bigger projects.

I built houses for myself.

I planted vineyards, fields where grapes grow.

![A cluster of grapes grows on a vine.](../pictures/vine-and-grapes.svg)

**Find the grapes**

Follow the branch to the grapes. Imagine rows of vines like this in the field the Teacher planted.

## Ecclesiastes 2:5

I made gardens and parks for myself.

I planted all kinds of fruit trees there.

## Ecclesiastes 2:6

I built pools to store water.

With that water I could keep whole groups of trees growing.

## Everything I wanted

## Ecclesiastes 2:7

I acquired men and women to serve me.

Others were born into service in my house.

I owned more cattle and sheep than anyone before me in Jerusalem.

## Ecclesiastes 2:8

I collected silver, gold, and treasures from kings and their lands.

Men and women sang for me.

I had many concubines, women who lived with me as partners.

## Ecclesiastes 2:9

I became greater than anyone before me in Jerusalem.

Through it all, I kept my wisdom.

## Ecclesiastes 2:10

If I saw something I wanted, I took it.

I let myself enjoy every pleasure.

My work itself gave me joy.

That joy was my reward.

## Ecclesiastes 2:11

Then I looked at everything I had worked to build.

What could I keep from all that work on earth?

It was like trying to catch the wind.

## Wisdom and foolishness

## Ecclesiastes 2:12

Next I thought about wisdom, madness, and foolishness.

What could the next king do that no one had done before?

## Ecclesiastes 2:13

I saw that wisdom was better than foolishness.

It was like having light in a dark place.

## Ecclesiastes 2:14

A wise person can see the way ahead.

A foolish person walks as if in darkness.

Yet the same end comes to both.

## Ecclesiastes 2:15

I said to myself, “I will die just as a fool dies.

What have I gained by being wise?” This too seemed like wasted effort.

## Ecclesiastes 2:16

People will forget the wise and the foolish alike.

How sad that both must die!

## Who will get what I built?

## Ecclesiastes 2:17

I came to hate life.

All the work people did here on earth brought me grief.

It all seemed like chasing the wind.

## Ecclesiastes 2:18

I hated what I had worked so hard for.

I would have to leave it to someone else.

## Ecclesiastes 2:19

Will that person be wise or foolish?

I cannot tell.

Yet that person will control everything I worked so skillfully to build.

What did my work achieve?

## Ecclesiastes 2:20

I began to lose hope when I thought about all my work here on earth.

## Ecclesiastes 2:21

You can work with wisdom, knowledge, and skill.

Then someone who did none of the work gets what you earned.

To me that is empty and deeply wrong.

## A gift in an ordinary day

## Ecclesiastes 2:22

What do people gain from all their hard work and worry here on earth?

## Ecclesiastes 2:23

Their work fills their days with pain and sorrow.

Even at night their minds cannot rest.

This effort too seems wasted.

## Ecclesiastes 2:24

The best thing people can do is eat and drink and enjoy their work.

I see now that this joy is a gift from God.

## Ecclesiastes 2:25

Without God, who can eat?

Who can find joy?

## Ecclesiastes 2:26

God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy to those who please him.

He gives the sinner the work of gathering and storing things.

The sinner must hand them over to someone who pleases God.

That work too is like chasing the wind.

**Pause and answer**

Who does the Teacher thank for the joy he finds in his work?
