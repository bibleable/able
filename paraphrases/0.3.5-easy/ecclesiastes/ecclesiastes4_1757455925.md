# Ecclesiastes 4 — Someone to lift you up

**About this chapter**

The Teacher sees people crying alone and a man working alone. He asks what changes when someone stands beside you.

## Tears without comfort

## Ecclesiastes 4:1

Again I looked at the way people treated others on earth.

People with power hurt those who had none.

I saw the tears of the people they hurt.

No one came to comfort them.

## Ecclesiastes 4:2

Seeing this, I thought the people who had already died were better off than those still living.

## Ecclesiastes 4:3

I thought those not yet born were better off than either.

They had never seen the evil people do here on earth.

## When work becomes a race

## Ecclesiastes 4:4

I watched people work hard and succeed.

I saw envy behind it all.

Each wanted what a neighbor had.

That too was like chasing the wind.

## Ecclesiastes 4:5

A fool folds his hands and does nothing.

He eats away at his own life.

## Ecclesiastes 4:6

One handful with peace is better than two handfuls gained by endless work.

Trying to grasp more can be like trying to catch the wind.

## A man who works alone

## Ecclesiastes 4:7

Then I saw something else on earth that seemed empty.

## Ecclesiastes 4:8

A man lived alone.

He had no son or brother.

He worked without stopping, yet his wealth never satisfied him.

He asked, “Who am I working for?

Why do I deny myself all enjoyment?” What a sad, empty way to work!

## Two people together

## Ecclesiastes 4:9

Two people do better together.

Their work brings a good reward.

## Ecclesiastes 4:10

If one falls, the other can help them stand.

How hard it is to fall with nobody there to help!

## Ecclesiastes 4:11

Two people lying down together can keep each other warm.

How can someone lying alone keep warm?

## Ecclesiastes 4:12

An attacker may overpower one person.

Two can stand together and resist.

Think of a cord made from three strands.

It does not break easily.

![Ropes cross and join to form a net.](../pictures/net.svg)

**Follow one rope**

Trace a single rope in the net. Now imagine looking closer at the rope itself. The Teacher pictures three strands twisted together to make one strong cord.

## A king and the one who follows him

## Ecclesiastes 4:13

A poor young person with wisdom is better off than an old and foolish king who refuses to listen to warnings.

## Ecclesiastes 4:14

That young man was born poor in the kingdom.

He came out of prison and became king.

## Ecclesiastes 4:15

I saw people everywhere follow the young man.

He had taken the old king’s place.

## Ecclesiastes 4:16

There was no counting all the people who came before them.

Yet later generations would have no praise for the new king.

That too seemed like chasing the wind.

**Pause and answer**

What can a companion do when someone falls?
