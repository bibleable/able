# Ecclesiastes 7 — What sorrow can teach

**About this chapter**

The Teacher visits places of sorrow and joy. He asks what people can learn there. He also tells us where his own search for wisdom fell short.

## At a house where people grieve

## Ecclesiastes 7:1

A good name is worth more than fine perfume.

A good name means people respect how you live.

The Teacher says the day of death is better than the day of birth.

![A small perfume jar with a narrow neck.](../pictures/perfume-jar.svg)

**Picture the open jar**

Imagine the scent as someone opens the jar. The Teacher asks you to consider something worth even more. Think of someone you respect and remember why.

## Ecclesiastes 7:2

It is better to visit a house where people grieve than to go to a feast.

Everyone will die one day.

Those of us still living should remember this.

## Ecclesiastes 7:3

Sorrow is better than laughter.

Even when your face is sad, your heart can learn something good.

## Ecclesiastes 7:4

Wise people give thought to the house where people grieve.

Foolish people think only of the house where people celebrate.

## Words worth hearing

## Ecclesiastes 7:5

Listen when a wise person corrects you.

That does more good than listening to foolish people sing.

## Ecclesiastes 7:6

Dry thorns crackle loudly as they burn under a cooking pot.

Then the sound is gone.

Foolish laughter is just as empty.

## Ecclesiastes 7:7

Taking money by threats can turn a wise person into a fool.

Accepting a secret payment to act unfairly spoils a person’s judgment.

## Patience and wisdom

## Ecclesiastes 7:8

The end of a matter is better than its beginning.

Patience is better than pride.

## Ecclesiastes 7:9

Be slow to take offense.

Anger settles in and stays with a foolish person.

## Ecclesiastes 7:10

Avoid asking, “Why were the old days better than today?” That question shows little wisdom.

## Ecclesiastes 7:11

Wisdom is good, like property left to you by family.

It helps those who live to see the sun.

## Ecclesiastes 7:12

Money can shelter you, and so can wisdom.

Knowing how to act wisely can save your life.

## Good days and hard days

## Ecclesiastes 7:13

Look at what God has done.

Who can straighten something God has bent?

## Ecclesiastes 7:14

Enjoy the good days.

On a hard day, remember that God made both kinds of days.

None of us can see what will come next.

## Ecclesiastes 7:15

In my brief life I have seen both of these things.

A good person dies while still doing right.

A wicked person goes on doing wrong and lives a long life.

## Two warnings to remember

## Ecclesiastes 7:16

Do not go to extremes trying to be righteous.

Do not try to make yourself too wise.

Why bring ruin on yourself?

## Ecclesiastes 7:17

Do not give yourself over to evil or act foolishly.

Why die before your time?

## Ecclesiastes 7:18

Hold on to both warnings.

Someone who deeply respects God pays attention to both.

## Ecclesiastes 7:19

Wisdom gives one person more strength than ten rulers in a city.

## What people say

## Ecclesiastes 7:20

Nobody on earth always does right.

Everyone sometimes does wrong.

## Ecclesiastes 7:21

Do not listen closely to everything people say.

You might hear even your own servant cursing you.

## Ecclesiastes 7:22

You know you have often spoken that way about other people.

## The limits of the Teacher’s search

## Ecclesiastes 7:23

I tested all this with the wisdom I had.

I said, “I will be wise.” Yet the wisdom I sought was beyond my reach.

## Ecclesiastes 7:24

What I hoped to understand lay far beyond me.

It was like water too deep to measure.

Who can reach the bottom?

## Ecclesiastes 7:25

I kept looking for wisdom and for reasons behind things.

I wanted to understand why doing evil is foolish.

I wanted to understand behavior that makes no sense.

## Ecclesiastes 7:26

I found something more bitter than death.

A woman who sets out to trap someone is like a net.

Her hands hold fast like chains.

A man who pleases God escapes, but a sinner gets caught.

## What the Teacher found

## Ecclesiastes 7:27

The Teacher said, “I put one thing beside another.

I was trying to find an answer.”

## Ecclesiastes 7:28

I kept searching without finding what I hoped for.

Among a thousand people I found one upright man.

Among the women I looked at, I found none.

## Ecclesiastes 7:29

This much I did find.

God made human beings upright.

They went looking for all kinds of schemes of their own.

What does the Teacher ask people to remember when a hard day comes?
