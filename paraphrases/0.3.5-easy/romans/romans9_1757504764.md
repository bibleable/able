# Romans 9 — Paul speaks about Israel and God’s mercy

**About this chapter**

Paul writes about his own people, the people of Israel.

He asks how God’s promises and mercy reach people.

## Paul grieves for his own people

## Romans 9:1

I belong to Christ, and I am telling the truth.

The Holy Spirit confirms this in my conscience.

## Romans 9:2

I feel deep sorrow.

The pain in my heart never stops.

## Romans 9:3

I could even wish to take a curse myself and lose my place with Christ if that would help my own people.

They are my family.

## Romans 9:4

They are the people of Israel.

God adopted them as his children.

He showed them his glory and made agreements with them.

He gave them his law, worship in the temple, and his promises.

## Romans 9:5

The great ancestors of our people belong to their family.

Christ himself was born into that family.

Christ is God over all.

He deserves praise for ever!

Amen.

## God keeps his promise

## Romans 9:6

God has kept his word.

Being born into the family of Israel does not by itself make someone part of the Israel God promised.

## Romans 9:7

Abraham had many descendants.

Yet God told Abraham, “Through Isaac I will count your descendants.”

## Romans 9:8

Birth alone does not make someone a child of God.

God counts the children of his promise as Abraham’s family.

## Romans 9:9

God made this promise to Abraham: “I will return at the time I have set.

Sarah will have a son.”

## Romans 9:10

Think also about Rebecca’s two children.

Both had the same father, our ancestor Isaac.

## Romans 9:11

Before the twins were born, neither had done anything good or bad.

God had already made his choice.

His choice would stand.

## Romans 9:12

It depended on God, who calls people, rather than on anything the twins did.

God told Rebecca, “The older son will serve the younger.”

## Romans 9:13

The Scriptures record God’s words: “I loved Jacob, but I hated Esau.”

## Paul asks whether God is fair

## Romans 9:14

Does this mean God is unfair?

Of course not!

## Romans 9:15

God told Moses, “I choose whom I will show mercy to.

I choose whom I will treat with compassion.”

**Help with these words**

Mercy means showing kindness to someone you have the power to punish. Compassion means caring about someone’s suffering.

## Romans 9:16

People cannot gain God’s mercy just by wanting it or working for it.

It depends on God, who shows mercy.

## Romans 9:17

The Scriptures tell what God said to Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.

“I raised you up to show my power through you.

People everywhere will tell others about me.”

## Romans 9:18

God shows mercy to those he chooses.

He also hardens those he chooses to harden.

**Help with these words**

When Paul says God hardens someone, he is speaking about that person becoming stubborn.

**Pause and answer**

Who decides to show mercy?

## A potter shapes the clay

## Romans 9:19

Someone may ask me, “Then why does God still blame us?

Who can stop what God wants to do?”

## Romans 9:20

But who are we to talk back to God?

Can something say to its maker, “Why did you make me this way?”

## Romans 9:21

Think of a potter working with one lump of clay.

The potter can make one bowl for special occasions.

The potter can make another for everyday use.

Doesn’t the potter have that right?

## Romans 9:22

Suppose God wanted to show his anger and his power.

What if he waited patiently with people who faced his anger and were ready for destruction?

## Romans 9:23

What if he waited to show the greatness of his glory to those who receive mercy?

God prepared them long ago to share in his glory.

## Romans 9:24

That includes us, the people God has called.

He has called both Jews and Gentiles.

**Help with these words**

Gentiles are people who are not Jewish. In Paul’s picture, God is the potter. The pots stand for people.

**Pause and answer**

## Hosea and Isaiah speak about God’s people

## Romans 9:25

In the book of Hosea, God says, “I will call people who did not belong to me ‘my people.’

I will call her ‘the one I love’ who was not my loved one.”

## Romans 9:26

God also says, “Once they heard these words: ‘You are not my people.’

In that same place people will call them children of the living God.”

## Romans 9:27

Isaiah cries out about Israel, “Even if Israel’s people are as many as grains of sand by the sea, only some will be saved.

## Romans 9:28

The Lord will carry out his judgment on the earth.

He will finish what he has decided to do.”

## Romans 9:29

Isaiah also warned, “The Lord who commands heaven’s armies left some of our family alive.

Otherwise we would have ended up like Sodom and Gomorrah.”

**Help with these words**

Sodom and Gomorrah were cities that God destroyed. Isaiah says Israel still had a future because God let some of its people live.

**Pause and answer**

What new name does God give people who once did not belong to him?

## Paul explains the stone people stumble over

## Romans 9:30

What does all this mean?

Gentiles who were not trying to make themselves right with God became right with him.

They received this by faith.

## Romans 9:31

The people of Israel tried to become right with God by keeping the law.

They did not reach their goal.

## Romans 9:32

Why did they fall short?

They relied on what they did instead of trusting God.

They tripped over the stone in their path.

## Romans 9:33

The Scriptures say, “Look, I am putting a stone in Zion.

People will stumble over this rock and take offence.

But whoever trusts in him will never be put to shame.”

**Help with these words**

Zion is a name for Jerusalem. Paul uses the stone as a picture of Christ. Some people reject him. Others put their trust in him.

**Pause and answer**

How did the Gentiles Paul describes become right with God?
