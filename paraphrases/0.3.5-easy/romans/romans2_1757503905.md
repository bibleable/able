# Romans 2 — God judges everyone fairly

**About this chapter**

Paul asks his readers to look at their own actions.

He explains how God judges people who have the law and people who have never received it.

## When you judge someone else

## Romans 2:1

Suppose you blame someone for doing wrong.

Yet you do the very same thing.

Your own words show you are guilty too.

You have no excuse.

## Romans 2:2

We know God judges such actions according to the truth.

## Romans 2:3

You judge other people but do the same things yourself.

Do you think you will escape God’s judgment?

## Romans 2:4

Do you think God’s great kindness matters so little?

He puts up with you and waits patiently.

His kindness leads you to turn away from sin.

## Romans 2:5

Yet you are stubborn and refuse to change.

You bring more of God’s anger on yourself.

The day will come when God shows his anger.

Then everyone will see that he judges fairly.

**Pause and answer**

What does God’s kindness lead a person to do?

## God looks at what each person does

## Romans 2:6

The Scriptures say that God will give each person what their actions deserve.

## Romans 2:7

Some people keep doing good.

They seek glory, honour and life that never ends.

God will give them that life.

## Romans 2:8

Others care only about getting their own way.

They reject the truth and choose evil.

They will face God’s anger.

## Romans 2:9

Trouble and suffering will come to everyone who does evil.

This applies first to Jewish people, then to Greeks.

## Romans 2:10

Everyone who does good will receive glory, honour and peace.

This too applies first to Jewish people, then to Greeks.

## Romans 2:11

God shows no favourites.

**Pause and answer**

What does Paul say God will give people who keep doing good?

## Hearing the law and doing what it says

## Romans 2:12

Some people sin without having the law of Moses.

They will still perish without that law.

Others have the law and sin.

God will use that law to judge them.

## Romans 2:13

Hearing the law does not make a person right with God.

God will declare those who do what the law says to be right with him.

## Romans 2:14

Gentiles do not have the law of Moses.

Yet sometimes they naturally do what the law says.

They show that they have a rule within themselves.

**Help with these words**

Gentiles are people who are not Jewish.

## Romans 2:15

Their actions show that what the law asks is written on their hearts.

Their conscience also tells them about their actions.

Sometimes they think, “I did wrong.”

Sometimes they think, “What I did was right.”

**Help with these words**

“Written on their hearts” means they recognise right and wrong within themselves. A conscience is the sense that helps someone judge their own actions.

## Romans 2:16

The good news I tell includes this promise.

One day God will judge what people keep secret.

He will do this through Jesus Christ.

**Pause and answer**

How can people show they know what the law asks?

## Paul asks a teacher to look at his own actions

## Romans 2:17

Suppose you call yourself a Jew.

You rely on the law and take pride in God.

## Romans 2:18

The law teaches you what God wants.

It helps you recognise what is best.

## Romans 2:19

You think you can guide people who are blind.

You think you are a light for people in darkness.

**Help with these words**

Paul is describing a teacher who believes he can show others how to live.

## Romans 2:20

You see yourself as someone who can teach foolish people and young children.

You are sure the law gives you knowledge and truth.

## Romans 2:21

You teach other people.

Do you teach yourself?

You tell others not to steal.

Do you steal?

## Romans 2:22

You say people must not commit adultery.

Do you commit adultery?

You hate idols.

Do you steal from temples?

**Help with these words**

Adultery means having sex with someone other than your husband or wife when one of you is married. An idol is an object people worship as a god.

## Romans 2:23

You boast about the law.

Do you bring shame on God by breaking it?

## Romans 2:24

The Scriptures say, “Because of you, Gentiles speak against God’s name.”

## An outward sign and a changed heart

## Romans 2:25

Circumcision has value if you obey the law.

If you break the law, it is as if you had never received that sign.

**Help with these words**

Circumcision means removing a small piece of skin from a boy’s or man’s penis. For Jewish men, it was a sign of their people’s agreement with God.

## Romans 2:26

Suppose a man has never received circumcision but obeys the law.

Will God not count him as someone who has received the sign?

## Romans 2:27

That man obeys the law without the sign on his body.

You have the written law and the sign, yet break the law.

His actions show that you are guilty.

## Romans 2:28

Outward signs alone do not make someone a Jew in God’s sight.

Circumcision means more than a mark on the body.

## Romans 2:29

What matters to God is who a person is inside.

This is what it means to have a circumcised heart.

God’s Spirit changes the person within.

The written law cannot make that change.

The praise that person receives comes from God, not from people.

Who changes a person’s heart?
