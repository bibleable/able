# Acts 25 — Paul asks Caesar to hear his case

**About this chapter**

Governor Festus hears the charges against Paul.

Later he explains to King Agrippa why Paul is still a prisoner.

## Festus meets the leaders in Jerusalem

## Acts 25:1

Festus was the new governor.

Three days after he arrived in the province, he left Caesarea and went to Jerusalem.

## Acts 25:2

The chief priests and other Jewish leaders met Festus there.

They told him what they accused Paul of doing.

They pressed him to do them a favour.

## Acts 25:3

They wanted Festus to send for Paul.

They were planning to kill Paul on the way to Jerusalem.

## Acts 25:4

Festus answered, “Paul is a prisoner in Caesarea.

I am going back there soon.

## Acts 25:5

Let some of your leaders come with me.

If Paul has done wrong, bring your charges against him in Caesarea.”

## Paul answers his accusers

## Acts 25:6

Festus stayed with the leaders about eight or ten days.

Then he returned to Caesarea.

The next day he took his seat as judge.

He ordered his men to bring Paul in.

## Acts 25:7

The men from Jerusalem stood around Paul.

They accused him of many serious wrongs.

They could not prove their charges.

## Acts 25:8

Paul answered, “I have done nothing against the Jewish law.

I have done nothing against the temple or against Caesar.”

**Help with these words**

Caesar was a title for the Roman emperor.

## Acts 25:9

Festus wanted to please the Jewish leaders.

He asked Paul, “Will you go to Jerusalem?

Will you let me hear your case there?”

**Pause and answer**

What could the men from Jerusalem prove about their charges?

## Paul appeals to Caesar

## Acts 25:10

Paul answered, “I already stand in Caesar’s court.

This is where I should have my trial.

You know very well I have done the Jewish people no wrong.

## Acts 25:11

If I have done something that deserves death, I am willing to die.

But if their charges are false, no one has the right to hand me over to them.

I appeal to Caesar!”

**Help with these words**

Paul was asking the Roman emperor to decide his case.

## Acts 25:12

Festus spoke with his advisers.

Then he told Paul, “You have appealed to Caesar.

You will go to Caesar.”

**Pause and answer**

Who did Paul ask to decide his case?

## Festus explains the case to King Agrippa

## Acts 25:13

Several days later King Agrippa and Bernice came to Caesarea.

They came to greet Festus and show him respect.

## Acts 25:14

They stayed for several days.

Festus told the king about Paul.

He said, “Felix left a man in prison here.

## Acts 25:15

When I went to Jerusalem, the chief priests and elders accused him.

They asked me to judge him guilty.

## Acts 25:16

I told them what we Romans do.

First a man must have the chance to face his accusers.

He must be able to answer what they say.

Only then can we hand him over.

## Acts 25:17

So when the leaders came here, I acted at once.

The very next day I took my seat as judge.

I sent for Paul.”

**Pause and answer**

What chance did Festus say a prisoner must have?

## Agrippa asks to hear Paul

## Acts 25:18

Festus continued, “The accusers stood up to speak.

I had expected them to name certain crimes.

They did not accuse Paul of any of those crimes.

## Acts 25:19

They argued with Paul about their own religion.

They also argued about a man named Jesus who had died.

Paul kept saying that Jesus was alive.

## Acts 25:20

I did not know how to find out more about this.

I asked Paul if he would go to Jerusalem for his trial.

## Acts 25:21

Paul asked to stay here until the emperor could decide his case.

So I ordered the guards to keep him here until I could send him to Caesar.”

## Acts 25:22

Agrippa told Festus, “I would like to hear Paul myself.”

Festus answered, “You will hear him tomorrow.”

**Pause and answer**

What did Paul say about Jesus that his accusers disputed?

## Festus needs to explain why he is sending Paul

## Acts 25:23

The next day Agrippa and Bernice arrived with great ceremony.

Army commanders and leading men of the city entered the hall with them.

Festus ordered his men to bring Paul in.

## Acts 25:24

Festus said, “King Agrippa and everyone here, look at this man.

The whole Jewish community has appealed to me about him.

Both here and in Jerusalem, people shouted that he should die.

## Acts 25:25

I found nothing he had done that deserved death.

But Paul himself appealed to the emperor.

So I decided to send him.

## Acts 25:26

I have nothing definite to write to the emperor about him.

That is why I have brought him before you all, especially you, King Agrippa.

I hope this hearing gives me something to write.

## Acts 25:27

How can I send a prisoner without saying what the charges are?”
