# Acts 26 — Paul tells King Agrippa why he follows Jesus

**About this chapter**

Paul stands before King Agrippa and Governor Festus.

He explains how Jesus changed the work he did.

## Agrippa gives Paul time to speak

## Acts 26:1

King Agrippa told Paul, “You may speak for yourself.”

Paul held out his hand and began to speak.

## Acts 26:2

“King Agrippa, I am glad to stand before you today.

I can answer everything the Jewish leaders accuse me of doing.

## Acts 26:3

You know our Jewish customs well.

You also know the things we disagree about.

Please listen patiently.

## Acts 26:4

My people know how I lived as a child.

I grew up among them and lived in Jerusalem.

## Acts 26:5

They have known me for a long time.

If they are willing, they can tell you this.

I lived as a Pharisee.

The Pharisees followed the strictest rules in our religion.”

**Pause and answer**

Why was Paul glad to speak to King Agrippa?

## Paul speaks about his hope and his past

## Acts 26:6

Paul continued, “I am on trial because of my hope.

I trust the promise God made to our ancestors.

## Acts 26:7

The twelve tribes of Israel serve God faithfully day and night.

They hope to see God keep that promise.

Yet, my king, Jewish people accuse me because of that same hope.

## Acts 26:8

Why should any of you find it hard to believe that God raises the dead?

## Acts 26:9

I once thought I should do everything I could against Jesus of Nazareth.

## Acts 26:10

In Jerusalem the chief priests gave me authority to act.

I put many of God’s people in prison.

When people sentenced them to death, I voted against them.

## Acts 26:11

I often had believers punished in synagogues.

I tried to make them speak against God.

I was so angry that I even went to foreign cities to hurt them.”

**Help with these words**

Synagogues are places where Jewish people meet to worship.

**Pause and answer**

What did Paul once do to believers in Jerusalem?

## A voice speaks to Paul on the road

## Acts 26:12

Paul said, “The chief priests gave me orders to continue this work.

That was why I was travelling to Damascus.

## Acts 26:13

My king, about noon I saw a light from heaven.

It was brighter than the sun.

It shone all around me and my companions.

## Acts 26:14

We all fell to the ground.

I heard a voice speaking to me in Hebrew.

‘Saul, Saul, why are you hurting me?

It hurts you to kick against the goads.’

**Help with these words**

Saul was another name for Paul. A goad was a pointed stick a farmer used to guide an ox. An ox that kicked against it hurt itself. Jesus meant that Saul was hurting himself by fighting against him.

## Acts 26:15

I asked, ‘Who are you, Lord?’

The Lord answered, ‘I am Jesus, the one you are hurting.’”

**Pause and answer**

Who spoke to Paul on the road?

## Jesus gives Paul work to do

## Acts 26:16

Paul told Agrippa what else Jesus had said.

“Jesus said, ‘Get up and stand on your feet.

I have come to make you my servant and my witness.

Tell people what you have seen of me.

Tell them what I will show you.

## Acts 26:17

I will rescue you from your own people and from the Gentiles.

I am sending you to them.

## Acts 26:18

You will open their eyes.

They can turn from darkness to light.

They can turn from Satan’s power to God.

They can receive forgiveness for their sins.

They can share what God gives his people, whom he makes holy through faith in me.’”

**Help with these words**

Gentiles are people who are not Jewish. Opening people’s eyes means helping them understand. Turning towards the light means turning to God.

**Pause and answer**

What did Jesus ask Paul to tell people?

## Paul explains how he obeyed Jesus

## Acts 26:19

Paul said, “King Agrippa, I obeyed what Jesus showed me in that vision from heaven.

## Acts 26:20

I spoke first in Damascus and Jerusalem.

Then I spoke throughout Judea and among the Gentiles.

I told people to turn away from sin and turn to God.

Their actions should show that they had changed.

## Acts 26:21

That is why some Jewish people seized me in the temple courts.

They tried to kill me.

## Acts 26:22

God has helped me to this day.

So I stand here speaking to everyone, ordinary and important people alike.

I tell only what Moses and the prophets said would happen.

![An open scroll with lines of writing.](../pictures/scroll.svg)

The words of Moses and the prophets were written on scrolls.

## Acts 26:23

They said the Christ would suffer.

He would be the first to rise from the dead.

He would bring a message of light to our people and the Gentiles.”

**Help with these words**

The Christ is the King God chose. Paul is speaking about Jesus.

**Pause and answer**

How could people show they had turned to God?

## Agrippa asks Paul a question

## Acts 26:24

Governor Festus interrupted Paul and shouted, “Paul, you are out of your mind!

All your learning has driven you mad!”

## Acts 26:25

Paul answered, “Honoured Festus, my mind is clear.

What I am saying is true and makes sense.

## Acts 26:26

King Agrippa knows about these things.

That is why I can speak freely to him.

These things happened in public.

I am sure the king knows about them.

## Acts 26:27

King Agrippa, do you believe what the prophets said?

I know you do.”

## Acts 26:28

Agrippa asked Paul, “Do you think you can persuade me to become a Christian so quickly?”

## Acts 26:29

Paul answered, “Whether it takes a short time or a long time, I pray to God for you.

I pray for everyone listening today.

I want you all to follow Jesus as I do.

But I do not want you to wear chains like mine.”

**Pause and answer**

What did Paul want for everyone listening?

## The rulers discuss Paul’s case

## Acts 26:30

King Agrippa and Governor Festus stood up.

Bernice and the people sitting with them stood up too.

## Acts 26:31

As they left, they said to each other, “Paul has done nothing that deserves death or prison.”

## Acts 26:32

Agrippa told Festus, “We could have let Paul go free if he had not appealed to Caesar.”

**Help with these words**

Paul had asked Caesar, the Roman emperor, to decide his case.

**Pause and answer**

Why did Agrippa say they could have set Paul free?
