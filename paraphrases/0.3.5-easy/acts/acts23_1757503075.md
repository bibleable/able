# Acts 23 — Paul’s nephew warns the Roman commander

**About this chapter**

Paul speaks before the Jewish council.

Later his nephew hears a threat and speaks up.

## Paul speaks before the council

## Acts 23:1

Paul looked straight at the members of the Jewish council.

He said, “Brothers, my conscience is clear.

To this day I believe I have done what is right before God.”

## Acts 23:2

Ananias, the high priest, gave an order to the people near Paul.

He told them to hit Paul on the mouth.

## Acts 23:3

Paul said to Ananias, “God will strike you, you whitewashed wall!

You sit there to judge me by the law.

Yet you break the law by ordering them to hit me.”

**Help with these words**

Whitewash gives a wall a clean white surface. Paul meant that Ananias looked like a fair judge but acted unfairly.

## Acts 23:4

The people nearby asked Paul, “How dare you insult God’s high priest?”

## Acts 23:5

Paul answered, “Brothers, I did not know he was the high priest.

The Scriptures say, ‘Do not speak evil about the ruler of your people.’”

**Pause and answer**

Why did Paul say the high priest had broken the law?

## The council disagrees about life after death

## Acts 23:6

Paul knew some council members were Sadducees and others were Pharisees.

He called out, “Brothers, I am a Pharisee.

My father was a Pharisee too.

I am on trial because I hope that the dead will rise.”

## Acts 23:7

The Pharisees and Sadducees began to argue.

The council split into two sides.

## Acts 23:8

The Sadducees said the dead do not rise.

They also said there are no angels or spirits.

The Pharisees believed in all three.

## Acts 23:9

People began shouting.

Some teachers of the law who were Pharisees stood up.

They argued, “We find nothing wrong with Paul.

Perhaps a spirit or an angel spoke to him.”

## Acts 23:10

The argument became violent.

The Roman commander feared the people would tear Paul apart.

He ordered the soldiers to pull Paul away from them.

They were to take Paul back to the barracks.

**Help with these words**

Barracks are buildings where soldiers stay.

## Acts 23:11

The next night the Lord Jesus stood near Paul.

He said, “Be brave!

You have told people in Jerusalem about me.

You must tell people in Rome about me too.”

**Pause and answer**

In which city did Jesus say Paul must speak about him next?

## Paul’s nephew hears a threat

## Acts 23:12

The next morning some Jewish men made a secret plan.

They swore to eat and drink nothing until they killed Paul.

## Acts 23:13

More than forty men had made this plan.

## Acts 23:14

They went to the chief priests and elders.

They said, “We have made a solemn promise.

We will eat nothing until we have killed Paul.”

## Acts 23:15

The men wanted the council to ask for Paul to come back.

They said they would kill him before he reached the council.

## Acts 23:16

The son of Paul’s sister heard about the plan.

He went into the barracks and warned Paul.

**Pause and answer**

How did Paul find out that his life was in danger?

## The commander listens to the young man

## Acts 23:17

Paul called a Roman officer over.

He said, “Take this young man to the commander.

He has something to tell him.”

## Acts 23:18

The officer brought Paul’s nephew to the commander.

He said, “Paul, the prisoner, asked me to bring this young man.

He has something to tell you.”

## Acts 23:19

The commander took the young man by the hand.

He led him aside to speak privately.

He asked, “What do you need to tell me?”

## Acts 23:20

Paul’s nephew answered, “Some men want you to bring Paul before the council tomorrow.

They will say the council needs to hear more about Paul.

## Acts 23:21

Please do not agree.

More than forty men are waiting to hurt him.

They have sworn to eat and drink nothing until they kill him.

Now they are waiting for your answer.”

## Acts 23:22

The commander sent the young man away.

He said, “Do not tell anyone you spoke to me about this.”

**Pause and answer**

What did Paul’s nephew ask the commander to do?

## The commander sends a letter to Felix

## Acts 23:23

The commander called two officers.

He said, “Get two hundred soldiers ready to go to Caesarea.

Take seventy men on horses and two hundred men with spears.

Be ready at nine tonight.

## Acts 23:24

Have horses ready for Paul to ride.

Take him safely to Governor Felix.”

## Acts 23:25

The commander also wrote a letter.

![An open scroll with lines of writing, like a letter.](../pictures/scroll.svg)

## Acts 23:26

He wrote, “From Claudius Lysias.

Greetings to you, honoured Governor Felix.

## Acts 23:27

Some Jewish people seized this man and nearly killed him.

I learned he was a Roman citizen.

I came with my soldiers and rescued him.

## Acts 23:28

I wanted to know what they accused him of doing.

So I brought him before their council.

## Acts 23:29

Their charges concerned questions about their own law.

I found no charge serious enough for prison or death.

## Acts 23:30

Someone told me about a plan to kill him.

So I sent him to you at once.

I told his accusers to bring their case to you.”

**Pause and answer**

What did the commander tell Felix about the charges against Paul?

## Paul reaches Caesarea

## Acts 23:31

The soldiers followed their orders.

That night they took Paul as far as Antipatris.

## Acts 23:32

The next day the soldiers on foot returned to the barracks.

The men on horses went on with Paul.

## Acts 23:33

They arrived in Caesarea and gave the letter to Governor Felix.

Then they brought Paul before him.

## Acts 23:34

Felix read the letter.

He asked Paul which province he came from.

Felix learned that Paul came from Cilicia.

## Acts 23:35

Felix said, “I will hear your case when your accusers arrive.”

He ordered guards to watch Paul in Herod’s palace.

**Pause and answer**

Who did Felix want to hear from before he heard Paul’s case?
