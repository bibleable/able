# Acts 27 — Everyone survives the shipwreck

**About this chapter**

Paul sets sail for Italy as a prisoner.

During a storm he gives the people on the ship courage.

## Julius takes Paul aboard a ship

## Acts 27:1

We were to sail to Italy.

A Roman officer named Julius took charge of Paul and some other prisoners.

Julius served in the emperor’s regiment.

**Help with these words**

The writer says “we” because he travelled with Paul.

## Acts 27:2

We boarded a ship from Adramyttium and set sail.

It was going to ports along the coast of Asia.

Aristarchus from Thessalonica in Macedonia came with us.

## Acts 27:3

The next day we reached Sidon.

Julius treated Paul kindly.

He let Paul visit friends who cared for him.

## Acts 27:4

We left Sidon and sailed close to Cyprus.

The island sheltered us from the wind blowing against us.

## Acts 27:5

We crossed the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia.

Then we reached Myra in Lycia.

## Acts 27:6

There Julius found a ship from Alexandria going to Italy.

He took us aboard.

**Pause and answer**

How did Julius show kindness to Paul in Sidon?

## Paul warns the travellers

## Acts 27:7

For many days we sailed slowly.

At last we came near Cnidus.

The wind blocked our way.

We sailed past Salmone on the sheltered side of Crete.

## Acts 27:8

We struggled along the coast to Fair Havens, near Lasea.

## Acts 27:9

Much time had passed.

The Jewish day of fasting was over.

By then it was dangerous to sail.

Paul warned the men.

## Acts 27:10

He said, “I can see great danger ahead.

We may lose the ship and the goods on board.

We may even lose our lives.”

## Acts 27:11

Julius listened to the ship’s pilot and its owner.

He set aside Paul’s advice.

## Acts 27:12

Fair Havens was a poor place to spend the winter.

Most of the men wanted to sail on to Phoenix.

They hoped to spend the winter in that harbour on Crete.

It faced southwest and northwest.

**Pause and answer**

Whose advice did Julius follow about sailing on?

## The wind drives the ship out to sea

## Acts 27:13

A gentle wind began to blow from the south.

The sailors thought this was their chance.

They lifted the anchor and sailed close to Crete.

**Help with these words**

An anchor is a heavy weight that holds a ship in place.

## Acts 27:14

Soon a fierce storm swept down from the island.

People called this wind the Northeaster.

## Acts 27:15

The ship could not sail against the wind.

We had to let the wind carry us along.

## Acts 27:16

We passed behind a small island called Cauda.

Even there we could barely pull the small lifeboat to safety.

## Acts 27:17

The sailors lifted the lifeboat aboard.

They tied ropes around the ship to hold it together.

They feared the ship would hit the shallow sandbanks of Syrtis.

They lowered a sea anchor and let the wind carry them.

**Help with these words**

A sea anchor drags through the water to slow a drifting ship.

## Acts 27:18

The storm tossed us about.

The next day the men began throwing goods into the sea.

## Acts 27:19

On the third day they threw the ship’s equipment overboard with their own hands.

## Acts 27:20

For many days we saw neither sun nor stars.

The storm kept beating against us.

We lost all hope of surviving.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the sailors tie ropes around their ship?

## Paul tells the men what God has promised

## Acts 27:21

The men had gone a long time without food.

Paul stood up and said, “You should have listened to me and stayed in Crete.

We could have avoided this trouble and loss.

## Acts 27:22

Now take courage.

We will lose the ship, but every one of you will live.

## Acts 27:23

I belong to God and serve him.

Last night an angel from God stood beside me.

## Acts 27:24

The angel said, ‘Do not be afraid, Paul.

You must stand before Caesar.

God has granted that everyone sailing with you will live.’

## Acts 27:25

So take courage, men.

I trust God.

It will happen just as he told me.

## Acts 27:26

But our ship must run aground on an island.”

**Help with these words**

Caesar was the Roman emperor. A ship runs aground when its bottom gets stuck on sand or rock.

**Pause and answer**

What promise gave Paul courage?

## The sailors sense land nearby

## Acts 27:27

On the fourteenth night the storm still drove us across the Adriatic Sea.

Around midnight the sailors sensed land nearby.

## Acts 27:28

They measured how deep the water was.

It was about thirty-seven metres deep.

A little farther on, it was only about twenty-seven metres deep.

## Acts 27:29

The sailors were afraid we would hit rocks.

They dropped four anchors from the back of the ship.

They prayed for daylight.

## Acts 27:30

Some sailors tried to escape.

They pretended to lower anchors from the front of the ship.

They were really lowering the lifeboat into the sea.

## Acts 27:31

Paul told Julius and the soldiers, “These sailors must stay on the ship for you to survive.”

## Acts 27:32

So the soldiers cut the lifeboat ropes.

The small boat drifted away.

**Pause and answer**

What showed the sailors that the water was getting shallower?

## Paul shares bread before dawn

## Acts 27:33

Just before dawn Paul urged everyone to eat.

He said, “For fourteen days you have waited and worried.

You have gone without food.

## Acts 27:34

Please eat something to keep up your strength.

None of you will lose even a hair from your head.”

**Help with these words**

Paul meant that everyone would survive.

## Acts 27:35

Paul took bread and thanked God in front of everyone.

He broke the bread and began to eat.

## Acts 27:36

This gave the others courage.

They ate some food too.

## Acts 27:37

There were 276 of us on the ship.

## Acts 27:38

When everyone had eaten enough, the men threw the grain into the sea.

This made the ship lighter.

## Everyone reaches land

## Acts 27:39

When daylight came, the sailors did not recognise the land.

They saw a bay with a sandy beach.

They hoped to run the ship onto the beach.

## Acts 27:40

They cut the anchors loose and left them in the sea.

They untied the ropes holding the rudders used for steering.

They raised the front sail and headed for the beach.

## Acts 27:41

The ship hit a bank of sand under the water.

The front stuck fast.

Waves pounded the back of the ship and broke it apart.

## Acts 27:42

The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners.

They feared the prisoners would swim away and escape.

## Acts 27:43

Julius wanted to save Paul, so he stopped the soldiers.

He ordered those who could swim to jump in first and swim to land.

## Acts 27:44

The others followed, holding on to boards and pieces of the ship.

In this way everyone reached land safely.

**Pause and answer**

How did the people who could not swim reach land?
