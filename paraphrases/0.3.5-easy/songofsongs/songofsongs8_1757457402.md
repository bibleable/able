# Song of Solomon 8 — Rivers cannot sweep love away

**About this chapter**

The woman wants to greet the man she loves openly. The song pictures their love as a fire that even a river cannot put out.

## A wish to greet him freely

## Song of Solomon 8:1

I wish I could greet you like a brother my mother had nursed.

Then if I met you outside, I could kiss you.

Nobody would look down on me.

## Song of Solomon 8:2

I would take you home to my mother, who taught me.

I would give you wine flavored with spices.

You could drink the sweet juice of my pomegranates.

## Song of Solomon 8:3

His left hand rests under my head.

His right arm holds me close.

## Song of Solomon 8:4

Women of Jerusalem, promise me this.

Let love sleep until it is ready to wake.

Give love its own time.

## Under the apple tree

## Song of Solomon 8:5

Who is coming up from the wild country, leaning on the one she loves?

Under the apple tree I woke you.

There your mother conceived you.

There she labored and gave birth to you.

## Love as strong as death

## Song of Solomon 8:6

Keep me like a seal over your heart and on your arm.

A seal carries a mark that belongs to one person.

Love is as strong as death.

Its jealousy holds on as firmly as the grave.

Love burns like the fiercest fire.

## Song of Solomon 8:7

Even deep waters cannot put out that fire.

Rivers cannot sweep love away.

Suppose someone offered everything he owned to buy love.

People would scorn his offer.

## The family speaks and the woman answers

## Song of Solomon 8:8

We have a little sister.

Her breasts have not yet grown.

What shall we do for her when someone asks to marry her?

## Song of Solomon 8:9

If she is a wall, we will build a silver tower on it.

If she is a door, we will cover it with cedar boards.

Her family pictures ways to guard her.

## Song of Solomon 8:10

I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers.

In the eyes of the one I love, I have become someone who brings peace.

## A vineyard of her own

## Song of Solomon 8:11

Solomon owned a vineyard at Baal-hamon.

He rented it to people who grew grapes there.

Each owed him a thousand shekels of silver for the fruit.

A shekel was a measure of weight.

## Song of Solomon 8:12

My own vineyard is mine to give.

Solomon, the thousand shekels are yours.

Two hundred are for those who care for the fruit.

**Find the fruit**

Trace the branch to the grapes. Think of the people who cared for the vine until its fruit was ready. The woman sets aside silver for those workers.

## The lovers call to each other

## Song of Solomon 8:13

You who live in the gardens, my friends are listening for your voice.

Let me hear it too!

## Song of Solomon 8:14

Come away, my love!

Come like a gazelle or a young deer across the mountains fragrant with spices.
