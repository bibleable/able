# Ezekiel 18 — Each person answers for what they do

**About this chapter**

God tells Ezekiel about a father, his son, and his grandson. They make different choices. God asks the people of Israel to turn from evil and live.

## A saying about sour grapes

## Ezekiel 18:1

The LORD spoke to me.

## Ezekiel 18:2

He asked the people of Israel about a saying they repeated.

“The fathers ate sour grapes. The children’s teeth felt strange.”

People meant that the children took the punishment for their fathers’ sins.

![Grapes on a vine. In the saying, the fathers ate the sour grapes.](../pictures/vine-and-grapes.svg)

## Ezekiel 18:3

The Lord GOD said, “As surely as I live, you will stop using that saying in Israel.”

## Ezekiel 18:4

“Every life belongs to me.

The father belongs to me. His son belongs to me too.

The person who sins is the one who will die.”

## The father lives fairly

## Ezekiel 18:5

God said, “Suppose a man does what is fair and right.”

## Ezekiel 18:6

This man avoids meals held to worship idols on the mountains.

He does not turn to Israel’s idols for help.

He does not have sex with his neighbor’s wife.

He does not have sex with a woman during her period.

## Ezekiel 18:7

He treats people fairly. He takes nothing by force.

A borrower may leave something with him as a promise to repay.

He gives it back to the borrower.

He shares his bread with hungry people.

He gives clothes to people who have none.

## Ezekiel 18:8

When he lends, he does not demand extra money in return.

He refuses to do wrong.

When people disagree, he judges between them fairly.

## Ezekiel 18:9

The Lord GOD said, “He follows my laws faithfully.

This man does what is right. He will live.”

## The son hurts others

## Ezekiel 18:10

“Now suppose this man has a violent son.

The son kills people or does other evil things.”

## Ezekiel 18:11

The father did none of these things.

But the son eats meals to worship idols on the mountains.

He has sex with his neighbor’s wife.

## Ezekiel 18:12

The son mistreats people who are poor and need help.

He robs people. He keeps what borrowers leave with him.

He looks to idols and does things God hates.

## Ezekiel 18:13

When he lends, he demands too much money back.

God asked, “Will this son live?”

He will die for the evil he has done.

He is responsible for his own death.

## The grandson chooses another way

## Ezekiel 18:14

Now suppose the violent son becomes a father.

His boy, the grandson, sees what his father does.

The grandson thinks about it and chooses a different way.

## Ezekiel 18:15

The grandson avoids meals held for idols on the mountains.

He does not turn to Israel’s idols.

He does not have sex with his neighbor’s wife.

## Ezekiel 18:16

He does not mistreat or rob anyone.

He gives back what people leave with him when they borrow.

He shares his bread with hungry people.

He gives clothes to people who have none.

## Ezekiel 18:17

The grandson refuses to harm poor people.

He never charges extra when he lends money.

He follows God’s laws.

He will live. His father’s sins will not cause his death.

## Ezekiel 18:18

His father used threats to take things from people.

He robbed his own brother and harmed his neighbors.

The father will die for his own sins.

## Ezekiel 18:19

God said, “You ask why the son does not bear his father’s guilt.”

This son, the grandson, did what was fair and right.

He kept God’s laws carefully. He will live.

## Ezekiel 18:20

The person who sins will die.

A son does not carry his father’s guilt.

A father does not carry his son’s guilt.

God credits people for the good they themselves do.

He holds each person responsible for their own evil.

## What happens when a person changes?

## Ezekiel 18:21

Suppose someone turns from every evil thing they have done.

They keep God’s laws and do what is fair and right.

That person will live.

## Ezekiel 18:22

God will no longer hold their past sins against them.

They will live because they now do what is right.

## Ezekiel 18:23

The Lord GOD asked, “Does it please me when a wicked person dies?

I want that person to turn back and live.”

## Ezekiel 18:24

Suppose a person once did what was right.

Then they turn away and do the evil things God hates.

Their earlier good deeds will no longer count for them.

They will die because they turned against God and sinned.

## Ezekiel 18:25

Israel’s people said, “The Lord is unfair.”

God answered, “Listen, Israel. Have I acted unfairly?

Look at the way you yourselves act.”

## Ezekiel 18:26

A person may stop doing right and start doing evil.

That person will die for the evil they have done.

## Ezekiel 18:27

A person who does evil can turn away from it.

By doing what is fair and right, they save their life.

## Ezekiel 18:28

They thought about what they had done.

Then they turned away from all their sins.

They will live. They will not die.

## Ezekiel 18:29

Still Israel’s people said, “The Lord is unfair.”

God asked again, “Israel, are my ways unfair?

Isn’t it your own behavior that is unfair?”

## Turn back and live

## Ezekiel 18:30

The Lord GOD said, “Israel, I will judge each of you by what you do.

Turn back from every sin.

Then your wrongdoing will no longer bring you down.”

## Ezekiel 18:31

“Leave your sins behind. Make yourselves a new heart and a new spirit.”

God wanted them to change deep inside.

He asked, “People of Israel, why should you die?”

## Ezekiel 18:32

The Lord GOD said, “No one’s death brings me joy.

Turn from your sins and live!”

**Think about it**

**What did the grandson choose to do when he saw his father hurting others?**
