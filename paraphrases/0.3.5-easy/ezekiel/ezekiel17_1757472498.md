# Ezekiel 17 — Two eagles, a vine, and a broken promise

**About this chapter**

Ezekiel tells Israel a story about a vine that reaches toward an eagle. Then God explains how the story shows what Jerusalem’s king has done.

## The first eagle plants a seed

## Ezekiel 17:1

The LORD gave me a message.

## Ezekiel 17:2

He called me “son of man.”

He told me to give Israel a riddle.

It was a story with a meaning for them to discover.

## Ezekiel 17:3

The Lord GOD told this story.

A great eagle flew to Lebanon.

Its wings were wide, with long feathers of many colors.

It took the top of a cedar tree.

## Ezekiel 17:4

The eagle broke off the highest new shoot.

It carried the shoot to a land of merchants.

Merchants are people who buy and sell things.

The eagle planted the shoot in their trading city.

## Ezekiel 17:5

Then the eagle took a seed from the land.

It planted the seed in good soil beside plenty of water.

It set it there as someone might plant a willow tree.

## Ezekiel 17:6

The seed grew into a vine.

The vine spread out close to the ground.

Its branches turned toward the eagle that planted it.

Its roots stayed in the soil where it had sprouted.

The vine kept growing new branches and shoots.

## The vine reaches toward another eagle

## Ezekiel 17:7

Another great eagle came.

It too had wide wings and many feathers.

The vine bent its roots toward this second eagle.

It stretched its branches toward the bird.

The vine wanted this eagle to water it.

## Ezekiel 17:8

Yet the vine already had good soil and plenty of water.

There it could grow branches and bear fruit.

It could become a beautiful vine.

![Grapes growing on a vine show the fruit its branches can bear.](../pictures/vine-and-grapes.svg)

## Ezekiel 17:9

The Lord GOD asked, “Will that vine thrive?”

Someone would pull it up and strip off its fruit.

Its leaves would dry up.

Even one person could pull out its roots without much effort.

## Ezekiel 17:10

“Will planting it again make it grow?

When the east wind strikes, the vine will dry up.

It will wither right where it began to grow.”

## God explains the story

## Ezekiel 17:11

The LORD spoke to me again.

## Ezekiel 17:12

The people kept disobeying God.

He told me to ask them, “Do you know what the story means?”

The first eagle stood for the king of Babylon.

He came to Jerusalem and took away its king and officials.

He brought them back to Babylon.

## Ezekiel 17:13

Babylon’s king chose someone else from Jerusalem’s royal family.

He made a covenant with him.

A covenant is a serious agreement.

The new king swore to keep it.

Babylon’s king took the leading men away from the land.

## Ezekiel 17:14

Without its leaders, the kingdom would stay weak.

The new king could keep his kingdom only by keeping the agreement.

## Ezekiel 17:15

But the new king turned against Babylon.

Like the vine reaching for the second eagle, he turned to Egypt.

He sent messengers to ask for horses and a large army.

God asked, “Can he break his agreement and escape?”

## Ezekiel 17:16

The Lord GOD said, “As surely as I live, he will die in Babylon.”

Babylon’s king had given him his throne.

Yet Jerusalem’s king treated his promise as worthless.

He broke the agreement he had made.

## Ezekiel 17:17

Pharaoh, Egypt’s king, had many soldiers.

Even that great army would be no help in battle.

Jerusalem’s enemies would build ramps and walls around the city.

Many people would die.

## Ezekiel 17:18

Jerusalem’s king had given his hand as a sign of his promise.

Then he broke his word. He would not escape.

## The LORD holds the king to his word

## Ezekiel 17:19

The Lord GOD said, “As surely as I live, I will hold him responsible.

It was my oath he treated as worthless.

It was my covenant he broke.”

## Ezekiel 17:20

“I will spread my net and catch him.”

Like a bird in a net, the king could not escape.

God would bring him to Babylon.

There God would judge the king for turning against God.

## Ezekiel 17:21

The king’s best soldiers would die in battle.

Those who lived would scatter in every direction.

God said, “Then you will know that I, the LORD, have spoken.”

## The LORD plants a new shoot

## Ezekiel 17:22

The Lord GOD said, “I myself will take a shoot from the cedar.

I will pick a tender new branch from its high top.

I will plant it on a high mountain.”

## Ezekiel 17:23

“I will plant it high in the mountains of Israel.

It will grow branches and bear fruit.

It will become a great cedar tree.

Birds of every kind will nest in its shade.”

## Ezekiel 17:24

“Then all the trees will know I am the LORD.

I bring tall trees low. I make small trees tall.

I dry up green trees. I make dry trees grow again.

I am the LORD. I have spoken, and I have done it.”

The trees show how God can bring rulers down or raise them up.
