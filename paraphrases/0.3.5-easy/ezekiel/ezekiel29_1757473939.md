# Ezekiel 29 — Egypt is like a reed that breaks

**About this chapter**

Egypt’s king boasts that he made the Nile River. Israel has counted on Egypt for help. The LORD gives Ezekiel a message for Egypt and a promise for Israel.

## A message for Pharaoh

## Ezekiel 29:1

The LORD spoke to me in the tenth year.

It was day twelve of the tenth month.

## Ezekiel 29:2

He called me “son of man.”

He told me, “Speak against Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.

Give my message to him and to all Egypt.”

## Ezekiel 29:3

The Lord GOD said, “Pharaoh, I am against you.”

He compared Pharaoh to a huge creature lying in Egypt’s rivers.

Pharaoh boasted, “The Nile belongs to me. I made it myself.”

## Ezekiel 29:4

God said, “I will put hooks in your jaws.

Fish from your streams will cling to your scales.

I will pull you out of the rivers.

The fish will come out with you.”

## Ezekiel 29:5

“I will leave you and the fish in the desert.

You will lie out in the open.

No one will gather you up for burial.

Wild animals and birds will feed on you.”

## A walking stick that breaks

## Ezekiel 29:6

“Then everyone in Egypt will know I am the LORD.”

God compared Egypt to a walking stick made from a reed.

A reed is a tall plant with a narrow stem.

Israel tried to lean on Egypt for support.

## Ezekiel 29:7

When Israel took hold of that reed, it split.

Its sharp pieces tore their shoulders.

When they leaned on it, it broke. They hurt their backs.

They had counted on Egypt, and Egypt failed them.

## Ezekiel 29:8

The Lord GOD said to Egypt, “I will bring war against you.

People and animals will die.”

## Ezekiel 29:9

Egypt would become an empty wasteland.

Then the people would know he was the LORD.

Pharaoh had boasted that he had made the Nile himself.

## Ezekiel 29:10

God said, “That is why I am against you and your rivers.

I will leave your land in ruins.”

This would reach from Migdol to Syene and to the border of Cush.

## Ezekiel 29:11

Neither people nor animals would pass through the land.

No one would live there for forty years.

## Ezekiel 29:12

Egypt would lie empty among other empty lands.

Its cities would lie in ruins for forty years.

God would scatter the Egyptians across many countries.

## The Egyptians will come home

## Ezekiel 29:13

The Lord GOD said, “After forty years I will gather the Egyptians.

I will bring them back from the countries where I scattered them.”

## Ezekiel 29:14

God would free them from captivity.

He would bring them home to Pathros.

That was the land their people came from.

There they would be a kingdom with little power.

## Ezekiel 29:15

“Egypt will be the weakest kingdom.

It will never again rise above other nations.

I will take away its power to rule them.”

## Ezekiel 29:16

Israel would never again depend on Egypt for help.

Egypt would remind Israel of the wrong they had done by turning to it.

Then Israel would know he was the Lord GOD.

## A later message about Babylon and Egypt

## Ezekiel 29:17

The LORD spoke to me again in the twenty-seventh year.

It was the first day of the first month.

## Ezekiel 29:18

God said, “Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, made his army work hard against Tyre.”

The soldiers wore the hair off their heads.

Their shoulders grew sore and raw.

Yet the king and his army got no pay from Tyre for their work.

## Ezekiel 29:19

The Lord GOD said, “I will give Egypt to Nebuchadnezzar.”

The king would take Egypt’s riches and carry them away.

Those riches would be his army’s pay.

## Ezekiel 29:20

God said, “I have given him Egypt as his reward.

He and his soldiers did that work for me.”

## Ezekiel 29:21

“On that day I will make a horn grow for Israel.”

A horn was a sign of strength.

God was promising new strength to his people.

He told me, “I will open your mouth to speak among them.

Then they will know that I am the LORD.”
