# Proverbs 31 — Speak up for others and honor good work

**About this chapter**

King Lemuel remembers his mother telling him whose voices a king must hear. Then a poem honors a woman who farms, trades, teaches, and helps her neighbors.

## A mother speaks to her son

## Proverbs 31:1

King Lemuel passed on these words.

His mother taught him this message.

## Proverbs 31:2

My son, what shall I tell you?

I gave birth to you.

I made promises to God about you.

## Proverbs 31:3

Do not spend your strength chasing women.

Do not give your energy to those who ruin kings.

## A king must judge fairly

## Proverbs 31:4

Lemuel, drinking wine is no work for kings.

A ruler should not long for strong drink.

## Proverbs 31:5

A king who drinks may forget the law.

Then people who suffer may lose their right to a fair hearing.

## Proverbs 31:6

His mother spoke of strong drink for someone near death.

She spoke of wine for someone in deep sorrow.

## Proverbs 31:7

She wanted those people to forget their poverty for a while.

She wanted them to have relief from their misery.

## Proverbs 31:8

Speak up for people who cannot speak for themselves.

Defend those who have lost everything.

## Proverbs 31:9

Use your voice to judge fairly.

Stand up for people who are poor and need help.

## A woman her family trusts

## Proverbs 31:10

Who can find a wife of such good character?

She is worth more than precious red stones called rubies.

## Proverbs 31:11

Her husband trusts her completely.

He has all the good things he needs.

## Proverbs 31:12

Every day of her life, she does him good.

She keeps him from harm.

## Proverbs 31:13

She chooses wool from sheep and fibers from flax plants.

She is eager to work with them.

## Proverbs 31:14

Like trading ships that cross the sea, she brings home food from far away.

## A field and a vineyard

## Proverbs 31:15

She gets up before daylight to feed her household.

The women who work for her receive their share.

## Proverbs 31:16

She looks over a field and buys it.

With money she has earned, she plants grapevines.

## Proverbs 31:17

She gets ready for hard work.

Her arms are strong.

## Proverbs 31:18

She sees that her work earns good money.

Her lamp still burns at night.

## Hands that make and give

## Proverbs 31:19

One hand reaches for the stick holding the loose fibers.

Her fingers turn a tool called a spindle.

It twists the fibers into thread.

## Proverbs 31:20

Those same hands reach out to help poor people.

She welcomes people in need.

## Proverbs 31:21

When snow comes, she has no fear for her family.

Each one has clothes of deep red cloth.

## Proverbs 31:22

She makes covers for her bed.

She wears fine linen and purple cloth.

**Follow the fold**

Trace the edge of the cloth. Picture the thread that went into it. The poem has already shown the woman turning loose fibers into thread.

## Work that earns respect

## Proverbs 31:23

People know her husband at the city gates.

There he sits with the elders, the leaders of the land.

## Proverbs 31:24

She makes linen clothes and sells them.

She supplies traders with bands of cloth to wear around the waist.

## Proverbs 31:25

She wears strength and honor like clothing.

She can look ahead and laugh without fear.

## Proverbs 31:26

She speaks with wisdom.

People can trust what she teaches.

## Proverbs 31:27

She watches over everything her household needs.

She works for the bread she eats.

## Her family speaks

## Proverbs 31:28

Her children stand up and call her blessed.

Her husband praises her too.

## Proverbs 31:29

Her husband says, “Many women have done fine things.

You have done even more than all of them.”

## Proverbs 31:30

Charm can fool you.

Beauty fades.

Praise the woman who deeply respects the LORD.

## Proverbs 31:31

Give her what her work has earned.

At the city gates, let people honor her for what she has done.
