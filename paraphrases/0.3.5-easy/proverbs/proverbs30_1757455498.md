# Proverbs 30 — What Agur learned by looking

**About this chapter**

Agur asks God for enough to live on. Then he asks us to notice what animals do. Even an ant has something to teach him.

## Agur speaks

## Proverbs 30:1

Agur was the son of Jakeh.

He gave this message to Ithiel.

Agur said, “God, I am tired and worn out.”

## Proverbs 30:2

I know less than anyone else, Agur said.

I lack even ordinary human understanding.

## Proverbs 30:3

I have not learned wisdom.

I do not know the Holy One.

## Proverbs 30:4

Who has gone up to heaven and come down?

Who has held the wind in his hands?

Who has wrapped the waters in his coat?

Who has set the whole earth in place?

What is his name?

What is his Son’s name?

Surely you know!

## Two things to ask of God

## Proverbs 30:5

Every word God speaks is true and free from fault.

God is like a shield for people who turn to him for safety.

## Proverbs 30:6

Do not add words of your own to God’s words.

God may correct you and show that you lied.

## Proverbs 30:7

God, I ask two things of you.

Please grant them before I die.

## Proverbs 30:8

Keep lies and dishonest words far from me.

Give me just the food I need.

Spare me both poverty and great wealth.

## Proverbs 30:9

With too much, I might turn from you.

I might say, “Who is the LORD?” With too little, I might steal.

That would dishonor your name, my God.

## People who look down on others

## Proverbs 30:10

Never tell lies about a servant to the master.

The servant may call down a curse on you.

You will have to answer for what you said.

## Proverbs 30:11

Some people curse their fathers.

They have no blessing for their mothers.

## Proverbs 30:12

Some people think they are clean.

Yet the wrong they have done still clings to them like dirt.

## Proverbs 30:13

Some people lift their heads high with pride.

They look down on everyone around them.

## Proverbs 30:14

Some people are like beasts with swords for teeth and knives for jaws.

They destroy people who are poor and helpless.

## Four things that never have enough

## Proverbs 30:15

A leech is a small animal that sucks blood.

Picture its two daughters, both named “Give!” Three things are never satisfied.

In fact, four never say, “Enough!”

## Proverbs 30:16

The grave keeps receiving the dead.

An empty womb waits for a child.

Dry land keeps taking in water.

Fire keeps burning as long as there is fuel.

## Proverbs 30:17

Agur warns against looking at your father with scorn or refusing to obey your mother.

He pictures ravens in the valley plucking out that mocking eye.

Young vultures eat it.

## Four things that fill Agur with wonder

## Proverbs 30:18

Three things fill me with wonder, says Agur.

In fact, there are four I cannot explain.

## Proverbs 30:19

Watch an eagle fly through the sky.

Watch a snake slide over rock.

Watch a ship cross the sea.

Then think of a man and a young woman drawn to each other.

## Proverbs 30:20

A woman who breaks her marriage promise acts as if nothing happened.

She eats, wipes her mouth, and says, “I have done nothing wrong.”

## Four changes that shake the earth

## Proverbs 30:21

Three things make the earth tremble, says Agur.

In fact, there are four it cannot bear.

## Proverbs 30:22

One is a servant who becomes king.

Another is a fool with all he can eat.

## Proverbs 30:23

A third is an unloved woman who marries.

The fourth is a servant who takes her mistress’s place.

## Four small, wise creatures

## Proverbs 30:24

Four creatures on earth are small.

Yet each shows great wisdom.

## Proverbs 30:25

Ants have little strength.

Yet in summer they store food for later.

![A head of ripe grain at the top of a stalk.](../pictures/grain.svg)

**Find the seeds**

Look at the seeds on the stalk. Imagine one falling to the ground. An ant can carry food away and store it for later.

## Proverbs 30:26

Rock badgers are small animals with little power.

Yet they find safe homes among the rocks.

## Proverbs 30:27

Locusts are insects that have no king.

Yet they move together in orderly groups.

## Proverbs 30:28

A lizard is small enough to catch in your hands.

Yet it finds its way into kings’ palaces.

## Four that walk with dignity

## Proverbs 30:29

Three things walk with dignity, says Agur.

In fact, four are impressive to watch.

## Proverbs 30:30

First comes the lion, strong among animals.

Nothing makes it turn back.

## Proverbs 30:31

Then comes a rooster walking proudly.

Next comes a male goat.

Last comes a king surrounded by his army.

## Before anger becomes a fight

## Proverbs 30:32

Have you foolishly boasted or planned to do harm?

Put your hand over your mouth.

Stop before you say more.

## Proverbs 30:33

Churning milk makes butter.

Twisting a nose makes it bleed.

In the same way, stirring up anger starts a fight.

**Pause and answer**

How do ants prepare for the time when food is harder to find?
