# Psalm 9 — The LORD hears people in trouble

**About this psalm**

David thanks the LORD for judging fairly. Then he asks the LORD to see what his enemies are doing to him.

## David thanks the LORD

## Psalm 9:1

David wrote this song for the music leader.

Sing it to the tune “The Death of the Son.”

LORD, I thank you with my whole heart.

I will tell about all the wonderful things you have done.

## Psalm 9:2

You make me glad, and I rejoice in you.

God Most High, I will sing to praise your name.

## The LORD hears David’s case

## Psalm 9:3

My enemies turn back.

They stumble and die in your presence.

## Psalm 9:4

You heard my case and stood up for me.

You sat on your throne and judged fairly.

## Psalm 9:5

You spoke against the nations for doing wrong.

You destroyed those who did evil.

You wiped out their names forever.

## Psalm 9:6

My enemies have come to ruin for good.

You tore their cities from the ground.

Even the memory of them has gone.

## The LORD judges the world

## Psalm 9:7

The LORD reigns forever.

He has set up his throne to judge.

## Psalm 9:8

The LORD judges the whole world fairly.

He treats every people fairly as he rules.

## Psalm 9:9

People who suffer cruelty can run to the LORD.

He keeps them safe like a strong fort in troubled times.

## Psalm 9:10

LORD, those who know your name trust you.

You have never left those who look for you.

**Pause and answer**

Who can run to the LORD for safety?

## David calls the people to sing

## Psalm 9:11

Sing to the LORD who lives in Zion!

Tell the nations what the LORD has done.

## Psalm 9:12

The LORD remembers those whose blood others have shed.

He calls their killers to account.

He hears people who cry out in pain.

## David asks for mercy

## Psalm 9:13

LORD, have mercy on me.

See how my enemies hurt me!

Pull me back from the gates of death.

## Psalm 9:14

Then I can praise you at the gates of Zion.

There I will rejoice because you saved me.

**The two gates**

The gates of death picture how close David feels to dying. Zion is Jerusalem. David wants to live and praise God there with other people.

## The nations fall into their own trap

## Psalm 9:15

The nations dug a pit and fell into it.

They hid a net and caught their own feet.

## Psalm 9:16

People know the LORD by his fair judgments.

Those who do evil get caught in their own traps.

Higgaion. Selah.

![A net made of crossing ropes.](../pictures/net.svg)

**Look at the net**

Trace the ropes with your finger. Imagine a foot caught between them. David says the harm the nations planned for others has caught up with them.

**Words in the song**

Higgaion and Selah appear here. Their exact meaning in this song is uncertain.

## Psalm 9:17

People who do evil go back to Sheol, the place of the dead.

So do all the nations that forget God.

## People in need have hope

## Psalm 9:18

God will remember people in need.

Those who suffer cruelty still have reason to hope.

## Psalm 9:19

Rise up, LORD!

Do not let human beings have the victory over you.

Gather the nations before you and judge them.

## Psalm 9:20

Fill them with fear, LORD.

Let the nations learn they are only human.

Selah.

**Pause and answer**

In your own words, why do people in need still have hope?
