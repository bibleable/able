# Psalm 137 — We remembered Jerusalem

**About this psalm**

People taken from Jerusalem sit beside rivers in Babylon. Their captors ask for a happy song. The singers remember their home, and their grief turns to anger.

## The harps hang in the trees

## Psalm 137:1

We sat by the rivers of Babylon.

We cried when we remembered Zion, our home in Jerusalem.

## Psalm 137:2

We hung our harps on the willow trees there.

![A harp with strings stretched across its frame.](../pictures/davids-harp.svg)

**Look at the harp**

Imagine hanging the harp in a tree and leaving its strings silent. That is what the singers did when they remembered home.

## Psalm 137:3

The people who held us captive asked for a song.

Those who hurt us demanded happy music.

They said, “Sing us a song from Zion!”

## A promise to remember Jerusalem

## Psalm 137:4

How can we sing the LORD’s song so far from home?

## Psalm 137:5

Jerusalem, if I ever forget you, may my right hand stop working.

## Psalm 137:6

If I forget you, may my tongue stick to the roof of my mouth.

May that happen if I ever love another joy more than Jerusalem.

## The singer cries out about the violence

## Psalm 137:7

LORD, remember what the people of Edom said when Jerusalem fell.

They shouted, “Tear it down! Tear it down to its very foundations!”

## Psalm 137:8

Babylon, your city too will fall.

Happy is the one who pays you back for what you did to us.

## Psalm 137:9

Happy is the one who takes your babies and strikes them against the rocks.

**Whose words are these?**

These are the words of a grieving captive. He wants Babylon to suffer the harm his people suffered.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the people cry beside the rivers?
