# 1 Samuel 13 — Saul makes an offering before Samuel arrives

**About this chapter**

At Gilgal, Saul’s soldiers begin to leave. Saul makes an offering while he waits for Samuel. When Samuel arrives, he asks Saul to explain.

## Saul chooses men from Israel

## 1 Samuel 13:1

Saul was 30 years old when he became king.

He ruled Israel for 42 years.

## 1 Samuel 13:2

Saul chose 3,000 men from Israel.

He kept 2,000 at Michmash and in the hills of Bethel.

Another 1,000 stayed with Jonathan at Gibeah in Benjamin.

Saul sent the rest home.

## 1 Samuel 13:3

Jonathan attacked the Philistine soldiers stationed at Geba.

The Philistines heard the news.

Saul sounded a ram’s horn to spread the news across the land.

He said, “Let the Hebrews hear!”

**Reading help**

Hebrews is another name for the Israelites.

## 1 Samuel 13:4

All Israel heard this report about their king.

“Saul has attacked a Philistine army post.

Now the Philistines cannot stand us!”

The Israelites received the call to join Saul at Gilgal.

**Pause and answer**

Where did the Israelites gather to join Saul?

## The Israelites are afraid

## 1 Samuel 13:5

The Philistines gathered to fight Israel.

They had 3,000 chariots and 6,000 horsemen.

Their soldiers seemed as many as grains of sand on a beach.

They camped at Michmash, east of Beth-aven.

**Reading help**

The picture of sand on a beach tells us the army was too large to count by sight.

## 1 Samuel 13:6

The Israelites saw how much danger their army was in.

Some men hid in caves, thick bushes, or rocky places.

Others hid in cellars or in pits for storing water.

## 1 Samuel 13:7

Some Israelites crossed the Jordan River into Gad and Gilead.

Saul stayed at Gilgal.

The soldiers with him shook with fear.

## Saul stops waiting for Samuel

## 1 Samuel 13:8

Samuel had told Saul to wait seven days.

Saul waited, but Samuel had still not arrived at Gilgal.

Saul’s soldiers began leaving him.

## 1 Samuel 13:9

Saul said, “Bring me the burnt offering and the peace offerings.”

Then Saul gave the burnt offering himself.

**Reading help**

A burnt offering was a gift people burned for God. A peace offering was a gift that included a shared meal.

## 1 Samuel 13:10

Just as Saul finished the offering, Samuel arrived.

Saul went out to greet him.

## Samuel asks Saul what happened

## 1 Samuel 13:11

Samuel asked, “What have you done?”

Saul answered, “I saw my soldiers leaving.

You had not come at the time we agreed.

The Philistines were gathering at Michmash.

## 1 Samuel 13:12

I thought they would come down here and attack me.

I had not yet asked the LORD for his favor.

So I felt I had to give the burnt offering.”

## 1 Samuel 13:13

Samuel said, “You have acted foolishly.

You disobeyed the command the LORD your God gave you.

If you had obeyed, the LORD would have let your family rule Israel forever.

## 1 Samuel 13:14

Now your family’s rule will come to an end.

The LORD has looked for a man who wants what he wants.

He has chosen that man to lead his people because you disobeyed him.”

**Reading help**

The Bible calls this person a man after God’s own heart. This means he wants to do what pleases God.

**Pause and answer**

Why did Samuel say Saul’s family would lose the kingdom?

## Saul counts the men who stayed

## 1 Samuel 13:15

Samuel left Gilgal for Gibeah in Benjamin.

Saul counted the soldiers who stayed with him.

There were about 600 men.

## 1 Samuel 13:16

Saul and his son Jonathan stayed at Geba in Benjamin with their men.

The Philistines camped at Michmash.

## 1 Samuel 13:17

Three groups left the Philistine camp to raid the land.

One group went toward Ophrah in the land of Shual.

## 1 Samuel 13:18

Another went toward Beth-horon.

The third took the border road facing the wilderness.

That road looked down into the Valley of Zeboim.

## Israel has no blacksmiths

## 1 Samuel 13:19

There were no blacksmiths anywhere in Israel.

The Philistines wanted to keep the Hebrews from making swords or spears.

**Reading help**

A blacksmith makes and repairs metal tools.

## 1 Samuel 13:20

So Israelites took their farm tools to the Philistines for sharpening.

They brought plow blades, digging tools, axes, and curved blades for cutting grain.

## 1 Samuel 13:21

The Philistines charged one pim to sharpen a plow blade or a digging tool.

They charged a third of a shekel to sharpen a pitchfork or an axe.

They charged the same to fix the point of a stick for guiding oxen.

**Reading help**

A pim and a shekel were weights people used to measure out silver for payment.

## 1 Samuel 13:22

When the day of battle came, the soldiers had no swords or spears.

Only Saul and his son Jonathan had these weapons.

## 1 Samuel 13:23

A group of Philistine soldiers went out to the mountain pass at Michmash.
