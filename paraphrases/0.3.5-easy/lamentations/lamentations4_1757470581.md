# Lamentations 4 — The children of Jerusalem ask for bread

**About this chapter**

The poet remembers Jerusalem before enemies entered its gates. Now even people who once ate the finest food are hungry. The poet tells what happened to his people and speaks about the end of their exile.

## Gold loses its shine

## Lamentations 4:1

Look how dull the pure gold has become!

Precious stones from the temple lie scattered on street corners.

## Lamentations 4:2

The sons of Zion once seemed worth their weight in gold.

Zion is another name for Jerusalem.

Now people treat them like ordinary jars made of clay.

![An ordinary clay jar, like the jars the poet mentions.](../pictures/clay-jar.svg)

## Lamentations 4:3

Even wild jackals give their young milk.

But my people have become cruel to their children.

The poet compares them to an ostrich in the desert.

## Lamentations 4:4

A thirsty baby’s tongue sticks to the roof of its mouth.

Little children ask for bread. No one gives them any.

## Lamentations 4:5

People who ate the finest food now have nothing.

They sit in the streets.

People who grew up wearing costly red clothes huddle on piles of ashes.

## Lamentations 4:6

My people’s punishment has been worse than Sodom’s.

That city fell in a moment. No one helped it.

## Hunger changes the people

## Lamentations 4:7

Our leaders once looked bright as snow and white as milk.

Their skin had a healthy red glow, richer than rubies.

Their beauty reminded people of precious stones called sapphires.

## Lamentations 4:8

Now they look darker than soot, the black dust from a fire.

In the streets no one recognizes them.

Their skin clings to their bones, dry as a stick.

## Lamentations 4:9

The poet says those who died by the sword suffered less.

Those dying of hunger waste away in pain.

The fields have no food for them.

## Lamentations 4:10

Women who had tenderly cared for others cooked their own children.

Those children became their food as our city fell.

## Lamentations 4:11

The LORD poured out all his fierce anger.

He started a fire in Zion.

It burned down to the stones beneath the city.

## Even the priests must leave

## Lamentations 4:12

The kings of the earth thought no enemy could enter Jerusalem.

People everywhere thought its gates would keep enemies out.

## Lamentations 4:13

Yet the city fell because its prophets and priests had sinned.

In the city they had killed people who did what was right.

## Lamentations 4:14

Now they stumbled through the streets as if blind.

The blood they had shed made them unclean.

No one dared touch even their clothes.

## Lamentations 4:15

People shouted, “Go away! You are unclean!

Go away! Do not touch us!”

They fled and wandered from place to place.

People in other nations said, “They cannot stay here.”

## Lamentations 4:16

The LORD himself scattered them. He no longer looked on them with favor.

People showed no honor to the priests.

No one welcomed the elders.

## The people remember waiting for help

## Lamentations 4:17

We watched from our towers until our eyes grew tired.

We waited for help from another nation.

That nation could not save us.

## Lamentations 4:18

Our enemies followed our every step.

We could no longer walk in our own streets.

Our time had run out. The end had come.

## Lamentations 4:19

Those who chased us moved faster than eagles in the sky.

They chased us over the mountains.

They waited in hiding for us in the desert.

## Lamentations 4:20

They caught our king in their pits.

The LORD had chosen him. To us he was like the breath we needed to live.

We had said, “Even among other nations, we can live in his shade.”

We thought our king would protect us.

## The poet speaks to Edom and Zion

## Lamentations 4:21

People of Edom who live in Uz, go ahead and celebrate!

But the cup will come to you too.

You will drink until you are drunk and stand naked in shame.

The cup is a picture of the punishment coming to Edom.

## Lamentations 4:22

People of Zion, your punishment is complete.

The LORD will not keep you in exile any longer.

People of Edom, he will punish you for doing wrong.

He will bring your sins into the open.
