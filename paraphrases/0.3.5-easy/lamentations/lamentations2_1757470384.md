# Lamentations 2 — Jerusalem cries out for her children

**About this chapter**

The poet looks at Jerusalem after its fall. He calls the city “Daughter of Zion” and speaks to her as if she could answer. At the end, Jerusalem speaks to the LORD.

## The city loses its protection

## Lamentations 2:1

The Lord’s anger hangs like a cloud over Jerusalem.

Israel’s glory seemed as high as heaven. He threw it to the ground.

In his anger he abandoned even his holy place.

The poet calls that place God’s footstool.

## Lamentations 2:2

The Lord showed no pity as he destroyed the homes of Jacob’s people.

In anger he tore down Judah’s walled towns.

He brought the kingdom and its princes down in disgrace.

## Lamentations 2:3

In fierce anger he cut off Israel’s horns.

An animal’s horns show its strength. Israel had lost its strength.

When the enemy came, the Lord drew back his protecting hand.

His anger spread through Jacob’s people like a fire burning everything around it.

## Lamentations 2:4

The Lord drew his bow like an enemy ready to shoot.

He killed people dear to Jerusalem.

His anger swept like fire through the place her people called home.

## Lamentations 2:5

The Lord acted like an enemy. Israel lay ruined.

He destroyed her palaces and the defenses that kept her safe.

Across Judah more and more people wept for what they had lost.

## Worship stops and the walls fall

## Lamentations 2:6

The LORD destroyed his meeting place like a simple garden shelter.

In Zion people no longer kept the feasts or the Sabbath rest days.

In fierce anger he turned away from both king and priest.

## Lamentations 2:7

The Lord rejected his altar and left his holy place.

He let enemies take the city’s palace walls.

Their shouts filled the LORD’s house.

They were as loud as crowds at a feast.

## Lamentations 2:8

The LORD decided to tear down Jerusalem’s wall.

He stretched out a measuring cord to mark it off.

He kept on until the defenses fell.

The poet pictures even the ruined walls crying together.

## Lamentations 2:9

The gates sank into the ground. The Lord broke their locking bars.

The king and princes now live in exile among other nations.

The people have lost the guidance of the law.

Even the prophets receive no vision from the LORD.

## Lamentations 2:10

Jerusalem’s elders sit on the ground in silence.

They put dust on their heads and wear rough cloth to show their grief.

The young women bend their heads to the ground.

## The poet sees the hungry children

## Lamentations 2:11

I have wept until I can hardly see.

My stomach churns. Grief overwhelms me because my people are ruined.

Children and babies faint in the streets.

## Lamentations 2:12

The children ask their mothers, “Where is the grain and wine?”

They collapse in the streets like wounded people.

Their lives slip away in their mothers’ arms.

## Lamentations 2:13

Jerusalem, what can I say to comfort you?

What other sorrow could I compare to yours?

Your wound is as deep as the sea. Who can heal you?

## Lamentations 2:14

Your prophets told you about visions that led you astray.

They hid your guilt when a warning might have spared you exile.

The messages they gave you were empty promises.

## Lamentations 2:15

People passing by clap their hands to mock you.

They hiss and shake their heads.

“Is this the city everyone called perfectly beautiful?

Is this the place that brought the whole earth joy?”

## Lamentations 2:16

Your enemies hiss and grind their teeth at you.

They say, “We have swallowed up Jerusalem!

We waited for this day. Now we have seen it!”

## Lamentations 2:17

The LORD did what he said long ago he would do.

He brought you down without pity.

He gave your enemies power and let them celebrate your fall.

## The poet urges Jerusalem to pray

## Lamentations 2:18

The people cry out to the Lord with all their hearts.

Walls of Jerusalem, let tears flow like a river!

Let them flow day and night without stopping.

The poet is calling the whole city to weep.

## Lamentations 2:19

Get up and cry out as soon as the night watch begins.

Pour out your heart like water before the Lord.

Tell him everything that weighs on you.

Raise your hands to him. Ask him to spare your children.

They are fainting from hunger on every street corner.

## Jerusalem speaks to the LORD

## Lamentations 2:20

Jerusalem cries, “Look, LORD! Who else have you treated like this?

Should mothers eat the babies they have cared for?

Should anyone kill priests and prophets in your holy place?

## Lamentations 2:21

Young and old lie together in the dusty streets.

The sword has killed my young women and men.

In your anger you killed them. You showed no pity.

## Lamentations 2:22

You called terrors from every side like guests invited to a feast.

On the day of your anger, no one escaped or survived.

My enemy killed the children I cared for and raised.”
