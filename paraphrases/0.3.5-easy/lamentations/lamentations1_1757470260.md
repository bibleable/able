# Lamentations 1 — Jerusalem weeps and calls to the LORD

**About this chapter**

A lament is a cry of grief. This poem calls Jerusalem a woman. When she weeps and speaks, we hear the grief of the people who lived there.

## The roads into Jerusalem are empty

## Lamentations 1:1

Jerusalem once bustled with people. Now she sits alone.

She is like a widow who has lost her husband.

Once she was like a princess ruling many lands.

Now she must serve like a slave.

## Lamentations 1:2

At night she sobs. Tears run down her cheeks.

None of those she loved comes to comfort her.

The friends she trusted have become her enemies.

## Lamentations 1:3

Judah’s people suffered and worked as slaves.

Now they must live far from home among other nations.

They find no rest. Their enemies caught them in their distress.

## Lamentations 1:4

Even the roads to Zion seem to grieve.

Zion is another name for Jerusalem.

No one takes those roads to celebrate the feasts.

The city gates stand empty. The priests groan.

The young women mourn. The whole city aches with grief.

## Lamentations 1:5

Her enemies now rule her and live at ease.

The LORD has brought her grief because of her many sins.

Her enemies have led her children away as prisoners.

## Lamentations 1:6

Zion has lost all her beauty and honor.

Her leaders are like deer that can find no grass.

They have no strength to run from the hunter.

## The people remember what they lost

## Lamentations 1:7

The people wander in misery. They remember Jerusalem’s treasures.

When enemies seized the people, no one helped.

Their enemies watched them fall and laughed.

## Lamentations 1:8

Jerusalem has sinned greatly. People now make fun of her.

In the poem she stands naked, her shame plain to see.

Even those who once honored her look down on her.

She groans and turns away.

## Lamentations 1:9

Her wrongs show like stains on her skirt.

She never thought about how it would end.

Her fall shocked everyone. No one comforted her.

Jerusalem cries, “LORD, see how I suffer! My enemy has won.”

## Lamentations 1:10

The enemy took all her treasures.

Foreigners entered the holy place in her temple.

LORD, these were people you had forbidden to join your worshiping assembly.

## Lamentations 1:11

Her people groan as they search for bread.

They trade their treasures for food to stay alive.

Jerusalem cries, “Look at me, LORD! See how people despise me.”

## Jerusalem speaks to people passing by

## Lamentations 1:12

Jerusalem says, “Does my grief mean anything to you who pass by?

Stop and look. Have you seen sorrow like mine?

The LORD brought this suffering on me in his fierce anger.

## Lamentations 1:13

He sent fire from above. I felt it deep in my bones.

He caught my feet as if in a net and turned me back.

He left me alone, weak all day long.

## Lamentations 1:14

The Lord tied my sins together like a heavy yoke.

A yoke is a wooden bar laid across an animal’s neck.

I feel that weight on my neck. The Lord took away my strength.

He handed me over to people too strong for me.

![Two oxen carry a wooden yoke across their necks.](../pictures/job-yoke.svg)

## Lamentations 1:15

The Lord turned away from the strong soldiers inside my city.

He called an army to crush my young fighters.

People tread on grapes to squeeze out the juice.

That is how completely the Lord has crushed Judah’s people.

## Lamentations 1:16

That is why I weep. Tears pour from my eyes.

No one comes close to comfort me or give me strength.

My children have nothing left. The enemy has won.”

## No one comes to comfort her

## Lamentations 1:17

Zion holds out her hands. No one comforts her.

The LORD ordered that the neighbors of Jacob’s people become their enemies.

Those neighbors treat Jerusalem as something unclean.

## Lamentations 1:18

Jerusalem says, “The LORD does what is right.

I rebelled against his command.

People everywhere, listen. Look at how I suffer.

Enemies have taken my young women and young men prisoner.

## Lamentations 1:19

I called to those I loved. They betrayed me.

My priests and elders searched the city for food to stay alive.

They died there.

## Jerusalem asks the LORD to see her grief

## Lamentations 1:20

Look, LORD! See my distress.

My stomach churns. My heart pounds. I have rebelled against you.

Outside, the sword takes my loved ones. Inside, there is death.

## Lamentations 1:21

Others hear me groan. No one comforts me.

My enemies heard what you did. They were glad to hear of my trouble.

Bring the day you announced. Let them face what I face.

## Lamentations 1:22

Look at all the evil they have done.

You dealt with me for my many sins. Deal with them too.

I keep groaning. My heart grows faint.”
