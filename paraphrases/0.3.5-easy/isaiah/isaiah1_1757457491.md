# Isaiah 1 — Learn to do right

**About this chapter**

Isaiah speaks to people who pray with their hands held out. The LORD asks what those same hands have done. He tells the people to defend neighbors who cannot get a fair hearing.

## Isaiah calls heaven and earth to listen

## Isaiah 1:1

God showed Isaiah a vision about Judah and its city, Jerusalem.

Isaiah was the son of Amoz.

Four kings ruled Judah during those years.

They were Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah.

## Isaiah 1:2

Listen, heavens!

Listen, earth!

The LORD says, “I raised my people like children.

They grew up and turned against me.”

## Isaiah 1:3

An ox knows its owner.

A donkey knows where its master puts its food.

Yet Israel does not know me.

My people do not understand.

## Isaiah 1:4

What sorrow for this nation!

The people carry a heavy load of guilt.

One generation after another does wrong.

They have turned their backs on the LORD.

They treat the Holy One of Israel with contempt.

## A wounded people and a ruined land

## Isaiah 1:5

Why keep turning against God and taking more blows?

Picture the whole nation as one wounded person.

Your head is hurt.

Your heart is sick.

## Isaiah 1:6

From head to foot, you have bruises and open sores.

No one has washed them or wrapped them in bandages.

No one has put oil on them to ease the pain.

## Isaiah 1:7

Your land lies in ruins.

Fire has burned your cities.

You watch strangers eat what your fields produce.

They leave your land bare.

## Isaiah 1:8

Jerusalem stands alone.

Picture a watchman’s hut in a field of grapes or cucumbers.

The city is as lonely as that hut, surrounded by enemies.

## Isaiah 1:9

The LORD who commands heaven’s armies left a few of us alive.

Otherwise we would have been wiped out like Sodom and Gomorrah.

## What the LORD sees at worship

## Isaiah 1:10

Isaiah calls Jerusalem’s rulers “rulers of Sodom.” He calls the people “people of Gomorrah.” Listen to the LORD!

Pay attention to what our God teaches.

## Isaiah 1:11

The LORD asks, “What use are all your animal offerings to me?” I have had enough burnt rams and fat from well-fed cattle.

The blood of bulls, lambs, and goats gives me no pleasure.

## Isaiah 1:12

When you come before me, who asked you to do this?

You come stamping through the courtyards of my house.

## Isaiah 1:13

Stop bringing empty gifts.

I hate the incense you burn.

You meet at each new moon and on your Sabbath days of rest.

I cannot bear your sacred meetings while you keep doing evil.

## Isaiah 1:14

I hate those new moon celebrations and feasts of yours.

They weigh me down.

I am tired of carrying them.

## Isaiah 1:15

You hold out your hands in prayer.

I will look away.

However often you pray, I will not listen.

I see the blood on your hands.

You have hurt and killed people.

## What the LORD asks his people to do

## Isaiah 1:16

Wash yourselves clean.

Put away the evil I see you doing.

Stop doing wrong.

## Isaiah 1:17

Learn to do right.

Work for fair treatment.

Stop those who mistreat others.

Defend children who have no father.

Speak in court for women whose husbands have died.

## Isaiah 1:18

The LORD says, “Come, let us settle this together.” Your sins may stain you bright red.

Yet you can become white as snow, white as wool.

This is how completely God can take away guilt.

## Isaiah 1:19

If you are willing to listen and obey, you will eat the best food your land grows.

## Isaiah 1:20

If you refuse and turn against me, the sword will take your lives.

The LORD himself has spoken.

## A city that once judged fairly

## Isaiah 1:21

Jerusalem was once faithful.

Now Isaiah compares the city to a woman who sells sex.

People once judged cases fairly there.

Now murderers live where people once did right.

## Isaiah 1:22

Your silver has turned to waste metal.

Someone has watered down your fine wine.

Jerusalem has lost what made it good.

**Picture the silver**

Look at a single coin. Imagine discovering that what looked like silver was only waste metal. Isaiah uses that disappointment to describe what happened to justice in Jerusalem.

## Isaiah 1:23

Your rulers rebel and make friends with thieves.

They love secret payments and chase after gifts.

They ignore children who have no father.

Widows never get a hearing.

## The LORD promises new judges

## Isaiah 1:24

Now the Lord GOD speaks.

He commands heaven’s armies.

He is Israel’s Mighty One.

He says, “I will be free of my enemies.

I will repay them for what they have done.”

## Isaiah 1:25

I will turn my hand against you.

Like a worker cleaning silver, I will remove every bit of waste.

I will take away what has spoiled you.

## Isaiah 1:26

I will give you judges and advisers like those you had at first.

Then people will call Jerusalem the City That Does Right.

They will call it the Faithful City.

## Isaiah 1:27

God will rescue Zion by doing what is just.

Zion is another name for Jerusalem.

God will do right by the people who turn back to him and save them.

## An oak with dry leaves

## Isaiah 1:28

Those who rebel and cling to sin will come to ruin together.

Those who leave the LORD will perish.

## Isaiah 1:29

You took delight in worshiping at sacred oak trees.

Those trees will bring you shame.

So will the gardens you chose for worship.

## Isaiah 1:30

You will be like an oak whose leaves dry up.

You will be like a garden with no water.

## Isaiah 1:31

Even a strong person will be like dry material ready to burn.

That person’s own work will be the spark.

Both will burn, and no one will put out the fire.
