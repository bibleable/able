# Isaiah 16 — Moab asks for shelter

**About this chapter**

People from Moab need somewhere safe to stay. Isaiah tells how it feels to see their homes and harvests lost.

## A plea for a safe place

## Isaiah 16:1

Send lambs as payment to the ruler of the land.

Send them from Sela in the desert to Mount Zion in Jerusalem.

## Isaiah 16:2

The women of Moab gather where people cross the Arnon River.

They are like birds fluttering about after someone pushes them from their nest.

They have lost their homes.

## Isaiah 16:3

The people of Moab plead, “Tell us what to do.

Decide how you will help us.

At midday give us shade as dark as night.

Hide the people who have fled.

Keep their hiding place secret.

## Isaiah 16:4

“Let those who fled Moab stay with you.

Shelter them from the one who destroys their homes.” One day the destruction will stop.

The cruel rulers will disappear from the land.

## A king from David’s family

## Isaiah 16:5

Then a king from David’s family will sit on the throne.

Steady love will hold up His rule.

People can count on Him when He judges.

He will find out what is fair.

He will be quick to do what is right.

## Moab loses its harvest

## Isaiah 16:6

We have heard how proud Moab is.

Its people boast about how great they are.

Their boasts are empty words.

## Isaiah 16:7

So the people of Moab cry out together for their land.

The people suffer terribly.

They mourn the loss of the raisin cakes of Kir-hareseth.

Raisins are dried grapes.

## Isaiah 16:8

The fields of Heshbon have dried up.

So have the grapevines of Sibmah.

The vines once spread as far as Jazer and toward the desert.

Their branches reached across the sea.

Now rulers from other nations have crushed the finest vines.

**Find the grapes**

Follow the branch to the bunch of grapes. People could dry those grapes to make raisins. When the vines dried up, the people lost that food.

## Isaiah 16:9

I weep with the people of Jazer for Sibmah’s vines.

I weep for Heshbon and Elealeh.

My tears could soak both towns.

People once cheered when they gathered fruit and crops.

Now those shouts are silent.

## Isaiah 16:10

Joy has gone from the fruit trees.

No one sings among the grapevines.

No one steps on the grapes to press out their juice.

The cheering has come to an end.

The LORD says, “I have stopped it.”

## Grief for Moab

## Isaiah 16:11

Deep inside, I grieve for Moab.

My sorrow sounds like a sad tune on a harp.

I feel that same sorrow for Kir-heres.

## Isaiah 16:12

The people of Moab climb to their place of worship.

They wear themselves out there.

They go inside their holy place to pray.

Their prayers will bring them no help.

## The LORD gives a time

## Isaiah 16:13

This was the LORD’s earlier message about Moab.

## Isaiah 16:14

Now the LORD says, “Count three years as carefully as workers count their time on a job.

Then people will look down on everything that makes Moab proud.

Moab has many people now.

Only a few will remain, and they will be weak.”
