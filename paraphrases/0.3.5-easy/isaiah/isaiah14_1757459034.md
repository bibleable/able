# Isaiah 14 — The cruel king loses his power

**About this chapter**

Isaiah gives Israel a song for the day Babylon’s king loses his power. Then Isaiah tells what the LORD has planned for Assyria and Philistia.

## Israel comes home

## Isaiah 14:1

The LORD will show kindness to Jacob’s family, the people of Israel.

He will choose them again.

He will settle them in their own land.

People from other lands will join them and become part of Jacob’s family.

## Isaiah 14:2

Other nations will help Israel travel home.

In the LORD’s land, Israelites will have men and women from those nations as servants.

They will take prisoner the people who once held them prisoner.

They will rule over those who made them suffer.

## A song about the king of Babylon

## Isaiah 14:3

The LORD will give you rest from your pain and fear.

No one will force you to do that hard work anymore.

## Isaiah 14:4

Then you will sing a song that mocks Babylon’s king.

The song begins, “The king who hurt us has stopped!

His terrible anger has come to an end!”

## Isaiah 14:5

The LORD has broken the stick the wicked rulers held.

A ruler’s stick was a sign of his power.

## Isaiah 14:6

Those rulers struck people again and again in anger.

They hunted people down and forced whole nations to obey.

## Isaiah 14:7

Now the whole earth is quiet and at rest.

People burst into song.

## Isaiah 14:8

In the song, even the trees are glad the king has fallen.

The cypress trees and the cedars of Lebanon say, “Since you fell, no one comes to cut us down.”

## The dead kings greet him

## Isaiah 14:9

The song pictures Sheol, the place of the dead.

It stirs at the news that Babylon’s king is coming.

The spirits of dead rulers get ready to greet him.

The kings of the nations rise from their thrones.

## Isaiah 14:10

Those kings say to Babylon’s king, “Now you are weak too!

You have become just like us.”

## Isaiah 14:11

All your royal splendor has gone down to the dead.

The sound of your harps has gone there too.

Maggots make a bed beneath you.

Worms cover you like a blanket.

## The king wanted to rise above the clouds

## Isaiah 14:12

The singers call the king a bright star of the morning.

“How you have fallen from the sky!

You brought nations down.

Now you yourself lie on the ground.”

## Isaiah 14:13

The singers recall what the king said to himself.

“I will climb to heaven.

I will put my throne above God’s stars.

I will sit on the mountain in the far north where the assembly meets.

## Isaiah 14:14

“I will climb higher than the clouds.

I will make myself like God Most High.”

## Isaiah 14:15

The song tells the king where he will go.

He will go down to the place of the dead, to the very bottom of the pit.

## Isaiah 14:16

People will stare at you and wonder what happened.

They will ask, “Is this the man who shook the earth?

Is this the man who made kingdoms tremble?

## Isaiah 14:17

“Did he turn the world into a desert and ruin its cities?

Did he keep prisoners from going home?”

## A king without a royal grave

## Isaiah 14:18

Other kings receive honor when they die.

Each king lies in his own tomb.

## Isaiah 14:19

But you have no place in your tomb.

People throw you away like a broken branch.

The bodies of men killed in battle cover you.

You lie in a rocky pit like a body under people’s feet.

## Isaiah 14:20

You ruined your own land and killed your own people.

So you will never share the burial other kings receive.

No one will speak of the wicked king’s family again.

## Isaiah 14:21

The song calls for the king’s sons to die because their ancestors did evil.

His sons will never take over the land.

They will never fill the earth with their cities.

## God speaks about Babylon

## Isaiah 14:22

The LORD who commands heaven’s armies says, “I will rise against Babylon.

I will wipe out its name and the people who remain.

No children or grandchildren will carry on its name.”

## Isaiah 14:23

The LORD says, “Owls will live there.

The land will turn into a swamp.

I will sweep Babylon away.” Isaiah pictures someone sweeping with a broom until nothing remains.

## Assyria’s power will break

## Isaiah 14:24

The LORD who commands heaven’s armies has made a promise.

“What I have planned will happen.

What I have decided will stand.

## Isaiah 14:25

“I will break Assyria’s power in my land.

I will crush Assyria on my mountain.

I will take its yoke off my people.

I will lift the load from their shoulders.”

![Two oxen joined by a wooden yoke.](../pictures/job-yoke.svg)

**Find the yoke**

Point to the wooden bar that holds the animals together. Imagine someone lifting it off. Isaiah uses that picture to show God freeing His people from Assyria’s rule.

## Isaiah 14:26

This is the LORD’s plan for the whole earth.

Isaiah pictures the LORD reaching His hand over every nation.

## Isaiah 14:27

Who can stop what the LORD has planned?

He has stretched out His hand.

Who can make Him draw it back?

## A warning for the Philistines

## Isaiah 14:28

Isaiah received another warning the year King Ahaz died.

## Isaiah 14:29

People of Philistia, the stick that struck you has broken.

Do not celebrate yet.

One snake will give rise to a more dangerous snake.

From its egg a flying snake will come.

Isaiah pictures a new danger following the old one.

## Isaiah 14:30

The poorest families will find food like sheep finding grass.

People in need will rest in safety.

But the LORD warns Philistia, “Hunger will destroy your people down to the roots.

Those who remain will die by the sword.”

## Isaiah 14:31

Cry out, people at the city gate!

Weep, people of the city!

All Philistia shakes with fear.

Smoke rises in the north.

An army is coming, and none of its soldiers lag behind.

## Isaiah 14:32

What should we tell the messengers from Philistia?

“The LORD established Zion.

There His people can find shelter when they suffer.” Zion is Jerusalem.
