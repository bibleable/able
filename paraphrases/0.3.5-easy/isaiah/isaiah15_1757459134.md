# Isaiah 15 — Moab weeps for its towns

**About this chapter**

Isaiah describes a night when two towns in Moab fall. As people flee their homes, Isaiah grieves with them.

## Two towns fall in one night

## Isaiah 15:1

Isaiah gives this warning about Moab.

In one night the town of Ar lies in ruins.

In one night the town of Kir lies in ruins too.

## Isaiah 15:2

The people of Dibon go up to their temple.

They weep at the places where they worship on the heights.

The people of Moab cry for Nebo and Medeba.

Everyone shaves their head.

The men cut off their beards.

This is how they show their grief.

## Isaiah 15:3

In the streets, people wear sackcloth.

Sackcloth is rough cloth people wore when they grieved.

On rooftops and in the town squares, people cry aloud.

They fall to the ground and weep.

![Thick threads woven together to make rough sackcloth.](../pictures/job-sackcloth.svg)

**Look at the cloth**

Follow one thick thread across the picture. Imagine wearing that rough cloth. People in Moab wore it so others could see their sorrow.

## Isaiah 15:4

People in Heshbon and Elealeh cry out.

People as far away as Jahaz hear them.

Even Moab’s soldiers cry out.

They shake with fear.

## People leave their homes

## Isaiah 15:5

Isaiah says, “My heart cries out for Moab.”

People flee as far as Zoar and Eglath-shelishiyah.

They weep as they climb the hill at Luhith.

On the road to Horonaim, they cry over all they have lost.

## Isaiah 15:6

The waters of Nimrim have dried up.

The grass has dried up too.

The plants are gone.

Nothing green remains.

## Isaiah 15:7

So the people gather their riches and belongings.

They carry them across a stream called the Brook of the Willows.

## Cries reach the border

## Isaiah 15:8

The sound of weeping reaches Moab’s border.

People in Eglaim hear the cries.

People in Beer-elim hear them too.

## Isaiah 15:9

Blood fills the waters of Dimon.

God says, “I will bring more trouble on Dimon.

I will send a lion against those fleeing Moab.

It will also attack those who stay in the land.”
