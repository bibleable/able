# Isaiah 53 — The servant carries our sorrows

**About this chapter**

People remember how they treated the LORD’s servant. As they tell his story, they come to understand whose troubles he was carrying.

## People looked away

## Isaiah 53:1

Who has believed what we told them?

Who has seen the strength of the LORD’s arm?

His arm pictures His power at work.

## Isaiah 53:2

The servant grew up before the LORD like a young shoot.

He was like a root growing in dry ground.

Nothing about him looked royal.

We saw no beauty that made us want to come near.

## Isaiah 53:3

People looked down on him and turned him away.

He knew pain and grief well.

We turned our faces away from him.

We treated him as if he had no worth.

## Our wrongs and his wounds

## Isaiah 53:4

Yet he took up our weaknesses.

He carried our sorrows.

We thought God had struck him down and made him suffer.

## Isaiah 53:5

He suffered deep wounds because we had done wrong.

Our sins were the reason he was crushed.

He took the punishment that brought us peace.

His wounds bring us healing.

## Isaiah 53:6

All of us wandered off like sheep.

Each of us went our own way.

The LORD laid the wrongs of us all on him.

Find the pen. Imagine the sheep leaving it and each taking a different path. That is how the speakers describe their own choices.

## The servant stays silent

## Isaiah 53:7

People treated him cruelly and made him suffer.

Yet he stayed silent.

They led him away like a lamb going to its death.

A sheep stays quiet while someone cuts its wool.

In the same way, he did not open his mouth.

## Isaiah 53:8

People took him away after an unjust judgment.

Who can tell of his descendants?

His life on earth ended.

He suffered for the wrongs my people had done.

## Isaiah 53:9

They assigned him a grave with people who had done evil.

In death he was with a rich man.

Yet he had never hurt anyone by violence.

He had never told a lie.

## After his suffering

## Isaiah 53:10

Still, it was the LORD’s will for him to suffer and be crushed.

His life became an offering for guilt.

That means he gave his life for the wrong others had done.

He will see his offspring and live a long life.

Through him the LORD’s plan will succeed.

## Isaiah 53:11

After all his suffering he will see the light of life.

He will be satisfied.

The LORD says, “My servant does what is right.

Through what he knows, he will put many people right with me.

He will carry their sins.

## Isaiah 53:12

“So I will give him a share among the great.

Like a victor, he will share the reward with the strong.

He gave his life and died.

People counted him among those who broke the law.

Yet he carried the sins of many.

He pleaded for the very people who had done wrong.”

**Pause and answer**

Whose sorrows did the servant carry?
