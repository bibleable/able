# Isaiah 36 — The people on the wall stay silent

**About this chapter**

The king of Assyria sends an officer to Jerusalem. At first the officer speaks to three officials. Then he shouts so the people on the city wall can hear him.

## An army comes to Jerusalem

## Isaiah 36:1

Hezekiah had ruled Judah for fourteen years.

Sennacherib, king of Assyria, attacked Judah’s walled cities.

He captured them all.

## Isaiah 36:2

From Lachish, Sennacherib sent a high officer with a large army to King Hezekiah in Jerusalem.

The officer’s title was Rabshakeh.

He stopped beside the channel that carried water to the upper pool.

It was on the road to the field where people washed clothes.

## Isaiah 36:3

Three of Hezekiah’s officials went out to meet him.

Eliakim, son of Hilkiah, managed the palace.

Shebna was the king’s scribe, who wrote for him.

Joah, son of Asaph, kept the official records.

## The officer questions Hezekiah’s trust

## Isaiah 36:4

The officer said, “Take this message to Hezekiah from the great king of Assyria.

He asks, ‘What makes you so sure of yourself?

## Isaiah 36:5

“‘You talk about plans and strength for war.

Those are just words.

Who do you trust so much that you dare turn against me?

## Isaiah 36:6

“‘You are leaning on Egypt.

Imagine using a cracked reed as a walking stick.

When you lean on it, it pierces your hand.

That is what Pharaoh, Egypt’s king, is like to those who trust him.

![A tall reed bends to one side.](../pictures/bending-reed.svg)

**Look at the reed**

Find the thin stem. Imagine leaning on it after it cracks. The officer says counting on Egypt is like putting your weight on that stem.

## Isaiah 36:7

“‘You may say you trust the LORD your God.

But didn’t Hezekiah remove His altars and places of worship on the hills?

Didn’t he tell Judah and Jerusalem to worship at just one altar?’”

## An offer of horses

## Isaiah 36:8

The officer went on, “Make a bargain with my master, the king of Assyria.

I will give you two thousand horses if you can find riders for them!

## Isaiah 36:9

“You depend on Egypt for war chariots and riders.

How could you drive back even one of my master’s lowest officers?”

## Isaiah 36:10

The officer even claimed that the LORD had sent him.

He said, “The LORD Himself told me to come and destroy this land.”

## Two languages at the wall

## Isaiah 36:11

Eliakim, Shebna and Joah said, “Please speak to us in Aramaic.

We understand that language.

When you speak Hebrew, the people on the wall can understand you too.”

## Isaiah 36:12

The officer answered, “Do you think my master sent me to speak only to you and your king?

The people on the wall need to hear this too.” He threatened them all with terrible hunger and thirst.

He said they would have to eat their own waste and drink their own urine.

## A message for everyone

## Isaiah 36:13

Then the officer stood and shouted in Hebrew.

“Listen to the great king of Assyria!

## Isaiah 36:14

“The king says, ‘Do not let Hezekiah fool you.

He cannot rescue you.

## Isaiah 36:15

“‘Hezekiah says the LORD will save you and keep this city out of my hands.

Do not let him talk you into trusting the LORD.

## Isaiah 36:16

“‘Stop listening to Hezekiah.

Make peace with me and come out of the city.

Each of you can eat grapes and figs from your own plants.

You can drink from your own water tank.

## Isaiah 36:17

“‘Later I will come and take you to another land.

It is like your own land.

You will have grain, new wine, bread and fields of grapes.

## The officer challenges the LORD

## Isaiah 36:18

“‘Hezekiah says the LORD will rescue you.

Do not let him mislead you.

Has any nation’s god saved its land from the king of Assyria?

## Isaiah 36:19

“‘Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad?

Where are the gods of Sepharvaim?

Did they save Samaria from me?

## Isaiah 36:20

“‘Which of those gods kept its land out of my hands?

How then can the LORD save Jerusalem from me?’”

## A report for Hezekiah

## Isaiah 36:21

The people on the wall stayed silent.

Hezekiah had told them, “Do not answer him.” So no one said a word.

## Isaiah 36:22

Eliakim, Shebna and Joah went back to Hezekiah.

They had torn their clothes to show their distress.

They told the king what the Assyrian officer had said.
