# Job 3 — Job speaks about his grief

**About this chapter**

After seven days with his friends, Job speaks about his losses. Job wishes he had never been born. He asks why he must go on living in such pain.

## Job wishes his birth day had never come

## Job 3:1

After the long silence, Job spoke.

He cursed the day he was born.

He wished that day had never come.

## Job 3:2

These were Job’s words.

## Job 3:3

I wish the day of my birth could disappear.

I wish that night could disappear too, when someone said, “A boy has begun to grow in his mother’s womb.”

## Job 3:4

If only that day had been dark!

May God in heaven pay it no attention.

May no light shine on it.

## Job 3:5

Let deep darkness take that day back.

Let a cloud cover it.

Let the whole day go black.

**Pause and answer**

Which day does Job wish had gone dark?

## A night with no dawn

## Job 3:6

If only darkness had taken that night away!

Leave it out when you count the days of the year.

Give it no place in any month.

## Job 3:7

Let no child come from that night.

Let no one shout for joy that night.

## Job 3:8

Let those who curse days curse that day.

They are the ones ready to wake Leviathan, the great sea creature.

## Job 3:9

Let the morning stars go dark.

Let that night wait for daylight that never comes.

Let it never see the first light of morning.

![Stars above a dark horizon. Job imagines a night when the sun never rises.](../pictures/job-night-without-dawn.svg)

## Job 3:10

Why do I say this?

That night did not close the doors of my mother’s womb.

If it had, I would never have seen this sorrow.

**Reading help**

Job pictures the womb as a room with doors. He wishes his life had never begun.

**Pause and answer**

What does Job wish he had never lived to see?

## Job asks why he lived

## Job 3:11

Why did I not die at birth?

Why did I live when I came out of my mother’s womb?

## Job 3:12

Why did anyone take me onto their knees?

Why did my mother feed me at her breast?

## Job 3:13

By now I would be lying down in peace.

I would be asleep and at rest.

**Reading help**

Job speaks about death as sleep. He imagines relief from his pain.

## Job 3:14

I would lie with kings and their advisers.

They built cities for themselves that now lie in ruins.

## Job 3:15

I would lie with princes who owned gold.

Their houses were full of silver.

## Job 3:16

Why was I not hidden away like a stillborn child?

Such a baby dies before birth and never sees daylight.

**Pause and answer**

Who built the cities that now lie in ruins?

## Job imagines a place of rest

## Job 3:17

There the wicked stop hurting people in their anger.

There tired people can rest.

## Job 3:18

There prisoners have peace.

They no longer hear the voice of anyone who mistreated them.

## Job 3:19

People of low and high rank are there together.

A slave is free from the master there.

**Pause and answer**

What does Job say tired people find there?

## Job asks why suffering continues

## Job 3:20

Why does someone in misery still see daylight?

Why does life go on for people in deep distress?

## Job 3:21

They long for death, but it does not come.

They search for it as people search for buried treasure.

## Job 3:22

Why do they live when reaching the grave would bring them such joy?

## Job 3:23

Why give life to someone who cannot see a way forward?

It is as if God has put a hedge all around him.

## Job 3:24

When food comes, I sigh.

My groans keep coming, like water pouring out.

## Job 3:25

The very thing I feared has happened to me.

What I dreaded has come.

## Job 3:26

I have no peace or quiet.

I cannot rest.

Trouble has come to me.
