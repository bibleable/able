# Job 39 — The LORD asks Job about the animals

**About this chapter**

The LORD is still speaking to Job. He asks Job to think about animals that live, feed, and raise their young far from people.

## Mountain goats and deer give birth

## Job 39:1

Job, do you know when mountain goats give birth?

Have you watched a deer give birth to her fawn?

## Job 39:2

Can you count the months they carry their young?

Do you know when the young will come?

## Job 39:3

The mothers bend low to the ground.

Then they give birth to their young.

## Job 39:4

The young animals grow strong in the open fields.

They leave their mothers and never come back.

**A word to know**

A fawn is a young deer.

## The wild donkey runs free

## Job 39:5

Who let the wild donkey run free?

Who took the straps off that fast donkey?

## Job 39:6

I gave the wild donkey a home in the wilderness.

He lives on open ground covered with salt.

## Job 39:7

The wild donkey has no use for noisy towns.

He hears no driver shouting orders.

## Job 39:8

He wanders over the mountains looking for grass.

He searches for anything green to eat.

## Will the wild ox work for Job?

## Job 39:9

Will a wild ox agree to work for you?

Will he spend the night beside your feeding box?

## Job 39:10

Can your straps keep him plowing a straight row?

Will he follow you and plow the valleys?

## Job 39:11

The wild ox is very strong.

Can you count on him to do your heavy work?

## Job 39:12

Can you trust him to bring in your grain?

Will he carry it to the place where you thresh it?

![Stalks of grain.](../pictures/grain.svg)

**Look at the grain**

A farmer cuts the stalks and gathers them together. To thresh grain means to loosen the seeds from the stalks.

**Pause and answer**

What crop does the LORD name when he talks about the wild ox?

## The ostrich and her eggs

## Job 39:13

The ostrich flaps her wings with joy.

But how different her wings are from a stork’s long wing feathers!

## Job 39:14

The ostrich leaves her eggs on the ground.

She lets the sand keep them warm.

## Job 39:15

She forgets that a foot could crush her eggs.

A wild animal could step on them.

## Job 39:16

She treats her chicks as though they belong to someone else.

She seems to care little if her work goes to waste.

## Job 39:17

God gave the ostrich little wisdom.

God did not give her understanding.

## Job 39:18

Yet see her spread her wings and run!

She can laugh at a horse and its rider.

**What does the laugh mean?**

The ostrich runs so fast that she can leave a horse behind. The LORD describes her speed as if she could laugh at the rider.

## The horse hears the call to battle

## Job 39:19

Job, did you give the horse his strength?

Did you give him the mane along his neck?

## Job 39:20

Did you make him leap like a locust?

His proud snort makes people shake with fear.

**A word to know**

A locust is an insect that can jump far.

## Job 39:21

The horse scrapes the valley floor with his hoof.

He delights in his strength and rushes into battle.

## Job 39:22

He laughs at danger.

Even a sword cannot make him turn back.

## Job 39:23

A case full of arrows rattles against his side.

A spear and a long lance flash beside him.

## Job 39:24

The horse shakes with excitement and races over the ground.

When the ram’s horn sounds, he cannot stand still.

## Job 39:25

He snorts eagerly when he hears the horn.

From far off, he smells the battle.

He hears the leaders shout and the soldiers cry out.

## The hawk and the eagle fly high

## Job 39:26

Did you teach the hawk how to fly?

Did you teach it to spread its wings toward the south?

## Job 39:27

Do you tell the eagle to fly high?

Does it build its nest up high at your command?

## Job 39:28

The eagle lives on a cliff.

High rocks give the eagle a safe home.

## Job 39:29

From there the eagle looks for food.

It can spot its food far away.

## Job 39:30

Its young feed on blood.

Where something lies dead, there you find the eagle.

**Pause and answer**

In your own words, how does an eagle find food from its high home?
