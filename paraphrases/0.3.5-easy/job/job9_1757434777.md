# Job 9 — Job longs to speak without fear

**About this chapter**

Job answers his friend Bildad. He imagines bringing his case before God. He wonders how anyone could speak freely when God is so powerful.

## Job answers Bildad

## Job 9:1

Then Job answered Bildad.

## Job 9:2

Yes, I know this is true.

But how can anyone stand before God and be found innocent?

## Job 9:3

Suppose someone wanted to argue a case against God.

That person could not answer even one question in a thousand.

## Job 9:4

God is wise and strong.

Who has ever stood against God and come out well?

**Pause and answer**

How many of God’s thousand questions does Job think a person could answer?

## God’s power over earth and sky

## Job 9:5

God moves mountains before they know it.

In his anger he turns them over.

## Job 9:6

He shakes the earth from its place.

Even the foundations beneath it tremble.

## Job 9:7

He can tell the sun to stop shining.

He can shut away the stars.

## Job 9:8

God alone spreads out the sky.

He walks on the waves of the sea.

## Job 9:9

God made the stars called the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades.

He also made the groups of stars in the southern sky.

## Job 9:10

God does great things beyond our understanding.

No one can count all his wonders.

**Pause and answer**

Who does Job say made the groups of stars?

## Who can question God?

## Job 9:11

If God passed me, I would not see him.

If he moved by me, I would not know it.

## Job 9:12

If God takes something away, who can stop him?

Who dares ask God, “What are you doing?”

## Job 9:13

God does not hold back his anger.

Even the helpers of Rahab, the great sea monster, crouch in fear before him.

## Job imagines standing in court

## Job 9:14

How could I answer God?

What words could I use to argue my case?

## Job 9:15

Even if I were right, I could not answer.

I could only ask my judge for mercy.

## Job 9:16

Suppose I called God to court and he answered.

Even then I would not believe he was listening to me.

**Pause and answer**

What would Job ask his judge to give him?

## Job 9:17

I feel he would crush me with a storm.

He would wound me again and again without cause.

## Job 9:18

He gives me no time to catch my breath.

My sorrow is more than I can bear.

## Job 9:19

Who could match God’s strength?

And who could call God to court to answer for what he does?

## Job 9:20

Even if I were right, my own words would condemn me.

Even if I were innocent, I would sound guilty.

## Job speaks about injustice

## Job 9:21

I am innocent.

Yet I no longer care what happens to me.

I hate my life.

## Job 9:22

It makes no difference, as I see it.

God destroys the innocent and the wicked alike.

## Job 9:23

When disaster suddenly kills people, the innocent lose hope.

It seems to me that God laughs at their distress.

## Job 9:24

Wicked people rule the earth.

God covers the judges’ eyes so they cannot see.

If God does not do this, who does?

## Days that hurry past

## Job 9:25

My days go faster than a runner.

They rush past before I see anything good.

## Job 9:26

My days slip by like light boats made of reeds.

They pass like an eagle diving to catch its food.

![A boat on the water. Job compares his passing days to a boat moving quickly.](../pictures/boat.svg)

**Pause and answer**

Which bird does Job compare his days to?

## Job 9:27

Suppose I said, “I will put my complaint aside.

I will change the look on my face and smile.”

## Job 9:28

God, I would still fear all the pain ahead.

I know you will not declare me innocent.

## Job 9:29

I already stand guilty.

Why wear myself out trying to prove my case?

## Job wants someone to hear both sides

## Job 9:30

Suppose I washed with snow.

Suppose I scrubbed my hands with lye, a strong cleaner.

## Job 9:31

You would still plunge me into a filthy pit.

Even my clothes would want nothing to do with me.

**Reading help**

Job pictures trying to wash himself clean. He feels that whatever he says, he will still look guilty.

## Job 9:32

God is no human being like me.

How could I answer him?

How could the two of us go to court together?

## Job 9:33

There is no mediator between us.

A mediator is someone who can stand between two people and hear both sides.

## Job 9:34

If only God would put his rod away!

Then I would no longer tremble in fear of him.

**Reading help**

A rod is a stick. Here it pictures the punishment Job fears.

## Job 9:35

Then I could speak to God without fear.

As things stand, I am alone.

**Pause and answer**

What does Job wish he could do without fear?
