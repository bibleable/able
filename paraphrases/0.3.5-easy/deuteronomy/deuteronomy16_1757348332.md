# Deuteronomy 16 — Three feasts and fair judges

**About this chapter**

Moses teaches Israel when to gather for three feasts. He also explains how judges must treat people.

## Passover remembers the night Israel left Egypt

## Deuteronomy 16:1

Moses told Israel to celebrate Passover in the month of Abib.

That was the month the LORD brought them out of Egypt.

They had left at night.

## Deuteronomy 16:2

For Passover, Israel must offer the LORD an animal from the herd or flock.

The LORD would choose the place where they would worship him.

## Deuteronomy 16:3

With the meal, they must eat bread made without yeast.

They must eat this bread for seven days.

Moses called it the bread of suffering.

It would remind them all their lives how quickly they had left Egypt.

**Reading help**

Yeast makes bread dough rise.

The word “unleavened” means made without anything that makes dough rise.

## Deuteronomy 16:4

For seven days there must be no yeast anywhere in their land.

They must leave none of the first evening’s sacrifice until morning.

## Where to eat the Passover meal

## Deuteronomy 16:5

They could not choose any town they liked for the Passover sacrifice.

## Deuteronomy 16:6

They must bring it only to the place the LORD chose for his name.

They must offer it at sunset.

That was the time they had left Egypt.

## Deuteronomy 16:7

They must roast the meat and eat it there.

In the morning they could return to their tents.

**Pause and answer**

When could the people return to their tents?

## Deuteronomy 16:8

For six days they must eat bread without yeast.

On the seventh day they must gather to honor the LORD.

Everyone must rest from work that day.

## Count seven weeks from the first grain

## Deuteronomy 16:9

They must start counting when they first cut the standing grain.

They must count seven weeks.

![A stalk of ripe grain](../pictures/grain.svg)

The first cutting of grain marked the start of the count.

**Pause and answer**

When did Israel start counting seven weeks?

## Deuteronomy 16:10

Then they must celebrate the Feast of Weeks for the LORD.

Each person could choose a gift to bring.

The gift should reflect how much the LORD had given them.

## Deuteronomy 16:11

They must celebrate together at the place the LORD chose.

Their sons, daughters and servants must join them.

So must the Levites and people from other lands living among them.

They must include children without fathers and women whose husbands had died.

## Deuteronomy 16:12

Israel must remember that they had been slaves in Egypt.

They must follow these laws carefully.

## Celebrate when the harvest is in

## Deuteronomy 16:13

After gathering their grain and wine, they must celebrate another feast.

The Feast of Tabernacles would last seven days.

**Reading help**

A tabernacle is a shelter.

This feast is also called the Feast of Shelters.

## Deuteronomy 16:14

Whole families must celebrate, including sons, daughters and servants.

They must include the Levites and people from other lands.

Children without fathers and widows must also share their joy.

**Pause and answer**

Who could join the families at the harvest feast?

## Deuteronomy 16:15

For seven days they must celebrate at the place the LORD chose.

The LORD would bless their harvest and all their work.

Then their joy would be complete.

## Bring a gift to each feast

## Deuteronomy 16:16

Three times each year all Israel’s men must come before the LORD at his chosen place.

They must come for the Feast of Unleavened Bread.

They must also come for the Feast of Weeks and the Feast of Tabernacles.

Each person must bring a gift.

## Deuteronomy 16:17

Each must give what he could afford.

His gift should reflect how much the LORD had given him.

**Pause and answer**

How did a person decide how much to bring?

## Choose judges who will be fair

## Deuteronomy 16:18

Each tribe must choose judges and officials in every town the LORD gave them.

The judges must hear people’s cases and decide fairly.

## Deuteronomy 16:19

They must never favor one person or deny anyone justice.

They must refuse bribes.

A bribe can keep even a wise judge from seeing what is right.

It can make an honest person’s words serve a lie.

**Reading help**

A bribe is a gift someone gives to get an unfair decision.

**Pause and answer**

Why must a judge refuse a bribe?

## Deuteronomy 16:20

Moses told the people to seek only what was just.

Then they could live and keep the land the LORD was giving them.

## The LORD’s altar

## Deuteronomy 16:21

Israel would build an altar to the LORD.

They must never place a wooden pole for worshiping Asherah beside it.

## Deuteronomy 16:22

They must never set up a stone pillar for worship.

The LORD their God hated such pillars.
