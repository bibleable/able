# Deuteronomy 23 — Laws for Israel’s gatherings and daily life

**About this chapter**

Moses gives Israel rules for the assembly and the camp. He also explains how to treat a person seeking safety and how to respect a neighbor’s crop.

## Who could join the assembly

## Deuteronomy 23:1

Moses gave Israel rules about the assembly of the LORD.

Under this law, a man whose genitals had been crushed or cut off could not join the assembly.

**Reading help**

An assembly is a gathering of people.

Here it means Israel gathered before the LORD.

## Deuteronomy 23:2

The law also excluded a person whose parents were not married to each other at the time of birth.

This rule extended to that person’s descendants, even to the tenth generation.

**Reading help**

Children, grandchildren and their children belong to different generations.

## Deuteronomy 23:3

Ammonites and Moabites could not join the assembly.

This rule also extended to their descendants, even to the tenth generation.

## Moses recalls Israel’s neighbors

## Deuteronomy 23:4

When Israel left Egypt, those peoples did not meet them with food and water.

They hired Balaam, son of Beor, to curse Israel.

Balaam came from Pethor in Aram-naharaim.

## Deuteronomy 23:5

The LORD refused to listen to Balaam.

He turned the curse into a blessing because he loved Israel.

**Reading help**

A curse calls for harm.

A blessing calls for good.

**Pause and answer**

What did the LORD do with Balaam’s curse?

## Deuteronomy 23:6

Moses told Israel never to look to those nations for peace or prosperity.

## Deuteronomy 23:7

Moses told Israel to treat the Edomites with respect.

The Edomites were their relatives.

Israel must also respect the Egyptians.

The Israelites had once lived as foreigners in Egypt.

## Deuteronomy 23:8

The third generation of Edomite and Egyptian children could join the LORD’s assembly.

## Keeping the camp holy

## Deuteronomy 23:9

When Israel camped during a war, the people must keep away from wrongdoing.

## Deuteronomy 23:10

A man might release semen in his sleep.

Under Israel’s law, this made him unclean for a time.

He must leave the camp and stay outside.

## Deuteronomy 23:11

Toward evening he must wash with water.

After sunset he could return to camp.

**Pause and answer**

When could the man return to camp?

## Deuteronomy 23:12

The people must set aside a place outside camp to use as a toilet.

## Deuteronomy 23:13

Each person must carry a digging tool.

They must dig a hole there and cover their waste.

## Deuteronomy 23:14

The LORD moved through the camp to protect Israel and give them victory.

The camp must be holy.

If he saw anything unclean there, he might turn away.

## Safety for someone who escaped slavery

## Deuteronomy 23:15

An enslaved person might escape and come to Israel for safety.

The Israelites must never send that person back to the slaveholder.

## Deuteronomy 23:16

The person could choose a town and live there among them.

The Israelites must never mistreat that person.

**Pause and answer**

Who chose the town where the escaped person would live?

## Gifts for worship

## Deuteronomy 23:17

No Israelite man or woman could sell sex as part of worship at a shrine.

## Deuteronomy 23:18

No one could use money earned from selling sex to fulfill a promise at the LORD’s house.

This applied to money earned by a man or a woman.

The LORD rejected both kinds of payment.

## Loans and promises

## Deuteronomy 23:19

An Israelite could lend money, food or other things to a fellow Israelite.

The lender must never charge that person interest.

**Reading help**

Interest is the extra amount a lender charges for a loan.

## Deuteronomy 23:20

A lender could charge interest to a foreigner.

A fellow Israelite could repay the loan without paying interest.

By following this law, Israel could receive the LORD’s blessing on their work in their new land.

## Deuteronomy 23:21

Someone who made a vow to the LORD must keep it promptly.

The LORD would hold that person to the promise.

Delaying would make the person guilty of sin.

**Reading help**

A vow is a serious promise to God.

## Deuteronomy 23:22

Anyone could choose to make no vow.

That choice was no sin.

## Deuteronomy 23:23

If someone freely spoke a promise to the LORD, that person must do what they said.

**Pause and answer**

What must someone do after making a vow?

## Eating in a neighbor’s field

## Deuteronomy 23:24

In a neighbor’s vineyard, a person could eat grapes until full.

They must leave their basket empty of the neighbor’s grapes.

![Grapes growing on a vine](../pictures/vine-and-grapes.svg)

A vineyard is a place where people grow grapes.

**Pause and answer**

What could a visitor do with the grapes?

## Deuteronomy 23:25

In a neighbor’s grainfield, a person could pick heads of grain by hand.

They must never use a sickle to cut the neighbor’s crop.

**Reading help**

A sickle is a curved tool for cutting grain.
