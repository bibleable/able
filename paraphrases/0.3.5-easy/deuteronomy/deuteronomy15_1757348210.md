# Deuteronomy 15 — Cancel debts and give generously

**About this chapter**

Moses teaches Israel how to help a neighbor who owes money and how to provide for a servant who leaves.

## When a loan comes to an end

## Deuteronomy 15:1

Moses told Israel to cancel debts at the end of every seven years.

**Reading help**

A debt is something one person owes another.

Canceling a debt means the person no longer owes it.

## Deuteronomy 15:2

When the LORD’s time of release was announced, each lender must cancel the loan to a fellow Israelite.

The lender could no longer ask that neighbor to pay.

## Deuteronomy 15:3

A lender could still collect a debt from a foreigner.

But the lender must cancel whatever a fellow Israelite owed.

**Pause and answer**

Whose debts did an Israelite lender have to cancel?

## The LORD’s promise to Israel

## Deuteronomy 15:4

Moses said no one among them would need to be poor.

The LORD would bless them in the land he was giving them.

## Deuteronomy 15:5

This promise called for Israel to obey the LORD.

They must carefully follow every command Moses gave them that day.

## Deuteronomy 15:6

The LORD had promised to bless Israel.

Then Israel could lend to many nations without borrowing from any.

Israel would rule many nations, and none would rule Israel.

## Help a neighbor in need

## Deuteronomy 15:7

Someone in an Israelite town might be poor.

Moses warned the people against closing their hearts and hands to that neighbor.

**Reading help**

Closing your hand to someone means holding back help you could give.

## Deuteronomy 15:8

They must open their hands instead.

That meant lending their neighbor whatever the neighbor needed.

## Deuteronomy 15:9

A lender might think the seventh year was too near.

The lender might refuse to help because the debt would soon end.

Moses called that thought wrong.

The neighbor could cry to the LORD about it.

The lender would be guilty of sin.

**Pause and answer**

Why might a lender hesitate to lend just before the seventh year?

## Deuteronomy 15:10

Moses told the people to give generously and willingly.

Then the LORD would bless them in all their work.

## Deuteronomy 15:11

There would always be poor people in the land.

That was why Moses told Israel to open their hands wide.

They must share with fellow Israelites who needed help.

## When a servant goes free

## Deuteronomy 15:12

Someone might buy a Hebrew man or woman as a servant.

After six years of service, the servant must go free in the seventh year.

## Deuteronomy 15:13

When the servant left, the household must give the servant something to take along.

## Deuteronomy 15:14

They must give generously from their flock, their grain and their wine.

They must share what the LORD had given them.

![A stalk of ripe grain](../pictures/grain.svg)

Grain was one gift a servant could take home.

**Pause and answer**

What could a household give a servant who was leaving?

## Deuteronomy 15:15

The Israelites had once been slaves in Egypt.

The LORD their God had freed them.

That was why Moses gave them this command.

## When a servant wants to stay

## Deuteronomy 15:16

A servant might choose to stay.

The servant might love the family and live well with them.

## Deuteronomy 15:17

In that case, the household marked the servant’s ear at the door with an awl.

This meant the servant would stay for life.

The same rule applied to a woman who chose to stay.

**Reading help**

An awl is a pointed tool for making a small hole.

## Deuteronomy 15:18

The household must willingly let a servant go free.

Six years of service was worth twice a hired worker’s pay.

The LORD would bless the household in all its work.

## The firstborn animals

## Deuteronomy 15:19

If the firstborn of a cow or sheep was male, it belonged to the LORD. This rule covered all their herds and flocks.

Israel must never make a firstborn ox work.

They must leave the wool on a firstborn sheep.

## Deuteronomy 15:20

Each year the family must eat the firstborn animal before the LORD.

They must go to the place he chose.

**Pause and answer**

Where did the family eat the firstborn animal?

## Deuteronomy 15:21

Some animals were blind or had trouble walking.

Israel must never offer an animal with a serious fault as a sacrifice to the LORD.

## Deuteronomy 15:22

The family could eat that animal in their town as they would eat gazelle or deer.

People could share the meal whether they were clean or unclean for worship.

## Deuteronomy 15:23

They must pour the blood on the ground like water.

They must never eat the blood.
