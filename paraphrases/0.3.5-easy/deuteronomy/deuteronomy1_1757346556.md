# Deuteronomy 1 — Moses remembers the journey

**About this chapter**

Moses looks back on Israel’s journey. He reminds the people how the LORD led them and how they answered him.

## Moses speaks in Moab

## Deuteronomy 1:1

Moses spoke to all Israel east of the Jordan River.

They were in the wilderness, in the Arabah opposite Suph.

Paran lay on one side.

Tophel, Laban, Hazeroth, and Dizahab lay on the other.

## Deuteronomy 1:2

The trip from Horeb to Kadesh-barnea takes 11 days by way of Mount Seir.

## Deuteronomy 1:3

It was the first day of month 11 in year 40.

Moses told Israel everything the LORD had commanded him to say.

## Deuteronomy 1:4

Moses had already defeated Sihon, king of the Amorites.

Sihon had lived in Heshbon.

Moses had also defeated Og, king of Bashan, at Edrei.

Og had lived in Ashtaroth.

## Deuteronomy 1:5

There in Moab, east of the Jordan, Moses began to explain God’s law.

## The LORD tells Israel to leave Horeb

## Deuteronomy 1:6

Moses reminded Israel what the LORD their God had said at Horeb.

They had stayed at that mountain long enough.

![A mountain](../pictures/mountain.svg)

Horeb was the mountain where Israel had camped.

## Deuteronomy 1:7

The LORD told them to go to the Amorite hills and the lands nearby.

These included the Arabah, the hills, the lower slopes, and the Negev.

They included the coast, the land of the Canaanites, and Lebanon.

The land reached as far as the great Euphrates River.

## Deuteronomy 1:8

The LORD had set the land before them.

They were to go in and take it.

The LORD had promised it to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and their descendants.

**Reading help**

Descendants are a person’s children, grandchildren, and later family members.

## Moses asks for help

## Deuteronomy 1:9

Moses remembered telling Israel he needed help.

He could not lead so many people alone.

## Deuteronomy 1:10

The LORD their God had made them a large people.

Moses compared them to the stars in the sky.

**Reading help**

The stars are hard to count. Moses meant there were now very many Israelites.

## Deuteronomy 1:11

Moses asked the LORD, the God of their ancestors, to bless them as he had promised.

May they grow a thousand times in number!

## Deuteronomy 1:12

How could Moses handle all their troubles alone?

Who would help him settle their arguments?

## Deuteronomy 1:13

Each tribe must choose wise men who understood people.

The people must respect those men.

Moses would make them leaders.

## Deuteronomy 1:14

The people agreed that this was a good plan.

## Judges must listen to everyone

## Deuteronomy 1:15

Moses appointed wise leaders whom the people respected.

Some led groups of 1,000, 100, 50, or 10.

Moses also appointed officers for the tribes.

**Pause and answer**

Who chose the men Moses appointed as leaders?

## Deuteronomy 1:16

Moses told the judges to listen when people disagreed.

They must judge fairly between two Israelites.

They must also judge fairly when someone from another land was involved.

## Deuteronomy 1:17

The judges must listen to everyone, including those with little power.

They must favor no one or fear anyone.

God had given them the work of judging.

Moses would hear any case too hard for them.

## Deuteronomy 1:18

At that time Moses told Israel everything they must do.

## Israel reaches the hills

## Deuteronomy 1:19

Israel left Horeb as the LORD their God had commanded.

They crossed the great, frightening wilderness toward the Amorite hills.

Then they reached Kadesh-barnea.

## Deuteronomy 1:20

Moses told the people they had reached the Amorite hills.

The LORD their God was giving them that land.

## Deuteronomy 1:21

The LORD had set the land before them.

Moses told them to go up and take it, as the God of their ancestors had said.

They could go without fear or loss of courage.

## Twelve men explore the land

## Deuteronomy 1:22

The people asked Moses to send men ahead.

The men could explore the land and report back.

Which road should they take?

Which cities should they enter?

## Deuteronomy 1:23

Moses liked the plan.

He chose 12 men, one from each tribe.

## Deuteronomy 1:24

The men went up into the hills.

They reached the Valley of Eshcol and explored the land.

## Deuteronomy 1:25

They brought back some fruit from the land.

They said the LORD their God was giving them good land.

**Pause and answer**

What did the men bring back from the land?

## The people are afraid

## Deuteronomy 1:26

But the people refused to go up.

They disobeyed the LORD their God.

## Deuteronomy 1:27

They complained in their tents.

They said the LORD hated them.

They claimed he had brought them out of Egypt so the Amorites could destroy them.

## Deuteronomy 1:28

The people wondered where they could go.

The men’s report had made them lose courage.

The men had seen people bigger and taller than themselves.

They described huge cities with walls that seemed to reach the sky.

They had even seen descendants of the Anakim there.

## Moses remembers God’s care

## Deuteronomy 1:29

Moses told Israel they need not fear those people.

## Deuteronomy 1:30

The LORD their God would go ahead and fight for them.

They had seen him do this in Egypt.

## Deuteronomy 1:31

In the wilderness, the LORD had carried them like a father carrying his son.

He had cared for them all the way to this place.

## Deuteronomy 1:32

Even after all this, Israel did not trust the LORD their God.

## Deuteronomy 1:33

The LORD had gone ahead to find their campsites.

He had shown them the road to take.

He led them in fire by night and cloud by day.

**Pause and answer**

How did the LORD lead the people at night?

## Who will enter the land?

## Deuteronomy 1:34

The LORD heard their words and grew angry.

Then the LORD made a solemn promise.

## Deuteronomy 1:35

The men of that generation had done wrong.

They would never see the good land the LORD had promised their ancestors.

## Deuteronomy 1:36

The LORD made an exception for Caleb, son of Jephunneh.

Caleb had followed the LORD with his whole heart.

Caleb would see the land where he had walked.

The LORD would give it to Caleb and his descendants.

## Deuteronomy 1:37

Moses said the LORD was angry with him too because of the people.

The LORD told Moses he would never enter the land.

## Deuteronomy 1:38

Joshua, son of Nun, served Moses.

Joshua would enter the land.

Moses must encourage Joshua, because Joshua would help Israel receive the land.

**Pause and answer**

Whom did Moses need to encourage?

## Deuteronomy 1:39

The people had feared someone would take their children prisoner.

Those little children could not yet tell right from wrong.

The LORD would give them the land.

They would enter it and live there.

## Deuteronomy 1:40

The LORD told the people to turn back.

They must take the wilderness road toward the Red Sea.

## The people go despite the warning

## Deuteronomy 1:41

The people admitted they had sinned against the LORD.

They said they would go and fight as he had commanded.

They took their weapons.

They thought going up into the hills would be easy.

## Deuteronomy 1:42

But the LORD told Moses to stop them from going to fight.

He would not go with them.

Their enemies would defeat them.

## Deuteronomy 1:43

Moses warned the people, but they refused to listen.

They disobeyed the LORD and went up into the hills anyway.

## Deuteronomy 1:44

The Amorites came down on them like a swarm of bees.

The Amorites chased and defeated them from Seir to Hormah.

## Deuteronomy 1:45

The Israelites came back and wept before the LORD.

But the LORD would not listen to them.

## Deuteronomy 1:46

So the people stayed at Kadesh for a very long time.

**Pause and answer**

Where did the people stay after they came back?
