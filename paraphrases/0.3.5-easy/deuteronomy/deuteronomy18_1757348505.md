# Deuteronomy 18 — Food for the priests and a prophet for Israel

**About this chapter**

Moses explains how the people will feed those who serve the LORD. He also tells Israel how a prophet will speak to them.

## The priests’ share

## Deuteronomy 18:1

The priests came from the tribe of Levi.

That whole tribe would receive no share of Israel’s land.

Their share was food from the offerings people brought to the LORD.

## Deuteronomy 18:2

The other tribes would receive land.

The LORD himself was Levi’s share, just as he had promised.

## Deuteronomy 18:3

When someone offered a bull or sheep, the priests received certain parts.

Their share was the shoulder, the cheeks and the stomach.

## Deuteronomy 18:4

The people must give the priests the first of their grain, new wine and oil.

They must also give the first wool they cut from their sheep.

**Pause and answer**

What did the people give from their sheep?

## Deuteronomy 18:5

The LORD had chosen Levi and his sons from all Israel’s tribes.

They and their descendants would always serve in the LORD’s name.

**Reading help**

Serving in the LORD’s name meant doing the work he gave them.

## A Levite comes from another town

## Deuteronomy 18:6

A Levite might live in any town in Israel.

He might truly want to go and serve at the place the LORD chose.

## Deuteronomy 18:7

There he could serve in the LORD’s name.

He could serve alongside the other Levites already there.

## Deuteronomy 18:8

Each Levite would receive an equal share of the food.

The newcomer might have money from selling property his father left him.

He would still receive the same share.

**Pause and answer**

How much food did the newcomer receive compared with the other Levites?

## Where Israel must never look for guidance

## Deuteronomy 18:9

Moses warned Israel about the nations in their new land.

Israel must never copy the practices the LORD hated there.

## Deuteronomy 18:10

No one in Israel was to offer a son or daughter in a fire.

No one was to use magic or try to tell the future by reading signs.

## Deuteronomy 18:11

No one was to cast spells or seek advice from the dead.

This included asking someone who claimed to speak with spirits.

## Deuteronomy 18:12

The LORD rejected anyone who did these things.

These practices were why the LORD was driving those nations out.

## Deuteronomy 18:13

Israel must live faithfully before the LORD, free from blame.

## Deuteronomy 18:14

The nations Israel would drive out listened to people who practiced magic or told fortunes.

The LORD had forbidden Israel to follow their example.

## A prophet like Moses

## Deuteronomy 18:15

Moses said the LORD would choose a prophet like him.

That prophet would come from among the Israelites.

The people must listen to him.

**Reading help**

A prophet is someone who speaks a message from God.

## Deuteronomy 18:16

Moses reminded them of the day they gathered at Horeb.

The people had asked to stop hearing the LORD’s voice and seeing the great fire.

They feared they would die.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the people ask for the voice and fire to stop?

## Deuteronomy 18:17

The LORD told Moses the people were right to ask this.

## Deuteronomy 18:18

He would choose an Israelite to be a prophet like Moses.

The LORD would give the prophet the words to say.

The prophet would tell Israel everything the LORD commanded.

## When someone claims to speak for the LORD

## Deuteronomy 18:19

The LORD would call people to account if they refused to listen.

They would have to answer to him for ignoring his words spoken by the prophet.

## Deuteronomy 18:20

Someone might claim the LORD had sent a message that the LORD never gave.

Or a prophet might speak for other gods.

Under Israel’s law, the penalty for either act was death.

## Deuteronomy 18:21

Moses knew the people might wonder how to recognize a message the LORD never gave.

## Deuteronomy 18:22

Suppose a prophet said the LORD had told him something would happen.

If it did not happen, the LORD had not given that message.

The prophet had claimed to speak without the right to do so.

Israel need not fear him.

**Pause and answer**

What would show that a prediction did not come from the LORD?
