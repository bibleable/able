# Deuteronomy 29 — Israel makes a covenant in Moab

**About this chapter**

Israel gathers in Moab to make a solemn promise. Moses asks the people to remember their journey and warns them against turning to other gods.

## Moses gathers Israel

## Deuteronomy 29:1

The LORD told Moses to make a covenant with Israel in Moab.

This was in addition to the covenant at Horeb.

**Reading help**

A covenant is a solemn agreement.

## Deuteronomy 29:2

Moses called all Israel together.

They had seen what the LORD did to Pharaoh in Egypt.

They saw what happened to his officials and his land.

## Deuteronomy 29:3

They saw great troubles and wonderful signs of God’s power.

## Deuteronomy 29:4

Yet the LORD had not given them the understanding they needed.

They had seen and heard these things without grasping their meaning.

## What happened on the journey

## Deuteronomy 29:5

The LORD had led Israel through the wilderness for 40 years.

Their clothes and sandals had lasted the whole time.

## Deuteronomy 29:6

They had eaten no bread and drunk no wine or strong drink.

Through this they were to learn that the LORD was their God.

## Deuteronomy 29:7

Moses reminded them of two kings who came to fight Israel there.

Sihon was king of Heshbon.

Og was king of Bashan.

Israel defeated them both.

## Deuteronomy 29:8

Israel took their land.

The tribes of Reuben and Gad received it as their share, along with half the tribe of Manasseh.

**Pause and answer**

Which tribes received land after the two kings lost?

## Deuteronomy 29:9

Moses told the people to do what the covenant said.

Then they could do well in all their work.

## Everyone stands before the LORD

## Deuteronomy 29:10

All Israel stood before the LORD that day.

The tribal leaders, elders, officials and all the men were there.

## Deuteronomy 29:11

Their wives and children stood there too.

So did the foreigners in the camp who cut wood and drew water.

**Pause and answer**

What work did the foreigners in the camp do?

## Deuteronomy 29:12

They had come to enter the covenant with the LORD.

That day they would bind themselves by a solemn promise.

## Deuteronomy 29:13

The LORD would make them his people and be their God.

He had promised this to them.

He had sworn it to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, their ancestors.

## Deuteronomy 29:14

Moses said the covenant and its solemn promise reached beyond the people he was addressing.

## Deuteronomy 29:15

It included everyone standing before the LORD that day.

It also included people who were not there.

## A warning about other gods

## Deuteronomy 29:16

The Israelites remembered living in Egypt.

They remembered passing through other nations on the way to Moab.

## Deuteronomy 29:17

They had seen the idols those peoples worshiped.

The idols were made of wood, stone, silver and gold.

The LORD hated those things.

## Deuteronomy 29:18

Moses warned every man, woman, family and tribe to stay faithful.

No one should turn away to serve those nations’ gods.

Moses pictured a root growing bitter, poisonous fruit.

**Reading help**

A root can grow into a plant that harms people.

In this picture, turning away from the LORD is the root.

The harm that follows is the fruit.

## Deuteronomy 29:19

Someone might hear the promise and still think, “I can insist on my own way and be safe.”

Moses warned that this would ruin both watered land and dry land.

## The curses in the book

## Deuteronomy 29:20

The LORD would refuse to forgive that person.

In anger and jealousy he would bring every curse in the book upon them.

He would erase even their name from the earth.

## Deuteronomy 29:21

The LORD would single out that person from Israel’s tribes for disaster.

The Book of the Law listed these curses of the covenant.

![An open scroll with lines of writing](../pictures/scroll.svg)

The warnings were written down so people could read them.

## What later generations would see

## Deuteronomy 29:22

Later, Israel’s children and visitors from far away would see the land.

They would see the plagues and sickness the LORD had brought upon it.

## Deuteronomy 29:23

Sulfur and salt would cover the scorched ground.

No one would plant there, and nothing would grow.

In his great anger the LORD had destroyed Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim.

The land would look like those ruined cities.

## Deuteronomy 29:24

People from every nation would ask why the LORD had done this.

What had made him so angry?

**Pause and answer**

What would visitors want to know about the ruined land?

## Why the people lost their land

## Deuteronomy 29:25

People would answer that Israel had abandoned the covenant.

The God of their ancestors had made that covenant when he brought them out of Egypt.

## Deuteronomy 29:26

Israel had worshiped and served unfamiliar gods.

The LORD had never given them those gods.

## Deuteronomy 29:27

So the LORD grew angry with the land.

He brought every curse in the book upon it.

## Deuteronomy 29:28

In his great anger the LORD uprooted the people.

He drove them from their homes into another land.

The people giving this answer would say, “That is where they live today.”

**Reading help**

To uproot a plant means to pull it out of the ground.

Here Moses means that the people would lose their place in the land.

## What the LORD has shown his people

## Deuteronomy 29:29

Some things remain hidden and belong to the LORD.

What he has shown us belongs to us and our children forever.

So we can do everything his law teaches.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the LORD show the people his law?
