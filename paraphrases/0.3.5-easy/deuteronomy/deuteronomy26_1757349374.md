# Deuteronomy 26 — A basket from the first harvest

**About this chapter**

Moses gives each farmer words to say when bringing the first crops to the LORD. The words tell how a small family became the people of Israel.

## Bring a basket to the priest

## Deuteronomy 26:1

Moses looked ahead to the time when Israel would settle in the land.

The LORD their God was giving them this land to keep.

## Deuteronomy 26:2

Each farmer must put some of the first crops of every kind in a basket.

The farmer must take it to the place the LORD chose for worship.

![A basket](../pictures/basket.svg)

The farmer carried the first crops in a basket.

## Deuteronomy 26:3

The farmer must speak to the priest serving there.

The farmer would say, “Today I tell the LORD your God that I have entered the land he promised our ancestors.”

## Deuteronomy 26:4

The priest would take the basket from the farmer.

He would set it in front of the LORD’s altar.

**Pause and answer**

Where did the priest put the basket?

## Remember the family’s story

## Deuteronomy 26:5

Then the farmer would tell the family’s story before the LORD.

“My ancestor was an Aramean who moved from place to place.

He went to Egypt with only a few people.

His family lived there and grew into a large, strong nation.

## Deuteronomy 26:6

The Egyptians treated us cruelly.

They made us do hard work.

## Deuteronomy 26:7

We cried out to the LORD, the God of our ancestors.

He heard us.

He saw our suffering, our hard work and the cruelty we faced.

**Pause and answer**

Who heard the people when they cried out?

## Deuteronomy 26:8

The LORD used his great power to bring us out of Egypt.

He did amazing things that filled people with terror.

These wonders showed his power.

## Deuteronomy 26:9

He brought us here and gave us this land.

It is a land flowing with milk and honey.

**Reading help**

“Flowing with milk and honey” describes a land with plenty of food.

## Bow down and celebrate together

## Deuteronomy 26:10

Now, LORD, I bring you the first crops from the land you gave me.”

After saying this, the farmer must set the basket before the LORD and bow down.

## Deuteronomy 26:11

The whole household could celebrate the good things the LORD had given them.

The Levites and the foreigners living among them must share their joy.

## Share food in the third year

## Deuteronomy 26:12

In the third year, each farmer must set aside a tenth of every crop.

It was for Levites, foreigners, children without fathers and widows.

They could eat in their towns until they had enough.

**Reading help**

This tenth was called a tithe. A widow is a woman whose husband has died.

**Pause and answer**

Who could eat the food set aside in the third year?

## Deuteronomy 26:13

Then the farmer must tell the LORD what happened to that share.

“I took the sacred share out of my house.

I gave it to the Levites, foreigners, children without fathers and widows.

I remembered and obeyed every command you gave me.

## Deuteronomy 26:14

I ate none of that share while I was grieving.

I took none of it out while I was unclean for worship.

I offered none of it to the dead.

LORD my God, I did everything you told me.

## Deuteronomy 26:15

Look down from heaven, your holy home.

Bless your people Israel and the land you gave us.

You promised our ancestors a land flowing with milk and honey.”

**Pause and answer**

What did the farmer ask the LORD to bless?

## Israel and the LORD make their promises

## Deuteronomy 26:16

Moses said the LORD was giving Israel these laws that day.

The people must follow them carefully with all their heart and soul.

## Deuteronomy 26:17

That day Israel declared that the LORD was their God.

They promised to live as he taught them.

They would keep his laws and listen to him.

## Deuteronomy 26:18

That day the LORD declared that Israel was his own precious people, just as he had promised.

They must keep all his commands.

## Deuteronomy 26:19

The LORD would give Israel praise, a great name and honor above all the nations he had made.

Israel would be holy, a people set apart for him.

The LORD had promised this.
