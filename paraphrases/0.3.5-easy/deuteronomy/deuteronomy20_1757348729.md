# Deuteronomy 20 — Moses speaks about Israel at war

**About this chapter**

Moses gives Israel laws about war. He describes who could go home, what would happen to cities, and which trees the people must protect.

## What the priest would say

## Deuteronomy 20:1

Moses spoke about a time when Israel might face a larger army.

Israel might see horses and chariots and feel afraid.

Moses reminded them who was with them.

The LORD their God had brought them out of Egypt.

**Reading help**

A chariot was a cart pulled by horses.

## Deuteronomy 20:2

Before a battle, a priest would come forward and speak to the army.

## Deuteronomy 20:3

The priest would call the people of Israel to listen.

They were about to face their enemies.

He would tell them to take courage, even if they felt afraid.

## Deuteronomy 20:4

The priest would remind them that the LORD went with them.

Their God would fight for them and give them victory.

**Pause and answer**

Who did the priest say would go with Israel?

## Who could go home

## Deuteronomy 20:5

Then the officers would ask about anyone with a new house.

A man who had yet to dedicate his house could go home.

Otherwise, he might die and someone else would dedicate it.

**Reading help**

To dedicate a house means to mark the start of its use as a home.

## Deuteronomy 20:6

A man might have planted a vineyard but never tasted its grapes.

He could go home too.

Otherwise, he might die and someone else would enjoy the fruit.

![Grapes on a vine](../pictures/vine-and-grapes.svg)

A vineyard is a place where people grow grapes.

**Pause and answer**

Why could the man who planted a vineyard go home?

## Deuteronomy 20:7

A man might be engaged to a woman but still waiting to marry her.

He could go home.

Otherwise, he might die and someone else would marry her.

## Deuteronomy 20:8

The officers would also ask whether anyone felt afraid.

That man could go home so the others would keep their courage.

**Reading help**

Moses spoke of hearts melting. He meant that others might become afraid too.

## Deuteronomy 20:9

After speaking to the people, the officers would choose commanders to lead the army.

## Peace terms for a distant city

## Deuteronomy 20:10

Moses said that Israel must offer peace to a city it approached for battle.

## Deuteronomy 20:11

If the people accepted peace, they would open their gates.

Everyone in the city would then have to work for Israel.

**Pause and answer**

What would happen to people in a distant city that accepted peace?

## Deuteronomy 20:12

If the city refused peace and fought, the law called for a siege.

**Reading help**

During a siege, an army surrounds a city and tries to take it.

## Deuteronomy 20:13

Moses said that if the LORD gave Israel the city, every male there would die by the sword.

## Deuteronomy 20:14

Israel could take the women and children captive.

They could take the animals and goods for their own use.

Moses described these as things the LORD gave Israel from its enemies.

## Deuteronomy 20:15

That law concerned distant cities, outside the lands of the nearby nations.

## The cities in the land Israel would receive

## Deuteronomy 20:16

Moses gave a different law for cities in the land the LORD was giving Israel.

Nothing that breathed was to remain alive in those cities.

## Deuteronomy 20:17

Moses named the Hittites, Amorites, Canaanites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites.

He said the LORD had commanded Israel to destroy them completely.

## Deuteronomy 20:18

Moses said those nations did things the LORD hated when they worshiped their gods.

He said they might teach Israel to do the same.

Then Israel would sin against the LORD.

## Leave fruit trees standing

## Deuteronomy 20:19

Even during a long siege, Israel must leave fruit trees standing.

People could eat the fruit.

Moses asked whether trees were people who could fight them.

**Pause and answer**

Why did Moses tell Israel to leave fruit trees standing?

## Deuteronomy 20:20

The law allowed Israel to cut down trees known to bear no fruit.

It allowed the use of that wood in the siege until the city fell.
