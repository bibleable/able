# Deuteronomy 19 — Safe cities and truthful witnesses

**About this chapter**

Moses tells Israel how to protect a person who caused a death by accident. He also explains how judges must check a witness’s words.

## Three cities people could reach

## Deuteronomy 19:1

Moses spoke about the land the LORD was giving Israel.

The LORD would remove the nations living there.

Israel would drive them out and settle in their cities and houses.

## Deuteronomy 19:2

Once settled, Israel must choose three cities in that land.

They must set those cities apart as places of safety.

## Deuteronomy 19:3

Israel must divide the land into three regions and build roads.

Someone who had killed another person could run to one of these cities.

## An accident in the forest

## Deuteronomy 19:4

Moses explained who could run there and live.

Someone might kill a neighbor by accident.

That person had never meant to hurt the neighbor.

## Deuteronomy 19:5

Moses gave an example of two neighbors cutting wood together.

As one chopped a tree, the blade flew off the axe handle.

It struck the other neighbor, who died.

The woodcutter could run to a safe city and live.

**Pause and answer**

Where could the woodcutter go after the accident?

## Deuteronomy 19:6

A relative of the person who died might be angry and give chase.

If the city was too far away, the relative might catch and kill the woodcutter.

The woodcutter had meant no harm and did not deserve to die.

**Reading help**

This relative was called the avenger of blood.

## Deuteronomy 19:7

That was why Moses told Israel to set apart three cities.

## More land and more safe cities

## Deuteronomy 19:8

The LORD might give Israel more land.

He had sworn to give all this land to their ancestors.

## Deuteronomy 19:9

Israel must love the LORD and always follow his ways.

If they kept his commands and received the extra land, they must set apart three more cities.

**Pause and answer**

How many more cities would Israel set apart?

## Deuteronomy 19:10

These cities would help protect innocent people from death.

Then Israel would bear no guilt for an innocent person’s death in their land.

## When someone killed on purpose

## Deuteronomy 19:11

Moses described a different case.

Someone might hate a neighbor and wait for a chance to attack.

After killing the neighbor on purpose, the killer might run to a safe city.

## Deuteronomy 19:12

The elders of the killer’s hometown must have the killer brought back.

Under Israel’s law, they must hand the killer over to the dead person’s relative for the death penalty.

**Reading help**

Elders were leaders of the community.

## Deuteronomy 19:13

Moses said pity must never excuse such a killing.

Israel must deal with the guilt of taking an innocent life.

Then things could go well for the people.

## Leave the boundary marker in place

## Deuteronomy 19:14

Israel’s ancestors had put markers where one family’s land ended and another’s began.

Moses told Israel to leave each neighbor’s marker in place.

The markers showed each family’s share of the land the LORD was giving them.

## Check what witnesses say

## Deuteronomy 19:15

One witness alone could never prove someone guilty of any wrong.

Two or three witnesses had to give evidence to establish the charge.

**Reading help**

A witness tells the court what they know about a case.

## Deuteronomy 19:16

Moses described someone who told a lie in court.

This witness accused another person of a crime.

## Deuteronomy 19:17

Both the witness and the accused person must come before the LORD.

They must stand before the priests and judges serving at that time.

## Deuteronomy 19:18

The judges must check the facts carefully.

They might find that the witness had lied about the accused person.

**Pause and answer**

What did judges have to do before deciding who had lied?

## When a witness has lied

## Deuteronomy 19:19

Then the false witness must receive the penalty they wanted the accused person to suffer.

Moses said this would remove the evil from among the people.

## Deuteronomy 19:20

Others would hear about it and be afraid.

They would never want to do such a wrong thing again.

## Deuteronomy 19:21

Moses said pity must never change the penalty.

The law required the penalty to match the harm.

Moses named life for life, eye for eye and tooth for tooth.

He also named hand for hand and foot for foot.
