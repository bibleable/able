# Deuteronomy 14 — Food for Israel and a share for others

**About this chapter**

Moses tells Israel which foods they may eat. He explains how to save part of the harvest so others can eat too.

## Israel belongs to the LORD

## Deuteronomy 14:1

Moses reminded the Israelites that they were the LORD’s children.

When someone died, they must never cut themselves in grief.

They must also leave the hair on their foreheads unshaved.

## Deuteronomy 14:2

They were holy to the LORD their God.

Out of all the peoples on earth, the LORD chose Israel.

They belonged to him and were precious to him.

## Deuteronomy 14:3

Moses told Israel to avoid any food the LORD called unfit to eat.

## Land animals Israel could eat

## Deuteronomy 14:4

They could eat oxen, sheep and goats.

## Deuteronomy 14:5

They could also eat deer, gazelles and roe deer.

Moses listed wild goats, ibex, antelopes and mountain sheep too.

## Deuteronomy 14:6

Israel could eat land animals with both these features.

Each hoof had to divide into two parts.

The animal also had to chew the cud.

**Reading help**

A hoof is the hard covering on an animal’s foot.

Cud is food an animal brings back into its mouth to chew again.

## Animals that lacked one feature

## Deuteronomy 14:7

Moses listed the camel, the rabbit and the rock badger among those that chew the cud.

They do not have hooves divided in two.

The law called these animals unclean for Israel to eat.

## Deuteronomy 14:8

A pig has divided hooves but does not chew the cud.

The law called the pig unclean for Israel.

They must never eat its meat or touch its dead body.

## Food from the water

## Deuteronomy 14:9

Water creatures had to have both fins and scales.

Israel could eat those creatures.

**Reading help**

Fins help a fish move.

Scales are small plates that cover its skin.

**Pause and answer**

What two features did a water creature need?

## Deuteronomy 14:10

If a water creature lacked fins or scales, Israel must leave it uneaten.

The law called it unclean for them.

## Birds and other flying creatures

## Deuteronomy 14:11

Israel could eat any bird the law called clean.

## Deuteronomy 14:12

Moses named creatures Israel must never eat.

His list began with the eagle, the bearded vulture and the black vulture.

## Deuteronomy 14:13

The list included the red kite, the falcon and every other kind of kite.

## Deuteronomy 14:14

It included every kind of raven.

## Deuteronomy 14:15

It included the ostrich, the screech owl and the gull.

It also included every kind of hawk.

## Deuteronomy 14:16

The little owl, the great owl and the white owl were on the list.

## Deuteronomy 14:17

So were the desert owl, the osprey and the cormorant.

## Deuteronomy 14:18

The list included the stork and every kind of heron.

It ended with the hoopoe and the bat.

## Other rules about food

## Deuteronomy 14:19

The law called flying insects unclean for Israel.

The people must never eat them.

## Deuteronomy 14:20

They could still eat any bird the law called clean.

## Deuteronomy 14:21

Israel must never eat an animal they found dead.

They could give it to a foreigner living in their town to eat.

Or they could sell it to a foreigner.

Israel was a holy people belonging to the LORD.

They must never cook a young goat in its mother’s milk.

## Set aside a tenth each year

## Deuteronomy 14:22

Each year Israel must set aside one tenth of everything their fields produced.

![Ten equal boxes. One box has a check mark.](../pictures/one-tenth.svg)

Count the ten boxes. The marked box shows one tenth.

This share is called a tithe.

**Pause and answer**

How much of the harvest did each family set aside?

## Deuteronomy 14:23

Families must eat their share set aside for the LORD at the place he chose for his name.

This included a tenth of their grain, new wine and oil.

They also brought the firstborn animals from their herds and flocks.

Eating there taught them always to honor the LORD deeply.

## When the journey is too far

## Deuteronomy 14:24

The LORD might bless a family with a large harvest.

His chosen place might be too far away to carry their share there.

## Deuteronomy 14:25

Then the family could sell that share.

They could carry the money to the place the LORD chose.

**Pause and answer**

What could a family carry on a long journey instead of the food?

## Deuteronomy 14:26

There they could buy whatever they wanted for the feast.

They could buy cattle, sheep, wine or strong drink.

The whole household could eat and celebrate together before the LORD.

## Food for people in each town

## Deuteronomy 14:27

Moses told Israel to care for the Levites living in their towns.

The Levites had no share of land like the other tribes.

## Deuteronomy 14:28

At the end of every third year, the people must bring a tenth of that year’s harvest.

They must store it in their own towns.

## Deuteronomy 14:29

The Levites could come for food because they had no share of land.

Foreigners, children without fathers and widows in town could come too.

Everyone could eat until they had enough.

The LORD would bless the people in all their work.

**Reading help**

A widow is a woman whose husband has died.
