# Deuteronomy 17 — Witnesses, judges and the king

**About this chapter**

Moses explains how Israel must judge a serious charge. Even the king must learn and obey the law.

## What Israel could offer

## Deuteronomy 17:1

Moses told Israel to check any ox or sheep they offered to the LORD.

They must never offer an animal with a defect.

The LORD hated such an offering.

## Checking a report

## Deuteronomy 17:2

Moses described something that might happen in an Israelite town.

A man or woman might break the covenant with the LORD.

That person would be doing wrong in the LORD’s sight.

**Reading help**

A covenant is a solemn agreement.

## Deuteronomy 17:3

The person might bow down and worship other gods.

Or the person might worship the sun, moon or stars.

The LORD had forbidden Israel to worship them.

## Deuteronomy 17:4

Someone might report that this had happened.

The people must check the report carefully to learn whether it was true.

## Deuteronomy 17:5

If the report was true, the law set the same penalty for a man or a woman.

The penalty was death by stoning at the town gates.

## Why witnesses mattered

## Deuteronomy 17:6

Two or three witnesses had to give evidence.

One witness alone could never be enough for a death sentence.

**Reading help**

A witness tells what they know about what happened.

**Pause and answer**

Could one witness alone decide a death sentence?

## Deuteronomy 17:7

The law made the witnesses act first in carrying out the sentence.

Then the rest of the people took part.

Moses said this was how Israel must remove the evil from among them.

## When a case was too hard

## Deuteronomy 17:8

Some cases were too hard for the people in a town to decide.

A case might involve a killing, a lawsuit or an attack.

They must take it to the place the LORD would choose.

**Reading help**

A lawsuit is a disagreement people ask a court to settle.

## Deuteronomy 17:9

There they must ask the priests from Levi’s tribe and the judge then in office.

The priests and judge would hear the case and give their decision.

**Pause and answer**

Who would hear a case that was too hard for a town?

## Deuteronomy 17:10

The people must accept the decision given at that place.

They must do everything the priests and judge told them.

## Deuteronomy 17:11

The priests and judge would explain the law and announce their decision.

The people must follow that decision exactly.

## When someone refused the decision

## Deuteronomy 17:12

Someone might proudly refuse to obey the priest serving the LORD or the judge.

Under this law, the penalty for that refusal was death.

Moses said this would remove the evil from Israel.

## Deuteronomy 17:13

Others would hear what happened and be afraid.

They would stop acting as if they were above the law.

## Choosing a king

## Deuteronomy 17:14

Israel would settle in the land the LORD was giving them.

Then they might want a king like the nations around them.

## Deuteronomy 17:15

They must appoint the king the LORD chose.

He must be a fellow Israelite.

They could never appoint someone from another nation.

## Deuteronomy 17:16

The king must never gather many horses for himself.

He must never send people back to Egypt for more horses.

The LORD had told Israel never to go back that way.

**Pause and answer**

Where must the king never send people for horses?

## Deuteronomy 17:17

The king must never take many wives.

They might draw his heart away from the LORD.

He must never store up great riches in silver and gold for himself.

## The king must read the law

## Deuteronomy 17:18

When the king began to rule, he must write his own copy of the law on a scroll.

Priests from Levi’s tribe must be there while he wrote.

![An open scroll with lines of writing](../pictures/scroll.svg)

A scroll is a long sheet that rolls up. The king would write the law on it.

## Deuteronomy 17:19

The king must keep the scroll with him.

He must read it throughout his life.

By carefully obeying its words, he would learn to honor the LORD deeply.

**Pause and answer**

How long must the king keep reading the law?

## Deuteronomy 17:20

Then the king would remember that he was no better than other Israelites.

He would keep following the LORD’s commands.

That way he and his sons could rule Israel for many years.
