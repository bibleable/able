# Deuteronomy 25 — Fair measures and a brother’s duty

**About this chapter**

Moses explains a duty to a brother’s widow. He also tells Israel to limit punishment and to give people the full amount when trading.

## A limit on punishment

## Deuteronomy 25:1

Moses told Israel how to settle a dispute in court.

The judges must clear the innocent person of the charge.

They must declare the guilty person guilty.

## Deuteronomy 25:2

Sometimes the law called for a beating.

The judge had the guilty man lie down.

The judge watched to make sure the punishment matched the offense.

## Deuteronomy 25:3

The law allowed no more than 40 lashes.

More blows would shame a fellow Israelite in everyone’s eyes.

**Pause and answer**

What limit did the law set on a beating?

## An ox working with grain

## Deuteronomy 25:4

An ox sometimes walked over cut grain to loosen the seeds.

Israel must leave its mouth free so it could eat as it worked.

![A stalk with a head of grain](../pictures/grain.svg)

Point to the head of grain. This is where the seeds grow.

## Keeping a family name

## Deuteronomy 25:5

Moses spoke about two brothers who lived together.

One brother might die without leaving a son.

His widow must marry within the family.

The surviving brother had a duty to marry her.

**Reading help**

A widow is a woman whose husband has died.

## Deuteronomy 25:6

The widow’s first son would carry on her dead husband’s name.

In this way his family name would continue in Israel.

**Pause and answer**

Whose name would the first son carry on?

## When the brother refused

## Deuteronomy 25:7

The brother might refuse to marry the widow.

She could go to the town gate and speak to the elders.

She could tell them he had refused his duty to her.

He would not carry on his brother’s name.

## Deuteronomy 25:8

The elders would call the brother in and talk with him.

He might still say that he would not marry her.

**Pause and answer**

Who spoke to the brother about his decision?

## Deuteronomy 25:9

Then the widow would take off his sandal before the elders.

She would spit in his face.

She would say this was how Israel treated a man who refused to carry on his brother’s family line.

## Deuteronomy 25:10

People in Israel would call his family “the family of the man whose sandal was taken off.”

## A law about a fight

## Deuteronomy 25:11

Moses described a fight between two men.

One man’s wife came to protect her husband.

She grabbed the other man by his genitals.

## Deuteronomy 25:12

Under this law, the penalty was the loss of her hand.

The law allowed no mercy in this case.

## Weigh and measure honestly

## Deuteronomy 25:13

Traders must never carry two weights that stand for the same amount if one is heavier.

## Deuteronomy 25:14

They must never keep two measures that stand for the same amount if one holds more.

**Reading help**

A measure could be a container for grain. A smaller container would give the buyer less grain.

## Deuteronomy 25:15

They must weigh and measure goods honestly.

Then they could live long in the land the LORD was giving them.

## Deuteronomy 25:16

The LORD hated it when someone cheated others in this way.

## Remember the journey from Egypt

## Deuteronomy 25:17

Moses told Israel to remember the Amalekites.

Israel had met them on the journey from Egypt.

## Deuteronomy 25:18

The Israelites were tired from traveling.

The Amalekites attacked the people who had fallen behind.

The Amalekites had no fear of God.

**Pause and answer**

Which travelers did the Amalekites attack?

## Deuteronomy 25:19

The LORD would give Israel land and rest from nearby enemies.

Then Israel was to wipe out the memory of Amalek everywhere.

Moses told them never to forget this command.
