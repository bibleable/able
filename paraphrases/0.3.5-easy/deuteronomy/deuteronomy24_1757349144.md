# Deuteronomy 24 — A cloak for the night and food from the harvest

**About this chapter**

Moses gives laws about families, lenders and workers. He tells farmers who may gather the food left after harvest.

## When a second marriage ends

## Deuteronomy 24:1

Moses described a man who found something shameful in his wife and wanted a divorce.

He could write a paper saying the marriage had ended.

He would give her the paper and send her away from his home.

## Deuteronomy 24:2

After she left, the woman might marry another man.

## Deuteronomy 24:3

Her second husband might come to hate her.

He might give her a divorce paper and send her away.

Or the second husband might die.

## Deuteronomy 24:4

After that second marriage, her first husband could never marry her again.

Under this law, she was unclean for marriage to him.

The LORD hated such a remarriage.

Israel must never bring this sin on the land the LORD was giving them.

## A year at home and rules that protect people

## Deuteronomy 24:5

A newly married man could stay home for one year.

No one could send him to war or make him serve on other duties.

He was free to bring joy to his wife.

**Pause and answer**

How long could a newly married man stay home?

## Deuteronomy 24:6

A family used two heavy stones to grind grain for food.

A lender must never take those stones to secure a loan, even the top stone alone.

The family needed them to live.

**Reading help**

To secure a loan means to give the lender something to hold until you repay what you owe.

## Deuteronomy 24:7

A kidnapper might take a fellow Israelite and enslave or sell that person.

Under Israel’s law, the penalty for that crime was death.

Moses said Israel must remove this evil.

## Listen to the priests

## Deuteronomy 24:8

If someone had a spreading skin disease, Israel must follow the priests’ instructions carefully.

The priests from Levi’s tribe had received those instructions from Moses.

## Deuteronomy 24:9

Moses told Israel to remember what the LORD did to Miriam on their journey out of Egypt.

## Wait outside the borrower’s house

## Deuteronomy 24:10

A lender might ask a neighbor for something to hold until a loan was repaid.

The lender must never go into the neighbor’s house to take it.

## Deuteronomy 24:11

The lender must wait outside.

The neighbor who borrowed must bring the item out.

**Pause and answer**

Who brought the item out of the house?

## Deuteronomy 24:12

If a poor neighbor gave a cloak, the lender must never keep it overnight.

## Deuteronomy 24:13

The lender must give the cloak back by sunset.

Then the neighbor could sleep in it and bless the lender.

The LORD would count the lender’s act as right.

![A cloak](../pictures/cloak.svg)

A cloak is an outer garment. At night it could keep someone warm.

## Pay workers before sunset

## Deuteronomy 24:14

Employers must treat poor workers fairly.

This included Israelites and people from other lands living in their towns.

## Deuteronomy 24:15

Employers must pay these workers each day before sunset.

Poor workers depended on that day’s pay.

An unpaid worker might cry to the LORD for help.

The employer would be guilty of sin.

**Pause and answer**

Why did a poor worker need that day’s pay?

## Give each person justice

## Deuteronomy 24:16

A father must never face death for his child’s crime.

A child must never face death for the father’s crime.

A death sentence could apply only to the person who did the wrong.

## Deuteronomy 24:17

Israel must treat foreigners and children without fathers fairly in court.

A lender must never take a widow’s cloak to secure a loan.

**Reading help**

A widow is a woman whose husband has died.

## Deuteronomy 24:18

The Israelites had once been slaves in Egypt.

The LORD their God had freed them.

That was why Moses gave them this command.

## Leave some of the harvest

## Deuteronomy 24:19

A farmer might forget a bundle of grain in the field.

The farmer must leave it for foreigners, children without fathers and widows.

Then the LORD would bless the farmer’s work.

## Deuteronomy 24:20

Farmers beat the branches to gather olives.

They must leave behind the olives that stayed on the tree.

Foreigners, children without fathers and widows could take them.

**Pause and answer**

Who could gather the olives left on the tree?

## Deuteronomy 24:21

Farmers must also leave the grapes that remained after picking.

They must never go back over the vines.

Those grapes were for foreigners, children without fathers and widows.

## Deuteronomy 24:22

Moses told the Israelites to remember their own slavery in Egypt.

That was why he told them to leave food for others.
