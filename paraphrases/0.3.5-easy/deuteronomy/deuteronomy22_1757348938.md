# Deuteronomy 22 — Helping neighbors and hearing marriage cases

**About this chapter**

Moses gives Israel laws about helping neighbors and preventing harm. He also describes how Israel dealt with accusations in marriage and with sexual violence.

## Return what a neighbor has lost

## Deuteronomy 22:1

Moses told Israel to help when a neighbor’s animal wandered away.

Anyone who found the ox or sheep must take it back.

## Deuteronomy 22:2

The owner might live far away or be unknown.

The finder must take the animal home and care for it.

When the owner came looking, the finder must return it.

**Pause and answer**

What must the finder do when the owner comes looking?

## Deuteronomy 22:3

The same rule covered a lost donkey or cloak.

It covered anything else a neighbor had lost.

Anyone who found it must help return it.

![A cloak](../pictures/cloak.svg)

A lost cloak was one thing a finder must return.

## Deuteronomy 22:4

A donkey or ox might fall on the road.

Anyone who saw it must help the owner lift it up.

## Clothing, a bird’s nest and a safe roof

## Deuteronomy 22:5

Moses told Israel that women must not wear men’s clothes and men must not wear women’s clothes.

He said the LORD hated that practice.

## Deuteronomy 22:6

Someone might find a nest beside the road, in a tree or on the ground.

A mother bird might be sitting on her chicks or eggs.

The finder must never take the mother along with the young.

## Deuteronomy 22:7

The finder could take the young but must let the mother go.

Moses said this so the people could live long and do well.

## Deuteronomy 22:8

Anyone building a new house must put a railing around the roof.

The railing would keep someone from falling.

The household must take care to prevent a death.

**Pause and answer**

Why did a new roof need a railing?

## Seeds, animals and cloth

## Deuteronomy 22:9

Moses forbade planting two kinds of seed in a vineyard.

That would make the whole harvest unclean, both the crop and the grapes.

## Deuteronomy 22:10

Israel must never tie an ox and a donkey together to pull a plow.

## Deuteronomy 22:11

The people must never wear cloth woven from wool and linen together.

## Deuteronomy 22:12

They must put tassels on all four corners of their cloaks.

**Reading help**

A cloak is an outer garment.

A tassel is a bunch of threads tied together at one end.

**Pause and answer**

Where did the tassels go?

## A husband accuses his wife

## Deuteronomy 22:13

Moses described a man who married a woman and had sex with her.

Later the husband came to hate his wife.

## Deuteronomy 22:14

He accused her of having had sex before their marriage.

His accusation damaged her good name.

## Deuteronomy 22:15

Under this law, her parents would go to the town gate.

They would bring the elders the evidence that their daughter had been a virgin.

**Reading help**

A virgin is someone who has never had sex.

Elders were leaders of a town.

## Deuteronomy 22:16

Her father would remind the elders that he had given his daughter in marriage.

Now her husband hated her.

## Deuteronomy 22:17

The father would repeat the husband’s accusation.

The parents would spread a cloth before the elders.

They would present it as evidence against the accusation.

## How the elders decided the case

## Deuteronomy 22:18

The elders would then punish the husband for his false accusation.

**Pause and answer**

Who faced punishment for a false accusation?

## Deuteronomy 22:19

He had damaged an Israelite woman’s good name.

The elders would fine him 100 shekels of silver for her father.

She would remain his wife.

He could never divorce her.

**Reading help**

A shekel was a measure of weight.

## Deuteronomy 22:20

Moses also described a case where the accusation was true.

In that case, no one could present the evidence described above.

## Deuteronomy 22:21

Under this law, the town’s men would put her to death by stoning at her father’s door.

Moses called her sexual conduct while living in her father’s house a grave wrong.

He said Israel must remove this evil.

## Marriage and engagement

## Deuteronomy 22:22

If a man and another man’s wife were found having sex, the law called for both to die.

Moses said Israel must remove this evil.

## Deuteronomy 22:23

Moses described a young woman who had never had sex and was promised in marriage.

Another man met her in town and had sex with her.

## Deuteronomy 22:24

In the case Moses described, the penalty for both was death by stoning at the town gate.

The law cited her failure to cry out in town.

It held the man guilty of violating his neighbor’s wife.

Moses said Israel must remove this evil.

## An attack in the open country

## Deuteronomy 22:25

Moses then described a man who met an engaged woman in the open country and raped her.

Under the law, only the man faced death.

**Reading help**

Rape means forcing someone to have sex.

## Deuteronomy 22:26

The people must leave the woman unharmed.

She had done nothing to deserve death.

Moses compared the attack to a murder.

## Deuteronomy 22:27

The woman had cried out in the field.

No one was there to rescue her.

**Pause and answer**

Why did no one come when the woman called?

## Other marriage laws

## Deuteronomy 22:28

Moses also described a man who seized a young woman and had sex with her.

She had never had sex and was not engaged.

Someone discovered what had happened.

## Deuteronomy 22:29

Under this law, the man had to pay her father 50 shekels of silver.

The law required the man to marry her because he had violated her.

He could never divorce her.

## Deuteronomy 22:30

A man must never marry his father’s wife.

Doing so would dishonor his father’s marriage.
