# 1 Kings 8 — Solomon prays at the temple

**About this chapter**

The priests bring the ark into the temple. Solomon prays for people who will turn toward this place and ask God for help.

## The priests bring the ark

## 1 Kings 8:1

Solomon called Israel’s elders to Jerusalem.

They led the tribes and families of Israel.

They came to bring the LORD’s sacred chest, the ark of the covenant, up from Zion.

Zion was the City of David.

## 1 Kings 8:2

All the men of Israel gathered for the feast with Solomon.

It was Ethanim, the seventh month.

## 1 Kings 8:3

When all the elders arrived, the priests lifted the ark.

## 1 Kings 8:4

The priests and Levites brought up the LORD’s ark and the Tent of Meeting.

They also carried all the sacred things from the tent.

## 1 Kings 8:5

Solomon and the people gathered before the ark.

They offered more sheep and oxen than anyone could count.

## The ark rests in the Most Holy Place

## 1 Kings 8:6

The priests carried the ark into the temple’s inner room, the Most Holy Place.

They set it beneath the wings of the cherubim.

## 1 Kings 8:7

The wings of these figures spread over the ark.

They also covered the poles used to carry it.

## 1 Kings 8:8

The poles were so long that their ends showed in the Holy Place outside the inner room.

No one could see them from outside the Holy Place.

They were still there when this account was written.

## 1 Kings 8:9

Only two flat stones lay inside the ark.

Moses had put them there at Horeb.

There the LORD made a covenant with Israel after bringing them out of Egypt.

![The main hall leads to the Most Holy Place at the back of the temple.](../pictures/solomons-temple-rooms.svg)

**Reading help**

A covenant is a solemn agreement. The words on the two stones reminded Israel of God’s agreement with them.

## A cloud fills the temple

## 1 Kings 8:10

When the priests left the Holy Place, a cloud filled the temple.

## 1 Kings 8:11

The cloud kept the priests from standing there to serve.

The glory of the LORD filled his temple.

## 1 Kings 8:12

Solomon said, “The LORD said he would live in a thick cloud.

## 1 Kings 8:13

I have built you a great house.

May it be your home forever.”

## Solomon speaks to the people

## 1 Kings 8:14

All the people stood there together.

Solomon turned to face them and blessed them.

## 1 Kings 8:15

He said, “Praise the LORD, the God of Israel!

He has done what he promised my father David.

## 1 Kings 8:16

The LORD said, ‘I brought my people out of Egypt.

Since then I have chosen no city in Israel for a house to honor my name.

But I chose David to rule my people Israel.’

## The LORD keeps his promise to David

## 1 Kings 8:17

My father David wanted to build a house to honor the LORD, Israel’s God.

## 1 Kings 8:18

The LORD told my father, ‘It was good that you wanted to build a house for my name.

## 1 Kings 8:19

Your own son will build it in your place.’

## 1 Kings 8:20

Now the LORD has kept his word.

I sit on my father David’s throne, as the LORD promised.

I have built this house to honor the LORD, Israel’s God.

## 1 Kings 8:21

Here I have made a place for the ark.

It holds the record of the LORD’s covenant with our ancestors when he brought them out of Egypt.”

## Solomon begins his prayer

## 1 Kings 8:22

Solomon stood before the LORD’s altar in front of all Israel.

He stretched out his hands toward heaven.

## 1 Kings 8:23

He prayed, “LORD, God of Israel, no god is like you in heaven or on earth.

You keep your covenant of faithful love with those who serve you wholeheartedly.

## 1 Kings 8:24

You kept your promise to my father David, your servant.

Today we see that you have done what you said.

## 1 Kings 8:25

LORD, God of Israel, keep your other promise to David.

You said, ‘If your descendants live faithfully before me as you have, someone from your family will always rule Israel.’

## 1 Kings 8:26

God of Israel, please make that promise to my father come true.

## Hear us from heaven

## 1 Kings 8:27

But can God really live on earth?

Even the highest heavens cannot hold you.

How could this temple I built hold you?

## 1 Kings 8:28

Yet please listen to me, LORD my God.

Hear your servant’s prayer as I cry to you today.

## 1 Kings 8:29

Watch over this temple night and day.

You said, ‘My name will be there.’

Listen when I pray toward this place.

## 1 Kings 8:30

Listen when I pray and when your people Israel pray toward this place.

Hear us from heaven, your home.

Hear us and forgive us.

## When someone needs a fair judgment

## 1 Kings 8:31

Someone may wrong a neighbor and have to make a solemn promise to tell the truth.

That person may come before your altar to make that promise.

## 1 Kings 8:32

Then hear from heaven and act as judge.

Hold the guilty person responsible for the wrong.

Reward the innocent person for doing right.

## When Israel loses a battle

## 1 Kings 8:33

Your people may sin against you and lose a battle.

Then they may turn back to you and honor your name.

They may pray for help in this temple.

## 1 Kings 8:34

Then hear from heaven and forgive your people Israel.

Bring them back to the land you gave their ancestors.

## When the rain stops

## 1 Kings 8:35

You may stop the rain because your people have sinned.

In their suffering they may turn away from their sins.

They may honor your name and pray toward this place.

## 1 Kings 8:36

Then hear from heaven and forgive your people.

Teach them the good way to live.

Send rain on the land you gave them.

## When trouble comes to the land

## 1 Kings 8:37

There may be too little food or a deadly sickness.

Disease or swarms of insects may ruin the crops.

Enemies may surround the cities.

Any kind of sickness or disaster may come.

## 1 Kings 8:38

Each person knows what troubles them.

They may stretch out their hands toward this temple and ask for help.

## 1 Kings 8:39

Then hear from heaven, your home.

Forgive them and act.

Treat each person according to what they have done.

You alone know every human heart.

## 1 Kings 8:40

Then they will honor you all their lives in the land you gave our ancestors.

## When someone comes from another nation

## 1 Kings 8:41

Someone from another nation may come from far away because of your name.

## 1 Kings 8:42

People will hear how great you are.

They will hear of the mighty things you have done.

A visitor may come and pray toward this temple.

## 1 Kings 8:43

Then hear from heaven and do what the visitor asks.

May all peoples know and honor you as Israel does.

May they know that this house bears your name.

## When Israel goes to battle

## 1 Kings 8:44

You may send your people to fight their enemies.

They may turn toward your chosen city and this temple I built for your name.

They may pray to you, LORD, facing that way.

## 1 Kings 8:45

Then hear their plea from heaven.

Stand up for them.

## When enemies take the people away

## 1 Kings 8:46

Everyone sins.

When your people sin, you may become angry with them.

You may let an enemy take them to another land, near or far.

## 1 Kings 8:47

There they may realize what they have done and turn back to you.

They may plead, ‘We have sinned.

We did wrong and acted wickedly.’

## 1 Kings 8:48

In their enemy’s land they may return to you with their whole heart and soul.

They may pray facing the land you gave their ancestors.

They may face your chosen city and this temple I built for you.

## 1 Kings 8:49

Then hear their plea from heaven, your home.

Stand up for them.

## 1 Kings 8:50

Forgive your people for all their sins against you.

Move those who hold them captive to show them kindness.

## Solomon asks God to remember his people

## 1 Kings 8:51

These are your own people.

You brought them out of Egypt, out of the furnace for iron.

**Reading help**

A furnace is a place where a fire burns hot enough to work with metal. Solomon compares slavery in Egypt to that terrible heat.

## 1 Kings 8:52

Watch over me and your people Israel when we ask for help.

Listen whenever your people call to you.

## 1 Kings 8:53

Lord GOD, you chose them from all the peoples on earth to be your own.

You said so through Moses when you brought our ancestors out of Egypt.”

## Solomon blesses the people

## 1 Kings 8:54

Solomon finished his prayer to the LORD.

He had been kneeling before the altar with his hands stretched toward heaven.

Now he stood up.

## 1 Kings 8:55

Solomon blessed all the people of Israel in a loud voice.

## 1 Kings 8:56

“Praise the LORD! He promised rest to his people and gave them rest.

He kept every good promise he made through his servant Moses.

## 1 Kings 8:57

May the LORD our God be with us as he was with our ancestors.

May he never leave us.

## 1 Kings 8:58

May he help us want to follow him.

May we live his way and obey all the laws he gave our ancestors.

## A prayer for each day

## 1 Kings 8:59

May the LORD remember my prayer day and night.

May he stand up for me and for his people Israel as each day’s needs arise.

## 1 Kings 8:60

Then all peoples on earth will know the LORD is God.

He alone is God.

## 1 Kings 8:61

Give your whole heart to the LORD our God, as you do today.

Follow his laws and keep his commands.”

## The people celebrate together

## 1 Kings 8:62

Solomon and all the people offered sacrifices to the LORD.

## 1 Kings 8:63

Solomon gave 22,000 oxen and 120,000 sheep as peace offerings.

In this way Solomon and Israel dedicated the temple to the LORD.

**Reading help**

To dedicate the temple means to set it apart for the worship of God.

## 1 Kings 8:64

The bronze altar was too small for all the offerings.

That day Solomon set apart the middle of the courtyard in front of the temple.

There he gave burnt offerings, grain offerings, and the fat from the peace offerings.

## 1 Kings 8:65

People came from Lebo-hamath to the Brook of Egypt.

Solomon and this great crowd celebrated before the LORD.

They celebrated seven days, then seven more, making fourteen days in all.

## 1 Kings 8:66

On the fifteenth day Solomon sent the people home.

They blessed the king and went home happy.

They rejoiced over all the good the LORD had done for David and for Israel.
