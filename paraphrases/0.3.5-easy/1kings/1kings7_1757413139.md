# 1 Kings 7 — Solomon’s palace and the furnishings for the temple

**About this chapter**

Solomon builds halls for his work and a home for his family. A bronze worker named Huram makes pillars, basins, and other things for the temple.

## Solomon builds his palace

## 1 Kings 7:1

Solomon took 13 years to finish his whole palace.

## 1 Kings 7:2

One building was called the House of the Forest of Lebanon.

It was 100 cubits long, 50 wide, and 30 high.

Four rows of cedar pillars held up cedar beams.

**Reading help**

A cubit was about the distance from an elbow to a fingertip. A pillar stands upright. A beam rests across the tops of pillars.

## 1 Kings 7:3

Above the pillars, 45 beams supported a cedar roof.

There were 15 beams in each row.

## 1 Kings 7:4

High windows faced each other on three levels.

## 1 Kings 7:5

Each doorway had a frame shaped like a rectangle.

The openings faced each other on three levels.

## Halls for the king

## 1 Kings 7:6

Solomon built a hall with rows of pillars.

It was 50 cubits long and 30 cubits wide.

A porch stood in front of it.

Beyond the porch, pillars held up a covering.

## 1 Kings 7:7

Solomon also built the Hall of Justice for his throne.

There he would listen to cases and judge them.

Cedar boards covered the walls from floor to ceiling.

## 1 Kings 7:8

Solomon’s own home stood farther back.

The builders made it in much the same way.

Solomon built a similar palace for his wife, the daughter of Pharaoh.

## Stone and cedar for the buildings

## 1 Kings 7:9

The builders used costly stones for all these buildings.

They cut each stone to size, sawing both sides smooth.

They worked this way from the base to the roof edges.

They used such stones all the way to the great courtyard.

## 1 Kings 7:10

The builders laid large, costly stones at the base.

Some were 10 cubits long. Others were eight cubits long.

## 1 Kings 7:11

Above these they placed costly stones cut to size and cedar beams.

## 1 Kings 7:12

Around the great courtyard they laid three rows of cut stone and one row of cedar beams.

The temple’s inner courtyard and porch followed the same pattern.

## Huram makes two bronze pillars

## 1 Kings 7:13

King Solomon sent for a man from Tyre named Huram.

## 1 Kings 7:14

Huram’s mother was a widow from the tribe of Naphtali.

His father had worked with bronze in Tyre.

Huram understood how to make all kinds of bronze objects.

He came to Solomon and did the work the king gave him.

**Reading help**

Huram is the bronze worker. In chapter 5, Hiram is the king of Tyre who sent wood.

## 1 Kings 7:15

Huram made two bronze pillars by pouring hot metal into molds.

Each pillar was 18 cubits high and 12 cubits around.

## 1 Kings 7:16

Huram made a bronze top for each pillar.

Each top, called a capital, was five cubits high.

## 1 Kings 7:17

Huram decorated each capital with a net of bronze.

Each net had seven wreaths made of chains.

## Flowers and fruit on the pillars

## 1 Kings 7:18

He added two rows of bronze pomegranates around each net.

They covered the capitals on top of the pillars.

## 1 Kings 7:19

The capitals on the porch had tops shaped like lily flowers.

The lily shapes were four cubits high.

## 1 Kings 7:20

Around each capital were rows of 200 pomegranates.

They sat above the rounded part beside the bronze net.

## 1 Kings 7:21

Huram set up the pillars at the temple porch.

He named the south pillar Jachin and the north pillar Boaz.

## 1 Kings 7:22

The tops of the pillars looked like lilies.

The pillars were now finished.

## A great basin called the Sea

## 1 Kings 7:23

Huram also made a large round metal basin called the Sea.

It was 10 cubits across and five cubits high.

It measured 30 cubits around.

## 1 Kings 7:24

Two rows of flower buds circled the Sea below its rim.

There were ten buds in every cubit.

Huram shaped the buds and basin as one piece.

## 1 Kings 7:25

The Sea rested on the backs of twelve bronze oxen.

Three faced north, three west, three south, and three east.

All faced away from the center.

## 1 Kings 7:26

The metal was as thick as the width of a hand.

The rim curved like the edge of a cup or an open lily.

The Sea held 2,000 baths of water.

**Reading help**

Here a bath is a measure of liquid. The Sea was the name of a very large water basin.

## Ten stands for smaller basins

## 1 Kings 7:27

Huram made ten bronze stands that could move.

Each was four cubits long, four wide, and three high.

## 1 Kings 7:28

Each stand had side panels fixed between upright posts.

## 1 Kings 7:29

Lions, oxen, and cherubim decorated the panels.

The posts held a base at the top.

Below the lions and oxen were wreaths with sloping edges.

**Reading help**

Cherubim are heavenly beings. These designs showed them alongside animals.

## 1 Kings 7:30

Each stand had four bronze wheels on bronze axles.

Four supports held a basin, with wreaths at each side.

## 1 Kings 7:31

At the top was a round opening to hold the basin.

The opening was one cubit deep and one and a half cubits wide.

Carved designs surrounded the opening.

The side panels were square.

## Wheels and handles on the stands

## 1 Kings 7:32

The four wheels sat beneath the panels.

Their axles joined them to the stand.

Each wheel measured one and a half cubits across.

## 1 Kings 7:33

The wheels looked like chariot wheels.

Huram cast their axles, rims, spokes, and centers from metal.

## 1 Kings 7:34

A handle stuck out from each of the four corners.

## 1 Kings 7:35

A round band half a cubit high went around the top.

The supports, panels, and top formed one piece of bronze.

## 1 Kings 7:36

Huram carved cherubim, lions, and palm trees wherever there was room on the supports and panels.

Wreaths went around the designs.

## 1 Kings 7:37

Huram cast all ten stands the same way.

They had the same size and shape.

## Huram sets the basins in place

## 1 Kings 7:38

He made ten bronze basins, one for each stand.

Each basin measured four cubits across and held 40 baths of water.

## 1 Kings 7:39

Huram put five stands on the temple’s south side and five on its north side.

He placed the Sea on the south side, at the southeast corner.

## 1 Kings 7:40

Huram also made pots, shovels, and bowls for sprinkling.

He finished the work King Solomon gave him for the LORD’s temple.

## The finished bronze work

## 1 Kings 7:41

Huram made two pillars, each with a bowl-shaped capital.

A bronze net covered each capital.

## 1 Kings 7:42

He made 400 pomegranates for the two bronze nets.

Two rows circled each bowl-shaped capital.

## 1 Kings 7:43

He made ten stands and ten basins to go on them.

## 1 Kings 7:44

He made the Sea and the twelve oxen beneath it.

## 1 Kings 7:45

He also made the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls.

All these things Huram made for Solomon’s temple shone with polished bronze.

## Workers cast the bronze in clay molds

## 1 Kings 7:46

Solomon had workers cast these objects in clay molds.

They worked on the Jordan plain, between Succoth and Zarethan.

## 1 Kings 7:47

There were so many objects that Solomon left them unweighed.

No one knew the total weight of the bronze.

## Gold furnishings inside the temple

## 1 Kings 7:48

Solomon also provided furnishings for the LORD’s temple.

He made a gold altar and a gold table.

The Bread of the Presence rested on the table.

**Reading help**

The Bread of the Presence was bread set before the LORD in worship.

## 1 Kings 7:49

Pure gold lampstands stood in front of the inner room.

Five stood on the right and five on the left.

The flowers, lamps, and tongs were gold too.

## 1 Kings 7:50

Workers used pure gold for basins, lamp trimmers, sprinkling bowls, ladles, and incense holders.

They made gold hinges for the doors to the Most Holy Place and the main hall.

## 1 Kings 7:51

Solomon finished all his work on the LORD’s temple.

His father David had set apart silver, gold, and furnishings for the LORD.

Solomon brought them into the temple storerooms.
