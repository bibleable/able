# 2 Chronicles 4 — Basins, lamps, and tables for the temple

**About this chapter**

Solomon equips the temple for worship.

The priests need water for washing and lamps for light.

A skilled worker named Huram makes many things from bronze.

## The altar and the great water basin

## 2 Chronicles 4:1

Solomon made a bronze altar, a place to offer gifts to God.

It was 20 cubits long and 20 cubits wide.

It stood 10 cubits high.

A cubit was about the length from elbow to fingertip.

## 2 Chronicles 4:2

Solomon also made a huge round metal basin called the Sea.

It measured 10 cubits from one edge straight across to the other.

It stood 5 cubits high.

The distance around it was 30 cubits.

## 2 Chronicles 4:3

Two rows of small ox figures went around the basin below its rim.

There were ten figures to a cubit.

The figures and the basin formed one piece of metal.

## 2 Chronicles 4:4

Twelve large figures of oxen supported the Sea.

Three faced north and three faced west.

Three faced south and three faced east.

Their rear ends pointed toward the center beneath the basin.

## 2 Chronicles 4:5

The metal was as thick as the width of a hand.

The rim curved like the lip of a cup.

It looked like an open lily flower.

The basin could hold 3,000 baths of water.

A bath was a measure for liquids.

## 2 Chronicles 4:6

Solomon made ten smaller basins for washing.

He put five on the south side and five on the north.

The priests used these to rinse the parts of the burnt offerings.

They used the large Sea to wash themselves.

## Lamps, tables, and courtyards

## 2 Chronicles 4:7

Solomon followed the plans to make ten gold lampstands.

Inside the temple he put five on each side, south and north.

## 2 Chronicles 4:8

Solomon made ten tables for the temple too.

He placed five on the south side and five on the north.

He also made a hundred gold bowls.

## 2 Chronicles 4:9

Solomon made a courtyard for the priests and a larger courtyard.

He covered the doors of the large courtyard with bronze.

## 2 Chronicles 4:10

Solomon placed the Sea on the south side, at the southeast corner.

## Huram finishes the bronze work

## 2 Chronicles 4:11

Huram made pots, shovels, and bowls for sprinkling.

He finished the work King Solomon gave him for God’s temple.

Here are the things he made.

## 2 Chronicles 4:12

Huram made two pillars.

Each had a bowl-shaped top.

Over each top he fitted a net made of metal.

## 2 Chronicles 4:13

He made 400 decorations shaped like pomegranates, a kind of fruit.

He arranged them in two rows on each metal net.

## 2 Chronicles 4:14

Huram made the stands and the basins that rested on them.

## 2 Chronicles 4:15

He made the great Sea and the twelve oxen that held it up.

## 2 Chronicles 4:16

He also made pots, shovels, meat forks, and other tools.

Huram, also called Huram-abi, made all these things for King Solomon.

He used polished bronze for these things in the LORD’s temple.

## 2 Chronicles 4:17

The king had workers shape the bronze in clay molds.

They poured melted metal into the molds.

They worked on the Jordan plain between Succoth and Zeredah.

## 2 Chronicles 4:18

Solomon had so many things made from bronze that no one could tell its total weight.

## Gold furnishings inside the temple

## 2 Chronicles 4:19

Solomon also made the furnishings inside God’s temple.

These included the gold altar and the tables for the Bread of the Presence.

This was bread the priests set before the LORD.

## 2 Chronicles 4:20

Solomon made the lampstands and their lamps from pure gold.

As the instructions required, the lamps burned in front of the inner holy room.

## 2 Chronicles 4:21

The flower decorations, lamps, and tongs were pure gold.

Tongs are a tool for gripping things.

## 2 Chronicles 4:22

The tools for trimming lamp wicks were pure gold too.

So were the sprinkling bowls, scoops, and incense pans.

Incense gives off a sweet smell as it burns.

The doors to the Most Holy Place and the main hall were gold.
