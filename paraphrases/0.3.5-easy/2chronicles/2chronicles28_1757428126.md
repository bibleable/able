# 2 Chronicles 28 — Oded asks Israel to send the captives home

**About this chapter**

During the rule of King Ahaz, soldiers from Israel take families from Judah captive. A prophet named Oded meets the soldiers on their way back. He reminds them that the people they have taken are their own relatives.

## Ahaz turns away from the LORD

## 2 Chronicles 28:1

Ahaz was twenty when he became king.

He ruled in Jerusalem for sixteen years.

His ancestor David had done what pleased the LORD.

Ahaz did not follow David’s example.

## 2 Chronicles 28:2

He copied the kings of the northern kingdom of Israel.

He even made metal statues of gods called the Baals.

## 2 Chronicles 28:3

Ahaz burned incense in the Valley of Ben-hinnom.

He offered his sons as sacrifices in the fire.

He copied the terrible practices of the nations the LORD had driven out before Israel.

## 2 Chronicles 28:4

He offered sacrifices and burned incense at hilltop places of worship and under every leafy tree.

## Two armies attack Judah

## 2 Chronicles 28:5

The LORD let the king of Aram defeat Ahaz.

Aram’s soldiers took many prisoners to Damascus.

The LORD also let the king of Israel strike Judah a terrible blow.

## 2 Chronicles 28:6

In one day, Pekah son of Remaliah killed 120,000 brave men of Judah.

Judah’s people had left the LORD, the God their ancestors worshiped.

## 2 Chronicles 28:7

Zichri, a strong warrior from Ephraim, killed three men.

One was Maaseiah, the king’s son.

Another was Azrikam, who managed the palace.

The third was Elkanah, who was second in command to the king.

## 2 Chronicles 28:8

Israel’s soldiers took 200,000 women and children from their own relatives in Judah.

They took many belongings too and brought them to Samaria.

## Oded speaks up for the captives

## 2 Chronicles 28:9

Oded, a prophet who spoke for the LORD, met the army returning to Samaria.

He said, “The LORD, the God of your ancestors, was angry with Judah.

That is why he let you defeat them.

But you killed them in such anger that your rage has reached heaven.

## 2 Chronicles 28:10

Now you plan to make the men and women of Judah and Jerusalem your slaves.

You too have done wrong before the LORD your God.

## 2 Chronicles 28:11

Listen to me!

Send your captive relatives home.

The LORD is fiercely angry with you.”

## 2 Chronicles 28:12

Four leaders from Ephraim stood up to the returning soldiers.

They were Azariah son of Jehohanan, Berechiah son of Meshillemoth, Jehizkiah son of Shallum, and Amasa son of Hadlai.

## 2 Chronicles 28:13

The leaders said, “You must not bring the captives here.

We are already guilty before the LORD.

Taking these people would add to our guilt.

The LORD is already very angry with Israel.”

## 2 Chronicles 28:14

In front of the leaders and the gathered people, the soldiers gave up the captives and the goods they had taken.

## 2 Chronicles 28:15

The four leaders cared for the captives.

From the goods the soldiers had taken, they found clothes for those who had none.

They gave them sandals, food, and drink.

They put oil on their wounds.

Anyone too weak to walk rode on a donkey.

The leaders took the people to their relatives in Jericho, the City of Palms.

Then the leaders went back to Samaria.

## Ahaz asks Assyria for help

## 2 Chronicles 28:16

At that time King Ahaz asked the king of Assyria for help.

## 2 Chronicles 28:17

Edom had attacked Judah again and taken prisoners away.

## 2 Chronicles 28:18

The Philistines attacked towns in Judah’s foothills and in the dry south.

They took Beth-shemesh, Aijalon, and Gederoth.

They also took Soco, Timnah, Gimzo, and the villages around them.

The Philistines settled in the places they took.

## 2 Chronicles 28:19

The LORD brought Judah low because of King Ahaz.

Ahaz let wrongdoing spread in Judah.

He had been deeply unfaithful to the LORD.

## 2 Chronicles 28:20

Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria, came to Ahaz.

He made life harder for Ahaz and gave him no strength.

## 2 Chronicles 28:21

Ahaz had taken valuable things from the temple, the palace, and his officials.

He gave them to the king of Assyria.

The gifts brought Ahaz no help.

## Ahaz closes the temple

## 2 Chronicles 28:22

As his troubles grew, Ahaz turned even farther from the LORD.

## 2 Chronicles 28:23

He offered sacrifices to the gods worshiped in Damascus.

He thought, “The gods of Aram helped their kings defeat me.

Perhaps they will help me if I offer them sacrifices.” Worshiping those gods brought ruin to Ahaz and all Israel.

## 2 Chronicles 28:24

Ahaz gathered the objects from God’s house and cut them apart.

He shut the doors of the LORD’s house.

He put up his own altars on every street corner in Jerusalem.

## 2 Chronicles 28:25

In every town in Judah he built places to burn incense to other gods.

This made the LORD, the God of his ancestors, angry.

## 2 Chronicles 28:26

The Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel tells the rest of the story of Ahaz.

It records how he lived from beginning to end.

## 2 Chronicles 28:27

Ahaz died.

The people buried him in Jerusalem, outside the royal tombs.

His son Hezekiah became king.
