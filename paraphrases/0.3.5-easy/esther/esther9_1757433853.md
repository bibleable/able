# Esther 9 — The Jews remember with a feast

**About this chapter**

The Jews survive the day their enemies chose. They remember their days of rest with a feast called Purim.

## The day the enemies had chosen

## Esther 9:1

The day for the king’s order came.

It was day thirteen of Adar, the twelfth month.

The Jews’ enemies had hoped to overpower them that day.

Instead, the Jews overcame those who hated them.

## Esther 9:2

Throughout King Xerxes’ provinces, the Jews gathered in their cities.

They fought those who wanted to harm them.

People everywhere feared the Jews.

No one could stand against them.

## Esther 9:3

The regional rulers, governors, officials, and royal administrators helped the Jews.

These leaders were afraid of Mordecai.

## Esther 9:4

Mordecai held great power in the palace.

As his power grew, people throughout the provinces heard about him.

## Esther 9:5

The Jews killed their enemies with swords.

They did as they wished to those who hated them.

## The fighting in Susa

## Esther 9:6

In the fortress of Susa, the Jews killed 500 men.

## Esther 9:7

Among them were Parshandatha, Dalphon, and Aspatha.

## Esther 9:8

Poratha, Adalia, and Aridatha also died.

## Esther 9:9

So did Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha.

## Esther 9:10

These ten men were the sons of Haman.

Haman, son of Hammedatha, had been the Jews’ enemy.

The Jews took none of their enemies’ belongings.

## Esther asks for another day in Susa

## Esther 9:11

That day the king heard how many had died in Susa’s fortress.

## Esther 9:12

The king told Queen Esther, “The Jews killed 500 men here, including Haman’s ten sons.

What have they done in the other provinces?

Is there anything else you want?

Tell me, and I will grant it.”

## Esther 9:13

Esther answered, “If it pleases you, let the Jews in Susa act under the same order tomorrow.

Let the bodies of Haman’s ten sons hang on the gallows.”

## Esther 9:14

The king agreed and gave the order in Susa.

The bodies of Haman’s ten sons were hung on the gallows.

## Esther 9:15

On day fourteen of Adar, the Jews in Susa gathered again.

They killed 300 men there.

They took none of their enemies’ belongings.

## Two different days of rest

## Esther 9:16

Elsewhere in the provinces, the Jews gathered to defend themselves.

They gained relief from their enemies.

They killed 75,000 people who hated them.

They took none of their enemies’ belongings.

## Esther 9:17

The Jews outside Susa fought on day thirteen of Adar.

On day fourteen they rested.

They celebrated with a joyful feast.

## Esther 9:18

In Susa the Jews had gathered on days thirteen and fourteen.

They rested on day fifteen and held a joyful feast.

## Esther 9:19

That is why Jews in villages celebrate on day fourteen of Adar.

They hold a feast and send gifts to one another.

**Pause and answer**

On what day did the Jews in Susa rest?

## Mordecai asks the people to remember

## Esther 9:20

Mordecai wrote down what had happened.

He sent letters to Jews near and far, throughout King Xerxes’ provinces.

![An open scroll with lines of writing, like Mordecai’s letters about the feast.](../pictures/scroll.svg)

## Esther 9:21

He asked them to celebrate every year.

The dates were day fourteen and day fifteen of Adar.

## Esther 9:22

These days marked their rest from their enemies.

Grief had given way to joy that month.

The days of mourning had become a holiday.

Mordecai asked them to hold joyful feasts and exchange gifts.

They were also to give gifts to poor people.

## Esther 9:23

The Jews agreed to keep celebrating as they had begun.

They would follow what Mordecai had written.

## Why the feast is called Purim

## Esther 9:24

Haman the Agagite, son of Hammedatha, was the enemy of all the Jews.

He had planned to destroy them.

He had cast a lot to choose the day by chance.

The word for this lot was Pur.

## Esther 9:25

When the king heard about the plan, he gave a written order.

The harm Haman had planned came back on Haman himself.

The king ordered Haman and his sons to hang on the gallows.

## Esther 9:26

The name Purim comes from the word Pur.

The Jews remembered what they had seen and lived through.

They also remembered what Mordecai’s letter told them to do.

## Esther 9:27

The Jews promised to celebrate these two days every year at the set time.

This promise included their children and those who joined them.

## Esther 9:28

Families in every city and province were to remember and celebrate.

Each generation would keep Purim so their children would remember too.

## Esther confirms the dates

## Esther 9:29

Queen Esther was the daughter of Abihail.

Esther and Mordecai the Jew used their full authority to confirm a second letter about Purim.

## Esther 9:30

Mordecai sent letters to the Jews in all 127 provinces under King Xerxes.

He wrote words of peace and truth.

## Esther 9:31

The letters confirmed when to keep Purim.

Mordecai and Queen Esther had set those dates.

They had also agreed on times for fasting and mourning, for themselves and their children.

Fasting means going without food for a time.

## Esther 9:32

Esther’s order confirmed these rules for Purim.

The written record kept the rules for the future.
