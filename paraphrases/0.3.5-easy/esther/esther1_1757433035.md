# Esther 1 — Queen Vashti refuses the king’s order

**About this chapter**

King Xerxes holds a feast in Susa. When Queen Vashti refuses his order, he asks his advisers what to do.

## Xerxes shows his wealth

## Esther 1:1

King Xerxes ruled 127 provinces from India to Cush.

A province was one part of his kingdom.

## Esther 1:2

King Xerxes ruled from his throne in Susa.

His palace stood inside a fortress there.

## Esther 1:3

In his third year as king, Xerxes held a feast.

He invited all his officials and servants.

Army leaders came from Persia and Media.

Nobles and princes came from the provinces too.

## Esther 1:4

For 180 days Xerxes showed people his kingdom’s riches.

He wanted them to see how great and powerful he was.

**Pause and answer**

How long did Xerxes show his riches?

## Seven days in the palace garden

## Esther 1:5

Then Xerxes held a feast for everyone in the fortress of Susa.

People of every rank came to the palace garden.

The feast lasted seven days.

## Esther 1:6

White and blue linen curtains hung from marble pillars.

White and purple cords held them to silver rings.

Gold and silver couches stood on a floor made of small pieces fitted together.

The pattern included porphyry, marble, shining mother-of-pearl from shells, and other costly stones.

## Esther 1:7

Servants brought drinks in gold cups.

Each cup had a different design.

The king gave his guests plenty of royal wine.

## Esther 1:8

The king let each guest decide how much to drink.

He told his officials to serve each man whatever he wanted.

## Esther 1:9

Queen Vashti held a feast for the women.

They gathered in King Xerxes’ palace.

**Pause and answer**

Who held a feast for the women?

## The king sends for Vashti

## Esther 1:10

On the seventh day, the wine made the king merry.

He called his seven attendants, men known as eunuchs.

Their names were Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar, and Carkas.

## Esther 1:11

Xerxes ordered them to bring Queen Vashti wearing her royal crown.

Vashti was beautiful.

The king wanted his guests and officials to see her beauty.

## Esther 1:12

The attendants took the king’s order to Queen Vashti.

Vashti refused to come.

King Xerxes grew furious.

**Pause and answer**

What did Vashti do when the attendants came?

## Xerxes asks about the law

## Esther 1:13

The king asked his wise advisers what to do.

They understood the times they lived in.

He often asked these experts about law and justice.

## Esther 1:14

His closest advisers were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan.

These seven princes of Persia and Media could see the king in person.

They held the highest positions under him.

## Esther 1:15

Xerxes asked, “My attendants took my order to Queen Vashti.

She refused to obey.

What does the law say we should do?”

## Memucan gives his advice

## Esther 1:16

Memucan answered in front of Xerxes and the princes.

“Queen Vashti has wronged the king.

She has also wronged every prince and everyone throughout your provinces.

## Esther 1:17

“All the women will hear what the queen did.

They will look down on their husbands.

They will say, ‘King Xerxes sent for Queen Vashti, and she refused to come.’

## Esther 1:18

“Today the noblewomen of Persia and Media will hear about Vashti.

They will say the same thing to the king’s officials.

People will look down on each other and grow angry.

## Esther 1:19

“If it pleases the king, let him give this order.

Have it written into the laws of Persia and Media so no one can cancel it.

Vashti must never come before King Xerxes again.

Let the king choose a better woman to be queen.

## Esther 1:20

“People throughout your great kingdom will hear the order.

Then women of every rank will honor their husbands.”

**Pause and answer**

Who suggested that Xerxes choose another queen?

## The king sends letters

## Esther 1:21

The king and his princes liked this advice.

Xerxes did what Memucan suggested.

## Esther 1:22

Xerxes sent letters to every province.

The writers used each province’s writing and each people’s language.

The king’s message said every man should rule his own household.
