# Esther 3 — Haman turns against the Jews

**About this chapter**

Haman is angry with Mordecai. He turns that anger against all the Jewish people in the kingdom.

## Mordecai refuses to bow

## Esther 3:1

King Xerxes honored Haman, son of Hammedatha the Agagite.

He gave Haman a higher place than all his princes.

## Esther 3:2

The king told his servants to bow down before Haman.

At the king’s gate, the servants bowed to show Haman honor.

Mordecai refused to bow or show Haman this honor.

## Esther 3:3

The servants at the gate asked Mordecai a question.

“Why do you disobey the king’s order?”

## Esther 3:4

Day after day, the servants warned Mordecai.

Still he refused to bow.

Mordecai had told them he was Jewish.

They told Haman to see whether he would allow this.

## Haman chooses a date

## Esther 3:5

Haman saw for himself that Mordecai would not bow.

Haman was furious.

## Esther 3:6

Then Haman learned which people Mordecai belonged to.

Hurting Mordecai alone seemed too small a thing to Haman.

He wanted to kill all the Jews in Xerxes’ kingdom.

## Esther 3:7

It was Nisan, the first month of King Xerxes’ twelfth year as king.

Someone cast a lot in front of Haman.

The lot, called Pur, was a way to choose by chance.

They used it to choose a day and a month.

The month it picked was Adar, the twelfth month.

What did the lot help Haman choose?

## Haman asks the king for an order

## Esther 3:8

Haman told King Xerxes, “One people lives scattered throughout your provinces.

Their laws are different from everyone else’s.

They do not obey your laws.”

Haman claimed the king gained nothing by letting them stay.

## Esther 3:9

Haman asked the king for a written order to destroy these people.

Haman offered ten thousand talents of silver.

A talent was a large measure of weight.

He offered the silver for the king’s treasury to pay those who carried out the order.

## Esther 3:10

King Xerxes took his signet ring off his finger.

The ring made a mark that showed the king’s authority.

He gave it to Haman, son of Hammedatha the Agagite.

Haman was the enemy of the Jews.

## Esther 3:11

The king told Haman, “Keep your silver.

You may do as you please with these people.”

**Pause and answer**

## The letters go out

## Esther 3:12

On day thirteen of the first month, the royal writers came.

They wrote exactly what Haman told them.

The order went to the king’s regional rulers, provincial governors, and local officials.

The writers used each province’s writing and each people’s language.

They wrote in King Xerxes’ name.

The mark from his ring sealed the order.

## Esther 3:13

Messengers carried the letters to every province.

The letters ordered the killing of all the Jews on one day.

This included young and old, women and children.

The order also called for taking their belongings.

The date was day thirteen of Adar, the twelfth month.

## Esther 3:14

Every province was to publish a copy of the order.

Everyone was to hear it and be ready that day.

## Esther 3:15

The king’s command sent the messengers hurrying on their way.

People in the fortress of Susa also heard the order.

The king and Haman sat down to drink.

Throughout Susa, people wondered what was happening.
