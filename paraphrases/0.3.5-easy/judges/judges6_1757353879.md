# Judges 6 — The LORD calls Gideon

**About this chapter**

Midian leaves Israel without food. The LORD calls Gideon to rescue his people.

## Israel loses its food

## Judges 6:1

Again the LORD saw Israel doing evil.

So he let Midian rule Israel for seven years.

## Judges 6:2

Midian was stronger than Israel.

To escape the Midianites, the Israelites made shelters in the mountains.

They hid in caves and other places they could defend.

## Judges 6:3

The Israelites would plant their crops.

Then the Midianites, Amalekites, and other people from the east would invade.

## Judges 6:4

The invaders camped on Israel’s land as far as Gaza.

They destroyed the crops and left no food for Israel.

They left no sheep, oxen, or donkeys.

## Judges 6:5

The Midianites brought their animals and their tents.

They came like a great swarm of locusts.

No one could count them or their camels.

They came to ruin the land.

**Reading help**

Locusts are insects that can eat whole fields of crops.

The invaders came in huge numbers and left little behind.

## Judges 6:6

Midian left Israel very poor.

The Israelites cried to the LORD for help.

## The LORD sends a prophet

## Judges 6:7

The LORD heard Israel’s cry for help against Midian.

## Judges 6:8

He sent a prophet to speak to them.

The prophet said, “The LORD, Israel’s God, says this.

I brought you out of Egypt, where you were slaves.”

## Judges 6:9

“I rescued you from Egypt and everyone who treated you cruelly.

I drove your enemies away and gave you their land.”

## Judges 6:10

“I told you, ‘I am the LORD your God.

You live in the Amorites’ land.

Do not fear their gods.’ But you did not obey me.”

## Gideon hears a message

## Judges 6:11

The angel of the LORD sat under an oak tree in Ophrah.

The tree belonged to Joash, from Abiezer’s family.

His son Gideon was beating wheat to loosen the grain.

He worked in a winepress to hide the wheat from Midian.

**Reading help**

A winepress was a place for crushing grapes.

## Judges 6:12

The angel of the LORD appeared to Gideon.

“Brave warrior, the LORD is with you,” he said.

## Judges 6:13

Gideon asked, “My lord, if the LORD is with us, why has this happened? Our ancestors told us how he brought us out of Egypt.

Where are his wonders now? Now the LORD has left us under Midian’s power.”

## Judges 6:14

The LORD turned to Gideon.

“Go with the strength you have,” he said.

“Rescue Israel from Midian.

I am sending you.”

## Judges 6:15

Gideon asked, “My lord, how can I rescue Israel? My family group is the weakest in Manasseh.

I am the youngest in my father’s house.”

## Judges 6:16

The LORD answered, “I will be with you.

You will defeat all the Midianites as if they were one man.”

## Gideon brings an offering

## Judges 6:17

Gideon said, “If you are pleased with me, give me a sign.

Show me that you are the one speaking to me.”

## Judges 6:18

“Please stay until I bring my offering to you.” The LORD answered, “I will wait for you.”

## Judges 6:19

Gideon prepared a young goat, bread without yeast, and a large measure of flour.

He put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot.

He brought the food out under the oak tree.

## Judges 6:20

God’s angel told Gideon to put the meat and bread on a rock.

He told Gideon to pour out the broth.

Gideon did what the angel said.

## Judges 6:21

The angel touched the meat and bread with the tip of his walking stick.

Fire rose from the rock and burned up the food.

Then the angel disappeared.

## The LORD gives Gideon peace

## Judges 6:22

Gideon realized who he had seen.

He cried, “Oh, Lord GOD! I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!”

## Judges 6:23

The LORD told him, “Peace be with you.

Do not be afraid.

You will live.”

## Judges 6:24

Gideon built an altar there.

He named it The LORD Is Peace.

It still stood in Ophrah of the Abiezrites when this account was written.

## Gideon tears down Baal’s altar

## Judges 6:25

That night the LORD told Gideon to take his father’s young bull and a second bull.

The second bull was seven years old.

Gideon was to tear down his father’s altar to Baal.

He was to cut down the Asherah pole beside it.

## Judges 6:26

The LORD told Gideon to build a proper altar to him on top of the stronghold.

Gideon was to burn the second bull as an offering.

He would use the wood from the Asherah pole.

**Reading help**

An altar was a place to offer gifts in worship.

The pole was a wooden object used to worship Asherah.

A stronghold was a place people could defend.

## Judges 6:27

Gideon took ten servants and obeyed the LORD.

He was afraid of his family and the townsmen.

So he did the work at night.

## Judges 6:28

In the morning the townsmen saw the ruins of Baal’s altar.

The Asherah pole lay cut down.

The second bull had been offered on the new altar.

## Joash speaks for Gideon

## Judges 6:29

The townsmen asked who had done this.

They made careful inquiries.

Someone told them, “Gideon, son of Joash, did it.”

## Judges 6:30

The townsmen demanded that Joash bring out his son.

They wanted Gideon to die for destroying Baal’s altar and the Asherah pole.

## Judges 6:31

Joash asked, “Must you defend Baal? Does Baal need you to save him? Anyone who defends Baal will die by morning.

If Baal is a god, let him defend his own altar against Gideon.”

## Judges 6:32

That day people began calling Gideon Jerubbaal.

The name means “Let Baal argue his case against him.” Gideon had torn down Baal’s altar.

## The tribes gather

## Judges 6:33

The Midianites, Amalekites, and other people from the east gathered together.

They crossed the Jordan River and camped in the Valley of Jezreel.

## Judges 6:34

The Spirit of the LORD came upon Gideon.

Gideon blew a ram’s horn.

The people of Abiezer’s family gathered to follow him.

## Judges 6:35

Gideon sent messengers throughout Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali.

He called them to join him in battle.

People from those tribes came to meet him.

## Gideon asks for two signs

## Judges 6:36

Gideon spoke to God about his promise.

God had said he would use Gideon to rescue Israel.

## Judges 6:37

Gideon said, “I will put a fleece on the threshing floor.

Let dew wet only the fleece.

Let the ground stay dry.

Then I will know you will rescue Israel through me.”

**Reading help**

A fleece is a sheep’s woolly coat.

A threshing floor is a flat place for separating grain from stalks.

Dew is water that forms in drops overnight.

## Judges 6:38

The next morning Gideon squeezed the wet fleece.

The water from the fleece filled a bowl.

The ground was dry, just as Gideon had asked.

## Judges 6:39

Gideon said to God, “Please do not be angry.

Let me ask for one more sign with the fleece.

This time let the fleece stay dry.

Let dew cover the ground.”

## Judges 6:40

That night God did what Gideon asked.

The fleece alone stayed dry.

Dew covered the ground.
