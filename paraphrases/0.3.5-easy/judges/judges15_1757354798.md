# Judges 15 — Samson fights at Lehi and cries out for water

**About this chapter**

Samson and the Philistines keep trying to pay each other back. After the fight at Lehi, Samson needs water.

## Samson returns at harvest time

## Judges 15:1

At wheat harvest, Samson took a young goat to visit his wife.

He wanted to see her in her room.

Her father would not let him in.

![A stalk of ripe grain](../pictures/grain.svg)

Point to the grain at the top of the stalk.

Harvest is the time when people gather ripe crops.

## Judges 15:2

Her father said, “I was sure you hated her.

I gave her to one of your wedding companions.

Her younger sister is even more beautiful.

Please marry her instead.”

## Judges 15:3

Samson said, “This time no one can blame me for hurting the Philistines.”

## The crops burn

## Judges 15:4

Samson caught three hundred foxes.

He used the foxes and torches to take revenge.

## Judges 15:5

Fire spread through the Philistines’ grain fields.

It burned the standing grain and the piles already cut.

It also burned their grapevines and olive trees.

## Judges 15:6

The Philistines demanded to know who had done this.

They learned that it was Samson, the son-in-law of a man from Timnah.

His wife had married his wedding companion.

The Philistines burned the woman and her father to death.

## Judges 15:7

Samson told the Philistines, “Because you did this, I will pay you back.

Until then I will not rest.”

## Judges 15:8

Samson attacked them without mercy and killed many.

Then he stayed in a cave at the rock of Etam.

Where did Samson stay after the fighting?

## The Philistines come to Judah

## Judges 15:9

The Philistines set up camp in Judah.

They gathered near the town of Lehi.

## Judges 15:10

The men of Judah asked, “Why are you attacking us?” The Philistines answered, “We came to arrest Samson.

We want to pay him back for what he did.”

## Judges 15:11

Three thousand men of Judah went to Samson’s cave at Etam.

They asked, “Don’t you know the Philistines rule us? What have you done to us?” Samson answered, “I treated them as they treated me.”

## The men of Judah take Samson to Lehi

## Judges 15:12

The men of Judah said, “We came to arrest you.

We will hand you over to the Philistines.” Samson asked them to promise they would not kill him themselves.

## Judges 15:13

The men promised to hand him over alive.

They tied Samson with two new ropes and led him up from the rock.

## Judges 15:14

When Samson reached Lehi, the Philistines came toward him shouting.

The Spirit of the LORD gave Samson great strength.

The ropes on his arms became as weak as burned fibers.

His hands came free.

**Reading help**

The account compares the ropes to burned flax.

Flax is a plant with fibers people use for thread.

Burned fibers break easily.

## Samson speaks after the fight

## Judges 15:15

Samson found a fresh jawbone from a donkey.

He used it to kill a thousand men.

## Judges 15:16

Samson said, “With a donkey’s jawbone, I left them in heaps.

With a donkey’s jawbone, I killed a thousand men.”

## Judges 15:17

Then Samson threw the jawbone away.

He named the place Ramath-lehi.

**Reading help**

Ramath-lehi means “Jawbone Hill.”

The name recalled what happened there.

## Samson asks God for help

## Judges 15:18

Samson was very thirsty.

He cried out, “LORD, I am your servant.

You gave this great victory through me.

Must I now die of thirst and fall into the Philistines’ hands?”

## Judges 15:19

God opened a hollow place in Lehi.

Water came out, and Samson drank.

His strength returned, and he felt alive again.

He named the spring En-hakkore.

It was still there when this account was written.

**Reading help**

En-hakkore means “spring of the one who called.”

At this spring, Samson called to God for help.

## Judges 15:20

Samson led Israel as judge for twenty years.

The Philistines still held power during those years.
