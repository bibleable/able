# Judges 5 — Deborah and Barak sing

**About this chapter**

After the battle, Deborah and Barak praise the LORD. Their song remembers who came to help and who stayed home.

## A song of praise

## Judges 5:1

That day Deborah and Barak sang this song.

Barak was the son of Abinoam.

## Judges 5:2

Israel’s leaders stepped forward.

The people freely offered to help.

Praise the LORD!

## Judges 5:3

Listen, you kings! Listen, you princes! I will sing to the LORD.

I will praise the LORD, the God of Israel.

## Judges 5:4

LORD, you marched out from Seir, from the land of Edom.

The earth shook.

Rain came pouring down from the clouds.

## Judges 5:5

The mountains shook before you, LORD.

You are the LORD of Sinai, the God of Israel.

## Deborah remembers the empty roads

## Judges 5:6

In the days of Shamgar, son of Anath, the main roads were empty.

In the days of Jael, travelers took the smaller paths.

## Judges 5:7

Life in Israel’s villages stopped.

It stopped until I, Deborah, stood up.

I became like a mother to Israel.

**Reading help**

Deborah calls herself a mother to Israel.

She cared for the people and led them.

## Judges 5:8

Israel chose new gods.

Then war reached the city gates.

Among forty thousand Israelites, who could find a shield or spear?

## Judges 5:9

I am proud of Israel’s leaders and everyone who offered to help.

Praise the LORD!

## Singers at the watering places

## Judges 5:10

Some of you ride white donkeys on saddle blankets.

Others walk along the road.

All of you, think about what the singers say.

## Judges 5:11

Listen where people come to draw water.

The singers tell what the LORD did that was right.

They tell the good deeds of Israel’s villagers too.

Then the LORD’s people went down to the city gates.

## Judges 5:12

Wake up, wake up, Deborah! Wake up, wake up and sing! Get up, Barak, son of Abinoam! Lead away your prisoners.

## Who came to help?

## Judges 5:13

Those who were left came down to the leaders.

The LORD’s people came down to join me against powerful enemies.

## Judges 5:14

Some came from Ephraim, from land once associated with Amalek.

People from Benjamin followed them.

Commanders came from Machir.

Officers from Zebulun carried the staffs that showed their rank.

## Judges 5:15

Issachar’s leaders joined Deborah.

Issachar’s men rushed down into the valley behind Barak.

But Reuben’s families could not decide what to do.

## Judges 5:16

Reuben, why sit by the sheep pens, listening for the flocks to be called? Reuben’s families could not decide what to do.

**Pause and answer**

Which families kept wondering whether to go?

## Judges 5:17

Gilead’s people stayed across the Jordan River.

Dan, why did your people stay by the ships? Asher’s people stayed on the coast, by their harbors.

## Judges 5:18

Zebulun’s people risked their lives.

Naphtali’s people did too, on the high ground where they fought.

## The Kishon River

## Judges 5:19

Kings came and fought.

The kings of Canaan fought at Taanach by the waters of Megiddo.

They took no silver home from the battle.

## Judges 5:20

Even the stars in the sky fought against Sisera.

They fought him from their paths across the sky.

## Judges 5:21

The Kishon River swept the enemy away.

That ancient river, the Kishon, swept them away.

Be strong, my soul, and march on!

**Reading help**

The singers describe the stars as fighters.

The picture shows that even the sky was on Israel’s side.

“My soul” means the singer is speaking to herself.

## Judges 5:22

The horses galloped wildly.

Their hooves struck the ground like thunder.

## Judges 5:23

The angel of the LORD said, “Call down a curse on Meroz.

Call down a bitter curse on its people.

They would not come to help the LORD against the powerful.”

## The song honors Jael

## Judges 5:24

May God bless Jael above all women who live in tents.

May God bless Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite!

## Judges 5:25

Sisera asked for water.

Jael gave him milk.

She brought him thickened milk in a fine bowl.

## Judges 5:26

Jael took a tent peg and a worker’s hammer.

She struck Sisera and broke his skull.

## Judges 5:27

At her feet he fell.

He fell and lay still.

There at her feet he fell down dead.

## Sisera’s mother waits

## Judges 5:28

Sisera’s mother looked out through the strips of wood across her window.

She cried, “Why is his chariot so late? Why can’t I hear the sound of his chariots?”

**Reading help**

The song now imagines the people waiting at Sisera’s home.

His mother still expects him to return.

## Judges 5:29

Her wisest women gave her an answer.

She kept telling herself the same thing.

## Judges 5:30

“They must be sharing out what they took.

Each soldier must be taking one or two captive girls.

Sisera must be getting brightly colored clothes.

The men must be taking cloth with patterns stitched into it to wear around their necks.”

## A prayer and forty years of peace

## Judges 5:31

LORD, may all your enemies perish like Sisera.

May those who love you shine like the sun at its brightest! Then Israel had peace for forty years.

**Reading help**

The song uses the bright sun as a picture of strength.
