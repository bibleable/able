# Judges 11 — Jephthah leads Gilead and makes a vow

**About this chapter**

Gilead’s leaders ask Jephthah for help. Jephthah makes a promise that brings grief to his family.

## Jephthah leaves home

## Judges 11:1

Jephthah came from Gilead.

He was a brave fighter.

His father’s name was also Gilead.

His mother was a prostitute.

**Reading help**

A prostitute is someone who has sex for payment.

## Judges 11:2

Gilead’s wife had sons too.

When they grew up, they drove Jephthah away.

They said, “Your mother is a different woman.

You will get no share of our father’s property.”

## Judges 11:3

Jephthah fled from his brothers and settled in Tob.

Some men with a bad reputation joined him there.

They traveled with him.

## The leaders ask Jephthah to return

## Judges 11:4

Some time later, the Ammonites attacked Israel.

## Judges 11:5

During the war, the leaders of Gilead went to Tob.

They went to find Jephthah.

## Judges 11:6

They asked him to come back.

“Lead our army so we can fight the Ammonites.”

## Judges 11:7

Jephthah asked, “Didn’t you hate me? You drove me from my father’s home.

Why come to me now that you are in trouble?”

## Judges 11:8

The leaders said, “We want you to come with us.

Fight the Ammonites, and lead everyone who lives in Gilead.”

## Gilead makes a promise

## Judges 11:9

Jephthah asked, “Suppose I come back and fight.

If the LORD gives me victory, will you really let me lead you?”

## Judges 11:10

The leaders promised to do as Jephthah said.

They called on the LORD to witness their promise.

## Judges 11:11

Jephthah went with the leaders.

The people made him their leader and army commander.

At Mizpah, Jephthah repeated their agreement before the LORD.

## The king asks for land

## Judges 11:12

Jephthah sent messengers to the Ammonite king.

He asked, “Why have you come to fight in my land? What wrong have I done to you?”

## Judges 11:13

The king answered, “Israel took my land after leaving Egypt.

It stretched from the Arnon to the Jabbok and as far as the Jordan.

Give it back in peace.”

## Judges 11:14

Jephthah sent messengers back to the Ammonite king.

## Judges 11:15

They brought Jephthah’s answer.

“Israel took no land from Moab or the Ammonites.”

## Jephthah tells of Israel’s journey

## Judges 11:16

“After leaving Egypt, Israel crossed the wilderness to the Red Sea.

Then the people reached Kadesh.”

## Judges 11:17

“Israel asked Edom’s king for permission to cross his land.

He refused.

Israel asked Moab’s king too.

He also refused, so Israel stayed at Kadesh.”

## Judges 11:18

“Then Israel went around Edom and Moab through the wilderness.

Israel camped east of Moab, across the Arnon River.

The river marked Moab’s border.

Israel stayed outside it.”

## Judges 11:19

“Israel sent messengers to Sihon.

He was the Amorite king who ruled in Heshbon.

They asked to cross his land to reach their own.”

## Judges 11:20

“Sihon did not trust Israel to pass through.

He gathered his people at Jahaz and fought Israel there.”

## Jephthah explains whose land Israel took

## Judges 11:21

“The LORD, Israel’s God, gave Israel victory over Sihon and his people.

Israel took the land where those Amorites lived.”

**Reading help**

Amorites and Ammonites were two different peoples.

Sihon was an Amorite.

The king who received this message was an Ammonite.

## Judges 11:22

“It stretched from the Arnon River to the Jabbok River.

It reached from the wilderness to the Jordan River.”

## Judges 11:23

“The LORD drove out the Amorites for his people Israel.

Why should you have their land now?”

## Judges 11:24

“You keep what your god Chemosh gives you.

We keep what the LORD our God gives us.”

## Jephthah asks the LORD to judge

## Judges 11:25

“Are you any better than Balak, son of Zippor? He was king of Moab.

Did he ever dispute this with Israel or fight Israel?”

## Judges 11:26

“Israel has lived here for three hundred years.

We lived in Heshbon, Aroer, and their villages.

We lived in the cities along the Arnon.

Why didn’t you take them back during those years?”

## Judges 11:27

“I have done you no wrong.

You wronged me by coming to fight.

May the LORD judge between Israel and Ammon today.”

## Judges 11:28

The Ammonite king ignored Jephthah’s message.

## Jephthah makes a vow

## Judges 11:29

The Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah.

Jephthah went through Gilead and Manasseh.

He passed through Mizpah in Gilead and went on to fight the Ammonites.

## Judges 11:30

Jephthah made a vow to the LORD.

He spoke about what he would do if the LORD gave him victory.

## Judges 11:31

He said, “When I come home in victory, something will come out of my door to greet me.

Whatever comes out will belong to the LORD.

I will give it as a burnt offering.”

**Reading help**

A vow is a solemn promise.

A burnt offering was a gift burned on an altar.

## Judges 11:32

Jephthah went across to fight the Ammonites.

The LORD gave him victory.

## Judges 11:33

Jephthah devastated twenty cities from Aroer to near Minnith and as far as Abel-keramim.

Israel defeated the Ammonites.

## His daughter comes to meet him

## Judges 11:34

Jephthah came home to Mizpah.

His daughter came out to greet him, dancing to the sound of tambourines.

She was his only child.

## Judges 11:35

When Jephthah saw her, he tore his clothes in grief.

“Oh, my daughter! Seeing you has brought me terrible sorrow.

I gave my word to the LORD.

I cannot take it back.”

## Judges 11:36

His daughter answered, “Father, you gave your word to the LORD.

Do to me what you promised.

The LORD has given you victory over your enemies, the Ammonites.”

## The women remember Jephthah’s daughter

## Judges 11:37

She asked her father for one thing.

She wanted two months in the mountains with her friends.

There she could grieve because she would never marry.

## Judges 11:38

Jephthah agreed.

His daughter went to the mountains with her friends.

For two months she grieved there because she would never marry.

## Judges 11:39

After two months she returned to her father.

Jephthah did to her what he had vowed.

She had never slept with a man.

A custom began in Israel.

## Judges 11:40

Every year the young women of Israel went away for four days.

They mourned for Jephthah’s daughter.
