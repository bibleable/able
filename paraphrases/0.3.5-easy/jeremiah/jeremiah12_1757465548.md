# Jeremiah 12 — Why do people who do wrong live so well?

**About this chapter**

Jeremiah asks the LORD a hard question about fairness.

The LORD warns Jeremiah that even his family has betrayed him.

Then the LORD speaks about his people and their neighbors.

## Jeremiah brings his question to the LORD

## Jeremiah 12:1

LORD, you are fair when I bring you my case.

Yet I want to ask about your decisions.

Why do people who do evil do so well?

Why do people who break faith live so comfortably?

## Jeremiah 12:2

You planted them, LORD, and they took root.

They have grown and produced fruit.

Jeremiah compares their success to a plant growing well.

They often speak of you, but their hearts are far from you.

## Jeremiah 12:3

But you know me, LORD.

You see me and test how I feel about you.

Drag the wicked away like sheep going to slaughter.

Set them apart for the day they will be killed.

## Jeremiah 12:4

How long must the land grieve?

How long must the grass lie dry in every field?

The people who live here do evil.

Because of them, the animals and birds are gone.

The people say, “He cannot see what will happen to us.”

## The LORD answers Jeremiah

## Jeremiah 12:5

The LORD asks, “If racing against people tires you out, how will you race against horses?

If you stumble in peaceful country, how will you manage in the thick bushes by the Jordan?”

The LORD warns Jeremiah that harder times lie ahead.

## Jeremiah 12:6

“Even your brothers have turned against you.

Your own family has shouted against you.

They may say good things about you.

Even then, do not trust them.

## The LORD grieves over his people and their land

## Jeremiah 12:7

“I have left my house.

I have given up what belonged to me.

I have handed the people I dearly love over to their enemies.

## Jeremiah 12:8

“My own people have become like a lion in the forest.

They roar at me.

That is why I hate them.

## Jeremiah 12:9

“My people are like a spotted hunting bird.

Other hunting birds circle it, ready to attack.

Go and gather the wild animals.

Bring them to eat their prey.

## Jeremiah 12:10

“Many shepherds have ruined my vineyard.

They have trampled my land.

My lovely field now lies empty and bare.”

A vineyard is land where people grow grapes.

The ruined vineyard pictures what has happened to the LORD’s land.

![Grapes growing on a vine, the crop Jeremiah describes in the vineyard.](../pictures/vine-and-grapes.svg)

## Jeremiah 12:11

The LORD says, “They have left my land in ruins.

It lies before me as if it were grieving.

The whole land is ruined, and no one takes it to heart.

## Jeremiah 12:12

“Attackers have come over all the bare hills in the desert.

The LORD’s sword brings death from one end of the earth to the other.

No one has peace.

## Jeremiah 12:13

“The people planted wheat and gathered only thorns.

They wore themselves out and gained nothing.

People, feel the shame of that harvest.

It came because the LORD was very angry.”

## The LORD speaks about Israel’s neighbors

## Jeremiah 12:14

The LORD says, “Israel’s neighbors have attacked the land I gave my people.

I will pull those nations out of their lands like plants pulled up by the roots.

I will also take Judah’s people out from among them.

## Jeremiah 12:15

“After I have pulled them up, I will show them mercy again.

I will bring each one back to their own land.

## Jeremiah 12:16

“Those neighbors once taught my people to make promises in Baal’s name.

Now they must carefully learn the ways of my people.

They must make their promises in my name.

They must say, ‘As surely as the LORD lives.’

Then they will have a lasting place among my people.

## Jeremiah 12:17

“But if a nation refuses to obey, I will pull it up by the roots.

I will destroy that nation.”

The LORD has spoken.

What does the LORD promise to do after he takes the people away from their lands?
