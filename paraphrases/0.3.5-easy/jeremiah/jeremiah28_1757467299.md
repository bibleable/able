# Jeremiah 28 — Hananiah breaks Jeremiah’s wooden yoke

**About this chapter**

Hananiah says the people taken to Babylon will soon come home.

Jeremiah longs for that to happen.

But has the LORD really sent Hananiah?

## Hananiah promises a return from Babylon

## Jeremiah 28:1

Zedekiah was in his fourth year as king of Judah.

In the fifth month, Hananiah spoke to me, Jeremiah.

Hananiah was a prophet from Gibeon, the son of Azzur.

We were in the LORD’s temple.

The priests and all the people heard him.

## Jeremiah 28:2

Hananiah said, “The LORD commands heaven’s armies.

He is the God of Israel.

This is his message. ‘I have broken the king of Babylon’s yoke.’”

A yoke is a bar that animals wear to pull a load.

Here it stands for Babylon’s power to make nations serve its king.

![Two oxen wearing a wooden yoke across their necks.](../pictures/job-yoke.svg)

## Jeremiah 28:3

Hananiah continued, “King Nebuchadnezzar took things from the LORD’s temple to Babylon.

The LORD says he will bring them all back here within two years.

## Jeremiah 28:4

“He will bring back Judah’s king Jeconiah, son of Jehoiakim.

He will also bring back everyone taken from Judah to Babylon.

The LORD says he will break the Babylonian king’s yoke.”

## Jeremiah asks how people will know

## Jeremiah 28:5

Jeremiah answered Hananiah in the temple.

The priests and all the people standing there heard him.

## Jeremiah 28:6

Jeremiah said, “Amen! May the LORD do what you say.

May he bring back everything taken from his temple.

May he bring all our people home from Babylon.

## Jeremiah 28:7

“But listen to what I have to say.

I want everyone here to hear it.

## Jeremiah 28:8

“Long before you and I spoke, other prophets gave warnings.

They warned many lands and powerful kingdoms about war.

They warned of disaster and deadly disease.

## Jeremiah 28:9

“Suppose a prophet promises peace.

People will know the LORD sent that prophet only if the promise comes true.”

## Hananiah breaks the yoke

## Jeremiah 28:10

Jeremiah was wearing a wooden yoke.

Hananiah took it off Jeremiah’s neck and broke it.

## Jeremiah 28:11

In front of everyone Hananiah said, “This is the LORD’s message.

‘Within two years I will break Nebuchadnezzar’s yoke just like this.

I will lift it from the necks of all the nations.’”

Then Jeremiah went on his way.

## The LORD sends Jeremiah back to Hananiah

## Jeremiah 28:12

Soon after Hananiah broke the wooden yoke, the LORD spoke to Jeremiah.

## Jeremiah 28:13

“Go and give Hananiah my message.

Tell him, ‘You broke a wooden yoke.

In its place you have made an iron yoke.’”

Iron is harder to break than wood.

The nations would still have to serve Babylon.

## Jeremiah 28:14

The LORD who commands heaven’s armies, the God of Israel, said,

“I have put an iron yoke on all these nations.

I am making them serve Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon.

They will serve him.

I have even put the wild animals under his power.”

## Jeremiah 28:15

Jeremiah told Hananiah, “Listen, Hananiah!

The LORD did not send you.

You have made these people believe a lie.

## Jeremiah 28:16

“So this is what the LORD says.

‘I will take your life this year.

You have urged people to turn against me.’”

## Jeremiah 28:17

Hananiah died in the seventh month of that same year.
