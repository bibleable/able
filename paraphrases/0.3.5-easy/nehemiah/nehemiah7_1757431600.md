# Nehemiah 7 — Nehemiah finds a record of the families

**About this chapter**

Nehemiah finds a list of people who returned from Babylon. It names families and towns. Each count tells how many came from that group.

## People guard Jerusalem

## Nehemiah 7:1

We finished rebuilding the wall.

I put the gate doors in place.

The gatekeepers, singers, and Levites received their duties.

The Levites were people from the family of Levi.

## Nehemiah 7:2

I put my brother Hanani in charge of Jerusalem.

Hananiah, who commanded the fort, shared that work.

I could trust Hananiah.

He honored God more than most people did.

## Nehemiah 7:3

I told them, “Wait until the sun is hot before opening Jerusalem’s gates.

Have the guards shut and fasten the doors while still on duty.

Choose people who live in Jerusalem to stand guard.

Some should guard the places you assign them.

Others should guard their own homes.”

## Nehemiah 7:4

Jerusalem had plenty of room but few people.

The people still had to rebuild the houses.

## Nehemiah finds a family record

## Nehemiah 7:5

God gave me the idea to gather everyone.

I would gather the nobles, officials, and other people.

I wanted to list them by family.

I found the family record of the first people who returned.

Here is what I read.

![An open scroll shows lines of writing, like a written family record.](../pictures/scroll.svg)

## Nehemiah 7:6

King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon had taken people away as prisoners.

These people came back from Babylon to Jerusalem and Judah.

Each went home to their own town.

## Nehemiah 7:7

Zerubbabel, Jeshua, Nehemiah, Azariah, Raamiah, and Nahamani went with them.

So did Mordecai, Bilshan, Mispereth, Bigvai, Nehum, and Baanah.

The record counted the men of Israel who returned.

## Families search for their records

## Nehemiah 7:61

Some people returned from Tel-melah, Tel-harsha, Cherub, Addon, and Immer.

They could not show that their families came from Israel.

## Nehemiah 7:62

They came from the families of Delaiah, Tobiah, and Nekoda.

Together they numbered 642.

## Nehemiah 7:63

Some men from priestly families also lacked family records.

Their ancestors were Hobaiah, Hakkoz, and Barzillai.

This last ancestor had married a daughter of Barzillai from Gilead.

He took her family name.

## Nehemiah 7:64

These men looked for their family records.

They could not find them.

So the community treated them as unclean for priestly service.

This meant they could not serve as priests.

## Nehemiah 7:65

The governor told them to wait before eating the most holy food.

A priest first had to ask God for a decision.

The priest would use the Urim and Thummim to ask God.

## People and animals

## Nehemiah 7:66

The whole gathering numbered 42,360 people.

## Nehemiah 7:67

Besides these, there were 7,337 servants and 245 singers.

Both groups included men and women.

## Nehemiah 7:68

The people had 736 horses and 245 mules.

## Nehemiah 7:69

They also had 435 camels and 6,720 donkeys.

## Gifts for the work

## Nehemiah 7:70

Some family leaders gave gifts for the work.

The governor gave 1,000 gold coins called darics.

He also gave 50 bowls and 530 garments for priests.

These gifts went into the treasury, where people kept valuable gifts.

## Nehemiah 7:71

Some family leaders gave the treasury 20,000 gold darics.

They also gave 2,200 minas of silver for the work.

A mina was a measure of weight.

## Nehemiah 7:72

The rest of the people gave 20,000 gold darics together.

They also gave 2,000 minas of silver and 67 garments for priests.

## Nehemiah 7:73

The priests and Levites settled in their own towns.

So did the gatekeepers, singers, temple servants, and other Israelites.

By the seventh month the Israelites were living in their towns.
