# Exodus 12 — The first Passover and the journey out of Egypt

**About this chapter**

The Israelites are still in Egypt. The LORD tells each family to prepare a meal and get ready to leave. Later, their children will ask why they keep eating this special meal.

## Families prepare the Passover meal

## Exodus 12:1

In Egypt, the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron.

## Exodus 12:2

He said, “Count this month as the first month of your year.

## Exodus 12:3

Tell all the people of Israel what to do.

On day 10 of this month, each family must choose a lamb for its household.

## Exodus 12:4

A family that is too small to eat a whole lamb can share one with the family next door.

Count the people and work out how much each person can eat.

## Exodus 12:5

Choose a male sheep or goat that is one year old.

The animal must have nothing wrong with it.

## Exodus 12:6

Keep the animal until day 14.

That evening, as daylight fades, all the people of Israel must kill their Passover animals.

## Exodus 12:7

Put some of the blood on both sides and across the top of the doorway of each house where you will eat the meal.

## Exodus 12:8

That night, roast the meat over a fire.

Eat it with bitter-tasting herbs and bread made without yeast.

## Exodus 12:9

Roast the whole animal, including its head, legs and inner parts.

Do not eat the meat raw or boil it in water.

## Exodus 12:10

Leave none of the meat until morning.

Burn whatever you have left before morning comes.

## Exodus 12:11

Eat dressed for travel, with your sandals on and your walking stick in your hand.

Eat quickly.

This is the LORD’s Passover.

## The LORD explains the sign

## Exodus 12:12

That night I will go through Egypt.

I will kill every firstborn son and every firstborn male animal.

I will judge all the gods of Egypt.

I am the LORD.

## Exodus 12:13

The blood on your houses will be a sign.

When I see the blood, I will pass over you.

When I strike Egypt, you will be safe from this deadly blow.

## Exodus 12:14

Remember this day with a special meal to honor the LORD.

Your children and their children must keep celebrating it.

## Seven days of special bread

**Reading help**

Yeast makes bread dough rise. The Bible calls bread made without anything to make it rise “unleavened bread.”

## Exodus 12:15

For seven days, eat bread made without yeast.

On the first day, take out of your houses anything used to make dough rise.

Anyone who eats food made with it during those seven days must be cut off from the people of Israel.

## Exodus 12:16

On the first day and again on the seventh day, gather to worship.

The only work you may do on those two days is to prepare food.

## Exodus 12:17

Keep the Feast of Unleavened Bread.

On this day I brought your people out of Egypt group by group.

Each new generation must keep this feast.

## Exodus 12:18

In the first month, eat bread made without yeast from the evening of day 14 until the evening of day 21.

## Exodus 12:19

For those seven days, keep your homes free of anything used to make dough rise.

Anyone who eats food made with it must be cut off from the people of Israel.

This applies both to people born in the land and to people from other lands.

## Exodus 12:20

Wherever you live, eat bread made without yeast.

Eat nothing made with anything that makes dough rise.”

## Moses speaks to the leaders

## Exodus 12:21

Moses called the leaders of Israel together.

He said, “Go now and choose a lamb for each family.

Kill the lamb for the Passover meal.

## Exodus 12:22

Dip a bunch of stems from a plant called hyssop into the bowl of blood.

Brush the blood across the top and both sides of the doorway.

Everyone must stay inside until morning.

## Exodus 12:23

The LORD will go through Egypt to strike down the Egyptians.

When he sees the blood across the top and sides of your doorway, he will pass over it.

He will keep the destroyer from coming into your house to kill you.

## Exodus 12:24

You and your children after you must always follow this command.

## Exodus 12:25

The LORD promised to give you a land of your own.

When you get there, keep celebrating the Passover.

## Exodus 12:26

Your children will ask you, ‘What does this celebration mean to you?’

## Exodus 12:27

Tell them, ‘This is the Passover sacrifice we offer to the LORD.

In Egypt, he passed over our people’s houses.

He struck down the Egyptians and kept our families safe.’” Then the people bowed down and worshiped.

## Exodus 12:28

The Israelites went and did exactly what the LORD had told Moses and Aaron.

## Pharaoh tells the Israelites to go

## Exodus 12:29

At midnight the LORD struck down every firstborn son in Egypt.

The king on his throne lost his firstborn son.

So did the prisoner in the underground prison.

The firstborn males of the farm animals died too.

## Exodus 12:30

Pharaoh, his officials and all the Egyptians got up that night.

People cried out in grief all over Egypt.

Someone had died in every house.

## Exodus 12:31

That night Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron.

He said, “Get up! Leave my people! Take the Israelites and go worship the LORD, as you asked.

## Exodus 12:32

Take your sheep, goats and cattle too, as you said.

Go! And ask God to bless me too.”

## Exodus 12:33

The Egyptians urged the Israelites to leave the land quickly.

The Egyptians said, “If you stay, we will all die!”

## Exodus 12:34

The Israelites took their bread dough before it had anything in it to make it rise.

They wrapped the bowls of dough in clothes and carried them on their shoulders.

## Exodus 12:35

The Israelites did what Moses had told them.

They asked the Egyptians for things made of silver and gold.

They also asked for clothes.

## Exodus 12:36

The LORD made the Egyptians willing to give the Israelites what they asked for.

In this way the Israelites carried away the Egyptians’ wealth.

## The Israelites leave Egypt

## Exodus 12:37

The Israelites walked from Rameses to Succoth.

About 600,000 men walked with them.

There were women and children too.

## Exodus 12:38

Many people from other groups went with them.

They took large flocks of sheep and goats and herds of cattle.

## Exodus 12:39

The people baked flat loaves from the dough they had carried out of Egypt.

The dough had nothing in it to make it rise.

The Egyptians had made them leave so quickly that they had no time to prepare food for the trip.

## Exodus 12:40

The Israelites lived in Egypt for 430 years.

## Exodus 12:41

On the very day those 430 years ended, all the LORD’s people left Egypt group by group.

## Exodus 12:42

The LORD kept watch that night to bring his people out of Egypt.

Each year on that night, the Israelites and their children after them must keep watch to honor the LORD.

## Who may share the Passover meal?

**Reading help**

Circumcision means removing the fold of skin at the tip of the penis. For the Israelites, it was a sign of God’s agreement with their people.

## Exodus 12:43

The LORD told Moses and Aaron, “These are the rules for the Passover meal.

A person from another nation may not eat it.

## Exodus 12:44

If you have bought a man as a slave, he may eat the meal after he has been circumcised.

## Exodus 12:45

Someone staying for a short time or working for pay may not eat the meal.

## Exodus 12:46

Eat each Passover animal inside one house.

Keep all its meat inside that house.

Do not break any of its bones.

## Exodus 12:47

All the people of Israel must celebrate the Passover.

## Exodus 12:48

A man from another nation who lives among you may want to celebrate the LORD’s Passover.

First, every male in his household must be circumcised.

Then he may join the celebration just like someone born in the land.

No man who has not been circumcised may eat the meal.

## Exodus 12:49

The same rule applies to people born in the land and to people from other lands who live among you.”

## Exodus 12:50

All the Israelites did exactly what the LORD had told Moses and Aaron.

## Exodus 12:51

On that very day, the LORD brought the Israelites out of Egypt group by group.
