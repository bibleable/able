# Numbers 5 — The camp, repayment and a husband’s suspicion

**About this chapter**

The LORD gives Moses rules for Israel’s camp. He tells the people how to repay someone they have wronged. He also gives a law for a husband who suspects his wife.

## Outside the camp

## Numbers 5:1

The LORD spoke to Moses.

## Numbers 5:2

Moses was to tell Israel who must leave the camp.

This included anyone with a skin disease or a bodily discharge.

It also included anyone made unclean by a dead body.

## Numbers 5:3

Both men and women had to go outside.

The LORD lived among the people.

The camp where he lived must stay clean.

**Reading help**

A bodily discharge is fluid coming from the body. Here, clean and unclean describe a person’s standing under Israel’s rules for worship.

## Numbers 5:4

The Israelites sent these people outside the camp.

They did what the LORD had told Moses.

**Pause and answer**

Why was the camp to stay clean?

## Paying someone back

## Numbers 5:5

The LORD spoke to Moses again.

## Numbers 5:6

Moses was to tell Israel about wrongs between people.

A man or woman who wronged someone was guilty.

That person had also been unfaithful to the LORD.

## Numbers 5:7

The person must admit the wrong and pay back the full amount.

They must add one fifth of its value.

The person they wronged must receive the whole payment.

**Reading help**

| Add one fifth | ● |

## Numbers 5:8

Sometimes the person who suffered the wrong had no relative to receive the payment.

Then the payment belonged to the LORD.

The person who did wrong gave it to the priest.

They also brought a ram for the priest to make atonement for them.

**Reading help**

A ram is a male sheep. Making atonement means dealing with the wrong before God.

## Numbers 5:9

Holy gifts that Israelites brought to the priest belonged to the priest.

## Numbers 5:10

A person’s holy gifts remained theirs until they gave them.

Whatever they gave the priest then belonged to him.

**Pause and answer**

Who usually received the repayment?

## A husband suspects his wife

## Numbers 5:11

The LORD spoke to Moses again.

## Numbers 5:12

Moses was to give Israel a law about marriage.

A wife might have been unfaithful to her husband.

## Numbers 5:13

She might have had sex with another man in secret.

Her husband did not see it.

No one caught her or could speak as a witness.

## Numbers 5:14

A husband might feel jealous and suspect his wife.

His suspicion might be right.

Or his wife might have been faithful all along.

## Numbers 5:15

The husband brought his wife to the priest.

For her offering he brought one tenth of an ephah of barley flour.

He added no oil or frankincense.

This grain offering brought the suspected wrong before the LORD.

**Reading help**

An ephah was a measure for dry food. Frankincense gives off a sweet smell when burned.

**Pause and answer**

Could the husband suspect a wife who had been faithful?

## The woman stands before the LORD

## Numbers 5:16

The priest brought the woman forward.

She stood before the LORD.

## Numbers 5:17

The priest had holy water in a clay jar.

He added dust from the floor of the holy tent.

## Numbers 5:18

As the woman stood before the LORD, the priest loosened her hair.

She held the grain offering that brought the matter before the LORD.

The priest held the bitter water connected with the curse.

**Pause and answer**

What did the woman hold?

## The words of the oath

## Numbers 5:19

The priest asked the woman to take an oath.

He said that if she had been faithful to her husband and had not had sex with another man, the bitter water should leave her unharmed.

## Numbers 5:20

The oath also spoke about the other possibility.

While married, she might have had sex with another man and been unfaithful to her husband.

## Numbers 5:21

The priest asked her to accept a curse if that was true.

Under the curse, her thigh would waste away and her belly would swell.

Her people would speak of what the LORD had done to her when they spoke a curse.

## Numbers 5:22

The priest said the water would enter her stomach.

Under the curse her belly would swell and her thigh would waste away.

The woman answered, “Amen, amen.”

**Reading help**

An oath is a serious promise made before God. By saying “Amen,” the woman agreed to the words of the oath.

**Pause and answer**

What word did the woman say twice?

## The offering and the water

## Numbers 5:23

The priest wrote the words of the curse on a scroll.

He washed the writing off into the bitter water.

**Reading help**

A scroll is a long sheet used for writing. It can be rolled up.

## Numbers 5:24

The law called for the woman to drink the bitter water.

The curse could bring her great suffering.

## Numbers 5:25

The priest took the grain offering from her hands.

He waved it before the LORD.

Then he brought it to the altar.

## Numbers 5:26

The priest burned a handful of flour on the altar.

This part stood for the whole offering.

After that, he had the woman drink the water.

**Pause and answer**

What did the priest burn on the altar?

## The two outcomes

## Numbers 5:27

The law said what would happen if she had been unfaithful.

She would suffer after drinking the water.

Her belly would swell and her thigh would waste away.

She would bear the curse among her people.

## Numbers 5:28

If she had been faithful, she would be unharmed.

She would still be able to have children.

## Numbers 5:29

This was Israel’s law about jealousy.

It covered the case of a wife who had been unfaithful during her marriage.

## Numbers 5:30

It also covered a husband who felt jealous and suspected his wife.

He brought her to stand before the LORD.

The priest followed the whole law.

## Numbers 5:31

Under this law, the husband would be free from guilt.

If the wife had done wrong, she would answer for her own sin.

**Pause and answer**

What did the law say would happen to a faithful wife?
