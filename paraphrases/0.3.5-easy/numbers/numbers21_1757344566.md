# Numbers 21 — The bronze snake and the journey to Bashan

**About this chapter**

On the road around Edom, snakes bite the people. Moses prays for them. As Israel travels on, the people sing at a well and face kings who fight them.

## The king of Arad attacks

## Numbers 21:1

The Canaanite king of Arad lived in the Negev.

He heard that Israel was on the road to Atharim.

He attacked and took some Israelites prisoner.

## Numbers 21:2

Israel made a promise to the LORD.

They said, “If you give us victory over these people, we will destroy their cities completely.”

## Numbers 21:3

The LORD heard Israel and gave them victory over the Canaanites.

The Israelites destroyed those people and their cities as promised.

They named the place Hormah.

## Moses prays for the people

## Numbers 21:4

The people left Mount Hor on the road toward the Red Sea.

They took this road to go around Edom.

Along the way, they grew impatient.

## Numbers 21:5

They spoke against God and Moses.

“Why did you bring us out of Egypt to die here?

We have no bread or water.

We hate this food!”

## Numbers 21:6

The LORD sent snakes among the people.

The snakes bit many Israelites, and their venom killed them.

## Numbers 21:7

The people came to Moses.

They said, “We sinned when we spoke against the LORD and against you.

Please ask the LORD to take the snakes away.” Moses prayed for them.

## Numbers 21:8

The LORD told Moses to make the shape of a fiery snake and put it on a pole.

Anyone the snakes had bitten could look at it and live.

## Numbers 21:9

Moses made a snake out of bronze.

He put it on a pole.

When people who had been bitten looked at it, they lived.

![A bronze-colored snake winds around an upright pole.](../pictures/bronze-snake-on-pole.svg)

Moses lifted the bronze snake where the people could see it.

**Reading help**

Bronze is a metal. The snake on the pole was the one Moses made.

What did the people ask Moses to do for them?

## Camps along the way

## Numbers 21:10

The Israelites traveled on and camped at Oboth.

## Numbers 21:11

From Oboth they went to Iye-abarim.

They camped there in the wilderness east of Moab.

## Numbers 21:12

Then they camped in the Valley of Zered.

## Numbers 21:13

Next they camped across the Arnon, in the wilderness that reaches into Amorite land.

The Arnon marked the border between Moab and the Amorites.

## Numbers 21:14

The Book of the Wars of the LORD names places along the way.

It mentions Waheb in Suphah and the river valleys of the Arnon.

## Numbers 21:15

It describes the slopes of those valleys.

They reach toward Ar and run along Moab’s border.

**Pause and answer**

Which river marked Moab’s border with the Amorites?

## A song at the well

## Numbers 21:16

Israel went on to Beer.

There the LORD told Moses, “Gather the people at the well.

I will give them water.”

## Numbers 21:17

Then Israel sang, “Let water rise from the well!

Everyone, sing to the well!”

## Numbers 21:18

They sang about the leaders who dug it.

The leaders used their royal rods and walking sticks.

From the wilderness Israel went to Mattanah.

## Numbers 21:19

From Mattanah they went to Nahaliel.

From Nahaliel they went to Bamoth.

## Numbers 21:20

From Bamoth they reached a valley in Moab.

There the peak of Pisgah looks out over the wasteland.

**Follow the journey**

Wilderness → Mattanah → Nahaliel → Bamoth → valley in Moab near Pisgah

**Pause and answer**

What did the LORD promise the people at the well?

## Sihon refuses to let Israel pass

## Numbers 21:21

Israel sent messengers to Sihon, king of the Amorites.

## Numbers 21:22

They said, “Please let us cross your land.

We will stay out of fields and vineyards.

We will leave the water in your wells.

We will stay on the King’s Highway until we leave your land.”

## Numbers 21:23

Sihon refused.

He gathered his whole army and marched into the wilderness.

At Jahaz he fought Israel.

## Numbers 21:24

The Israelites killed Sihon with the sword.

They took his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok.

They stopped at the Ammonite border because it had strong defenses.

## Numbers 21:25

Israel took all the Amorite cities and lived in them.

These included Heshbon and all its villages.

## Numbers 21:26

Heshbon had belonged to Sihon.

Sihon had fought an earlier king of Moab.

He had taken that king’s land as far as the Arnon.

## A poem about Heshbon

## Numbers 21:27

That is why the poets called out, “Come to Heshbon!

Build Sihon’s city again!”

## Numbers 21:28

The poets described a fire spreading from Heshbon, Sihon’s city.

It burned up Ar in Moab and the rulers on the heights of the Arnon.

## Numbers 21:29

The poets cried, “What sorrow for Moab!

You people of Chemosh have been destroyed.

Chemosh let his sons flee from their homes.

King Sihon of the Amorites took his daughters prisoner.”

**Reading help**

Chemosh was the god the people of Moab worshiped. The poem calls those people his sons and daughters. Its picture of a spreading fire describes the destruction caused by war.

## Numbers 21:30

The poem ends, “We defeated them.

We destroyed their towns from Heshbon to Dibon.

The ruin reached Nophah and stretched to Medeba.”

**Pause and answer**

Which city did the poets call on people to rebuild?

## Israel reaches Bashan

## Numbers 21:31

So Israel settled in the Amorites’ land.

## Numbers 21:32

Moses sent scouts to Jazer.

Then Israel captured its villages.

The Israelites drove out the Amorites who lived there.

## Numbers 21:33

Israel turned onto the road to Bashan.

Og, king of Bashan, came out with his whole army.

They met Israel for battle at Edrei.

## Numbers 21:34

The LORD told Moses, “Do not fear Og.

I have given you victory over him, his people and his land.

Treat him as you treated Sihon, the Amorite king of Heshbon.”

## Numbers 21:35

Israel killed Og, his sons and his whole army.

None of them survived.

Israel took possession of Og’s land.
