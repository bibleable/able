# Numbers 14 — Israel turns back from the promised land

**About this chapter**

The people are afraid to enter Canaan. Joshua and Caleb urge them to trust the LORD. Moses asks the LORD to forgive the people.

## The people want to go back to Egypt

## Numbers 14:1

Everyone cried out loud.

That night the people wept.

## Numbers 14:2

The Israelites complained to Moses and Aaron.

They said, “If only we had died in Egypt!

Or here in the wilderness!”

## Numbers 14:3

They asked, “Why is the LORD taking us there to die in battle?

Our enemies will take our wives and children.

Wouldn’t we be better off back in Egypt?”

## Numbers 14:4

They said to each other, “Let’s choose a leader and go back to Egypt.”

**Pause and answer**

Where did the people want their new leader to take them?

## Joshua and Caleb speak

## Numbers 14:5

Moses and Aaron fell face down in front of everyone.

## Numbers 14:6

Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh had explored the land.

They tore their clothes in distress.

## Numbers 14:7

They told the people, “We traveled through that land.

It is very good.”

## Numbers 14:8

Joshua and Caleb said the land flowed with milk and honey.

If the LORD was pleased with Israel, he would lead them there.

He would give them the land.

## Numbers 14:9

They said, “Do not turn against the LORD.

Do not fear the people there.

They will be like bread for us.

Their protection is gone.

The LORD is with us.

Do not fear them.”

**Reading help**

Milk and honey picture a land with plenty of food. Calling the people “bread for us” means Joshua and Caleb believed Israel could defeat them easily.

## Numbers 14:10

The people threatened to kill Joshua and Caleb with stones.

Then everyone saw the LORD’s glory at the Tent of Meeting.

**Pause and answer**

Who said the LORD was with Israel?

## Moses speaks for the people

## Numbers 14:11

The LORD asked Moses, “How long will these people treat me with scorn?

They have seen the great signs I have done among them.

How long will they refuse to trust me?”

## Numbers 14:12

The LORD said he would destroy the people with a plague.

He would make Moses’ family into a greater, stronger nation.

## Numbers 14:13

Moses answered, “The Egyptians will hear about this.

You used your great power to bring Israel out of Egypt.”

## Numbers 14:14

Moses said the Egyptians would tell the people of Canaan.

Those people already knew the LORD was with Israel.

They had heard that Israel saw him face to face.

His cloud stood over Israel.

He led them in a tall cloud by day and in fire by night.

## Numbers 14:15

Moses asked the LORD to consider what other nations would say.

They knew his reputation.

What if he killed all his people at once?

## Numbers 14:16

Moses said those nations would say, “The LORD could not bring Israel into the land he promised.

So he killed them in the wilderness.”

**Pause and answer**

What did Moses think other nations would say?

## Moses asks the LORD to forgive

## Numbers 14:17

Moses prayed, “My Lord, show your great power now, just as you said.”

## Numbers 14:18

Moses recalled the LORD’s own words.

The LORD is slow to get angry.

His faithful love is great.

He forgives people who sin and rebel.

Yet he punishes the guilty.

The wrong that parents do brings punishment on their children, even three and four generations later.

**Reading help**

A generation is one step in a family, from parents to children.

## Numbers 14:19

Moses asked, “Because your love is so great, please forgive these people.

You have forgiven them ever since they left Egypt.”

## Numbers 14:20

The LORD answered Moses, “I have forgiven them as you asked.”

**Pause and answer**

How did the LORD answer Moses’ prayer?

## The LORD makes a promise about Caleb

## Numbers 14:21

The LORD made a solemn promise.

It was as sure as his own life.

It was as sure as his glory filling the whole earth.

## Numbers 14:22

The people had seen his glory.

They had seen his signs in Egypt and the wilderness.

Yet ten times they had tested him and disobeyed.

## Numbers 14:23

Those who treated the LORD with scorn would never see the land he had promised their ancestors.

## Numbers 14:24

The LORD said, “My servant Caleb has a different spirit.

He has followed me with his whole heart.

I will bring him into the land he explored.

His descendants will receive it as their own.”

## Numbers 14:25

The Amalekites and Canaanites lived in the valleys.

The LORD told Israel to turn back the next day.

They must take the wilderness road toward the Red Sea.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the LORD promise Caleb the land?

## Who will enter the land?

## Numbers 14:26

Then the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron.

## Numbers 14:27

He asked how long this wicked gathering would complain against him.

He had heard what the Israelites were saying.

## Numbers 14:28

They must tell Israel what the LORD had decided.

As surely as he lived, he would do exactly what he had heard them say.

## Numbers 14:29

Those who had complained against him would die in the wilderness.

This meant those aged twenty or older who had been counted in the census.

## Numbers 14:30

The LORD had promised them a land to live in.

Of those counted, only Caleb son of Jephunneh and Joshua son of Nun would enter it.

## Numbers 14:31

The parents had said enemies would take their children.

The LORD would bring those children into the land.

They would enjoy the land their parents had refused.

**Pause and answer**

What did the LORD promise the children?

## One year for each day

## Numbers 14:32

The parents themselves would die in the wilderness.

## Numbers 14:33

Their children would tend flocks in the wilderness for forty years.

They would suffer because their parents had been unfaithful.

This would last until the last of those parents died there.

## Numbers 14:34

The scouts had explored the land for forty days.

Israel would bear the punishment for forty years.

Each day stood for one year.

They would learn what it meant to have the LORD against them.

**Reading help**

| Who? | How long? |
| --- | --- |
| The scouts exploring the land | Forty days |
| Israel living in the wilderness | Forty years |

## Numbers 14:35

The LORD had spoken.

He would do what he said to those who had joined together against him.

They would die there in the wilderness.

**Pause and answer**

How many years in the wilderness matched the forty days?

## Two scouts remain alive

## Numbers 14:36

Moses had sent men to explore the land.

Some returned with a bad report.

They stirred up the whole gathering to complain against Moses.

## Numbers 14:37

The scouts who gave the bad report died of a plague before the LORD.

## Numbers 14:38

Of all the scouts, only two remained alive.

They were Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh.

## Numbers 14:39

Moses told the Israelites what the LORD had said.

The people were filled with grief.

**Pause and answer**

Which two scouts survived?

## The people go up to the hills

## Numbers 14:40

Early the next morning they headed for the ridge in the hills.

They said, “We have sinned.

Now we will go where the LORD promised.”

## Numbers 14:41

Moses asked, “Why are you disobeying the LORD’s command?

You will not succeed.”

## Numbers 14:42

Moses warned them to stay.

The LORD would not go with them.

Their enemies would defeat them if they went up.

## Numbers 14:43

The Amalekites and Canaanites would meet them there.

The Israelites would die in battle.

They had turned away from the LORD, and he would not go with them.

## Numbers 14:44

Even so, the people went up to the ridge.

Moses stayed in camp.

The ark of the LORD’s covenant stayed there too.

**Reading help**

The ark was the sacred chest that held the record of the LORD’s agreement with Israel.

## Numbers 14:45

The Amalekites and Canaanites who lived in those hills came down.

They attacked the Israelites and drove them back as far as Hormah.

**Pause and answer**

Who stayed in camp when the people went up to the ridge?
