# Joshua 5 — Israel keeps the Passover at Gilgal

**About this chapter**

Israel camps near Jericho. The people eat food from Canaan. Then Joshua meets the commander of the LORD’s army.

## The kings hear about the river

## Joshua 5:1

News reached the Amorite kings west of the Jordan River.

The Canaanite kings along the coast also heard.

The LORD had dried up the Jordan for Israel.

The river stayed dry until everyone crossed.

The kings feared the Israelites and lost all courage.

## A new generation receives the covenant sign

## Joshua 5:2

The LORD told Joshua to make knives from flint.

He told Joshua to circumcise the males of Israel again.

**Reading help**

Flint is a hard stone.

Circumcision means removing the skin covering the tip of the penis.

For Israel, circumcision was a sign of the covenant.

The covenant was God’s solemn agreement with his people.

Here a new generation would receive the sign.

## Joshua 5:3

Joshua made the flint knives.

He circumcised the males of Israel at Gibeath-haaraloth.

## Joshua 5:4

Why did Joshua do this?

The fighting men who left Egypt had all died.

They died on the journey through the wilderness.

## Joshua 5:5

The males who left Egypt had already received circumcision.

But boys were born during the journey.

Nobody had circumcised those boys in the wilderness.

## Joshua 5:6

Israel wandered in the wilderness for forty years.

The fighting men who left Egypt disobeyed the LORD.

The LORD swore those men would never see the land.

They all died in the wilderness.

The LORD had promised their ancestors a land flowing with milk and honey.

**Reading help**

Milk and honey describe a land with plenty to eat.

## Joshua 5:7

The LORD gave their sons their fathers’ place.

Nobody had circumcised these sons during the journey.

So Joshua circumcised them at Gilgal.

## Joshua 5:8

Afterward, the people stayed in camp.

They stayed until those who received circumcision had healed.

## Joshua 5:9

The LORD told Joshua, “Today I have rolled away the shame of Egypt from you.”

People called that place Gilgal.

It still had that name when this account was written.

**Reading help**

“Rolled away” means the LORD took away their shame.

The name Gilgal sounds like the Hebrew word for rolling.

## Israel eats food from the land

## Joshua 5:10

Israel camped at Gilgal on the flat land near Jericho.

On the fourteenth day of the month, evening came.

That evening the people celebrated the Passover.

**Reading help**

At Passover, Israel remembered how the LORD spared their families in Egypt.

## Joshua 5:11

The next day they ate food grown in the land.

They ate bread made without yeast.

They also ate roasted grain.

## Joshua 5:12

The next day the manna stopped coming.

Manna was the food God gave Israel in the wilderness.

That year the Israelites ate crops grown in Canaan.

## Joshua meets the commander

## Joshua 5:13

Near Jericho, Joshua looked up.

A man stood facing him, holding a drawn sword.

Joshua walked up to the man.

“Are you on our side?” Joshua asked.

“Or are you on our enemies’ side?”

## Joshua 5:14

“Neither,” the man answered.

“I have come to lead the LORD’s army.”

Joshua bowed with his face to the ground in respect.

Joshua asked, “My lord, what do you want to tell me, your servant?”

## Joshua 5:15

The commander of the LORD’s army answered Joshua.

“Take off your sandals. The ground where you stand is holy.”

So Joshua took off his sandals.

**Reading help**

Holy ground is a place set apart for God.
