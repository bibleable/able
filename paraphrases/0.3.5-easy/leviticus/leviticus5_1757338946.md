# Leviticus 5 — Admitting wrong and bringing an offering

**About this chapter**

The LORD tells Israel how to respond to a wrong. Someone who cannot afford an animal from the flock can still bring an offering.

## When someone becomes guilty

## Leviticus 5:1

Suppose there is a public call for witnesses.

Someone saw what happened or learned about it.

If that person stays silent, they bear the guilt for that sin.

## Leviticus 5:2

A person might touch the dead body of an unclean animal.

It might be a wild animal, a farm animal or a crawling creature.

Even without knowing it, the person becomes unclean and guilty.

**Reading help**

Here “unclean” means unfit to take part in worship under Israel’s law.

## Leviticus 5:3

A person might touch something from another person that makes them unclean.

They might not know it at the time.

When they realize what happened, they are guilty.

## Leviticus 5:4

Someone might make a solemn promise without thinking.

The promise might be to do good or harm.

Later the person realizes what they promised.

They are guilty of making that careless promise.

**Pause and answer**

What should a witness do when there is a public call for witnesses?

## Admit the wrong

## Leviticus 5:5

Anyone guilty in one of these ways must admit what they did wrong.

## Leviticus 5:6

The person brings an offering to the LORD for that wrong.

They bring a female lamb or a female goat from the flock as a sin offering.

The priest makes atonement for the person’s sin.

**Reading help**

To make atonement means to deal with the sin that has come between a person and God.

**Pause and answer**

What must the person admit?

## Two birds for someone who cannot afford a lamb

## Leviticus 5:7

Someone who cannot afford a lamb may bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the LORD.

One bird is a sin offering.

The other is a burnt offering.

## Leviticus 5:8

The person brings both birds to the priest.

The priest offers the bird for sin first.

He twists its head at the front of the neck.

The head stays attached.

## Leviticus 5:9

The priest sprinkles some blood on the side of the altar.

The rest drains out at the base.

This bird is the sin offering.

## Leviticus 5:10

The priest offers the second bird as a burnt offering.

He follows the rule for that offering.

In this way the priest makes atonement for the person.

The LORD forgives their sin.

**Pause and answer**

Which bird does the priest offer first?

## Flour for someone who cannot afford two birds

## Leviticus 5:11

Someone who cannot afford the two birds may bring fine flour.

They bring one tenth of an ephah.

They leave out olive oil and frankincense because this is a sin offering.

**Reading help**

An ephah was a measure for dry food. Frankincense gives off a scent when it burns.

## Leviticus 5:12

The person brings the flour to the priest.

The priest takes a handful as a reminder before the LORD.

He burns it on top of the food offerings on the altar.

It is a sin offering.

## Leviticus 5:13

In this way the priest makes atonement for any of these sins.

The LORD forgives the person.

The priest keeps the flour that is left, as with a grain offering.

| What could the person afford? | What could they bring? |
| --- | --- |
| An animal from the flock | A female lamb or goat |
| Two birds | Two turtledoves or two young pigeons |
| Less than the cost of two birds | One tenth of an ephah of fine flour |

**Pause and answer**

What could a person bring if two birds cost too much?

## Repay the loss

## Leviticus 5:14

Then the LORD spoke to Moses.

## Leviticus 5:15

A person might misuse something holy that belongs to the LORD without meaning to.

The person must bring the LORD a ram without any defect as a guilt offering.

The ram must be worth the proper amount in silver.

Use the holy tent’s standard weight for a shekel of silver.

## Leviticus 5:16

The person must repay the value of the holy thing they harmed.

They must add one fifth of its value and give the payment to the priest.

The priest makes atonement with the ram.

The LORD forgives the person.

**Reading help**

Suppose the loss was worth five shekels. One fifth of five is one. The payment would be six shekels in all.

**Pause and answer**

If the loss was worth five shekels, how much extra would the person pay?

## A wrong done without knowing

## Leviticus 5:17

Someone might break the LORD’s command without knowing it.

That person is still guilty and must bear the punishment.

## Leviticus 5:18

The person brings the priest a ram from the flock as a guilt offering.

The ram must have no defect and be worth the proper amount.

The priest makes atonement for the wrong the person did without knowing it.

The LORD forgives the person.

## Leviticus 5:19

The ram is a guilt offering.

The person who brings it is indeed guilty before the LORD.

**Pause and answer**

Who forgives the person after the priest makes atonement?
