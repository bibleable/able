# Leviticus 25 — Rest for the land and the Year of Jubilee

**About this chapter**

The LORD tells Israel when to let the land rest. He gives families a way to return to their land. He also gives rules about loans, workers and slavery.

## The land rests

## Leviticus 25:1

The LORD spoke to Moses on Mount Sinai.

## Leviticus 25:2

He gave Moses a message for Israel.

The LORD was giving the people a land.

Once they entered it, even the land must have a Sabbath rest for the LORD.

## Leviticus 25:3

For six years the people could plant their fields.

They could trim their grapevines and gather their crops.

![Grapes grow in a bunch on a vine.](../pictures/vine-and-grapes.svg)

For six years the people can tend the vines and gather grapes.

## Leviticus 25:4

The seventh year was a Sabbath for the LORD.

The land must have a full year of rest.

The people must leave their fields unplanted and their vines untrimmed.

## Leviticus 25:5

Grain might grow from seeds left after harvest.

The people must not harvest that grain or gather a crop from the untended vines.

They must let the land rest.

## Leviticus 25:6

Everyone could eat what grew on its own that year.

This included the household and its male and female servants.

Hired workers and foreigners staying with them could eat too.

## Leviticus 25:7

Farm animals and wild animals could eat it too.

Whatever grew could serve as food.

## Announce the Jubilee

## Leviticus 25:8

The people must count seven groups of seven years.

That makes forty-nine years.

## Leviticus 25:9

Then they must sound a horn throughout the land.

It must sound on day ten of the seventh month.

That day was the Day of Atonement.

## Leviticus 25:10

They must set year fifty apart as holy.

They must announce freedom to everyone in the land.

This year was called the Jubilee.

Each person was to return to their family and their family land.

**Reading help**

A Jubilee was a special year of return. People who had sold family land could go home to it.

## Leviticus 25:11

In this fiftieth year, the people must not plant.

They must not harvest grain that grew by itself.

They must not gather a harvest from untended vines.

## Leviticus 25:12

The Jubilee was a holy year.

They could eat only what they took straight from the field.

## Leviticus 25:13

In the Year of Jubilee, each person returned to their own land.

**Pause and answer**

What did people return to at Jubilee?

## Count the harvests

## Leviticus 25:14

When neighbors bought or sold land, neither could cheat the other.

## Leviticus 25:15

The buyer must count the years since the last Jubilee.

The seller must count the harvests left before the next Jubilee.

## Leviticus 25:16

More harvests meant a higher price.

Fewer harvests meant a lower price.

The buyer was paying for those harvests.

## Leviticus 25:17

They must not take advantage of each other.

They must show deep respect for the LORD their God.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the price go down as Jubilee came closer?

## Food during the year of rest

## Leviticus 25:18

The LORD told Israel to follow all his laws carefully.

Then they could live safely in the land.

## Leviticus 25:19

The land would produce food for them.

They could eat their fill and live in safety.

## Leviticus 25:20

The people might ask, “What will we eat in year seven? We will not plant or gather a harvest.”

## Leviticus 25:21

The LORD promised to bless their land in year six.

That harvest would feed them for three years.

## Leviticus 25:22

In year eight they would plant again.

They would still eat food from the earlier harvest.

It would last until the harvest came in during year nine.

**Reading help**

| Year | Where the food comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Six | The people gather an extra-large harvest. |
| Seven | The land rests. The people have stored food. |
| Eight | The people plant and keep eating stored food. |
| Nine | The new harvest comes in. |

**Pause and answer**

Which harvest fed the people while the land rested?

## Buying family land back

## Leviticus 25:23

The LORD said the land belonged to him.

The Israelites lived there as his guests.

No one could sell the land forever.

## Leviticus 25:24

For every piece of land, the people must allow a way for the family to buy it back.

**Reading help**

Buying back what a family has sold is called redemption.

## Leviticus 25:25

An Israelite might grow poor and sell some land.

The nearest relative could buy it back for the family.

## Leviticus 25:26

The seller might have no relative to buy it back.

Later the seller might gain enough money to do so.

## Leviticus 25:27

The seller must count the years since the sale.

Then the seller pays the buyer for the years still left.

The seller can return to the land.

## Leviticus 25:28

If the seller lacks the money, the buyer keeps the land until Jubilee.

At Jubilee the buyer must give it back.

Then the seller can return home.

**Pause and answer**

When could the seller return if the family could not buy the land back?

## Houses and grazing fields

## Leviticus 25:29

Someone might sell a house inside a walled city.

The seller has one full year to buy it back.

## Leviticus 25:30

After a full year, the buyer and the buyer’s descendants keep that house forever.

It does not go back at Jubilee.

## Leviticus 25:31

Houses in villages without walls follow the rules for fields.

The family can buy the house back.

At Jubilee it goes back to the family.

## Leviticus 25:32

The Levites have their own towns.

They always have the right to buy back their houses there.

## Leviticus 25:33

A Levite can buy back a house sold in a Levite town.

The house must go back at Jubilee.

These houses are the Levites’ share of property in Israel.

## Leviticus 25:34

The fields where their animals graze belong to them forever.

They must not sell those fields around their towns.

**Pause and answer**

How long could a Levite buy back a house in a Levite town?

## Help a neighbor who is poor

## Leviticus 25:35

A fellow Israelite might become too poor to support himself.

Help him as you would help a foreigner staying with you.

Then he can keep living among you.

## Leviticus 25:36

Do not make him pay extra or make a profit from him.

Show deep respect for your God.

Let your neighbor keep living among you.

## Leviticus 25:37

When you lend him silver, do not charge interest.

When you sell him food, do not charge extra for profit.

**Reading help**

Interest is extra money someone must pay for a loan.

## Leviticus 25:38

The LORD reminded Israel that he brought them out of Egypt.

He did this to give them Canaan and to be their God.

**Pause and answer**

How could a neighbor help someone who needed to borrow silver?

## An Israelite worker goes home

## Leviticus 25:39

A poor Israelite might sell himself to another Israelite.

The buyer must not make him work as a slave.

## Leviticus 25:40

He must live there like a hired worker or a temporary resident.

He works there until the Year of Jubilee.

## Leviticus 25:41

Then he and his children go free.

He can go home to his extended family.

He can return to the land his ancestors owned.

## Leviticus 25:42

The LORD said the Israelites were his servants.

He had brought them out of Egypt.

No one was to sell them as slaves.

## Leviticus 25:43

Israelites must not treat these workers harshly.

They must show deep respect for their God.

**Pause and answer**

Who went home with the worker at Jubilee?

## What the law allowed for foreign slaves

## Leviticus 25:44

This law allowed Israelites to buy men and women as slaves from the nations around them.

## Leviticus 25:45

They could also buy people from foreign families living among them.

This included family members born in Israel.

The law allowed Israelites to own these people.

## Leviticus 25:46

Israelites could leave these enslaved people to their sons as property.

They could keep them as slaves for life.

The law forbade an Israelite to rule harshly over another Israelite.

**Pause and answer**

What could happen to an enslaved person when the owner died?

## Buying freedom

## Leviticus 25:47

A foreigner living in Israel might grow rich.

An Israelite living nearby might grow poor.

He might sell himself to the foreigner or to someone in the foreigner’s family.

## Leviticus 25:48

After the sale, the Israelite still had a right to freedom if someone paid for his release.

One of his brothers could pay.

## Leviticus 25:49

His uncle, cousin or another close relative could also pay.

If he gained enough money, he could pay for his own freedom.

## Leviticus 25:50

The worker and the buyer count the years from the sale to Jubilee.

They use a hired worker’s daily pay to work out the price for those years.

## Leviticus 25:51

If many years remain, the worker pays back a large share of the original price for his freedom.

## Leviticus 25:52

If only a few years remain, he pays less.

He pays only for the years left before Jubilee.

**Pause and answer**

How did the years left before Jubilee affect the price of freedom?

## Freedom at Jubilee

## Leviticus 25:53

The owner must treat him like a worker hired each year.

The Israelites must not let the foreign owner treat him harshly.

## Leviticus 25:54

Perhaps no one pays for his freedom.

Even so, he and his children go free at Jubilee.

## Leviticus 25:55

The LORD said, “The Israelites are my servants.

I brought them out of Egypt.

I am the LORD your God.”

**Pause and answer**

What happened at Jubilee if no one had paid for the worker’s freedom?
