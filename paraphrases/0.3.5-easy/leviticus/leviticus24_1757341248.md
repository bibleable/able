# Leviticus 24 — Lamps, bread and a law for everyone

**About this chapter**

Aaron looks after the lamps. The priests set out bread each Sabbath. Later, a fight in the camp brings a man before Moses.

## Aaron tends the lamps

## Leviticus 24:1

The LORD spoke to Moses.

## Leviticus 24:2

He told Moses to ask Israel for pure olive oil.

The people pressed olives to get the oil.

The lamps needed this oil to keep burning.

## Leviticus 24:3

Aaron cared for the lamps in the Tent of Meeting.

They stood outside the curtain in front of the chest holding God’s law.

Aaron kept the lamps burning before the LORD from evening until morning.

This rule was for every generation.

## Leviticus 24:4

The lamps stood on a stand of pure gold.

Aaron was to care for them regularly before the LORD.

**Pause and answer**

When did Aaron keep the lamps burning?

## Twelve loaves on the table

## Leviticus 24:5

The LORD also told Moses to bake twelve loaves.

Each loaf needed two tenths of an ephah of fine flour.

**Reading help**

An ephah was a measure for dry food like flour.

## Leviticus 24:6

Moses was to put the loaves on the pure gold table before the LORD.

He was to make two rows, with six loaves in each row.

## Leviticus 24:7

He was to put pure frankincense beside each row.

The frankincense served as the part offered to the LORD in memory of the bread.

This was a food offering.

**Reading help**

Frankincense comes from tree sap. It gives off a sweet smell when burned.

## Leviticus 24:8

Every Sabbath, the priests set out the bread before the LORD.

They did this on behalf of Israel.

It was part of Israel’s lasting covenant with the LORD.

**Reading help**

A covenant is a lasting agreement.

## Leviticus 24:9

The bread belonged to Aaron and his sons.

They were to eat it in a holy place.

It was a most holy part of the LORD’s food offerings.

The priests would always receive this share.

**Pause and answer**

Who could eat the bread in the holy place?

## A fight in the camp

## Leviticus 24:10

One man had an Israelite mother and an Egyptian father.

He went among the Israelites in the camp.

There he fought with an Israelite man.

## Leviticus 24:11

The man with the Egyptian father cursed the LORD’s name.

The people brought him to Moses.

His mother was Shelomith, the daughter of Dibri.

She belonged to the tribe of Dan.

**Reading help**

To blaspheme God’s name means to speak about God with deep disrespect.

## Leviticus 24:12

The people kept the man under guard.

They waited to learn what the LORD wanted them to do.

**Pause and answer**

Why did the people wait?

## The LORD answers Moses

## Leviticus 24:13

Then the LORD spoke to Moses.

## Leviticus 24:14

The LORD said the man must go outside the camp.

Those who heard the curse were to lay their hands on his head.

The whole community was to stone him.

## Leviticus 24:15

The LORD told Moses to give Israel this law.

Anyone who cursed their God would answer for that sin.

## Leviticus 24:16

Under this law, the penalty for cursing the LORD’s name was death by stoning.

The whole community carried out the sentence.

The law applied to people born there and to foreigners living there.

**Pause and answer**

Who heard the LORD’s answer and gave it to Israel?

## One law for everyone

## Leviticus 24:17

If someone took another person’s life, the law required death.

## Leviticus 24:18

If someone killed another person’s animal, they owed the owner a living animal in its place.

## Leviticus 24:19

If someone injured a neighbor, the penalty had to match the injury.

## Leviticus 24:20

The law said, “A broken bone for a broken bone, an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth.” The person who caused the injury faced the same injury.

## Leviticus 24:21

Anyone who killed an animal had to replace it.

Anyone who killed a person faced the death penalty.

## Leviticus 24:22

The same law applied to foreigners living among the Israelites and to people born there.

The LORD said, “I am the LORD your God.”

## Leviticus 24:23

Moses spoke to the Israelites.

They took the man who had cursed outside the camp.

There they stoned him to death.

The Israelites did what the LORD had told Moses.
