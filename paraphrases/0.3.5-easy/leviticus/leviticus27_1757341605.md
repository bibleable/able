# Leviticus 27 — Promises and gifts to the LORD

**About this chapter**

Someone in Israel could make a promise to the LORD. This chapter explains what people gave and which gifts they could buy back.

## A promise involving a person

## Leviticus 27:1

The LORD spoke to Moses.

## Leviticus 27:2

He told Moses to teach the Israelites about a special vow.

Someone could promise the LORD silver on behalf of a person.

That person’s age affected the amount.

**Reading help**

A vow is a serious promise to God.

## Leviticus 27:3

For a man aged twenty to sixty, the amount was fifty shekels of silver.

The people used the weight called the sanctuary shekel.

## Leviticus 27:4

For a woman in that age group, the amount was thirty shekels.

## Leviticus 27:5

For ages five to twenty, the amount was twenty shekels for a boy or young man.

It was ten shekels for a girl or young woman.

## Leviticus 27:6

For ages one month to five years, the amount was five shekels of silver for a boy.

It was three shekels of silver for a girl.

## Leviticus 27:7

For someone sixty or older, the amount was fifteen shekels for a man.

It was ten shekels for a woman.

## Leviticus 27:8

The person making the promise might be too poor to pay.

They must bring the person named in the promise to the priest.

The priest sets an amount the person making the promise can afford.

**Pause and answer**

Who sets the amount when the giver is too poor to pay?

## An animal promised to the LORD

## Leviticus 27:9

Someone might promise the LORD an animal suitable for an offering.

Once given, that animal is holy.

It belongs to the LORD.

## Leviticus 27:10

The giver must not exchange it for another animal.

This holds even if the other animal is better or worse.

If the giver does exchange it, both animals are holy.

## Leviticus 27:11

Someone might promise an animal the law calls unclean.

Israel cannot offer that kind of animal on the altar.

The giver must bring it to the priest.

## Leviticus 27:12

The priest sets its price, whether high or low.

That price stands.

## Leviticus 27:13

The owner can buy the animal back.

The owner pays its price plus one fifth more.

**Reading help**

To redeem something means to buy it back. If the price is five shekels, one fifth is one more shekel. The owner pays six shekels in all.

**Pause and answer**

How much extra does the owner pay to buy an animal back?

## A house or family field

## Leviticus 27:14

Someone might set apart a house for the LORD.

The priest looks at its condition and sets a price.

That price stands.

## Leviticus 27:15

The person who gave the house can buy it back.

They pay its price plus one fifth more.

Then the house belongs to them again.

## Leviticus 27:16

Someone might give the LORD part of their family land.

The price depends on how much seed the field needs.

Each homer of barley seed means fifty shekels of silver.

**Reading help**

A homer was a measure for grain. A field needing more seed had a higher price.

## Leviticus 27:17

If the owner gives the field during the Year of Jubilee, the full price stands.

## Leviticus 27:18

If the owner gives it later, the priest counts the years until the next Jubilee.

With fewer years left, the priest sets a lower price.

**Reading help**

Jubilee was the fiftieth year. Family land usually went back to the family that first owned it.

## Leviticus 27:19

The owner can buy the field back.

They pay its price plus one fifth more.

Then the field belongs to them again.

**Pause and answer**

What happens to the field’s price as Jubilee gets closer?

## What happens to a field at Jubilee

## Leviticus 27:20

The owner might leave the field without buying it back.

Or the owner might sell it to someone else.

In either case, the owner loses the right to buy it back.

## Leviticus 27:21

At Jubilee the field becomes holy.

It is like land given to the LORD for good.

It belongs to the priests.

## Leviticus 27:22

Someone might give the LORD a field they bought.

That field came from another family’s land.

## Leviticus 27:23

The priest sets its price for the years until Jubilee.

That same day the giver pays the full amount.

The payment is a holy gift to the LORD.

## Leviticus 27:24

At Jubilee the field goes back to its original owner.

That is the person who sold it.

## Leviticus 27:25

Every price uses the sanctuary shekel.

Twenty smaller weights called gerahs make one shekel.

**Pause and answer**

Who gets the purchased field back at Jubilee?

## What already belongs to the LORD

## Leviticus 27:26

No one can promise the LORD the firstborn of their livestock as a new gift.

The firstborn already belongs to the LORD.

This holds for both cattle and sheep.

## Leviticus 27:27

If the firstborn animal is unclean, the owner can buy it back.

The owner pays its price plus one fifth more.

Otherwise someone may buy it for the set price.

## Leviticus 27:28

Someone might set apart a person, an animal or inherited land for the LORD for good.

Such a gift is most holy to the LORD.

No one may sell it or buy it back.

## Leviticus 27:29

Under this law, a person set apart for destruction faced death.

No one could pay to release that person.

**Pause and answer**

Why could the firstborn lamb never count as a new gift?

## Every tenth animal

## Leviticus 27:30

One tenth of the crops belongs to the LORD.

This includes grain from the land and fruit from trees.

That share is holy to the LORD.

## Leviticus 27:31

Someone might want to buy back part of that tenth.

They must pay its value plus one fifth more.

## Leviticus 27:32

A shepherd counts the animals passing under the shepherd’s staff.

Every tenth animal in the herd or flock belongs to the LORD.

## Leviticus 27:33

The shepherd must take the tenth animal as it comes.

The shepherd must not choose by quality or exchange it.

If the shepherd exchanges it, both animals become holy.

No one may buy them back.

## Leviticus 27:34

These are the commands the LORD gave Moses for Israel on Mount Sinai.
