# Leviticus 13 — The priest examines skin, cloth and leather

**About this chapter**

The LORD gives Israel rules about marks on skin, cloth and leather. Sometimes the priest can decide at once. Sometimes he must wait and look again.

## A spot on the skin

## Leviticus 13:1

The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron.

## Leviticus 13:2

A person might have a swelling, rash or bright spot on the skin.

It might be a skin disease.

Someone must bring the person to Aaron or to one of his sons who serves as a priest.

## Leviticus 13:3

The priest examines the sore.

Has the hair there turned white?

Does the sore look deeper than the skin?

If both are true, it is a skin disease.

The priest declares the person unclean.

**Reading help**

In these rules, “unclean” describes a person’s standing under Israel’s law for worship. The priest announces the decision.

**Pause and answer**

Who examined the sore?

## Waiting and looking again

## Leviticus 13:4

Suppose the white spot looks only skin deep.

The hair there has not turned white.

The priest keeps the person apart for seven days.

## Leviticus 13:5

On the seventh day the priest looks again.

If the sore looks the same and has not spread, the person stays apart for seven more days.

![After seven days the priest checks the sore. If it is unchanged, another seven days follow.](../pictures/priest-checks-sore-again.svg)

The second wait applies when the sore has stayed the same.

## Leviticus 13:6

On the seventh day the priest looks again.

If the sore has faded without spreading, it is a rash.

The priest declares the person clean.

The person washes their clothes and is clean.

## Leviticus 13:7

Suppose the rash spreads after the priest has examined the person for cleansing.

The person must go back to the priest.

## Leviticus 13:8

The priest looks again.

If the rash has spread, it is a skin disease.

He declares the person unclean.

**Pause and answer**

What did the person do if the rash later spread?

## White swelling and raw skin

## Leviticus 13:9

When someone develops a skin disease, bring the person to the priest.

## Leviticus 13:10

The priest looks for a white swelling with white hair in it.

He checks for raw flesh in the swelling.

## Leviticus 13:11

If he sees all these signs, the disease is long-lasting.

The priest declares the person unclean.

He does not need a waiting period to decide.

## Leviticus 13:12

Sometimes the condition spreads from head to foot.

It covers all the skin the priest can see.

## Leviticus 13:13

The priest examines the person.

If all the skin has turned white, he declares the person clean.

## Leviticus 13:14

But when raw flesh appears, the person is unclean.

## Leviticus 13:15

When the priest sees the raw flesh, he declares the person unclean.

The raw flesh is a sign of skin disease.

## Leviticus 13:16

If the raw flesh changes and turns white, the person goes back to the priest.

## Leviticus 13:17

The priest checks again.

If the affected area has turned white, he declares the person clean.

The person is clean.

**Pause and answer**

Where did the person go when the raw skin turned white?

## A mark where a boil healed

## Leviticus 13:18

Suppose someone has had a boil on the skin.

The boil has healed.

**Reading help**

A boil is a swollen sore. A scar is the mark left after skin heals.

## Leviticus 13:19

A white swelling or a reddish-white spot appears where the boil was.

The person must show it to the priest.

## Leviticus 13:20

The priest looks at the spot.

If it looks deeper than the skin and the hair there has turned white, he declares the person unclean.

A skin disease has appeared where the boil was.

## Leviticus 13:21

Suppose the spot has faded and looks only skin deep.

There is no white hair in it.

The priest keeps the person apart for seven days.

## Leviticus 13:22

If the spot spreads on the skin, it is an infection.

The priest declares the person unclean.

## Leviticus 13:23

If the spot stays the same without spreading, it is a scar from the boil.

The priest declares the person clean.

**Pause and answer**

What did the priest call the mark that stayed the same after a boil healed?

## A mark where skin was burned

## Leviticus 13:24

Suppose someone has a burn.

The raw skin turns reddish-white or white.

## Leviticus 13:25

The priest checks the spot.

Has the hair turned white?

Does the spot look deeper than the skin?

If both are true, a skin disease has appeared in the burn.

The priest declares the person unclean.

## Leviticus 13:26

Suppose the spot has faded and looks only skin deep.

There is no white hair in it.

The priest keeps the person apart for seven days.

## Leviticus 13:27

On the seventh day the priest looks again.

If the spot has spread, it is a skin disease.

He declares the person unclean.

## Leviticus 13:28

If the spot has faded without spreading or changing its shape, it is swelling from the burn.

The priest declares the person clean.

The mark is a scar.

**Pause and answer**

When did the priest look again at the faded spot?

## A scaly patch on the head or chin

## Leviticus 13:29

A man or woman may have a sore on the head or chin.

## Leviticus 13:30

The priest examines the sore.

If it looks deeper than the skin and has thin yellow hair, he declares the person unclean.

It is a scaly disease of the head or chin.

## Leviticus 13:31

Suppose the scaly patch looks only skin deep.

There is no black hair in it.

The priest keeps the person apart for seven days.

## Leviticus 13:32

On the seventh day the priest looks again.

He checks whether the patch has spread, whether yellow hair has appeared, and whether the patch looks deeper than the skin.

## Leviticus 13:33

If none of these signs appears, the person shaves around the patch.

The patch itself stays unshaved.

The priest keeps the person apart for seven more days.

## Leviticus 13:34

On the seventh day the priest examines the patch again.

If it has not spread and looks only skin deep, he declares the person clean.

The person washes their clothes and is clean.

**Pause and answer**

Which part did the person leave unshaved?

## Checking a patch again

## Leviticus 13:35

Sometimes the patch spreads after the priest has declared the person clean.

## Leviticus 13:36

Then the priest looks again.

If the patch has spread, the person is unclean.

The priest does not need to look for yellow hair.

## Leviticus 13:37

Suppose the patch has stayed the same size and black hair has grown in it.

The patch has healed.

The priest declares the person clean.

**Pause and answer**

What color was the new hair on the healed patch?

## White spots and hair loss

## Leviticus 13:38

A man or woman may have white spots on the skin.

## Leviticus 13:39

The priest examines the spots.

If they are dull white, the person has a harmless rash.

The person is clean.

## Leviticus 13:40

A man who loses his hair and becomes bald is clean.

## Leviticus 13:41

A man who loses the hair above his forehead is also clean.

## Leviticus 13:42

But a reddish-white sore on the bald head or forehead is a sign of skin disease.

## Leviticus 13:43

The priest examines the swelling on the bald head or forehead.

He looks to see if it is reddish-white like a skin disease.

## Leviticus 13:44

If it is, the man has the disease.

The priest declares him unclean because of the sore on his head.

**Pause and answer**

What did the law say about a man who had simply lost his hair?

## Life outside the camp

## Leviticus 13:45

The person with the disease must wear torn clothes and let their hair hang loose.

They must cover their mouth and call out, “Unclean!

Unclean!”

## Leviticus 13:46

The person remains unclean as long as the disease lasts.

During that time the person must live alone outside the camp.

## Mildew on cloth or leather

## Leviticus 13:47

Mildew can mark a piece of clothing made of wool or linen.

**Reading help**

Mildew is a growth that can leave a mark on cloth or leather.

## Leviticus 13:48

The rule also covers woven or knitted wool or linen.

It covers anything made of leather.

## Leviticus 13:49

A green or red mark on any of these things is a sign of mildew.

Show the item to the priest.

## Leviticus 13:50

The priest examines the mark.

He sets the item apart for seven days.

## Leviticus 13:51

On the seventh day the priest looks again.

If the mark has spread, the mildew is harmful.

The item is unclean, whatever it is used for.

## Leviticus 13:52

The priest burns the whole item because the mildew is harmful.

This applies to wool, linen and leather.

**Pause and answer**

What showed that the mildew was harmful?

## What happens after washing

## Leviticus 13:53

Suppose the priest sees that the mark has not spread in the cloth or leather.

## Leviticus 13:54

He orders someone to wash the item.

Then it stays apart for seven more days.

## Leviticus 13:55

The priest examines the washed item.

If the mark looks the same, the item is unclean even if the mark has not spread.

Burn it whether the damage is on the front or the back.

## Leviticus 13:56

If washing has made the mark fade, the priest cuts out the affected part of the cloth or leather.

## Leviticus 13:57

If the mark comes back, the mildew is spreading.

Burn the whole item.

## Leviticus 13:58

If the mark disappears after washing, wash the item again.

Then it is clean.

| What happened to the mark after washing? | What happened to the item? |
| --- | --- |
| It looked the same. | Burn the item. |
| It faded. | Cut out the marked part. |
| It came back. | Burn the item. |
| It disappeared. | Wash the item again. |

## Leviticus 13:59

These rules cover mildew on wool and linen, woven or knitted, and on leather.

The priest uses them to declare an item clean or unclean.
